# Sariyyat ʿUbaydah b. al-Ḥārith ilā Baṭn Rābigh (سرية عبيدة بن الحارث إلى بطن رابغ)
*The Expedition of ʿUbaydah b. al-Ḥārith to Baṭn Rābigh — The First Arrow Shot in Islam and the Escape of al-Miqdād and ʿUtbah*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 6, *al-Bāb al-Khāmis fī Sariyyat ʿUbaydah b. al-Ḥārith... ilā Baṭn Rābigh fī Shawwāl min al-Sanah al-Ūlā* (pp. 13–14), cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 4, p. 407, no. 5565; Vol. 3, p. 62, no. 3192). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the authoritative accounts of [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], and [[Ibn Isḥāq]].

---

## 1. Context & Commission

In the month of Shawwāl in the year one AH, eight months following the Hijrah, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commissioned his eldest second cousin, the venerable elder of Banū al-Muṭṭalib, **[[ʿUbaydah b. al-Ḥārith]]** ﵁, to lead a mobile reconnaissance and interdiction detachment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).

The force consisted of sixty mounted warriors drawn exclusively from **[[al-Muhājirūn]]**, with no members of the Anṣār included (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13). 

The Prophet ﷺ tied for him a white military standard (*liwāʾ abyaḍ*), which was borne by his nephew, the Badri pioneer **[[Misṭaḥ b. Uthāthah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).

---

## 2. The Encounter at Baṭn Rābigh

ʿUbaydah ﵁ and his sixty riders advanced along the caravan trail until they intercepted a large force of two hundred polytheists of **[[Quraysh]]** encamped at a watering place called **Aḥyāʾ** at the lower end of Thanyat al-Marrah in **Baṭn Rābigh** (*Baṭn Rābigh*), approximately ten miles from **[[al-Juḥfah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).

The polytheists were commanded by **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** (though alternate narrations mention Mikraz b. Ḥafṣ or ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl; al-Wāqidī and Ibn Saʿd confirm Abū Sufyān as the most established report) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13). 

The polytheists had veered off the main road to pasture their riding beasts (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).

---

## 3. The First Arrow Shot in Islam (*Awwal Sahm*)

When the two opposing parties encountered one another, they engaged in long-range missile exchanges without drawing swords or entering hand-to-hand combat (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).

During this skirmish, the young knight **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁ stepped forward before the Muslim ranks. Shielded by his companions, Saʿd emptied his quiver (*nathara kinānatahu*), which contained twenty arrows (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13). 

Saʿd loosed his arrows with legendary marksmanship, and every single arrow struck an enemy combatant or his mount (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13):

> «رَمَى سَعْدُ بْنُ أَبِي وَقَّاصٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى عَنْهُ بِسَهْمٍ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، فَكَانَ أَوَّلَ سَهْمٍ رُمِيَ بِهِ فِي الْإِسْلَامِ!»
> *"Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ ﵁ shot an arrow in the path of Allāh, and it was the very first arrow ever shot in the history of Islam!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

The accuracy and intensity of Saʿd's archery sowed panic among the Quraysh guards, who believed the Muslims were a vanguard concealing a massive army behind them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).

---

## 4. The Dramatic Escape of al-Miqdād and ʿUtbah

During the confusion of the archery exchange, two secret Muslims who had been trapped in Makkah and forced to march out with the polytheists broke away from the pagan camp and fled across the lines to the Muslim side (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13):
1. **[[al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr]] (al-Kindī)** ﵁ (confederate of Banū Zuhrah).
2. **[[ʿUtbah b. Ghazwān]]** ﵁ (confederate of Banū Nawfal b. ʿAbd Manāf).

Both men had embraced Islam early in Makkah but had lacked the tribal protection to migrate openly. They had joined the Quraysh caravan under the guise of traveling merchants specifically seeking an opportunity to reunite with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13; *al-Iṣābah*, 4:407). 

Their successful escape was celebrated as a major victory, adding two of the greatest future generals and heroes of Islam to the community in Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).

---

## 5. Disengagement and Strategic Legacy

Following the archery duel and the defection of al-Miqdād and ʿUtbah, both sides withdrew under rearguard protection (*inṣarafa al-farīqāni ʿalā ḥāmiyatihim*) without bloodshed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).

The expedition established several vital precedents:
- It confirmed that the Muslims possessed the military initiative to strike deep into the coastal route near al-Juḥfah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:13).
- It marked the martial debut of **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁ as the premier archer of Islam, anticipating his immortal role at Uḥud where the Prophet ﷺ said to him: «ارْمِ فِدَاكَ أَبِي وَأُمِّي» (*"Shoot, may my father and mother be sacrificed for you!"*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).
- It safely delivered al-Miqdād and ʿUtbah into the ranks of the Muhājirūn, who would both fight as prominent champions at Badr the following year (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:407).
