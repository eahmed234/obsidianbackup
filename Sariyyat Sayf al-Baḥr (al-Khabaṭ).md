# Sariyyat Sayf al-Baḥr (al-Khabaṭ) (سرية سيف البحر - سرية الخبط)
*The Expedition of the Coast & The Foliage of the Acacia Trees — The Severe Trial of Famine, The Generosity of Qays b. Saʿd, and The Miracle of the Colossal Whale*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Sources
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād*, Volume 6, *al-Bāb al-Thāmin wal-Arbaʿūn fī Sariyyat Abī ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ rḍy Allāh taʿālā ʿanhu... wa-tuʿrafu bi-Sariyyat al-Khabaṭ wa-Sariyyat Sayf al-Baḥr* (pp. 176–183), cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 3, pp. 475–477, no. 4410). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the canonical transmissions of *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[al-Wāqidī]], and [[Ibn Saʿd]].

---

## 1. Strategic Context & Mandate

In the month of Rajab of the year eight AH (shortly prior to the Conquest of Makkah), the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commissioned **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁ at the head of a detachment of **three hundred** Muhājirūn and Anṣār warriors (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176).

The expedition was directed toward the Red Sea coastal littoral (*Sayf al-Baḥr*) in the territory of **Juhaynah**, approximately five nights' journey from Medina, to observe movements along the coastal trade route and maintain regional deterrence (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176).

The detachment departed with limited provisions; the Prophet ﷺ provided them with a single leather bag (*jirāb*) filled with dates, finding nothing else to furnish (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176, 180).

---

## 2. The Trial of Severe Hunger & The Foliage (*al-Khabaṭ*)

As the detachment advanced along the arid coastal wilderness, their food supply was exhausted (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176). 

Abū ʿUbaydah rationed the remaining dates with extreme scrupulousness, issuing each warrior a handful, then a small cluster, until the rationing was reduced to **a single date per soldier each day** (*tamrah tamrah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176, 180). **[[Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh]]** ﵄ was asked: *"What good was a single date to you?"* He replied:
> «لَقَدْ وَجَدْنَا فَقْدَهَا حِينَ فَنِيَتْ! كُنَّا نَمُصُّهَا كَمَا يَمُصُّ الصَّبِيُّ، ثُمَّ نَشْرَبُ عَلَيْهَا مِنَ الْمَاءِ فَتَكْفِينَا يَوْمَنَا إِلَى اللَّيْلِ!»
> *"We truly recognized its immense value when it was completely finished! We would suck upon the single date just as an infant sucks, and then drink water over it, and it would suffice us for the entire day until nightfall!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*).

### The Beating of the Acacia Trees (*al-Khabaṭ*)
When even the single date ran out, the soldiers were reduced to beating the thorny *Salam* and acacia desert trees with their bowstaves and sticks so that the dry leaves (*al-khabaṭ*) fell upon their garments (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176, 182). They softened the harsh leaves with water and chewed them to sustain life, which caused the sides of their mouths to develop sores and ulcers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176, 182). 

Because of this intense trial, the expedition was perpetually known in Sīrah annals as **Sariyyat al-Khabaṭ** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176).

### The Nobility of Qays b. Saʿd b. ʿUbādah
Witnessing the suffering of the army, the young champion **Qays b. Saʿd b. ʿUbādah** ﵁ stepped forward (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176–177). Having no cash on hand, he proclaimed: *"Who will sell me a camel to slaughter here, and I will repay him with dried dates in Medina?"* A Bedouin of Juhaynah stepped forward, recognized Qays as the son of the supreme chief of Yathrib, and sold him slaughter camels on credit (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:177).

Qays slaughtered a camel on the first day, another on the second, and a third on the third day, feeding the famished army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176). Seeing this, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ expressed astonishment at the youth contracting debts without personal property, and the commander **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁ intervened to forbid Qays from further slaughters, fearing he would exhaust his mounts and drown his father's house in unpayable debt (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176–177). 

When the army returned to Medina and Caliph ʿUmar reported the matter to the Prophet ﷺ, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ smiled and affirmed: *«إِنَّ الْجُودَ لَمِنْ شِيمَةِ أَهْلِ ذَلِكَ الْبَيْتِ!»* (*"Indeed, generosity is the innate character of the people of that household!"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:177).

---

## 3. The Miracle of the Colossal Whale (*Dābbat al-ʿAnbar*)

When the army reached the shore of the Red Sea in an extreme state of exhaustion, the sea surged violently and cast ashore an enormous marine beast known as **al-ʿAnbar** (a colossal whale) resembling a vast rocky hill (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176, 181).

At first, when the companions sighted it from afar, they thought it was an immense mountain (*tilāl ḍakhm*). Approaching it, they found a dead creature (*maytah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176). 

Abū ʿUbaydah initially hesitated, saying: *"It is carrion (*maytah*)."* But considering their dire extremity and their sacred mission, he exercised his jurisprudential authority:
> «لَا، بَلْ نَحْنُ رُسُلُ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَآلِهِ وَسَلَّمَ، وَفِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، وَقَدِ اضْطُرِرْتُمْ، فَكُلُوا!»
> *"No! Rather, we are the envoys of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, marching in the cause of Allāh, and you have been compelled by dire necessity, so eat!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

### The Vastness of the Sea Creature
The entire army of three hundred men remained encamped by the whale for **half a month** (or eighteen to twenty days), carving huge chunks of meat, roasting and boiling it, and extracting immense barrels of pure oil from its eye sockets (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176, 181). Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh ﵄ testified (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176, 181):
- The soldiers scooped oil out of the whale's eye socket with large vessels; **thirteen men** could sit comfortably inside its single eye cavity without crowding!
- They carved strips of salted fat and meat to take back to Medina.
- When they prepared to march home, Abū ʿUbaydah ordered one of the beast's ribs (*ḍilʿ*) to be propped upright on the sand. He then selected the **tallest man** in the army (Qays b. Saʿd) mounted upon the **tallest camel**, and the rider passed directly beneath the arched rib without his head or spear touching it! (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176, 181).

---

## 4. The Prophetic Confirmation in Medina

When the expedition returned to Medina in full health and vigor, they narrated their experiences to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176). The Prophet ﷺ comforted their hearts, erased any lingering doubts concerning the legality of the meat, and affirmed:

> «هُوَ رِزْقٌ أَخْرَجَهُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ، فَهَلْ مَعَكُمْ مِنْ لَحْمِهِ شَيْءٌ تُطْعِمُونَا؟»
> *"It was provision that Allāh brought forth for you! Do you have any of its meat with you that you could feed us?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

A portion of the dried whale meat was brought to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and he ate from it (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:176). This prophetic endorsement permanently established the legal principle in Islamic jurisprudence:
> «هُوَ الطَّهُورُ مَاؤُهُ، الْحِلُّ مَيْتَتُهُ»
> *"Its water is purifying, and its dead creatures are lawful."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:179).
