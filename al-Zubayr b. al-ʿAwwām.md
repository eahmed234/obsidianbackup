# al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām (الزبير بن العوام الأسدي) ﵁
*Abū ʿAbdillāh al-Asadī al-Qurashī — Ḥawārī Rasūlillāh (The Disciple of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ), Knight of Islam*

## 1. Lineage, Sacred Kinship & Physical Description
### Patrilineal Descent & Clan Ties
He is **al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām b. Khuwaylid b. Asad b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. Quṣayy b. Kilāb al-Qurashī al-Asadī** (kunyas: **Abū ʿAbdillāh** and **Abū al-Ṭāhir**; title: **Ḥawārī Rasūlillāh**) (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:457; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:237, 312).

His lineage converges with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at **Quṣayy b. Kilāb** in the fifth generation (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:457). As a member of **Banū Asad b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā**, he was the paternal nephew of the Mother of the Believers **[[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid]]** ﵂ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:312).

### Maternal Lineage & Prophetic Blood
- **Mother**: **[[Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵂, paternal aunt of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and full sister of the Lion of Allāh **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:457; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:312).
- **Sacred Kinship**: Al-Zubayr was thus the direct first cousin of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ on his mother’s side, while also being the nephew of the Prophet's first wife Khadījah. His mother initially kunya'd him Abū al-Ṭāhir after her brother al-Zubayr b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, but he later took the kunya Abū ʿAbdillāh after his eldest son (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:457).

### Childhood & Early Endurance
Following the early death of his father al-ʿAwwām, al-Zubayr was raised by his mother Ṣafiyyah. She disciplined him sternly, explaining in verse that she hardened him so that he would become wise, route enemy armies, and return victorious with war spoils (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:458). 

Embracing Islam at twelve (or eight) years of age through **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, he was subjected to brutal torture by his uncle Nawfal b. Khuwaylid, who wrapped him in a coarse palm mat (*ḥaṣīr*) and suspended him over a smoking fire to suffocate him into apostasy; the youth steadfastly proclaimed: *"I will never return to disbelief!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:457).

### Physical Appearance & Battle Scars
- **Stature**: Exceptionally tall; when mounted on a horse or camel, his long legs nearly brushed the ground (*ṭawīlan takhuṭṭu riglāhu al-arḍ idhā rakib*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:458).
- **Features**: Lean, light-bearded, brown-complexioned (*ādam*), possessing immense athletic agility and martial endurance (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:458).
- **Combat Scars**: His son ʿUrwah recorded: *"My father bore three deep sword clefts across his torso into which I could insert my fingers: two sustained at Badr and one sustained at the Battle of the Yarmūk"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:459).

## 2. Marriages, Kinship Networks & Progeny
Al-Zubayr formed deep marital alliances linking his house to the caliphal families and the prominent tribes of Arabia:

1. **[[Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ (*Dhāt al-Niṭāqayn*):
   - Eldest daughter of Caliph **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and sister of **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂. She migrated while heavily pregnant and gave birth in Qubāʾ to the first child born to the Muhājirūn in Medina: **[[ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr]]** ﵄ (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:458).
   - **Children**: Bore him **ʿAbdullāh**, the eminent jurist and chronicler **ʿUrwah b. al-Zubayr**, **al-Mundhir**, **ʿĀṣim**, **al-Muhājir**, **Khadījah**, **Umm al-Ḥasan**, and **ʿĀʾishah**.
2. **Umm Kulthūm bint ʿUqbah b. Abī Muʿayṭ**:
   - Noblewoman of Banū Umayyah (maternal half-sister of Caliph ʿUthmān); bore him **Zaynab**.
3. **al-Ḥalāl (Umm Khālid) bint Khālid b. Saʿīd b. al-ʿĀṣ**:
   - Born during the migration in Abyssinia, fluent in the Abyssinian tongue; bore him **Khālid** and **ʿAmr**.
4. **al-Rabāb bint Unayf b. ʿUbayd al-Kalbiyyah**:
   - From Banū Kalb; bore him **Muṣʿab b. al-Zubayr** (the famed governor of Iraq), **Ḥamzah**, and **Ramlah**.
5. **ʿĀtikah bint Zayd b. ʿAmr b. Nufayl** ﵂:
   - Cousin of ʿUmar and sister of Saʿīd b. Zayd; bore him **Iyāḍ**.
6. **Tumāḍir bint al-Aṣbagh al-Kalbiyyah**:
   - Formerly wife of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf ﵁.

### The Universal Trustee of the Ṣaḥābah
Al-Zubayr possessed massive commercial wealth, receiving tributary income from one thousand retainers, yet he gave it all in open charity (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:460). Because of his unimpeachable trustworthiness, foremost Companions designated al-Zubayr as the sole executor (*waṣī*) of their estates and guardian of their orphan children, including **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]**, **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]**, **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd]]**, **[[al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr]]**, and Abū al-ʿĀṣ b. al-Rabīʿ (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:460). Caliph ʿUmar declared: *"Truly, al-Zubayr is a pillar from among the pillars of the religion!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:460).

## 3. Key Sīrah Milestones
### The First Sword Unsheathed in Islam
In early Makkah, a false rumor spread that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had been captured and killed. Young al-Zubayr immediately unsheathed his sword and pushed through the crowded streets of Makkah until he reached the Prophet ﷺ at the upper part of the city. The Prophet ﷺ marveled at his courage and made a special supplication for al-Zubayr and his sword, conferring upon him the everlasting distinction of being the first man to unsheathe a weapon for the sake of Allāh (*awwal man salla sayfan fī sabīlillāh*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:459; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:312).

### The Golden Knight of Badr (2 AH)
At **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, al-Zubayr and **[[al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr]]** were the only two cavalrymen in the entire Muslim army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:40). Al-Zubayr commanded the right wing clad in a striking yellow turban (*ʿimāmah ṣafrāʾ*). In an unprecedented celestial honor, the archangel Jibrīl ﵊ and the heavenly hosts descended to battle in the exact attire and likeness of al-Zubayr’s yellow turban (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:459; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:52; 11:313).

### Steadfastness at Uḥud & Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad (3 AH)
When the Muslim lines were breached at **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, al-Zubayr was among the staunch defenders around the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205). The following morning, wounded and exhausted, he rode out with Abū Bakr at **[[Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad]]** to pursue Quraysh, fulfilling the praise of Sūrat Āl ʿImrān (3:172: *«Those who responded to Allāh and the Messenger after injury had struck them»*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:459; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308).

### The Disciple of the Prophet at the Trench (5 AH)
During **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**, amid freezing winds and fear of the treachery of **[[Banū Qurayẓah]]**, the Prophet ﷺ asked thrice: *“Who will go and bring us intelligence of the people?”* Each time, al-Zubayr alone volunteered. Leaping upon his horse, he penetrated deep behind enemy lines and returned with precise tactical reports (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:459; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:380; 11:313). Thereupon the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ proclaimed:
> «إِنَّ لِكُلِّ نَبِيٍّ حَوَارِيًّا، وَحَوَارِيَّ الزُّبَيْرُ!»
> *"Truly, every Prophet has a disciple (*ḥawārī*), and my disciple is al-Zubayr!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:459; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:380; 11:313).

### Slaying Yāsir at Khaybar (7 AH)
At **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]**, the formidable Jewish champion Yāsir stepped out challenging the Muslims to single combat (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:126). His mother Ṣafiyyah cried out: *"O Messenger of Allāh, will my son be killed?!"* The Prophet ﷺ replied: *"Rather, your son will kill him, if Allāh wills!"* Al-Zubayr advanced and split Yāsir in two with a single downward stroke of his sword (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:126).

### Rawḍat Khākh & Fatḥ Makkah (8 AH)
Dispatched alongside **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** to intercept the clandestine letter of Ḥāṭib b. Abī Baltaʿah at Rawḍat Khākh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:202). During **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**, he commanded the left wing of the grand army, carrying the supreme banner of the Prophet ﷺ and planting it victoriously at al-Ḥajūn (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:208).

## 4. Caliphal Shūrā, Martyrdom & Burial
Appointed by Caliph ʿUmar as one of the six electors of the Caliphal Shūrā in the year twenty-three AH, al-Zubayr was revered as one of the greatest statesmen and military commanders of the early Caliphate (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:457).

During the turmoil of the Battle of the Camel in Jumādā al-Ūlā 36 AH, **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ called al-Zubayr between the battle lines and reminded him of the Prophet's words: *"You shall surely fight ʿAlī while you are in the wrong."* Remembering the prophecy, al-Zubayr immediately sheath his sword, turned his mount, and departed the field of conflict (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:460). 

As he stopped to perform the prayer at Wādī al-Sibāʿ, he was stalked and treacherously struck down in prostration by ʿAmr b. Jurmūz (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:460). When the assassin brought al-Zubayr’s sword to ʿAlī expecting a reward, ʿAlī banished him, declaring: *"Give tidings of the Fire to the murderer of the son of Ṣafiyyah!"* ʿAlī then kissed the blade of al-Zubayr, weeping and proclaiming: *"By Allāh, how frequently this sword removed anguish from the face of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:460; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:315). He died at the age of sixty-four and was buried at Wādī al-Sibāʿ (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:460; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:312).
