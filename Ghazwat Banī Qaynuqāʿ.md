# Ghazwat Banī Qaynuqāʿ (غزوة بني قينقاع)
*The Siege of Banū Qaynuqāʿ — The First Breach of the Madinan Covenant, The Intercession of the Hypocrite, and the Expulsion to Syria*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived directly from ***[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād fī Sīrat Khayr al-ʿIbād]]*** by **[[al-Ṣāliḥī|Imām Shams al-Dīn Muḥammad b. Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī al-Shāmī]]** (d. 942 AH), Volume 4, *al-Bāb al-Thānī ʿAshar fī Ghazwat Banī Qaynuqāʿ* (pp. 179–181), cross-referenced with biographical entries in ***al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah*** by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī.

---

## 1. Geopolitical Context & The Covenant of Medina

Following the Hijrah of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ to **[[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah]]**, the non-Muslim populations of the Arabian Peninsula stood in three distinct postures (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179):
1. **The Belligerents**: [[Quraysh]], who openly waged war against Islam.
2. **The Watchful Neutrals**: Outlying Bedouin tribes waiting to see how the struggle developed, including those inwardly sympathetic (such as **[[Banū Khuzāʿah]]**) and those hostile (such as Banū Bakr).
3. **The Covenanted Allies**: The Jewish tribes of Medina — **[[Banū Qaynuqāʿ]]**, **[[Banū al-Naḍīr]]**, and **[[Banū Qurayẓah]]** — with whom the Prophet ﷺ ratified the comprehensive constitutional treaty (**[[Dustūr al-Madīnah]]**).

Under this treaty, every group retained its domestic autonomy and religion while binding itself to joint defensive mutual pacts, explicitly stipulating that neither party would aid an external aggressor against Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179). 

Among the three Jewish tribes, **[[Banū Qaynuqāʿ]]** held a uniquely formidable position: they were an urban, artisan population of goldsmiths (*ṣāghah*), blacksmiths, and traders, inhabiting fortified residential compounds (*āṭām*) south-west of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179). Renowned as the bravest and most militarized Jewish clan, they fielded **seven hundred combat-ready warriors** — **four hundred without armor** (*ḥāsir*) and **three hundred clad in heavy coats of mail** (*dāriʿ*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180). Long established as military confederates (*ḥulafāʾ*) of the Khazraj, their principal confederate ties ran to both **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** and the noble companion **[[ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).

## 2. The Triumph of Badr and the Outbreak of Insolence

The stunning Muslim victory over the Qurayshite aristocracy at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** on the 17th of Ramaḍān, 2 AH, profoundly shook the political equilibrium of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179). Rather than welcoming stability, Banū Qaynuqāʿ were consumed by jealousy and indignation (*al-baghy wal-ḥasad*), becoming the very first of the Jewish tribes to repudiate their sworn pact (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).

Seeking to avert bloodshed, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ gathered their assembly in their commercial hub, **Sūq Banī Qaynuqāʿ**, and addressed them with a direct prophetic admonition:
> «يَا مَعْشَرَ يَهُودَ أَسْلِمُوا، فَوَاللَّهِ إِنَّكُمْ لَتَعْلَمُونَ أَنِّي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ! يَا مَعْشَرَ يَهُودَ احْذَرُوا مِنَ اللَّهِ مِثْلَ مَا نَزَلَ بِقُرَيْشٍ مِنَ النِّقْمَةِ فَأَسْلِمُوا، فَإِنَّكُمْ قَدْ عَرَفْتُمْ أَنِّي مُرْسَلٌ، تَجِدُونَ ذَلِكَ فِي كِتَابِكُمْ وَعَهْدِ اللَّهِ إِلَيْكُمْ»
> *"O assembly of Jews, embrace Islam! For by Allāh, you surely know that I am the Messenger of Allāh! O assembly of Jews, beware lest there descend upon you from Allāh the like of the retribution that befell Quraysh, and enter Islam; for you have recognized that I am a Messenger sent forth, finding that inscribed in your Scripture and Allāh's covenant unto you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).

Banū Qaynuqāʿ met the Prophet's ﷺ appeal with arrogant defiance, mocking the victory at Badr:
> *"O Muḥammad! Do you think we are like your people?! Do not be deceived that you encountered a folk devoid of knowledge in the art of war and seized an opportunity against them. By Allāh, if you fight us, you shall surely know that we are real men!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).

Regarding this bellicose repudiation, **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbbās]]** ﵄ narrated that Allāh revealed the opening challenge of **[[Sūrat Āl ʿImrān]]**:
$$\text{قُل لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا سَتُغْلَبُونَ وَتُحْشَرُونَ إِلَىٰ جَهَنَّمَ ۚ وَبِئْسَ الْمِهَادُ ۝ قَدْ كَانَ لَكُمْ آيَةٌ فِي فِئَتَيْنِ الْتَقَتَا ۖ فِئَةٌ تُقَاتِلُ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَأُخْرَىٰ كَافِرَةٌ}$$
> *"Say to those who disbelieve: You will be overcome and gathered together to Hell, and wretched is the resting place! Already there has been for you a sign in the two armies that met [at Badr]: one army fighting in the cause of Allāh and another of disbelievers..."* (Qurʾān 3:12–13; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).

## 3. The Marketplace Provocation (*Casus Belli*)

While tensions escalated, a provocative outrage occurred within **Sūq Banī Qaynuqāʿ** that precipitated immediate armed conflict (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179):
1. An Arab Muslim woman arrived from the countryside carrying dairy produce or market goods (*jalb*) to sell in the market. Having sold her wares, she sat before a Jewish goldsmith's shop to purchase jewelry.
2. The marketplace crowd gathered around her, demanding that she unveil her face. She refused and maintained her modesty.
3. The goldsmith secretly took the lower hem of her outer garment and fastened it with a thorn to the upper back of her cloak without her awareness.
4. When the woman stood up, her modesty was exposed, causing the gathered Jewish artisans to burst into derisive laughter and mockery.
5. The Muslim woman cried out for assistance. A Muslim bystander witnessed the humiliation, leaped upon the Jewish goldsmith, and struck him down dead.
6. The Jewish marketplace mob instantly swarmed the Muslim, overpowering and killing him.
7. The slain Muslim's family raised the alarm among the believers (*istakhraja ahl al-Muslim al-Muslimīn*), casting the city into turmoil as Banū Qaynuqāʿ formally tore up their covenant (*nabadhū al-ʿahd*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).

Allāh then revealed the injunction regarding overt anticipatory treachery:
$$\text{وَإِمَّا تَخَافَنَّ مِن قَوْمٍ خِيَانَةً فَانبِذْ إِلَيْهِمْ عَلَىٰ سَوَاءٍ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ الْخَائِنِينَ}$$
> *"And if you fear treachery from a people, throw [their treaty] back to them upon equal terms. Indeed, Allāh does not love the treacherous."* (Qurʾān 8:58; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).

The Prophet ﷺ declared: *«إِنَّمَا أَخَافُ مِنْ بَنِي قَيْنُقَاعَ»* (*"Indeed, I fear treachery from Banū Qaynuqāʿ!"*), and ordered immediate military mobilization (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).

---

## 4. Mobilization, March & The Fifteen-Day Siege

- **Date of Departure**: Saturday, 15 Shawwāl 2 AH (exactly twenty months after the Prophet's ﷺ Hijrah, and less than one month after the return from Badr) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:179).
- **The Great Banner**: The Prophet ﷺ entrusted the supreme white banner (*al-liwāʾ al-abyaḍ*) to his lion-hearted uncle **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180). Ibn Saʿd notes that specialized company pennants (*al-rāyāt*) had not yet been adopted in this early period (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).
- **Civil Deputy over Medina**: The Prophet ﷺ appointed the veteran Anṣārī leader **[[Abū Lubābah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir]]** ﵁ to govern the civic affairs of Medina in his absence (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).
- **The Encirclement**: Upon the approach of the Muslim army, Banū Qaynuqāʿ retreated into their fortified compounds and barred their gates. The Prophet ﷺ laid an ironclad siege (*ashadda al-ḥiṣār*) around their strongholds, cutting off all supplies, trade, and communication with the outside oasis (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).
- **Duration**: The siege was maintained relentlessly for **fifteen consecutive nights** (*khams ʿashrata laylah*), until terror was cast into their hearts (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

---

## 5. Unconditional Surrender & Custody

Realizing that no allied clans could relieve them and paralyzed by despair, Banū Qaynuqāʿ capitulated unconditionally to the sovereign judgment of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*nazalū ʿalā ḥukm Rasūlillāh ﷺ*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

The terms of capitulation dictated:
- All their extensive wealth, tools, armor, and properties were forfeited to the Prophet ﷺ.
- Their women and children were spared harm.
- The combatants surrendered themselves to complete detention.

The Prophet ﷺ commanded that the seven hundred male combatants be bound in ropes. He assigned the responsibility of binding and guarding the prisoners to the Badri companion **[[al-Mundhir b. Qudāmah]] al-Sulamī ﵁** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:172).

---

## 6. Loyalty vs. Hypocrisy: ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit & Ibn Ubayy

The capitulation of Banū Qaynuqāʿ brought into sharp relief the stark contrast between sincere faith and opportunistic hypocrisy through two Khazrajite chieftains who held identical pre-Islamic confederacies (*ḥilf*) with the besieged clan:

### The Faith & Disavowal of ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit ﵁
**[[ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit]]** ﵁ went directly to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and renounced his ancestral alliance with Banū Qaynuqāʿ, placing their fate entirely in the hands of the Prophet ﷺ:
> «يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، أَتَوَلَّى اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ وَالْمُؤْمِنِينَ، وَأَبْرَأُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ مِنْ حِلْفِ هَؤُلَاءِ الْقَوْمِ وَوَلَايَتِهِمْ!»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh, I take as my patrons Allāh, His Messenger, and the believers, and I disavow to Allāh and His Messenger the alliance and guardianship of these men!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

### The Arrogant Physical Assault of ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl
Conversely, the arch-hypocrite **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** rushed forward to save his political clients. Approaching the Prophet ﷺ, he demanded:
> *"O Muḥammad, treat my clients well!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ turned his face away and gave him no reply. Ibn Ubayy repeated his demand, and the Prophet ﷺ again turned away. Losing composure, Ibn Ubayy brazenly thrust his hand into the collar of the Prophet's ﷺ coat of mail, known as ***Dhāt al-Fuḍūl*** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

The Prophet's ﷺ face darkened with severe anger until shadows of wrath (*ẓulal*) were seen upon his countenance, and he commanded sternly:
> «وَيْحَكَ أَرْسِلْنِي!»
> *"Woe to you, release me!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

He repeated: *«Wayḥaka arsilnī!»*, but Ibn Ubayy clutched the armor obstinately, shouting:
> *"By Allāh, I will not release you until you treat my clients with goodness! Four hundred unarmored men and three hundred armored in coats of mail who shielded me against every foe, and you would harvest them in a single morning?! By Allāh, I am a man who fears the sudden turns of fortune (*al-dawāʾir*)!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

Seeing his obstinacy and wishing to demonstrate magnanimity while neutralizing the internal threat, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ relented from capital punishment, proclaiming:
> «خَلُّوهُمْ، لَعَنَهُمُ اللَّهُ وَلَعَنَهُ مَعَهُمْ!»
> *"Release them; may Allāh curse them, and curse him alongside them!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

---

## 7. The Decree of Banishment (*al-Ijlāʾ*)

Spared from execution, Banū Qaynuqāʿ were sentenced to permanent banishment from Medina:
- They were granted a grace period of **three days** to collect their families and depart the territory of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).
- The Prophet ﷺ commissioned **[[ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit]] ﵁** (and according to other reports, **[[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]] ﵁**) to supervise their eviction and ensure that none lingered past the third day (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).
- Banū Qaynuqāʿ migrated northward to **Adhriʿāt** (modern-day Daraa in the Hawrān plateau of southern Syria) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180–181). Their settlement in Syria proved ephemeral, and their communal footprint dwindled rapidly (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

---

## 8. Captured Spoils (*al-Ghanāʾim*) & The First Khumus

Because Banū Qaynuqāʿ surrendered their properties intact, vast armaments and manufacturing equipment were confiscated (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180):

### The Trustee of Spoils
The Prophet ﷺ appointed the steadfast Awsī companion **[[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]] ﵁** to take charge of collecting, cataloging, and safeguarding the seized properties (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

### Selected Prophetic Weapons
From their specialized armories, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ selected (*iṣṭafā*):
- **Three Bows**:
  1. *al-Katūm* (The Silent/Muffled Bow, which was later broken in the Prophet's ﷺ hand during **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**).
  2. *al-Rawḥāʾ*.
  3. *al-Bayḍāʾ*.
- **Two Coats of Mail**:
  1. *al-Sughdiyyah* (named after Sogdia).
  2. *Fiḍḍah* (Silver).
- **Three Spears** (*thalāthat rimāḥ*).
- **Three Swords**:
  1. *Sayf Qalʿī* (a blade of fine artisanal steel).
  2. *Battār* (The Cleaver).
  3. A third unnamed sword.

### The Historic Precedent of the Khumus
In their residential quarters, the Muslims uncovered immense caches of weaponry and the specialized metalworking and goldsmith apparatus (*ālat al-ṣiyāghah*) of the tribe (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180). 

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ set aside his elite portion (*ṣafī*) and deducted the **Khumus (one-fifth)** for the treasury of Allāh and His Messenger, distributing the remaining **four-fifths** equally among the combatants. This represented the **first formal division of the Khumus in Islamic history after the revelation of the rules of spoils following Badr** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180).

## 9. Scriptural Exegesis (*Asbāb al-Nuzūl*)

The crisis of Banū Qaynuqāʿ is the direct historical occasion for foundational Qurʾānic passages regarding the demarcation of political loyalty (*al-walāʾ wal-barāʾ*):

### Sūrat al-Māʾidah (5:51–56)
Revealed regarding the contrasting postures of ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy and ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:180–181):
$$\text{يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَتَّخِذُوا الْيَهُودَ وَالنَّصَارَىٰ أَوْلِيَاءَ ۘ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ ۚ وَمَن يَتَوَلَّهُم مِّنكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَهْدِي الْقَوْمَ الظَّالِمِينَ}$$
> *"O you who have believed, do not take the Jews and the Christians as allies. They are [in fact] allies of one another. And whoever is an ally to them among you—then indeed, he is [one] of them. Indeed, Allāh guides not the wrongdoing people."* (Qurʾān 5:51).

Regarding Ibn Ubayy's declaration (*«إِنِّي امْرُؤٌ أَخْشَى الدَّوَائِرَ»*):
$$\text{فَتَرَى الَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ يُسَارِعُونَ فِيهِمْ يَقُولُونَ نَخْشَىٰ أَن تُصِيبَنَا دَائِرَةٌ ۚ فَعَسَى اللَّهُ أَن يَأْتِيَ بِالْفَتْحِ أَوْ أَمْرٍ مِّنْ عِندِهِ فَيُصْبِحُوا عَلَىٰ مَا أَسَرُّوا فِي أَنفُسِهِمْ نَادِمِينَ}$$
> *"So you see those in whose hearts is disease hastening into [association with] them, saying: 'We fear a misfortune may strike us.' But perhaps Allāh will bring conquest or a decision from Him, and they will become, over what they have been concealing within themselves, regretful."* (Qurʾān 5:52).

Regarding ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit's allegiance to Allāh and His Messenger:
$$\text{إِنَّمَا وَلِيُّكُمُ اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا الَّذِينَ يُقِيمُونَ الصَّلَاةَ وَيُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ وَهُمْ رَاكِعُونَ ۝ وَمَن يَتَوَلَّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا فَإِنَّ حِزْبَ اللَّهِ هُمُ الْغَالِبُونَ}$$
> *"Indeed, your ally is none other than Allāh, His Messenger, and the believers—those who establish prayer, give zakāh, and bow down. And whoever allies himself with Allāh, His Messenger, and the believers—then indeed, the party of Allāh will be the victorious."* (Qurʾān 5:55–56).
