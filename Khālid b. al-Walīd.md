# Khālid b. al-Walīd (خالد بن الوليد المخزومي) ﵁
*Abū Sulaymān al-Makhzūmī al-Qurashī — Sayfullāh al-Maslūl (The Drawn Sword of Allāh), Undefeated Military Genius*

## 1. Lineage & Pre-Islamic Military Renown
He is Khālid b. al-Walīd b. al-Mughīrah b. ʿAbdillāh b. ʿUmar b. Makhzūm al-Qurashī al-Makhzūmī (kunya: Abū Sulaymān; title: *Sayfullāh al-Maslūl*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:351). He was the nephew of Umm al-Muʾminīn Maymūnah bint al-Ḥārith ﵂ and cousin of ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁. In pre-Islamic Mecca, he held the hereditary aristocratic office of *Qubbat Quraysh* and *al-Aʿinnah* (custody of the cavalry and military mobilization) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:351).

At **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]** (3 AH), commanding the polytheist right cavalry flank, Khālid demonstrated his tactical brilliance: when the Muslim archers abandoned their posts on **[[Jabal al-Rumāh]]**, Khālid executed a swift wheel-charge around the mountain, crushed the remaining archers, and spearheaded the rear flank assault that reversed the battle (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195, 202).

## 2. Conversion to Islam (8 AH)
Following the Pilgrimage of Fulfillment (*ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ*), his brother al-Walīd b. al-Walīd sent him a letter from Medina containing the Prophet’s words: *“A man like Khālid cannot remain ignorant of Islam; had he devoted his military talent with the Muslims against the polytheists, it would have been vastly better for him!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190).

In Ṣafar 8 AH, Khālid set out for Medina. Meeting **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** and ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah on the road, they entered Medina together (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190). When Khālid appeared before the Prophet ﷺ, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ beamed with joy and welcomed him, saying:
> «الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي هَدَاكَ، قَدْ كُنْتُ أَرَى لَكَ عَقْلًا رَجَوْتُ أَلَّا يُسْلِمَكَ إِلَّا إِلَى خَيْرٍ!»
> *"Praise be to Allāh Who guided you! I have always seen in you an intellect that I hoped would lead you only to goodness!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191).
The Prophet ﷺ prayed for divine forgiveness over all his previous actions against the believers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191).

## 3. Sayfullāh & Master Campaigns
- **The Miracle of Muʾtah (8 AH)**: Just months after converting, Khālid marched as a regular soldier at **[[Ghazwat Muʾtah]]** against the Byzantine Roman army of 100,000 (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:368). When all three appointed commanders (**Zayd b. Ḥārithah**, **Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib**, and **ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah**) were martyred, the soldiers thrust the standard into Khālid’s hands (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:369). Khālid fought with ferocious intensity: *“Nine swords broke in my hand on the day of Muʾtah; nothing remained in my hand except a broad Yemeni blade!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:370; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 4266). By rearranging the ranks overnight to create the illusion of massive reinforcements, he executed a flawless tactical withdrawal, saving the army. In Medina, the Prophet ﷺ described the battle in real time to the weeping companions, declaring:
  > «ثُمَّ أَخَذَ الرَّايَةَ سَيْفٌ مِنْ سُيُوفِ اللَّهِ، حَتَّى فَتَحَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ!»
  > *"Then the standard was taken by a Sword from the Swords of Allāh, until Allāh granted victory at his hands!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:371; 11:351).
  From that day, he was known as *Sayfullāh al-Maslūl* ("The Drawn Sword of Allāh").
- **Conquest of Mecca (8 AH)**: Commanded the right cavalry flank during **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**, entering the city through its southern gate and sweeping polytheist resistance aside (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:208). Dispatched by the Prophet ﷺ to Nakhla, where he destroyed the paramount pagan shrine and idol of **al-ʿUzzā** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:250).
- **The Northern Border at Tabūk & Dūmat al-Jandal (9 AH)**: Dispatched during **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]** with four hundred and twenty horsemen to **[[Dūmat al-Jandal]]**, capturing its Christian ruler Ukaydir b. ʿAbd al-Malik and securing northern Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:455; 11:351).

## 4. Demise & Legacy
Following the Prophet's death, Khālid was appointed by Caliph Abū Bakr as supreme general during the Riddah Wars, annihilating the false prophet Musaylimah at the Battle of Yamāmah. He subsequently orchestrated the conquests of Iraq and Syria, crushing the Byzantine imperial army at the historic Battle of Yarmūk (13 AH).

Undefeated in over one hundred battles throughout his lifetime, he died on his bed in Homs (Syria) in 21 AH at the age of fifty-five. Weeping on his deathbed, he uttered his famous lament:
> *"I have fought in such-and-such battles; there is not the space of a hand-span upon my body except that it carries the scar of a sword strike, a spear thrust, or an arrow wound. And here I am, dying upon my bed as a camel dies! So may the eyes of cowards never find sleep!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:351).
