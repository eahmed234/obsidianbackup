# Riḥlat al-Shām al-Ūlā (سفره ﷺ الأول إلى الشام)
*Two Journeys of His Youth — and the Monk Who Recognised Him at Buṣrā*

## 1. The Earlier Journey, to Yemen
Ibn al-Jawzī records that when the Prophet ﷺ was in his early teens (*"buḍʿ ʿasharah sanah"*), he
travelled with his uncle **al-Zubayr b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib**. Passing a valley where a stallion camel
blocked the way of travellers, the camel — on seeing him — knelt and scraped the ground with its
chest; he dismounted his own mount, rode the stallion across the valley, then released it. On the
return, they came to a valley flooded with rushing water and hesitated; the Prophet ﷺ said, *"follow
me,"* waded in, and the party followed — **and Allāh dried the water for them**. When they reached
Makkah and told what had happened, people said: *"this boy has some affair to him"*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:139).

## 2. The Journey to Syria With Abū Ṭālib
Reports on his age here vary — the book judges **twelve years** as *al-thabt*, the established figure
(al-Balādhurī). **[[Abū Ṭālib]]** intended to travel with a caravan to Syria; the boy, distressed at
being left behind, asked him, *"O uncle, to whom do you leave me here?"* Moved, Abū Ṭālib took him
along (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:140).

### The First Monk
The party stopped at the dwelling of a monk, who asked Abū Ṭālib: *"What is this boy to you?"* —
*"My son,"* he answered. *"He cannot be your son,"* the monk said, *"for it is not fitting that his
father be living — his face is the face of a prophet, and his eye is the eye of a prophet."* Asked
what a prophet was, he explained: *"one who receives revelation from heaven and brings news to the
people of the earth."* Then he warned: *"guard him against the Jews"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:140).

### Baḥīrā at Buṣrā
The party then stopped with a second monk, at Buṣrā, whom the book names **Baḥīrā**. He recognised in
him the same signs — the shading cloud that followed him, and the tree's shade bending unnaturally
over him as he sat beneath it — and, wishing to verify the seal of prophethood, prepared a feast and
invited "all of you, your young and old, your free and your slave," expressly so none would be left
behind. The Prophet ﷺ, being the youngest, had at first stayed back with the camels; when Baḥīrā
noticed his absence he insisted he be brought, and upon examining what he sought **between his
shoulders — a mark like an apple, at the base of the cartilage** — declared to Abū Ṭālib's party:

> «هَذَا نَبِيُّ هَذِهِ الْأُمَّةِ الَّذِي يُرْسِلُهُ اللَّهُ إِلَى النَّاسِ كَافَّةً»
> *"This is the prophet of this nation, whom Allāh will send to mankind entire."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:141)

### Seven Travellers From Rome
Immediately after, seven men from Byzantium arrived seeking *"this prophet who is due to appear this
very month."* Baḥīrā tested their resolve — *"can any man turn back what Allāh intends?"* — and,
finding them still bent on harm, went to Quraysh and had Abū Ṭālib turn the caravan homeward,
provisioning them himself with cake and oil for the return (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:142). Abū Ṭālib
composed verses on the journey, preserved by [[Ibn Isḥāq]] and Abū Hiffān in his dīwān, describing his
grief at parting from *"the son of Āminah, the trustworthy Muḥammad,"* and naming the informing monk
and the plotting travellers by name — Baḥīrā, Zubayrāʾ, Tammām, and Durays among them
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:142–143).

## 3. Who Was Baḥīrā?
Al-Zuhrī's *Sīrah* calls him a Jewish scholar of Taymāʾ; **Ibn Kathīr** judges from the narrative
itself that he was Christian; **[[Ibn Isḥāq]]** affirms this, and **al-Masʿūdī** specifies he was a
Christian of ʿAbd al-Qays. His dwelling is placed at **Mayfaʿah**, a village beyond a monastery in
al-Balqāʾ. Ibn Qutaybah records that shortly before Islam a voice was heard proclaiming: *"the best of
the people of the earth are Baḥīrā and Riʾāb b. al-Barāʾ al-Shannī, and a third, awaited one"* — the
third being the Prophet ﷺ himself (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:145).

> [!WARNING] A Detail the Book Itself Rejects
> Some transmissions of this episode add that Abū Bakr ﵁ sent **Bilāl** ﵁ along on this journey. The
> book flags this as an error: Abū Bakr ﵁ was not yet ten years old at the time, and Bilāl ﵁ did not
> come into Abū Bakr's ﵁ possession until more than thirty years later, when Abū Bakr ﵁ bought and
> freed him from persecution. Al-Ḥāfiẓ notes the phrase appears to be a fragment of an unrelated
> report mistakenly inserted here (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:144).
>
> The book also distinguishes this journey carefully from a **separate, later report** placing
> **Abū Bakr ﵁ himself** alongside the Prophet ﷺ — then twenty years old — on a different trading trip
> to Syria, where Abū Bakr ﵁ independently sought out Baḥīrā and was told the same thing: *"this is
> the prophet of this nation."* The two accounts should not be merged (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:144).
