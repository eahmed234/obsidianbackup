# Sariyyat Wādī al-Qurā (سرية وادي القرى)
*The punitive expedition against Banū Fazārah at Wādī al-Qurā commanded by Zayd b. Ḥārithah, the dissolution of Umm Qirfah's stronghold, and the joyous embrace of the Prophet ﷺ*

## 1. Background & The Ambush of the Trade Caravan

In late 6 AH, **[[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]** ﵁ set out toward Syria on a commercial venture carrying goods entrusted to him by several Companions of the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). Approaching **Wādī al-Qurā**, a large force from **Banū Fazārah** (specifically the clan of Banū Badr) ambushed the Muslim party, severely beating Zayd and his companions until they presumed them dead (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). Zayd was retrieved from among the wounded with faint breath remaining (*urtuththa Zaydun min wasaṭi al-qatlā*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).

Upon recovering from his wounds in Medina, Zayd took a solemn oath not to touch his head with water for major ritual washing (*allā yamassa raʾsahu ghuslun min janābah*) until he fought Banū Fazārah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). As soon as his wounds healed, the Prophet ﷺ dispatched him at the head of a punitive detachment in **Ramaḍān 6 AH**, giving them the strategic instruction:
> «أكمنوا النهار وسيروا الليل»
> *"Conceal yourselves by day and march by night!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).

## 2. Desert Deception & The Surprise Dawn Assault

Banū Fazārah were vigilant against retaliation and posted a dedicated lookout (*nāẓūr*) atop an overlooking mountain each morning to scout the northern approach a full day's journey away (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). Confident in their surveillance, they grazed their herds by day and slept securely by night (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).

A desert guide belonging to Banū Fazārah accompanied the Muslim column. About a night's march from their objective, the guide inadvertently took an alternate route, causing the Muslims to detour (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). This unintended error proved providential: it bypassed the enemy lookout entirely. Pressing forward under cover of darkness, Zayd and his force encircled the encampment (*al-ḥāḍir*) just as dawn broke (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).

Zayd raised the Takbīr, echoing throughout the valley as the Muslims charged (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). **[[Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ]]** ﵁ pursued one of the enemy champions across the terrain and dispatched him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). Qays b. al-Musahḥar slew al-Nuʿmān and ʿUbaydullāh, the two sons of Masʿadah b. Ḥakamah b. Mālik b. Badr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).

## 3. The Fall of Umm Qirfah's Stronghold

During the engagement, the Muslims captured **ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿadah** and took prisoner the daughter of Mālik b. Ḥudhayfah b. Badr, whose mother was **Umm Qirfah** (Fāṭimah bint Rabīʿah b. Badr) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). 

Umm Qirfah was an influential, aged matriarch of Banū Fazārah, renowned throughout Arabia for her aristocratic stature and fierce hostility to Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). The Arabs had a common proverb celebrating unassailable might: *«Law kunta aʿazza min Ummi Qirfah»* (*"Even if you were mightier than Umm Qirfah"*), because fifty swords belonging to unmarriageable male kin (*dhū maḥram*) hung suspended in her hall, representing fifty warriors devoted to her command (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). She had twelve sons, of whom Qirfah was slain by the Muslims and the rest later perished during the apostasy of Ṭulayḥah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).

Because of her active instigation, her open revilement of the Prophet ﷺ (*li-sabbihā Sayyidanā Rasūlallāh ﷺ*), and her arming of combatants against the Muslims, Zayd b. Ḥārithah ordered her executed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).

## 4. The Prophet's ﷺ Joyous Reception of Zayd

When Zayd b. Ḥārithah returned victorious to Medina, he knocked at the door of the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99). The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was so overwhelmed with joyous relief at Zayd's safe return and triumph that he hastened directly out, trailing his garment (*yajarru thawbahu*), threw his arms around Zayd's neck, and kissed him:
> «فقام إليه عريانا يجرّ ثوبه حتى اعتنقه وقبّله فأخبره زيد بما ظفّره اللَّه تعالى به»
> *"He stood up, trailing his cloak, until he embraced him and kissed him, whereupon Zayd informed him of the victory that Allāh the Exalted had granted him."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).

## 5. The Captive Daughter & The Ransom of Muslim Prisoners

Among the captives brought to Medina was the daughter of Umm Qirfah, who was initially allotted to **[[Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:92, 100). When the Prophet ﷺ encountered Salamah in the marketplace, he requested her:
> «يا سلمة هب لي المرأة للَّه أبوك»
> *"O Salamah, grant me the woman, may Allāh bless your father!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:92, 100).

Salamah gifted her to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:92). The Prophet ﷺ then dispatched her to Makkah, successfully using her in a bilateral exchange to ransom several Muslim prisoners held in captivity by the polytheists of Quraysh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:92, 100).

## 6. Strategic Significance

1. **Securing the Northern Trade Corridor**: The neutralization of Banū Fazārah at Wādī al-Qurā eliminated the persistent highway threat against northern commerce between Medina and Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).
2. **Dismantling Tribal Belligerence**: The fall of Umm Qirfah shattered the military prestige of the Badr clan of Fazārah, signaling that neither wealth nor aristocratic tribal alliances could protect hostile confederacies (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:99).
3. **Diplomatic Utility of Captives**: Rather than executing or enslaving high-value non-combatant captives, the Prophet ﷺ utilized the noble captive to liberate Muslim believers detained in Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:92).
