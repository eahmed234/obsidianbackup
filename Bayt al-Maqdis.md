# Bayt al-Maqdis (بيت المقدس)
*Jerusalem / al-Masjid al-Aqṣā — The Farthest Sanctuary and First Qiblah of Islam*

## 1. Sacred Antiquity & Topography
*Bayt al-Maqdis* (also designated *al-Quds* and *al-Masjid al-Aqṣā*, "The Farthest Mosque") is the sacred prophetic sanctuary in Jerusalem, established forty years after the Kaʿbah as a house of divine worship (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:60).

In the Sīrah, Bayt al-Maqdis stands as the historical convergence point of all Abrahamic messengers and the terrestrial pivot of the Prophet's celestial ascent (*al-Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj*).

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones
- **The Night Journey (*al-Isrāʾ*)**: In the tenth or twelfth year of Nubūwah, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was carried by night aboard the winged celestial steed **al-Burāq** from the Sacred Mosque in Mecca to Bayt al-Maqdis (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:60–75). Upon arrival, he tethered al-Burāq to the ring utilized by the past prophets, entered al-Masjid al-Aqṣā, and led all the Prophets and Messengers—from Adam to Jesus—in congregational prayer, before ascending through the seven heavens (**al-Miʿrāj**) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:65–70).
- **The First Qiblah (*Ūlā al-Qiblatayn*)**: Throughout the Makkan period and for sixteen to seventeen months following **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**, the Muslims prayed facing Bayt al-Maqdis as their primary direction of prayer (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:368–370).
- **The Turning of the Qiblah (Rajab 2 AH)**: While praying in Medina, the Prophet's longing to face the Kaʿbah was divinely answered in **[[Taḥwīl al-Qiblah]]** with the revelation of Sūrat al-Baqarah (2:144), turning the prayer permanently toward the Sacred Mosque in Mecca (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370–375).
