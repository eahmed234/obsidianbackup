# ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib (علي بن أبي طالب) ﵁
*Abū al-Ḥasan al-Hāshimī al-Qurashī — Asad Allāh (The Lion of Allāh), Cousin & Son-in-Law of the Prophet ﷺ, Fourth Caliph*

## 1. Lineage, Ancestry & Physical Description
### Patrilineal Descent & Sacred Kinship
He is **ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib (ʿAbd Manāf) b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib (Shaybah) b. Hāshim (ʿAmr) b. ʿAbd Manāf (al-Mughīrah) b. Quṣayy b. Kilāb al-Qurashī al-Hāshimī** (kunyas: **Abū al-Ḥasan** and **Abū Turāb**; titles: *Asad Allāh*, *Ḥaydarah*, *Amīr al-Muʾminīn*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:464; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:237, 287). 

His lineage merges directly with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at their shared paternal grandfather **[[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib b. Hāshim]]**, making him the Prophet's paternal first cousin (*ibn ʿamm*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:464). He was the full brother of the heroic martyr **[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ (Dhū al-Janāḥayn), ʿAqīl b. Abī Ṭālib, Ṭālib b. Abī Ṭālib, and Umm Hānīʾ (Fākhitah) bint Abī Ṭālib (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:287).

### Maternal Heritage & Nobility
- **Mother**: **Fāṭimah bint Asad b. Hāshim b. ʿAbd Manāf al-Qurashiyyah al-Hāshimiyyah** ﵂ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:464).
- **Distinction**: She was the first woman of Banū Hāshim to wed a Hāshimite man and the first Hāshimite mother of a Caliph. She embraced Islam early in Makkah, migrated to Medina, and died during the Prophet's lifetime. The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ held her in deep filial reverence, declaring: *"She was truly my mother after my own mother,"* shrouding her in his own shirt and lying down in her grave prior to her burial (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:287).

### Birth & Early Tutelage
ʿAlī was born in Makkah approximately ten years before the Prophetic mission (~23 BH / 600 CE) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:464; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:287). When severe drought struck Quraysh, the Prophet ﷺ took young ʿAlī into his household to relieve his uncle Abū Ṭālib, while al-ʿAbbās took Jaʿfar. Reared under the intimate care and spiritual refinement of the Prophet ﷺ and **[[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid]]** ﵂, ʿAlī never prostrated to an idol in his youth and became the very first child/youth to accept Islam (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:464; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:287).

### Physical Appearance & Valor
- **Build**: Medium stature tending toward shorter, exceptionally stout, broad-chested, and endowed with massive muscular shoulders and powerful arms (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:465).
- **Features**: Radiant countenance with large, dark eyes; bald on the crown of his head with white hair surrounding his temples (*aṣlaʿ*); possessing a full, handsome white beard that covered the breadth of his chest (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:465).
- **Martial Prowess**: Unrivaled in swordsmanship and physical courage; known as *Ḥaydarah* ("the Lion"). Foes avoided facing him in single combat, and poetical elegies celebrated that no opponent engaged by him survived (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:465).

## 2. Marriages, Kinship Networks & Progeny
ʿAlī's marital alliances spanned the Prophetic household, Banū Hāshim, Banū Ḥanīfah, Banū Kilāb, Banū Tamīm, and Banū Khathʿam:

1. **[[Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ]]** ﵂:
   - The youngest and most beloved daughter of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and Khadījah ﵂; mistress of the women of Paradise (*Sayyidat Nisāʾ Ahl al-Jannah*). Wed to ʿAlī in Medina in Dhū al-Qaʿdah 2 AH following Badr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:37, 288). ʿAlī wed no other woman during her lifetime.
   - **Children**: 
     - **al-Ḥasan** ﵁ and **al-Ḥusayn** ﵁, the twin masters of the youth of Paradise (*Sayyidā Shabāb Ahl al-Jannah*) and sole continuous ancestral source of the Prophetic bloodline (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:37, 288).
     - **al-Muḥassin**, who died in infancy (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:37).
     - **Zaynab** ﵂, who married her first cousin ʿAbdullāh b. Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:37).
     - **[[Umm Kulthūm bint ʿAlī]]** ﵂, who was wed to Caliph **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ for a dowry of 40,000 dirhams, bearing him Zayd and Ruqayyah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:464–465).
2. **Khawlah bint Jaʿfar b. Qays al-Ḥanafiyyah**:
   - Noblewoman from Banū Ḥanīfah assigned to ʿAlī following the Yamāmah campaign.
   - **Children**: Bore him the renowned scholar and warrior **Muḥammad b. al-Ḥanafiyyah** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:465).
3. **Umm al-Banīn Fāṭimah bint Ḥizām b. Khālid al-Kilābiyyah**:
   - Chosen from the martial Bedouin clan of Banū Kilāb for her ancestral courage.
   - **Children**: Bore him four sons: **al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAlī** (*al-ʿAbbās al-Akbar*, the hero of Karbalāʾ), **Jaʿfar**, **ʿAbdullāh**, and **ʿUthmān** (named after ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn), all four of whom fell alongside al-Ḥusayn at Karbalāʾ.
4. **Laylā bint Masʿūd al-Dārimiyyah**:
   - From Banū Tamīm; bore him **ʿUbaydullāh** and **Abū Bakr**.
5. **Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays al-Khathʿamiyyah** ﵂:
   - Prominent early Muhājirah, previously wife of ʿAlī's brother Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib ﵁ and then of Caliph Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq ﵁. After Abū Bakr's passing, ʿAlī wed her.
   - **Children**: Bore him **Yaḥyā** and **Muḥammad the younger** (*Muḥammad al-Aṣghar*). ʿAlī brought Abū Bakr's son **Muḥammad b. Abī Bakr** into his home, raising him as his own child and intertwining the houses of the two Caliphs (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:149, 464).
6. **Umāmah bint Abī al-ʿĀṣ b. al-Rabīʿ** ﵂:
   - Granddaughter of the Prophet ﷺ (daughter of Zaynab bint Rasūlillāh ﵂). Married to ʿAlī upon the explicit dying bequest of Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ; bore him **Muḥammad the middle** (*Muḥammad al-Awsaṭ*).

## 3. Key Sīrah Milestones
### The Heroic Sacrifice on the Night of the Hijrah
On the night of **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**, when Quraysh gathered assassins from every clan to encircle the Prophet's house, ʿAlī volunteered to lay in the Prophet's bed covered in his green Ḥaḍramī cloak to mislead the assassins (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:236–238; 11:288). He remained in Makkah for three days after the Prophet's departure to faithfully return every entrusted property and deposit (*amānāt*) left by the Makkans with the Prophet ﷺ, before making the arduous journey to Medina entirely on foot, his feet bleeding upon arrival (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:238).

### Brother of the Prophet in Medina
When the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ instituted the fraternal pact of **[[al-Muʾākhāh]]** between the Muhājirūn and Anṣār, ʿAlī wept that no brother had been assigned to him. The Prophet ﷺ embraced him, declaring:
> «أَنْتَ أَخِي فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ»
> *"You are my brother in this world and in the Hereafter!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:464; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:288).

### Tripartite Duel at Badr (2 AH)
At **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, ʿAlī carried the supreme black standard (*al-ʿUqāb*). Stepping into the arena alongside **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** and ʿUbaydah b. al-Ḥārith in the tripartite opening duel, ʿAlī struck down the formidable polytheist champion al-Walīd b. ʿUtbah in single combat (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:40; 11:289). Sūrat al-Ḥajj (22:19: *«These two adversaries have disputed regarding their Lord»*) was revealed concerning their duel (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:40).

### Defense at Uḥud (3 AH)
At **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, ʿAlī slew the successive hereditary standard-bearers of Banū ʿAbd al-Dār (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195). When the Muslim lines fractured, ʿAlī stood firm in the inner defensive ring protecting the Prophet ﷺ from fatal blows, using his shield to fetch fresh water from the mountain springs of Uḥud so Fāṭimah could wash the wounds of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205, 210; 11:290).

### Duel at the Trench (5 AH)
During **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**, when the legendary equestrian champion ʿAmr b. ʿAbd Wudd leaped the trench with his cavalry and challenged the Muslims, ʿAlī stepped forth. Trading blows, ʿAlī felled ʿAmr with a decisive strike and proclaimed the Takbīr, sending the confederate horsemen fleeing in disarray and shattering the siege morale (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:377–378; 11:291).

### Chief Scribe of Ḥudaybiyah (6 AH)
At **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]**, ʿAlī served as chief scribe. When the Meccan negotiator Suhayl b. ʿAmr refused the words *Rasūl Allāh*, the Prophet ﷺ asked ʿAlī to strike them out; ʿAlī bowed in deep veneration, stating: *“By Allāh, my hand shall never erase your name!”* whereupon the Prophet ﷺ erased it with his own hand (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:50; 11:292).

### The Conquest of Khaybar (7 AH)
When the fortresses of Khaybar resisted conquest, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ declared:
> «لَأُعْطِيَنَّ الرَّايَةَ غَدًا رَجُلًا يُحِبُّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ، وَيُحِبُّهُ اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ، يَفْتَحُ اللَّهُ عَلَى يَدَيْهِ!»
> *"Tomorrow I shall surely hand the banner to a man who loves Allāh and His Messenger, and whom Allāh and His Messenger love; Allāh will grant conquest at his hands!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:466; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:124; 11:293).
The next morning, the Prophet ﷺ summoned ʿAlī, cured his severe eye ailment (*ramad*) with his blessed saliva, and gave him the standard. ʿAlī slew the Jewish champion Marḥab, ripped down the heavy gate of the fortress to wield as a combat shield, and led the army to total victory (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:466; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:125–128; 11:293).

### Deputy at Tabūk (9 AH)
Appointed deputy over Medina during **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]**, ʿAlī was dismayed by hypocrite gossip. The Prophet ﷺ comforted him with the famous station:
> «أَمَا تَرْضَى أَنْ تَكُونَ مِنِّي بِمَنْزِلَةِ هَارُونَ مِنْ مُوسَى، إِلَّا أَنَّهُ لَا نَبِيَّ بَعْدِي؟»
> *"Are you not pleased to be to me as Aaron was to Moses, except that there is no prophet after me?!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:464, 468; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:448; 11:294).

## 4. Caliphate, Martyrdom & Legacy
Acclaimed Fourth Caliph in Medina following the martyrdom of **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ in Dhū al-Ḥijjah 35 AH, ʿAlī governed through five turbulent years of internal civil trial, ruling successively from Kūfah (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468).

### Assassination and Martyrdom
On Friday morning, 17 or 19 Ramaḍān 40 AH, as ʿAlī entered the Grand Mosque of Kūfah calling the people to the dawn prayer (*al-ṣalāh, al-ṣalāh!*), the Khārijite fanatic ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Muljam al-Murādī struck him across the forehead with a poisoned sword, the blade penetrating to his brain (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306). 

ʿAlī cried out: *"I have triumphed, by the Lord of the Kaʿbah!"* (*fuztu wa-rabbi al-kaʿbah!*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306). In his final testament, he commanded al-Ḥasan, al-Ḥusayn, and his household to maintain fear of Allāh, mend communal ties, protect the orphan and the neighbor, remain steadfast in the Qurʾān, and treat his assassin with strict legal equity without torture (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306).

He succumbed to martyrdom at the age of sixty-three on the soundest opinion (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306–307). He was washed by his sons al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn and nephew ʿAbdullāh b. Jaʿfar, shrouded in three white garments without shirt or turban, and anointed with camphor left over from the Prophet's burial (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:307). His funeral prayer was led by al-Ḥasan, and he was laid to rest in Kūfah near the government palace (*Qaṣr al-Imārah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:307).
