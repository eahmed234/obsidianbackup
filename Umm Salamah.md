# Umm Salamah (أم سلمة هند بنت أبي أمية) ﵂
*Umm al-Muʾminīn — The Sagacious Matron of the Household and Counselor of Ḥudaybiyah*

## 1. Lineage, Clan & Kinship Network
She is Hind bint Abī Umayyah (whose name was Ḥudhayfah or Suhayl, famously titled *Zād al-Rākib*) b. al-Mughīrah b. ʿAbdillāh b. ʿUmar b. Makhzūm al-Qurashiyyah al-Makhzūmiyyah (kunya: Umm Salamah) (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:404, no. 12065; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:187). Her father was hailed across Arabia as ***Zād al-Rākib*** (*"The Provisioner of the Rider"*) due to his peerless hospitality: whenever he embarked upon a journey, he forbade any traveling companion from carrying their own provisions, providing lavish food and mounts for the entire company (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:404; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:187). See **[[Banū Makhzūm]]**.

Her mother was **ʿĀtikah bint ʿĀmir b. Rabīʿah b. Mālik al-Kināniyyah**, belonging to the famed equestrian clan of Banū Firās of Kinānah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:404).

Through her illustrious Makhzūmī lineage, Umm Salamah held profound family ties across Mecca:
- Her first cousin was the undefeated commander **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁ (his father al-Walīd was the brother of Abū Umayyah) (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:215).
- Her first cousin was **Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)** b. al-Mughīrah.
- Her first husband was her paternal cousin, **[[Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān|Abū Salamah ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbd al-Asad al-Makhzūmī]]** ﵁ (his mother Barrah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib was paternal aunt to the Prophet ﷺ) (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:404).

## 2. Early Conversion & The Trial of the Two Migrations
Umm Salamah and her husband Abū Salamah were among the very first believers in Mecca to embrace Islam. To escape the relentless torture of Quraysh, they embarked together upon **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]**, where she gave birth to their eldest son, Salamah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:404; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:382).

Upon returning to Mecca, they prepared to perform the migration to Medina (**[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**). As Abū Salamah mounted her and infant Salamah upon his camel, the clansmen of Banū al-Mughīrah intercepted them, seizing the camel's reins and declaring: *“As for yourself, you have overcome us, but as for our daughter, we will never allow you to march across the land with her!”* Hearing this, the paternal clan of Banū ʿAbd al-Asad retaliated furiously, grabbing the child Salamah and pulling his arm until they dislocated his shoulder (*khalaʿū yadah*), carrying him off (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:405; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:235).

Abū Salamah was forced to proceed to Medina alone, leaving the family severed into three pieces. For nearly a year, Umm Salamah walked out each morning to the valley of al-Abṭaḥ in Mecca, weeping inconsolably until evening, until a cousin from Banū al-Mughīrah took pity upon her and rebuked the clan: *“Will you not release this poor woman? You have severed her from her husband and her child!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:405). The clan relented, Banū ʿAbd al-Asad returned her son, and she set out across the desert entirely alone with her infant until met at al-Tanʿīm by **ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah al-ʿAbdarī**, who chivalrously escorted her across the desert all the way to Qubāʾ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:405; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:235). In Medina, she bore Abū Salamah three more children: ʿUmar, Durrah, and Zaynab (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:404).

## 3. Marriage to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ
At **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]** (3 AH), Abū Salamah sustained a severe arrow wound to his arm, from which he was martyred in Jumādā al-Ākhirah 4 AH. During his final illness, the Prophet ﷺ visited him, and after his death, instructed Umm Salamah to recite the timeless supplication:
> «اللَّهُمَّ أْجُرْنِي فِي مُصِيبَتِي، وَأَخْلِفْ لِي خَيْرًا مِنْهَا»
> *"O Allāh, reward me in my affliction, and replace it for me with that which is better!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:404; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:188; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*).

When her mourning period concluded, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ proposed marriage to her. Umm Salamah modestly demurred, stating: *“O Messenger of Allāh, a woman like me is not suitable for you: I am an aged woman with no more youth for childbearing, I am intensely jealous, and I have dependent children.”* The Prophet ﷺ reassuringly replied:
> «أَنَا أَكْبَرُ مِنْكِ، وَأَمَّا الْغَيْرَةُ فَيُذْهِبُهَا اللَّهُ، وَأَمَّا الْعِيَالُ فَإِلَى اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ»
> *"As for age, I am older than you; as for jealousy, Allāh will remove it; and as for dependent children, they are entrusted to Allāh and His Messenger!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:406; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:188–189).
They were married in Shawwāl (or Jumādā al-Ākhirah) 4 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:404; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:188).

## 4. Pivotal Sīrah Milestones
- **The Decisive Counsel at [[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]] (6 AH)**: Following the signing of the Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah, the Prophet ﷺ commanded the companions thrice to slaughter their sacrificial animals and shave their heads. Paralyzed by grief and sorrow at the apparent concessions of the treaty, not a single man stood up (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:54). Perturbed, the Prophet ﷺ entered Umm Salamah's tent and sought her counsel. She provided the immortal strategic advice: *“O Messenger of Allāh, go forth, do not speak a single word to any of them, but slaughter your sacrificial camel and call your barber to shave your head!”* When the Prophet ﷺ emerged and took these physical actions before their eyes, the companions instantly leaped up, racing to emulate his every movement and slaughtering their animals, averting catastrophic disobedience (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:406; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:54; 11:190).
- **Sole Companion at [[Ghazwat Khaybar]] (7 AH)**: Umm Salamah was the sole wife chosen by lot to accompany the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ upon the victorious military expedition against the fortress redoubts of Khaybar (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:115).
- **The Chamber of the Purification Verse (33:33)**: It was in Umm Salamah's dwelling that the divine Verse of Purification (*Āyat al-Taṭhīr*) was revealed: *«إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنْكُمُ الرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًا»* (Sūrat al-Aḥzāb 33:33). Upon its revelation, the Prophet ﷺ gathered ʿAlī, Fāṭimah, al-Ḥasan, and al-Ḥusayn beneath his celestial cloak (*Ḥadīth al-Kisāʾ*). When Umm Salamah lifted the hem to enter, the Prophet ﷺ gently withheld her while affirming: *“You are upon goodness!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:6).

## 5. Scholarly Discernment, Longevity & Demise
Umm Salamah was revered as an intellectual pillar of the early Muslim community, celebrated for her eloquence, deep knowledge of legal traditions, and preservation of the domestic Prophetic Sunnah.

She was the last surviving Mother of the Believers (*ākhiruhunna mawtā*), outliving the other wives of the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:407). She passed away in Medina in Shawwāl 59 AH, or in late 61 AH shortly after receiving the heartrending news of the martyrdom of al-Ḥusayn b. ʿAlī at Karbalāʾ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:407; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:192). Her funeral prayer was led by **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁ (or Saʿīd b. Zayd), and she was buried beside her sisters in faith in **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:407; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:192).
