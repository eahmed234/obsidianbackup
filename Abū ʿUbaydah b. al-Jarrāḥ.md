# Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ (أبو عبيدة عامر بن الجراح) ﵁
*ʿĀmir b. ʿAbdillāh al-Fihrī al-Qurashī — Amīn hādhihi al-Ummah (The Trustworthy of this Ummah)*

## 1. Lineage & Early Preeminence
He is ʿĀmir b. ʿAbdillāh b. al-Jarrāḥ b. Hilāl b. Uhayb b. Ḍabbah b. al-Ḥārith b. Fihr al-Qurashī al-Fihrī (celebrated universally by his kunya **Abū ʿUbaydah**) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:237, 322). In pre-Islamic Mecca, he was renowned for his towering stature, courage, humility, and scrupulous trustworthiness.

He embraced Islam on the very day following **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, accepting the faith alongside ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf in the earliest dawn of Islam before **[[Dār al-Arqam]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:322). He endured the persecutions of Quraysh and migrated in both **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]** and **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**.

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ conferred upon him his most exalted title:
> «إِنَّ لِكُلِّ أُمَّةٍ أَمِينًا، وَإِنَّ أَمِينَ هَذِهِ الْأُمَّةِ أَبُو عُبَيْدَةَ بْنُ الْجَرَّاحِ!»
> *"Truly, every nation has a trustworthy guardian, and the trustworthy guardian of this Ummah is Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:322; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 4382; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, no. 2419).

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones
- **The Ultimate Test of Faith at Badr (2 AH)**: At **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, his polytheist father ʿAbdullāh persistently sought him out in the combat lines. Abū ʿUbaydah repeatedly avoided him to prevent confrontation, but when his father cornered him and blocked his path, Abū ʿUbaydah struck him down in defense of the faith (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:92; 11:322). Concerning this extraordinary detachment from pagan kinship, Allāh revealed in Sūrat al-Mujādilah:
  > «لَا تَجِدُ قَوْمًا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ يُوَادُّونَ مَنْ حَادَّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ وَلَوْ كَانُوا آبَاءَهُمْ أَوْ أَبْنَاءَهُمْ أَوْ إِخْوَانَهُمْ أَوْ عَشِيرَتَهُمْ»
  > *"You will not find a people who believe in Allāh and the Last Day loving those who oppose Allāh and His Messenger, even if they were their fathers, their sons, their brothers, or their clan..."* (Qurʾān 58:22; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:322).
- **The Heroism at Uḥud (3 AH)**: When the polytheist onslaught reached the Prophet ﷺ at **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, striking his face and embedding two iron links of his helmet (*ḥalaqatā al-mighfar*) deeply into his cheekbones, Abū Bakr rushed to remove them. Abū ʿUbaydah implored: *“I adjure you by Allāh, O Abū Bakr, leave it to me!”* Fearing to cause the Prophet ﷺ pain with his hands, Abū ʿUbaydah seized the first link with his front teeth and pulled firmly until it came free, snapping one of his central incisors. He then seized the second link and pulled until it came free, breaking his other incisor (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206; 11:323). The companions attested: *“We never saw a toothless man more handsome than Abū ʿUbaydah!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206; 11:323).
- **The Expedition of the Leaves (*Sariyyat al-Khabat*)**: Appointed commander of three hundred Muhājirūn and Anṣār dispatched to the coast. When provisions ran out, they survived on dried tree leaves (*khabat*) shaken from desert bushes, until the sea cast ashore a massive sea-creature (*al-ʿanbar*) from which the army ate for twenty-some days and recovered (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:185; 11:323).
- **The Envoy to Najrān (10 AH)**: When the Christian delegation of Najrān came to Medina and requested an arbiter, saying: *“Send with us a trustworthy man to judge between us in financial disputes,”* the Prophet ﷺ took Abū ʿUbaydah by the hand and declared: *“Arise, O Abū ʿUbaydah!”* dispatching him to Najrān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:323).

## 3. Demise & Legacy
At **[[Saqīfat Banī Sāʿidah]]** (11 AH), Abū Bakr offered him allegiance, saying: *“I am pleased with either of these two men: ʿUmar or Abū ʿUbaydah.”* Abū ʿUbaydah refused, insisting that none should precede the companion in the cave.

Under the Caliphate of ʿUmar, he was appointed supreme commander of the Islamic forces in Syria, leading the liberation of Damascus, Homs, and Antioch. When the devastating **Plague of ʿAmwās** struck Syria in 18 AH, Caliph ʿUmar wrote urgently commanding him to return to Medina to preserve his life. Abū ʿUbaydah replied with tears, declining to abandon his soldiers. He succumbed to the plague at the age of fifty-eight in the Jordan Valley, where his tomb stands (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:324–325).
