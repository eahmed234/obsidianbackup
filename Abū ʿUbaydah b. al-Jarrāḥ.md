# Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ (أبو عبيدة عامر بن الجراح) ﵁
*ʿĀmir b. ʿAbdillāh al-Fihrī al-Qurashī — Amīn hādhihi al-Ummah (The Trustworthy of this Ummah), Conqueror of the Levant*

## 1. Lineage, Ancestry & Physical Description
### Patrilineal Descent & Clan Kinship
He is **ʿĀmir b. ʿAbdillāh b. al-Jarrāḥ b. Hilāl b. Uhayb (Wuhayb) b. Ḍabbah b. al-Ḥārith b. Fihr al-Qurashī al-Fihrī** (universally celebrated by his kunya **Abū ʿUbaydah** and by patronymic attribution to his grandfather al-Jarrāḥ) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:475; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:237, 322).

His lineage merges with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at **Fihr b. Mālik** in the tenth generation (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:475). Belonging to **Banū al-Ḥārith b. Fihr**, one of the ancient core clans of **[[Quraysh]]**, he was renowned from his youth for his towering stature, unimpeachable integrity, humility, and scrupulous trustworthiness.

### Maternal Heritage & Clan Ties
- **Mother**: **Umaymah bint Ghanm b. Jābir b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. ʿĀmir b. ʿUmayrah al-Fihriyyah**, from his own clan of Banū al-Ḥārith b. Fihr (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:475).
- She lived to see the advent of Islam, accepted the faith, and pledged allegiance to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476).

### Brotherhood in Medina (al-Muʾākhāh)
Upon the migration to Medina, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ established fraternal brotherhood between Abū ʿUbaydah and the supreme chieftain of the Aws, **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁, uniting two of the most revered leaders of the early community (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476).

### Physical Appearance & the "Honorable Gap"
- **Build & Complexion**: Tall, slender, and lean-bodied (*ṭiwālan naḥīfan*), thin-faced (*maʿrūq al-wajh*), with a sparse beard (*khafīf al-liḥyah*) and slightly curved shoulders (*ajnāʾ*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476). In later years, he dyed his hair and beard with henna and katam (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:478).
- **The Broken Incisors (*al-Athram*)**: Possessed a distinct dental gap (*athram*) resulting from the Battle of Uḥud, where he pulled out the iron helmet rings embedded in the cheeks of the Prophet ﷺ using his front teeth, snapping both central incisors (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:475; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206). The Companions famously remarked: *"We never beheld an athram more strikingly handsome than Abū ʿUbaydah!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206; 11:323).

## 2. Peerless Trustworthiness & Early Status
Abū ʿUbaydah was among the very earliest pioneers of Islam, embracing the faith alongside **ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn**, **ʿUbaydah b. al-Ḥārith**, **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]**, and **Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Asad** in a single hour before the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ entered **[[Dār al-Arqam]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:475; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:322). He participated in both migrations to Abyssinia and Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:475).

The Prophet ﷺ conferred upon him the supreme title:
> «إِنَّ لِكُلِّ أُمَّةٍ أَمِينًا، وَإِنَّ أَمِينَ هَذِهِ الْأُمَّةِ أَبُو عُبَيْدَةَ بْنُ الْجَرَّاحِ!»
> *"Truly, every nation has a trustworthy custodian (*amīn*), and the trustworthy custodian of this Ummah is Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:475; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:322).

When **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ was asked which of the Companions was most beloved to the Prophet ﷺ, she answered: *"Abū Bakr, then ʿUmar, then Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:477). ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr recorded: *"Three men of Quraysh possess the most radiant faces, the finest character, and the deepest modesty: Abū Bakr, ʿUthmān, and Abū ʿUbaydah"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:477).

## 3. Key Sīrah Milestones
### The Ultimate Trial of Faith at Badr (2 AH)
At **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, Abū ʿUbaydah's polytheist father ʿAbdullāh continuously maneuvered through the battle ranks seeking to kill him. Abū ʿUbaydah repeatedly deflected and withdrew to avoid slaying his father; but when his father blocked his path and left him no escape, Abū ʿUbaydah struck him down in defense of the faith (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:92; 11:322). Concerning this complete subordination of pagan kinship to divine faith, Allāh revealed:
> «لَا تَجِدُ قَوْمًا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ يُوَادُّونَ مَنْ حَادَّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ وَلَوْ كَانُوا آبَاءَهُمْ أَوْ أَبْنَاءَهُمْ أَوْ إِخْوَانَهُمْ أَوْ عَشِيرَتَهُمْ»
> *"You will not find a people who believe in Allāh and the Last Day loving those who oppose Allāh and His Messenger, even if they were their fathers, their sons, their brothers, or their clan..."* (Qurʾān 58:22; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:476; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:322).

### Sacrifice at Uḥud (3 AH)
When polytheist strikes fractured the Prophet's helmet at **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, embedding two iron links (*ḥalaqatā al-mighfar*) deeply into his cheeks, Abū ʿUbaydah pleaded with Abū Bakr: *"I adjure you by Allāh, O Abū Bakr, leave it to me!"* Fearing to wrench the metal with his fingers and cause agony to the Prophet ﷺ, Abū ʿUbaydah bit the first link with his incisor and pulled steadily until it yielded, snapping his tooth. He then gripped the second link with his other incisor until it came free, breaking his second tooth (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:475; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206; 11:323).

### Sariyyat al-Khabat & the Giant Whale (8 AH)
Appointed commander over three hundred Muhājirūn and Anṣār dispatched toward the Red Sea coast (*Sariyyat al-Khabat*). When their rations ran out, they survived on dry leaves (*khabat*) beaten down from desert thorn-trees, until the sea cast ashore a colossal sperm whale (*al-ʿanbar*). Abū ʿUbaydah told his starving men: *"We are the emissaries of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and in the path of Allāh; eat!"* The entire three-hundred-man army fed upon its blubber and meat for over twenty days, regaining full vigor, and carried dried provisions back to Medina, where the Prophet ﷺ ate from it (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:185; 11:323).

### Reinforcements at Dhāt al-Salāsil
When ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ requested reinforcements at Dhāt al-Salāsil, the Prophet ﷺ dispatched an elite relief detachment under Abū ʿUbaydah, including Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, instructing him: *"Work together and do not dispute."* When ʿAmr insisted on retaining overall command, Abū ʿUbaydah gracefully deferred for the sake of unity, saying: *"Even if you disobey me, I shall obey you!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:477; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:200–202).

### Envoy to Najrān & Yemen
When the Christians of Najrān and the tribes of Yemen requested a trustworthy teacher, the Prophet ﷺ took Abū ʿUbaydah by the hand and dispatched him, declaring: *"This is the trustworthy man of this Ummah"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:323).

## 4. Caliphal Role, Levant Conquest & Demise
At **[[Saqīfat Banī Sāʿidah]]** (11 AH), Abū Bakr offered allegiance to either ʿUmar or Abū ʿUbaydah. Abū ʿUbaydah refused with absolute humility, affirming that none could precede Abū Bakr.

Under Caliph ʿUmar, Abū ʿUbaydah was named supreme commander of the Levant armies, orchestrating the liberation of Damascus, Homs, and Antioch (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476). When ʿUmar visited Syria and inspected Abū ʿUbaydah's meager dwelling, finding only a sword, a shield, and a travel saddle, ʿUmar wept and said: *"The world changed all of us except you, O Abū ʿUbaydah!"* Abū ʿUbaydah replied: *"O Amīr al-Muʾminīn, this is sufficient to reach the resting place"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:478).

### Martyrdom in the Plague of ʿAmwās (18 AH)
When the devastating **Plague of ʿAmwās** (*Ṭāʿūn ʿAmwās*) swept Syria in 18 AH, Caliph ʿUmar wrote urging Abū ʿUbaydah to return to Medina to preserve his indispensable life. Abū ʿUbaydah declined to abandon his soldiers, writing back: *"I am in an army of the Muslims; I do not wish to spare myself from that which afflicts them."* 

He succumbed to the plague at the age of fifty-eight (or according to Ibn Isḥāq forty-one) in 18 AH at Fiḥl in the Jordan Valley, where he was laid to rest (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:478; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:324–325). At his passing, Muʿādh b. Jabal ﵁ wept and proclaimed: *"By Allāh, you have been bereaved of a man than whom I have never seen anyone with less malice in his heart, a purer soul, or deeper sincerity for the common believers!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:478).
