# Ghazwat Tabūk (غزوة تبوك)
*The Expedition of Tabūk — Jaysh al-ʿUsrah, the Supreme Test of Faith, and the Divine Exposing of Sincerity*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 5, *al-Bāb al-Thalāthūn fī Ghazwat Tabūk* (pp. 433–490). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the primary accounts of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[Mūsā b. ʿUqbah]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], [[al-Bukhārī]], and [[Muslim]].

---

## 1. Strategic Context: The Byzantine Threat in Intense Heat
In Rajab 9 AH, during intense summer heat, drought, and the ripening of Medinan date-palm crops, intelligence reached Medina that the Roman Byzantine Emperor Heraclius had massed a massive imperial army reinforced by the Christian Arab client kingdoms of Ghassān, Lakhm, and Judhām on the southern borders of Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:433).

Unlike previous campaigns where the Prophet ﷺ employed strategic ambiguity (*warrā bi-ghayrihā*) to conceal his objective, here he openly declared the distant destination of **Tabūk** so the believers could equip themselves for the immense distance, famine, and heat (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:433). This grueling campaign was designated ***Jaysh al-ʿUsrah*** (The Army of Hardship).

---

## 2. Financing the Army of Hardship (*Jaysh al-ʿUsrah*)
To equip thirty thousand men across hundreds of miles of waterless desert, the Prophet ﷺ urged the believers to spend generously in the path of Allāh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:434):
- **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁: Financed one-third of the entire army. He contributed **three hundred camels** complete with saddles and saddlebags, fifty horses, and poured **one thousand gold dinars** directly into the lap of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:434). Overjoyed, the Prophet ﷺ turned the gold coins in his hands and proclaimed repeatedly:
  > «مَا ضَرَّ ابْنَ عَفَّانَ مَا عَمِلَ بَعْدَ الْيَوْمِ!»
  > *"Nothing ʿUthmān does after today can ever harm him!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:434).
- **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁: Brought his entire earthly wealth (four thousand dirhams). When the Prophet ﷺ asked what he had left for his family, he replied: *"I left them Allāh and His Messenger!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:434).
- **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁: Donated half of his entire wealth (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:434).
- **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁: Donated two hundred silver ounces (*ūqiyyah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:434).
- **The Modest Charity of the Poor**: Impoverished Companions brought handfuls of dates. The hypocrites mocked them, sneering that Allāh had no need of a measure of dates and that the rich were merely showing off. Allāh revealed **[[Sūrat al-Tawbah]]** (9:79) condemning the mockers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:435).
- **The Weepers (*al-Bakkāʾūn*)**: Seven impoverished Companions approached the Prophet ﷺ pleading for riding mounts. When he said: *«I find no mounts for you,»* they turned away with their eyes overflowing with tears out of grief that they could not spend in the cause of Allāh (commemorated in Qurʾān 9:92) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:438).

---

## 3. The Lieutenancy of Medina & The Station of Aaron
Prior to departing, the Prophet ﷺ appointed **Muḥammad b. Maslamah** ﵁ over the civil administration of Medina, and appointed **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ as deputy over his household and family (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:441).

The hypocrites began spreading slanderous rumors that the Prophet ﷺ had left ʿAlī behind because he found him a burden. Stricken by their words, ʿAlī seized his armor and weapons, galloped after the Prophet ﷺ, and caught up with him at **al-Jurf**, protesting: *"Do you leave me behind among the women and children?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:441).
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ uttered the immortal declaration:
> «أَمَا تَرْضَى أَنْ تَكُونَ مِنِّي بِمَنْزِلَةِ هَارُونَ مِنْ مُوسَى، إِلَّا أَنَّهُ لَا نَبِيَّ بَعْدِي؟!»
> *"Are you not pleased to be in relation to me as Aaron was to Moses, except that there is no Prophet after me?!"* (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 4416; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, no. 2404; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:441).
Reassured by this supreme spiritual station, ʿAlī returned contented to Medina.

---

## 4. The March of Thirty Thousand & The Sifting of Hypocrites
On Thursday in Rajab 9 AH, the Prophet ﷺ marched out with **thirty thousand men and ten thousand horses**—the largest armed host assembled in Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:442).
- **Desertion of Ibn Ubayy**: The hypocrite chief **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** encamped with his faction at Thaniyyat al-Wadāʿ. When the Muslim army advanced, Ibn Ubayy turned back with his followers, laughing that Muḥammad thought fighting the Byzantines was like fighting Arab tribes (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:442).
- **Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī's Solitary March**: **[[Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī]]** ﵁ was delayed when his emaciated camel collapsed. Rather than returning, Abū Dharr dismounted, loaded his heavy baggage upon his own back, and marched alone on foot under the scorching sun (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:443). As he approached the camp at Tabūk, the Prophet ﷺ gazed at him and exclaimed:
  > «رَحِمَ اللَّهُ أَبَا ذَرٍّ! يَمْشِي وَحْدَهُ، وَيَمُوتُ وَحْدَهُ، وَيُبْعَثُ وَحْدَهُ!»
  > *"May Allāh have mercy on Abū Dharr! He walks alone, he shall die alone, and he shall be resurrected alone!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:443).
- **Passing the Ruins of Thamūd at al-Ḥijr**: Passing through the petrified valley of **al-Ḥijr** (the ruins of the ancient Thamūd), the Prophet ﷺ veiled his face with his cloak, urged his mount forward, and forbade entering their homes except weeping (*bākīn*), lest the divine wrath strike them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:446). He ordered all dough kneaded with their well-water fed to camels and forbade drinking from it (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:446).

---

## 5. Miracles of the Desert & The Spring of Tabūk
During the march through waterless wastes, the troops suffered acute thirst, slaughtering camels to drink the water stored in their rumens (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:447).
- **The Rain Cloud**: The Prophet ﷺ raised his hands in prayer; a lone cloud formed over the camp and poured torrents of rain until every soldier and mount had quenched their thirst (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:447).
- **The Spring of Tabūk**: Reaching Tabūk, they found its natural spring trickling like a shoelace (*tabuḍḍu bi-shayʾin min māʾ*). The Prophet ﷺ dipped his blessed hands in it and washed his face; the spring burst forth like a raging stream (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:450). The Prophet ﷺ turned to Muʿādh b. Jabal and said:
  > «يُوشِكُ يَا مُعَاذُ، إِنْ طَالَتْ بِكَ حَيَاةٌ، أَنْ تَرَى مَا هَاهُنَا قَدْ مُلِئَ جِنَانًا!»
  > *"Soon, O Muʿādh, if your life is extended, you will see what is here turned into lush gardens!"* (*Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:450).

---

## 6. The Prayer Behind ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf
One morning at Tabūk, the Prophet ﷺ departed the camp to answer the call of nature with al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:433).
- Returning as the time of Fajr prayer was expiring, the Muslims had put forward **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁ to lead them (*Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, no. 274).
- The Prophet ﷺ joined the second row behind ʿAbd al-Raḥmān and prayed the final rakʿah behind him. When ʿAbd al-Raḥmān finished with the Salām, the Prophet ﷺ stood to complete his missed rakʿah.
- Seeing the Companions in awe, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ said:
  > «أَحْسَنْتُمْ! مَا قُبِضَ نَبِيٌّ قَطُّ حَتَّى يُصَلِّيَ خَلْفَ رَجُلٍ صَالِحٍ مِنْ أُمَّتِهِ!»
  > *"You did well! No prophet was ever taken in death until he prayed behind a righteous man from his Ummah!"* (*Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:433).

---

## 7. The Peaceful Encampment & Frontier Treaties
The Prophet ﷺ encamped at Tabūk for **twenty days** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:462).
- Hearing of the arrival of thirty thousand disciplined Muslim warriors, the Byzantine garrisons retreated deeper into Syria, completely abandoning their offensive plans (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:457).
- **The Treaties of Peace & Jizyah**: Frontier princes submitted voluntarily:
  1. **Yūḥannah b. Ruʾbah**, Christian ruler of **Aylah** (modern-day Aqaba), rode to Tabūk, presenting the Prophet ﷺ with a white mule and agreeing to pay annual Jizyah (three hundred dinars) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:460).
  2. The Christian communities of **Jarbā** and **Adhruḥ** in southern Jordan concluded formal protection treaties (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:460).
  3. **Khālid b. al-Walīd** was dispatched with 420 cavalry to **Dūmat al-Jandal**, where he captured its prince Ukaydir b. ʿAbd al-Malik al-Kindī and brought him before the Prophet ﷺ, establishing Islamic suzerainty over northern Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:460; 6:220).

---

## 8. The Interring of ʿAbdullāh Dhū al-Bijādayn
During the encampment at Tabūk, the devout penitent **[[ʿAbdullāh Dhū al-Bijādayn]]** al-Muzanī ﵁ succumbed to a sudden fever (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:459).
- **The Night Torch in the Trench**: In the dark of the desert night, **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd]]** ﵁ observed a lone flame at the perimeter of the camp.
- Approaching, he saw the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ standing personally within the deep trench of the grave, while Abū Bakr and ʿUmar lowered the body down. The Prophet ﷺ held ʿAbdullāh gently in his arms, weeping, and prayed:
  > «اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَمْسَيْتُ عَنْهُ رَاضِيًا فَارْضَ عَنْهُ!»
  > *"O Allāh, indeed I have entered upon this evening fully pleased with him, so be You pleased with him!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:139; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:459).

---

## 9. The Ambush at the Mountain Pass (*Laylat al-ʿAqabah*)
On the return journey to Medina, twelve to fifteen hypocrites plotted to assassinate the Prophet ﷺ by spooking his camel along a sheer mountain cliff (*al-ʿAqabah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:466–467).
- The Prophet ﷺ ordered the army to take the valley route while he rode across the pass guided by **[[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]]** ﵁ leading the bridle and **[[Ḥudhayfah b. al-Yamān]]** ﵁ driving from behind (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:466).
- The masked conspirators charged from behind. Divine lightning illuminated their masked faces; Ḥudhayfah attacked them with his curved staff, striking their mounts until they fled in terror into the army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:466).
- The Prophet ﷺ disclosed to Ḥudhayfah the exact names of all fifteen hypocrites, granting Ḥudhayfah the lifelong title of **Keeper of the Prophet's Secret (*Ṣāḥib Sirr Rasūlillāh*)** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:467).

---

## 10. Demolition of the Mosque of Dissension (*Masjid al-Ḍirār*)
Upon nearing Medina at Dhū Awān, revelation descended concerning a structure built by the hypocrites under the guise of piety:
- Led by the faction of the renegade Christian monk Abū ʿĀmir, they had constructed **Masjid al-Ḍirār** to sow discord among the believers and serve as an espionage outpost (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:470).
- Allāh revealed **[[Sūrat al-Tawbah]]** (9:107–108): *«And there are those who took a mosque for causing harm and disbelief and dividing the believers... Do not stand [for prayer] in it, ever!»*
- The Prophet ﷺ dispatched Mālik b. al-Dukhshum and Maʿn b. ʿAdī ﵃, who burned down the structure and razed it to the earth (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:471).

---

## 11. The Three Who Remained Behind (*al-Thalāthah alladhīna Khullifū*)
Upon entering Medina, over eighty hypocrites came before the Prophet ﷺ swearing deceitful oaths to explain their absence, which the Prophet ﷺ accepted outwardly (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:472).
- Three sincere veteran Companions refused to utter a single falsehood: **[[Kaʿb b. Mālik]]** ﵁, **[[Murārah b. al-Rabīʿ]]** ﵁, and **[[Hilāl b. Umayyah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:473).
- The Prophet ﷺ commanded a total social boycott: not a soul in Medina was permitted to speak to them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:474).
- For **fifty nights**, they endured agonizing spiritual purification until the earth constricted upon them despite its vastness.
- At the dawn of the fiftieth day, Allāh revealed their exoneration in **[[Sūrat al-Tawbah]]** (9:118). A horseman galloped to Mount Salʿ shouting the glad tidings, and the entire city rejoiced at the divine pardon (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:476–477).
