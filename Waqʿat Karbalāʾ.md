# Waqʿat Karbalāʾ (وقعة كربلاء)
*The Epic of Karbalāʾ: The Fulfillment of the Prophetic Portents, the Thirst upon the Euphrates, and the Martyrdom of Sayyid al-Shuhadāʾ al-Ḥusayn b. ʿAlī (10 Muḥarram 61 AH)*

## 1. Prophetic Portents & Divine Foretelling
Decades prior to the tragedy, the martyrdom of **[[al-Ḥusayn b. ʿAlī]]** ﵄ at Karbalāʾ was explicitly prophesied to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ through angelic visitation:
- The Archangel Jibrīl ﵊ descended to the Prophet ﷺ bringing red soil from the earth of Karbalāʾ, informing him: *“Indeed, your Ummah shall slay this son of yours in a land called Karbalāʾ”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:69; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:73).
- The Prophet ﷺ wept bitterly and entrusted the soil to his wife, the Mother of the Believers **[[Umm Salamah]]** ﵂, placing it in a glass vial and saying:
  > «إِذَا صَارَتْ هَذِهِ التُّرْبَةُ دَمًا عَبِيطًا، فَاعْلَمِي أَنَّ ابْنِي قَدْ قُتِلَ!»
  > *"When this earth turns into fresh red blood, know that my son has been slain!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:69; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:73).

---

## 2. The March to Iraq & Diplomatic Warnings
Following the death of Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān in 60 AH, al-Ḥusayn declined to swear allegiance to Yazīd b. Muʿāwiyah and departed Medina for Makkah. While in Makkah, delegations and hundreds of letters from the tribal chieftains of Kūfah reached him, imploring him to come to Iraq to establish righteousness and restore the prophetic covenant (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:78).

Senior surviving Companions sought urgently to dissuade al-Ḥusayn from journeying to Iraq:
- **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbbās]]** ﵁ pleaded with him: *“O son of my uncle! The people of Iraq are a treacherous folk; do not go near them! If you must depart, go to Yemen where there are mountain fortresses and partisans of your father”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:78).
- **ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar** and **ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr** likewise appealed to him to remain in the sanctuary of Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:78).
- Recognizing a moral imperative to enjoin what is right and respond to the plight of the believers, al-Ḥusayn set out with his immediate household, women, children, and a loyal cohort of approximately seventy-two companions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:78).

---

## 3. The Encirclement at Karbalāʾ
Upon reaching the plain of **Karbalāʾ** near the western bank of the Euphrates in early Muḥarram 61 AH, al-Ḥusayn asked the name of the valley. Being told it was *Karbalāʾ*, he wept and said:
> «كَرْبٌ وَبَلَاءٌ!»
> *"Sorrow and affliction (Karb wa-Balāʾ)!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:69).

An army of four thousand troops dispatched by ʿUbaydullāh b. Ziyād, commanded by ʿUmar b. Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ, encircled the camp. On the seventh of Muḥarram, cavalry detachments blocked the approaches to the Euphrates river, completely cutting off drinking water from the children and family of the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:70; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:79).

---

## 4. The Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ (10 Muḥarram 61 AH)
On the morning of Friday, 10 Muḥarram (*Yawm ʿĀshūrāʾ*), al-Ḥusayn mounted his steed, opened the Holy Qurʾān before his chest, and addressed the opposing army, reminding them:
> *“Look at my lineage and consider who I am! Am I not the son of your Prophet's daughter, the son of his cousin and the first believer?! Did not the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ declare of me and my brother: 'These two are the masters of the youth of Paradise'?!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:70).

When they demanded unconditional surrender to Ibn Ziyād, al-Ḥusayn refused, proclaiming that a life under oppression was unworthy compared to an honorable death in Allāh's path.

### The Combat and Martyrdoms
A fierce and unequal battle commenced:
- The loyal companions of al-Ḥusayn fought with breathtaking chivalry, falling one by one defending the grandson of the Prophet ﷺ.
- The brothers of al-Ḥusayn—**al-ʿAbbās**, **Jaʿfar**, **ʿAbdullāh**, and **ʿUthmān** (the sons of ʿAlī ﵁ by Umm al-Banīn)—were all slain defending the water carriers and the tents (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:79).
- His youthful son, **ʿAlī al-Akbar**, fell heroically in combat. Even the infant **ʿAbdullāh** (ʿAlī al-Aṣghar) was struck in al-Ḥusayn's arms by a stray arrow while begging for water (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:79).

### The Martyrdom of al-Ḥusayn
Left alone on the battlefield, suffering from extreme dehydration and covered in dozens of sword strikes and arrow wounds, al-Ḥusayn charged the ranks with unmatched courage (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:79). 

When he called out for water, an archer shot an arrow into his jaw/throat, blocking him from drinking; al-Ḥusayn prayed against the man, who was afflicted with unquenchable inner burning until his death (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:79). Finally, surrounded on all sides, al-Ḥusayn was struck down by Sinān b. Anas and Shimr b. Dhī al-Jawshan (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:71; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:80). 

He attained martyrdom at the age of fifty-six on the afternoon of 10 Muḥarram 61 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:70).

---

## 5. Cosmic Portents & Umm Salamah's Vision
In the aftermath of the slaughter, extraordinary signs were witnessed across the Islamic realm:
- In Medina, **[[Umm Salamah]]** ﵂ looked at the vial of earth entrusted to her by the Prophet ﷺ and saw it transformed into boiling red blood. She cried out in al-Masjid al-Nabawī: *“By Allāh, al-Ḥusayn has been killed!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:69; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:73).
- Transmissions recorded that for days after the battle, every stone lifted in Jerusalem or the Levant showed fresh blood beneath it, and the sky remained blood-red at dusk for months (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:70; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:80).
- When the head of al-Ḥusayn was carried on the road to Damascus, the troops halted at an ancient monastery and beheld a miraculous hand writing upon the wall with an iron stylus in letters of blood:
  > «أَتَرْجُو أُمَّةٌ قَتَلَتْ حُسَيْنًا ... شَفَاعَةَ جَدِّهِ يَوْمَ الْحِسَابِ؟!»
  > *"Does a nation that slaughtered Ḥusayn dare to hope ... for the intercession of his grandfather on the Day of Reckoning?!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:80).

---

## 6. Aftermath & Eternal Legacy
The sacred remains of al-Ḥusayn and the martyrs were buried by the people of Banū Asad on the battlefield where they fell, establishing the shrine of **Karbalāʾ** (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:71). His surviving son, **ʿAlī Zayn al-ʿĀbidīn** (who was incapacitated by illness during the fighting), was preserved through the courageous protection of his aunt **Zaynab bint ʿAlī**, ensuring the continuation of the blessed Prophetic lineage (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:80).

The tragedy of Karbalāʾ remains an eternal emblem in Islamic history of unwavering moral defiance against tyranny, supreme sacrifice for divine principle, and the sacred station of the Prophetic household (*Ahl al-Bayt*).
