# Khilāfat ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib (خلافة علي بن أبي طالب)
*The Era of Asad Allāh: The Caliphate of Justice, Suppression of Schism, and Martyrdom in Kūfah (35–40 AH)*

## 1. Succession & Public Pledge in Medina
Following the tragic martyrdom of **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ in Dhū al-Ḥijjah 35 AH, the capital city of Medina was plunged into turmoil. The senior Companions among the Emigrants (*al-Muhājirūn*) and Helpers (*al-Anṣār*) turned directly to **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁, insisting that none possessed greater precedence, virtue, or closeness to the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468).

ʿAlī initially refused the office, preferring to serve as a vizier and counselor rather than amīr (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468). However, as regional factions threatened to fracture the Muslim realm, he consented on the condition that the oath of allegiance (*al-bayʿah*) be taken publicly in al-Masjid al-Nabawī with the consent of the people. On 24 Dhū al-Ḥijjah 35 AH, the public pledge was solemnized in the Prophet's Mosque (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468).

---

## 2. Governance, Administrative Principles & Capital at Kūfah
ʿAlī assumed leadership during an unprecedented crisis of internal factionalism. His administrative policy was marked by uncompromising adherence to fiscal and judicial equity:
- **Relocation of the Administrative Capital**: Recognizing that Medina should be insulated from political discord and military turbulence, and to better oversee the expanding frontiers of Iraq, Persia, and Syria, ʿAlī transferred the seat of administration from Medina to Kūfah in 36 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468).
- **Fiscal Rigor**: He refused to grant political gifts or elevate tribal chiefs above common citizens in the distribution of state revenue (*Bayt al-Māl*), insisting on absolute equality between all Muslims (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:304).
- **Personal Simplicity**: Despite ruling an empire, ʿAlī wore coarse garments, rode an unadorned donkey, and personally purchased provisions from the market, declaring: *“I am the one who has abased worldly luxury”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:304).

---

## 3. The Tragic Civil Trials (al-Fitan)
ʿAlī's five-year administration was characterized by heroic attempts to restore stability while navigating complex internal disputes:
- **[[Waqʿat al-Jamal]] (Jumādā al-Ākhirah 36 AH)**: Encounter near Baṣrah arising from divergent ijtihād over the timing of prosecuting the assassins of ʿUthmān. When rogue elements in both camps incited fighting, ʿAlī prioritized reconciliation, secured the person and honor of **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂, and provided her a dignified escort back to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:148–149).
- **[[Waqʿat Ṣiffīn]] (Ṣafar 37 AH)**: Protracted standoff along the Euphrates between the forces of Iraq and the Levant, which concluded with an agreement to appoint two arbitrators (*Taḥkīm*) to halt the shedding of Muslim blood (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:149–150).
- **[[Waqʿat al-Nahrawān]] (Ṣafar 38 AH)**: The emergence of the renegade Khawārij faction, who renounced both ʿAlī and Muʿāwiyah and engaged in indiscriminate violence against civilians. ʿAlī decisively eliminated their military core at Nahrawān in fulfillment of Prophetic prophecies (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:44; 10:148; 11:305).

## 4. Assassination and Martyrdom (40 AH)
Three Khārijite conspirators convened in Makkah, plotting to simultaneously assassinate ʿAlī, Muʿāwiyah, and ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ on the same night. The assassin assigned to ʿAlī was **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Muljam al-Murādī** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:305–306).

### The Fatal Strike
The Prophet ﷺ had foretold this event decades earlier, asking ʿAlī:
> «مَنْ أَشْقَى الْأَوَّلِينَ؟ قَالَ: عَاقِرُ النَّاقَةِ. قَالَ: فَمَنْ أَشْقَى الْآخِرِينَ؟ قَالَ: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. قَالَ: قَاتِلُكَ!»
> *"Who was the most wretched of the early generations?"* ʿAlī replied: *"The slayer of the she-camel [of Ṣāliḥ ﵊]."* The Prophet ﷺ said: *"And who is the most wretched of the later generations?"* ʿAlī replied: *"Allāh and His Messenger know best."* The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Your assassin!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:305).

On the morning of Friday, 17 (or 19) Ramaḍān 40 AH, as ʿAlī emerged into the Grand Mosque of Kūfah calling the people to the dawn prayer (*«al-ṣalāh, al-ṣalāh!»*), Ibn Muljam struck him with a poisoned sword across the brow, cutting into his forehead and skull (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306).

As the blow struck, ʿAlī exclaimed:
> «فُزْتُ وَرَبِّ الْكَعْبَةِ!»
> *"I have triumphed, by the Lord of the Kaʿbah!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306).

### Testament and Legacy
ʿAlī lived for two days following the wound. In his final hours, he gathered his sons **[[al-Ḥasan b. ʿAlī]]** and **[[al-Ḥusayn b. ʿAlī]]**, bequeathing a profound spiritual and ethical testament:
- To fear Allāh, pursue the truth, show compassion to orphans, maintain ties of kinship, and uphold the Holy Qurʾān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306).
- He explicitly ordered that his assassin be fed and treated humanely, and that if he died, only a single retaliatory strike be exacted without mutilation or torture (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306).

ʿAlī attained martyrdom on the night of twenty-first of Ramaḍān 40 AH at the age of sixty-three (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:306–307). He was washed by al-Ḥasan, al-Ḥusayn, and ʿAbdullāh b. Jaʿfar, shrouded in three white garments, and laid to rest in Kūfah (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:307).

With ʿAlī's passing, the era of the Rightly Guided Caliphate reached its culmination, followed immediately by the brief six-month reconciliatory reign of his son **[[al-Ḥasan b. ʿAlī]]** ﵁ before his voluntary abdication to unify the Ummah (*ʿĀm al-Jamāʿah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:153).
