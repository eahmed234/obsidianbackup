# Taḥwīl al-Qiblah (تحويل القبلة إلى الكعبة المشرفة)
*The Divine Redirection of the Prayer Axis from Jerusalem to the Ancient Sanctuary of Makkah*

## 1. Historical & Spiritual Context
During the Makkan period, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ prayed facing **Bayt al-Maqdis** (Jerusalem) while positioning the **Kaʿbah** directly between his hands, thereby aligning toward both sanctuaries simultaneously (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370). Upon the [[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah|Hijrah]] to Medina, where geographical alignment made combining both directions impossible (Medina lying directly between Makkah to the south and Jerusalem to the north), the Prophet ﷺ was commanded by Allāh to orient the prayer exclusively northward toward the rock of Bayt al-Maqdis (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370).

The Muslims prayed toward Jerusalem for sixteen or seventeen months following the arrival in Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370, 3:373). Throughout this period, the Prophet ﷺ harbored a profound spiritual yearning (*shawq*) that the community be redirected toward the Kaʿbah—the foundational sanctuary raised by his forefather Ibrāhīm ﵊ and Ismāʿīl ﵊, and the ancient focal point of the Arabian peninsula. The Prophet ﷺ remarked to Jibrīl ﵊:
> «وَدِدْتُ أَنَّ اللَّهَ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ صَرَفَنِي عَنْ قِبْلَةِ يَهُودَ إِلَى غَيْرِهَا»
> *"I deeply yearn that Allāh, Mighty and Majestic, redirect me away from the qiblah of the Jews to another."*
> Jibrīl ﵊ replied: *«إِنَّمَا أَنَا عَبْدٌ مِثْلُكَ لَا أَمْلِكُ لَكَ شَيْئًا إِلَّا مَا أُمِرْتُ بِهِ، فَادْعُ اللَّهَ تَعَالَى»* (*"I am merely a servant like you, possessing no power for you except that which I am commanded; so supplicate Allāh the Exalted"*), whereupon the Prophet ﷺ frequently lifted his gaze toward the heavens in silent anticipation of divine instruction (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370).

## 2. The Climax at Masjid al-Qiblatayn
In the middle of Rajab (or according to the majority including al-Nawawī, mid-Shaʿbān) in the second year of the Hijrah, roughly two months prior to [[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]], the Prophet ﷺ visited the maternal home of **[[Bishr b. al-Barāʾ]] b. Maʿrūr** in the western settlement of **[[Banū Salimah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370).

His hostess prepared a meal for him and his companions. When the time for the noon prayer (*Ẓuhr*) arrived, the Prophet ﷺ led the congregation in the local mosque. Having completed the first two rakʿahs facing north toward Bayt al-Maqdis, the Angel Jibrīl ﵊ descended into the prayer niche (*miḥrāb*) with the long-awaited divine decree:
> «قَدْ نَرَىٰ تَقَلُّبَ وَجْهِكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ ۖ فَلَنُوَلِّيَنَّكَ قِبْلَةً تَرْضَاهَا ۚ فَوَلِّ وَجْهَكَ شَطْرَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ ۚ وَحَيْثُ مَا كُنتُمْ فَوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ شَطْرَهُ»
> *"We have certainly seen the turning of your face toward the heaven, and We will surely turn you to a qiblah with which you will be pleased. So turn your face toward al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, and wherever you [believers] are, turn your faces toward it..."* (Qurʾān 2:144; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370, 3:372).

Without severing or interrupting the prayer, the Prophet ﷺ pivoted completely southward toward Makkah, facing directly toward the rainwater spout (*al-Mīzāb*) of the Kaʿbah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370). The congregation smoothly mirrored his motion mid-prayer: the men advanced to occupy the rear area where the women had stood, and the women shifted backward into the former men's rows, completing the remaining two rakʿahs facing the Sacred Sanctuary. From that day forward, the sanctuary was commemorated as **Masjid al-Qiblatayn** (The Mosque of the Two Qiblahs) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370).

## 3. Dissemination Across the Madinan Oases

### The Midnight & Afternoon Proclamations
The news of the redirection rippled across the various clan settlements of Medina, highlighting the immediate obedience of the early community:
1. **Banū Ḥārithah & Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal**: The Companion **[[ʿAbbād b. Bishr]]** ﵁, having completed the Ẓuhr prayer alongside the Prophet ﷺ at the central sanctuary, rode past the mosque of Banū Ḥārithah while the congregation was bowing in the afternoon prayer (*ʿAṣr*). Standing at the threshold, he cried out:
   > «أَشْهَدُ بِاللَّهِ لَقَدْ صَلَّيْتُ مَعَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ قِبَلَ الْبَيْتِ!»
   > *"I bear witness by Allāh, I have just prayed alongside the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ facing the Sacred House!"*
   Without rising from their bowing position (*rukūʿ*), the entire congregation and their imām pivoted southward toward Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370–371).
2. **Masjid Qubāʾ**: The news reached the outlying southern settlement of Qubāʾ the following morning during the dawn prayer (*Fajr*). An announcer cried: *«إِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَدْ أُنْزِلَ عَلَيْهِ قُرْآنٌ، وَقَدْ أُمِرَ أَنْ يَسْتَقْبِلَ الْكَعْبَةَ فَاسْتَقْبِلُوهَا»* (*"Indeed, revelation has descended upon the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanding him to face the Kaʿbah, so face it!"*). The worshippers, whose faces were pointed toward Syria, immediately rotated in unison to face Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:371).

## 4. Polemical Reactions & Scriptural Commentary
The redirection served as a profound divine crucible, sorting sincere faith from hypocrisy and theological intransigence:

### The Accusations of the Opponents
- **The Jewish Rabbinate**: Rabbis such as Rīfāʿah b. Qays, Kardam b. ʿAmr, and Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf came to the Prophet ﷺ protesting: *"What has turned you from the qiblah of Mūsā, Yaʿqūb, and the ancient prophets? Return to our qiblah and we shall follow you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:371).
- **The Hypocrites (*al-Munāfiqūn*)**: Mocked the believers, claiming: *«خرّ محمد إلى أرضه»* (*"Muḥammad has grown homesick and lapsed back toward his native territory!"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:371).
- **The Polytheists of Quraysh**: Speculated that the Prophet ﷺ was acknowledging the superiority of their ancestral pagan sanctuary and would soon reconcile with their pantheon (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:371).

### The Divine Verdicts in Sūrat al-Baqarah
Allāh revealed a series of definitive responses in **[[Sūrat al-Baqarah]]**:
1. Foretelling their objections before they were uttered:
   > «سَيَقُولُ السُّفَهَاءُ مِنَ النَّاسِ مَا وَلَّاهُمْ عَن قِبْلَتِهِمُ الَّتِي كَانُوا عَلَيْهَا ۚ قُل لِّلَّهِ الْمَشْرِقُ وَالْمَغْرِبُ ۚ يَهْدِي مَن يَشَاءُ إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ»
   > *"The foolish among the people will say, 'What has turned them from their qiblah which they used to face?' Say, 'To Allāh belongs the east and the west. He guides whom He wills to a straight path.'"* (Qurʾān 2:142; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:371).
2. Designating the Muslim community as the arbiters of truth:
   > «وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا لِّتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ وَيَكُونَ الرَّسُولُ عَلَيْكُمْ شَهِيدًا»
   > *"And thus We have made you a median nation that you will be witnesses over the people and the Messenger will be a witness over you..."* (Qurʾān 2:143; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:371).
3. Confirming the trial of obedience:
   > «وَمَا جَعَلْنَا الْقِبْلَةَ الَّتِي كُنتَ عَلَيْهَا إِلَّا لِنَعْلَمَ مَن يَتَّبِعُ الرَّسُولَ مِمَّن يَنقَلِبُ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيْهِ»
   > *"And We did not make the qiblah which you used to face except that We might make evident who would follow the Messenger from who would turn back on his heels..."* (Qurʾān 2:143; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:371).

### Comfort for Deceased Believers
A profound anxiety struck the Companions regarding brethren who had passed away during the preceding years while praying toward Jerusalem—such as the Anṣārī leaders **[[al-Barāʾ b. Maʿrūr]]** and **[[Asʿad b. Zurārah]]**, as well as Muhājirūn who died in Makkah and Abyssinia (ten foremost individuals cataloged by classical authorities) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:374). The believers asked: *"O Messenger of Allāh, what of the prayers of our brethren who died facing Jerusalem?"* Allāh comforted their hearts by revealing:
> «وَمَا كَانَ اللَّهُ لِيُضِيعَ إِيمَانَكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِالنَّاسِ لَرَءُوفٌ رَّحِيمٌ»
> *"And never would Allāh have caused your faith [i.e., your prayers] to be lost. Indeed Allāh is, to the people, Kind and Merciful."* (Qurʾān 2:143; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:372).

## 5. Jurisprudential Significance (*Aḥkām*)
Classical jurists and hadith masters derived fundamental legal maxims from this event:
- **Abrogation (*al-Naskh*)**: The transfer of the Qiblah is universally recognized as the first explicit abrogation (*naskh*) of an established religious obligation in the Madinan era (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370).
- **Authority of the Solitary Report (*Khabar al-Wāḥid*)**: The congregations at Banū Ḥārithah and Qubāʾ altered their fundamental axis of worship mid-prayer based entirely upon the testimony of a single trustworthy narrator, establishing that *khabar al-wāḥid* constitutes actionable proof in religious law.
- **Physical Motion in Prayer**: Pivoting completely and adjusting rows without verbal utterance does not invalidate prayer when performed in direct obedience to divine mandate.
