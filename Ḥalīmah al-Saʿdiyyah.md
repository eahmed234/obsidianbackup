# Ḥalīmah al-Saʿdiyyah (حليمة السعدية)
*The Wet-Nurse Whose Poverty Turned to Abundance the Moment She Took Him*

## 1. Her People, and Ten Who Nursed Him
Al-Ṣāliḥī opens the chapter on his ﷺ wet-nurses by naming **ten women** said to have nursed him:
first his own mother **[[Āminah bint Wahb]]** for seven days; then **Thuwaybah**, freedwoman of
**[[Abū Lahab]]**, on the milk of her own son Masrūḥ (she had also nursed **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd
al-Muṭṭalib|Ḥamzah]]** ﵁ before him and **Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Asad** after him); and then Ḥalīmah,
of **Banū Saʿd b. Bakr** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:375).

## 2. Arrival in Poverty
Ḥalīmah came to Makkah with the other wet-nurses of her tribe seeking infants to nurse, in a year of
severe drought. She describes it herself:

> «قَدِمْتُ عَلَىٰ أَتَانٍ لِي قَمْرَاءَ قَدْ أَزَمَتْ بِالرَّكْبِ حَتَّىٰ شَقَّ ذَٰلِكَ عَلَيْهِمْ ضَعْفًا وَعَجَفًا، وَمَعِي صَبِيٌّ لَنَا، وَشَارِفٌ لَنَا وَاللَّهِ مَا تَبِضُّ بِقَطْرَةٍ، وَمَا نَنَامُ لَيْلَنَا أَجْمَعَ مِنْ صَبِيِّنَا ذَاكَ، لَا يَجِدُ فِي شَارِفِنَا مَا يَكْفِيهِ وَلَا فِي ثَدْيَيَّ مَا يُغْنِيهِ»
> *"I came upon a pale she-donkey of mine so slow it held back the whole caravan, weak and emaciated,
> with an infant of our own, and an aged she-camel that gave not a drop, by Allāh; and we did not
> sleep a single night entire, for that infant of ours found neither enough in our she-camel nor
> enough in my own breasts."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:386)

Each nurse in the party was given an orphaned child — most were reluctant, expecting no reward from a
fatherless boy's family, and only Ḥalīmah remained without one, so she took **[[Muḥammad b. ʻAbdullah|
Muḥammad]] ﷺ**, saying she could not bear to return empty-handed while her companions all had infants.
As she carried him back to her mount, her husband, **al-Ḥārith b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā**, said: *"Take note,
O Ḥalīmah — I think you have taken a blessed soul."*

## 3. Immediate Abundance
The moment she put him to her breast, milk flowed abundantly enough for both him and her own son. Her
husband went that night to their old she-camel — which had given nothing — and found her **udder
full**; they milked her and drank until satisfied and slept that whole night in comfort, the first in
a long while (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:386–387). On the return journey her weak she-donkey outpaced the
whole caravan, so that her companions asked in astonishment, *"O daughter of Abū Dhuʾayb, is that the
same donkey you set out with?"* — and she affirmed it, adding, *"By Allāh, it has some affair to
it."*

Arriving in the land of Banū Saʿd, her flock returned each evening full of milk when the whole land
lay in drought, so that other herdsmen told their own shepherds to graze wherever Ḥalīmah's flock
grazed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:387).

## 4. An Attempted Killing at ʿUkāẓ and Dhū al-Majāz
Passing through the market of **ʿUkāẓ**, a soothsayer saw the child and cried: *"O people of ʿUkāẓ,
kill this boy, for he shall have dominion!"* — Ḥalīmah slipped away with him and he was saved
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:387). A similar episode occurred at **Dhū al-Majāz**, where a diviner who examined
children brought to him saw the mark between his shoulders and the redness in his eyes and cried out
for the Arabs to kill him, *"for the people of his religion will kill you and shatter your idols"* —
again Ḥalīmah escaped with him. Al-Ṣāliḥī records that the man who had incited the crowd against him
went mad and died a disbeliever not long after (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:388).

## 5. Foster-Siblings
Through Ḥalīmah's household and Thuwaybah's, the Prophet ﷺ had several foster-siblings
(*al-Bāb al-Thānī fī ikhwatihi ﷺ min al-riḍāʿah*):

- **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ — nursed by both Thuwaybah and Ḥalīmah. Asked by
  **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ whether he would marry Ḥamzah's ﵁ daughter, the Prophet ﷺ answered:
  *"She is my foster-niece."*
- **Abū Salamah ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbd al-Asad** — nursed by Thuwaybah, confirmed in the Prophet's ﷺ own
  words in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* when declining to marry Abū Salamah's daughter: *"she is my foster-niece
  — Thuwaybah nursed me and Abū Salamah."*
- **ʿAbdullāh b. al-Ḥārith** — Ḥalīmah's own son, who shared drinking with him as a child.
- **Ḥafṣ b. al-Ḥārith** and **Umayyah bint al-Ḥārith**, her other children.
- **al-Shaymāʾ bint al-Ḥārith** (her given name recorded variously as Khudhāmah, Judhāmah, or
  Hudhāfah) — who helped nurse and carry him alongside her mother, and later composed verses of
  affection for him, calling him *"the best of mankind, more beautiful than the face of the moon."*

(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:379–381)

## 6. Return to Makkah, and the Extended Stay
At about two years — some reports say four — Ḥalīmah's household brought him to visit his mother,
reluctant to give him up given the blessing he had brought them. On the way, they fell in with a party
of Abyssinians who studied the seal of prophethood between his shoulders and the redness of his eyes
and declared: *"By Allāh, he is a prophet!"* Reaching Āminah, Ḥalīmah begged to keep him longer,
citing fear of Makkah's ill air for him, and Āminah agreed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:388).

## 7. The Splitting of His Chest
It was during this extended stay, while he was out with other boys, that the event of
**[[Shaqq al-Ṣadr]]** occurred — the opening of his chest by the angels and the removal of what is
described as *"the portion of Shayṭān"* from his heart. Alarmed, Ḥalīmah and her husband resolved to
return him to his mother without delay, fearing something had befallen him
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:389–390).

## 8. Lost, and Found by ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib's Prayer
On this return, Ḥalīmah lost track of him briefly amid the crowds approaching his family's quarter in
Makkah. She went to **[[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]**, telling him she had brought Muḥammad that night but lost
him at the edge of the city and did not know where he was. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib stood at the Kaʿbah calling
upon Allāh to return him, reciting:

> «لَا هُمَّ رُدَّ رَاكِبِي مُحَمَّدَا … اُرْدُدْهُ لِي ثُمَّ اتَّخِذْ عِنْدِي يَدَا»
> *"O Allāh, return my rider Muḥammad to me — return him to me, and thereby place a favour with me."*

A voice was heard from the sky telling the people not to be alarmed, for *"Muḥammad has a Lord who
will never forsake him nor let him be lost,"* and directing them to the valley of Tihāmah, by a tree,
where he was found safe. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib rewarded Ḥalīmah generously and sent her home
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:390–391).

## 9. Her Islam and Later Visits
**Ibn Kathīr** held that Ḥalīmah likely did not live to see the Prophet's ﷺ mission. **Ibn Ḥajar
al-ʿAsqalānī**, in *Sharḥ al-Durar*, disputes this on the strength of narrations from
**ʿAbdullāh b. Jaʿfar** ﵄, who reported directly from her, and from testimony that she came to the
Prophet ﷺ after his marriage to **[[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid]]** ﵂ complaining of drought, whereupon
Khadījah ﵂ gave her forty sheep and a camel — and again after the conquest of **Ḥunayn**, where the
Prophet ﷺ spread out his own cloak for her to sit upon (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:382–384). She is counted
among the female Companions who narrated from him, listed by Abū Bakr Aḥmad b. Abī Khaythamah under
the letter *ḥāʾ*: *"Ḥalīmah bint Abī Dhuʾayb."*

Her husband **al-Ḥārith**'s own conversion is reported to have come only **after the Prophet's ﷺ
death**, though he is said to have declared, on embracing Islam, that had his foster-son taken him by
the hand and shown him the way himself, he would not have let go until he entered Paradise
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:385).
