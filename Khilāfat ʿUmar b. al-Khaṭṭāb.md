# Khilāfat ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb (خلافة عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه)
*The Caliphate of ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb (13–23 AH) — The Era of al-Fārūq, The Great Imperial Conquests, Civic Institutions, and the Entry into Jerusalem*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Sources
> This master historical entry is derived strictly from *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 4, pp. 484–486, entry no. 5752 of **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁), cross-referenced with *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād* by Imām al-Ṣāliḥī (Volume 11, pp. 274–275; Vol. 12, p. 36).

---

## 1. Ascension & Designation as *Amīr al-Muʾminīn*

In Jumādā al-Ākhirah in the year thirteen AH, following the testament of **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and unanimous endorsement across the community, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ was inaugurated as the Second Rightly Guided Caliph (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275; *al-Iṣābah*, 4:484).

Initially addressed as *"Successor to the Successor of the Messenger of Allāh"* (*Khalīfat Khalīfat Rasūlillāh*), the Companions deemed this title cumbersome; ʿUmar was permanently designated with the formal title **Amīr al-Muʾminīn** (Commander of the Faithful), being the very first caliph in Islamic history to hold this sovereign title (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

---

## 2. Foundational State Institutions (*al-Awāʾil*)

During his decade of governance (13–23 AH), ʿUmar established the comprehensive administrative and legal framework of the Islamic state (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275; 12:36):
1. **The Hijrī Calendar**: In the sixteenth or seventeenth year of the Hijrah, upon consultation with **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁, ʿUmar formally established the Islamic calendar, setting the year of the Prophet's migration (**[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**) as year one and commencing with the month of Muḥarram (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:36).
2. **The Diwān & Bayt al-Māl**: Organized the central treasury, state registries (*dawāwīn*), military stipends based on priority in Islam (*al-sābiqah*), and public welfare for the poor, orphans, and non-Muslim subjects (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
3. **The Metropolitan Garrisons (*al-Amṣār*)**: Directed the foundation and urban layout of the great garrison cities: **Basra** (founded by **[[ʿUtbah b. Ghazwān]]** in 17 AH), **Kūfah** (founded by **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** in 17 AH), and **al-Fusṭāṭ** in Egypt (founded by **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** in 20 AH) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:364, 539; 3:62).
4. **Tarāwīḥ in Congregation**: Gathered the Muslims behind a single imam (**[[Ubayy b. Kaʿb]]** ﵁) in the Prophet's Mosque during the nights of Ramaḍān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
5. **Annual Pilgrimage Oversight**: Personally led the Hajj pilgrimage for the believers for ten consecutive years (*ḥajja bil-nās ʿashra sinīna mutawāliyāt*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

---

## 3. The Syrian Front & The Liberation of Jerusalem (13–16 AH)

On the northwestern front, ʿUmar coordinated the total expulsion of the Byzantine Empire from the Levant (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275):
- **Battle of al-Yarmūk (15 AH)**: Mobilized the unified Syrian armies under the overall field command of **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** and **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁, shattering the Roman legions of Emperor Heraclius in a six-day epic along the Yarmūk River (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:170; 3:476).
- **Conquest of Damascus**: Captured the ancient Syrian metropolis following a combined assault and treaty breach managed by Khālid and Abū ʿUbaydah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
- **Fatḥ Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem, 16 AH)**: When the holy city of Jerusalem was surrounded, the Christian Patriarch Sophronius refused to surrender the keys except to Caliph ʿUmar in person. ʿUmar undertook the historic journey from Medina to Jerusalem mounted on a single camel alternating with his servant; he concluded the famous covenant of protection (*al-ʿUhdah al-ʿUmariyyah*), entered the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, and cleansed the Temple Mount (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

---

## 4. The Iraqi & Persian Front (14–21 AH)

On the eastern front, ʿUmar appointed **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁ as supreme commander over the armies of Iraq in the year fifteen AH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:62):
- **Battle of al-Qādisiyyah (15 AH)**: A four-day cataclysmic engagement wherein Saʿd's thirty thousand warriors dismantled the massive Sasanian army under Rostam, killing Rostam and capturing the imperial jewel-encrusted standard (*Derafsh Kaviani*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).
- **Conquest of al-Madāʾin (Ctesiphon, 16 AH)**: The Muslim armies crossed the flooded Tigris River without losing a beast, capturing the white palace of Chosroes and distributing the legendary Persian royal treasures (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).
- **Battle of Jalūlāʾ (19 AH)**: Commanded by Saʿd, breaking the reorganized Persian army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
- **Battle of Nahāwand (Fatḥ al-Futūḥ, 21 AH)**: Commanded by **al-Nuʿmān b. Muqarrin al-Muzanī** ﵁, ending Sasanian centralized authority forever and opening the entirety of the Persian plateau (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

---

## 5. The Liberation of Egypt (19–21 AH)

Following the conference of al-Jābiyah, **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁ marched westward into Egypt with four thousand men, reinforced by **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ with eight thousand warriors (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:539). 

The Muslim army took the fortress of Babylon, liberated Alexandria, and concluded treaties with the native Coptic population, guaranteeing freedom of worship and property rights (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:539).

---

## 6. Martyrdom & The Consultative Shūrā Council (23 AH)

On Wednesday, four nights remaining in Dhū al-Ḥijjah in the year twenty-three AH (26 Dhū al-Ḥijjah 23 AH), while initiating the dawn prayer in **[[al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]**, ʿUmar was fatally stabbed by the Persian slave Abū Luʾluʾah Fīrūz (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:274–275).

On his deathbed, ʿUmar established the supreme consultative council (**al-Shūrā**) composed of the six surviving members of the Ten Promised Paradise:
1. **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁
2. **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁
3. **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁
4. **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁
5. **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁
6. **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

ʿUmar passed away at the age of sixty-three and was buried beside the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq in the sacred chamber of **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ on Sunday, the new moon of Muḥarram twenty-four AH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
