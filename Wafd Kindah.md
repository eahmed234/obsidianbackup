# Wafd Kindah (وفد كندة)
*The arrival of sixty to eighty silk-clad royal horsemen of Kindah under al-Ashʿath b. Qays, the shedding of imperial silks, and the clarification of Qurashī lineage*

## 1. Royal Cavalcade & Pre-Islamic Pride

In the 10th year of the Hijrah, the royal delegation of **Kindah**—the legendary southern aristocracy of Hadramawt and Najd—arrived in Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402). The delegation consisted of **sixty to eighty mounted noblemen** (*fī thamānīna aw sittīna rākiban min Kindah*), headed by their paramount prince, **[[al-Ashʿath b. Qays]]** al-Kindī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).

Reflecting their regal heritage, the horsemen entered the Prophetic Mosque in full royal ostentation: their hair combed and oiled into ringlets falling to their shoulders (*rajjalū jumamahum*), their eyes kohled (*iktaḥalū*), and wearing opulent Yemeni garments edged and trimmed with broad bands of precious silk (*jubāb al-ḥibarāt mukaffafatan bi-al-ḥarīr*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402, 403).

## 2. Stripping the Imperial Silks

When they entered the presence of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, the Prophet ﷺ cast a look upon their attire and asked:
> «أَوَلَمْ تُسْلِمُوا؟»
> *"Have you not submitted in Islam?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).

They replied: *"Yes, indeed, O Messenger of Allāh."* The Prophet ﷺ then sternly asked:
> «فَمَا هَذَا الْحَرِيرُ فِي أَعْنَاقِكُمْ؟»
> *"Then what is this forbidden silk around your necks?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).

Recognizing immediately the incompatibility of imperial vain luxury with the spiritual austerity of Islam, the noblemen of Kindah reached to their collars, tore off the silk edgings on the spot, and cast them onto the floor of the mosque (*fa-shaqqūhu wa-nazaʿūhu wa-alqaWhu*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).

## 3. Genealogy & The Rebuttal of Maternal Affiliation

Seeking to establish an elite familial kinship with the Prophet ﷺ, al-Ashʿath b. Qays declared:
> «يا رسول الله، نحن بنو آكل المرار، وأنت ابن آكل المرار»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh! We are the sons of Ākil al-Murār (the ancient royal progenitor of Kindah), and you are the son of Ākil al-Murār!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).

The Prophet ﷺ smiled, yet gently but firmly corrected him:
> «ناسِبْ بهذا النّسب ربيعة بن الحارث والعباس بن عبد المطلب... لا، بل نحن بنو النضر بن كنانة، لا نَقْفُو أُمَّنَا، ولا نَنْتَفِي مِنْ أَبِينَا!»
> *"Ascribe this lineage to Rabīʿah b. al-Ḥārith and al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib... Nay! We are the descendants of al-Naḍr b. Kinānah; we do not cast aside our mothers, nor do we disown our paternal ancestors!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).

The Prophet ﷺ explained that while some merchants of Quraysh—such as al-ʿAbbās and Rabīʿah—traveled through southern Arabia using maternal links to Kindah (through Hind or Daʿd bint Sharīd) to secure royal safe passage, Quraysh's immutable patrilineal identity traced directly to **al-Naḍr b. Kinānah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402). 

Al-Ashʿath was so profoundly impressed by this genealogical precision that he swore: *"Never shall any man be brought before me who denies that Quraysh descend from al-Naḍr b. Kinānah except that I will administer to him the prescribed lash!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402–403).

## 4. Prophetic Counsel Regarding Offspring

During the audience, the Prophet ﷺ asked al-Ashʿath about his household: *«Hal laka min walad?»* (*"Do you have any children?"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:403). Al-Ashʿath replied that just before departing on his journey, a baby boy had been born to him, adding carelessly: *"I would have preferred that a feast were provided for the people instead of having him!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:403).

The Prophet ﷺ immediately corrected this pre-Islamic callousness, imparting a lasting ethical maxim:
> «لا تقولنّ ذا! فإن فيهم قرّة عين وأجرا إذا قُبضوا... وإنهم لَمَجْبَنَةٌ مَبْخَلَةٌ»
> *"Do not say that! For in children there is solace of the eyes (*qurratu ʿayn*) and immense divine reward when they are taken in death; yet verily, they are an occasion for cowardice and miserliness!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:403).

The members of the delegation embraced Islam and departed with diplomatic honors, returning to Hadramawt (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).

---

## 5. Strategic Significance

1. **Abolition of Aristocratic Ostentation**: The immediate tearing off of the silk borders demonstrated the supremacy of Islamic egalitarian codes over ancient monarchical privileges (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).
2. **Canonical Lineage Protection**: The Prophet's formulation *«Lā naqfū ummanā wa-lā nantafī min abīnā»* established the legal principle governing patrilineal descent and genealogy in Islamic jurisprudence (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).
3. **Integration of Hadramawt**: The submission of Kindah brought the vast southern territories of Hadramawt and Najd into formal administrative integration with the Madinan Caliphate (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:402).
