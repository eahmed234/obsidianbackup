# Zaynab bint Jaḥsh (زينب بنت جحش) ﵂
*Umm al-Muʾminīn (Mother of the Believers) — Wed by the Lord of the Heavens, The Lady of the Long Hand in Charity, First of the Pure Wives to Rejoin the Prophet ﷺ*

## 1. Lineage, Sacred Kinship & Early Precedence
### Patrilineal Descent & Nobility
She is **Zaynab bint Jaḥsh b. Riʾāb b. Yaʿmar b. Ṣabrah b. Murrah b. Kabīr b. Ghanm b. Dūdān b. Asad b. Khuzaymah al-Asadiyyah** (original birth name: **Barrah**, which the Prophet ﷺ changed to **Zaynab**) (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:153; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:201). 

- **Father**: **Jaḥsh b. Riʾāb**, confederate (*ḥalīf*) of the chieftain ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib b. Hāshim in Makkah.
- **Mother**: **Umaymah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib**, the paternal aunt of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:153; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:201).
- **Distinction**: Through her mother, Zaynab was the direct first cousin (*bint ʿammah*) of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ.
- **Brothers**: The hero and martyr of Badr and Uḥud **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh]]** ﵁ and the poet Abū Aḥmad b. Jaḥsh; sister of **Ḥamnah bint Jaḥsh** ﵂.

### Early Conversion & Migration
Embracing Islam during the earliest dawn of the Makkan call, Zaynab endured the ostracism of Quraysh and was among the foremost Emigrants to Medina (**[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**), where the entire household of Banū Jaḥsh locked their houses and departed for Allāh's cause (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:153).

## 2. The Divine Marriage in Heaven (5 AH)
Zaynab was initially married in Medina to **[[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]** ﵁, the beloved freedman and adopted son of the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:153). When marital incompatibility arose and Zayd divorced her, Allāh Almighty dissolved the pre-Islamic practice of legal adoption (*tabannī*) and commanded the Prophet ﷺ to marry Zaynab directly (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:201).

### The Qurʾānic Solemnization (Sūrat al-Aḥzāb 33:37)
Her marriage was contracted directly by Allāh Almighty in the Seven Heavens without the intermediary of an earthly guardian (*walī*) or physical witnesses:
> «فَلَمَّا قَضَىٰ زَيْدٌ مِّنْهَا وَطَرًا زَوَّجْنَاكَهَا لِكَيْ لَا يَكُونَ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ حَرَجٌ فِي أَزْوَاجِ أَدْعِيَائِهِمْ إِذَا قَضَوْا مِنْهُنَّ وَطَرًا ۚ وَكَانَ أَمْرُ اللَّهِ مَفْعُولًا»
> *«So when Zayd had dissolved his marriage with her, We wedded her to you, so that there should be no difficulty for the believers in respect of the wives of their adopted sons when they have dissolved their marriage with them; and the command of Allāh must be fulfilled»* (al-Aḥzāb 33:37) (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:153; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:201).

Zaynab proudly and rightfully declared to the other Mothers of the Believers:
> «زَوَّجَكُنَّ أَهَالِيكُنَّ، وَزَوَّجَنِي اللَّهُ مِنْ فَوْقِ سَبْعِ سَمَاوَاتٍ!»
> *"Your families married you [to the Prophet ﷺ], while Allāh married me from above the Seven Heavens!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:153; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:201).

### The Wedding Feast & The Verse of Hijāb (33:53)
For her wedding feast (*walīmah*), the Prophet ﷺ slaughtered a sheep—the most generous feast he gave for any of his marriages—supplemented by date confection (*ḥays*) sent by Umm Sulaym (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:202). When guests lingered conversing after the meal in the small chamber, the divine command of the veil (**Hijāb**) was revealed:
> *«O you who believe! Enter not the Prophet's houses, except when leave is given unto you for a meal... and when you ask of them anything, ask of them from behind a screen»* (al-Aḥzāb 33:53) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:202).

## 3. "The Longest Hand in Charity" & Devotion
Zaynab was renowned across Medina for intense fear of Allāh, scrupulous honesty, constant night devotions, and consummate craftsmanship:
- The Prophet ﷺ once said to his wives:
  > «أَسْرَعُكُنَّ لُحُوقًا بِي أَطْوَلُكُنَّ يَدًا!»
  > *"The fastest of you to join me [after death] shall be the one with the longest hand!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:203).
- The wives initially measured their arms against the wall with a reed; only after her death did they realize the Prophet ﷺ meant the "length of hand" in dispensing generous charity (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:204).
- Zaynab was an expert manual artisan (*ṣanāʿ*): she tanned animal hides, stitched leather goods, and donated every single dirham earned exclusively to the destitute and orphans of Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:154; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:203).
- **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ bore supreme witness to her station:
  > «مَا رَأَيْتُ امْرَأَةً قَطُّ خَيْرًا فِي الدِّينِ مِنْ زَيْنَبَ، وَأَتْقَى لِلَّهِ، وَأَصْدَقَ حَدِيثًا، وَأَوْصَلَ لِلرَّحِمِ، وَأَعْظَمَ صَدَقَةً!»
  > *"I have never seen any woman better in religion than Zaynab, more fearful of Allāh, more truthful in speech, more devoted to maintaining kinship ties, or greater in giving charity!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:154; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:203).
- During the tragic trial of the slander (*al-Ifk*), despite being ʿĀʾishah's primary peer in status, Zaynab defended ʿĀʾishah with absolute righteousness, declaring: *“O Messenger of Allāh, I protect my hearing and sight! By Allāh, I know nothing of her except pure good!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:154).

## 4. The First to Join the Prophet ﷺ (20 AH)
When Caliph **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ instituted the imperial payroll registers (*al-dawāwīn*), he dispatched twelve thousand dirhams to Zaynab as her state allowance. 

Looking at the immense sum, she covered her eyes with her garment and cried: *“May Allāh forgive ʿUmar! Other sisters of mine were more capable of distributing this.”* When informed it was all for her, she ordered it poured beneath a cloth and commanded her maidservant Barzah bint Rāfiʿ to distribute handfuls to all the poor and orphans of her clan until not a dirham remained. Only eighty-five dirhams remained under the cloth, which she gave to Barzah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:154; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:204). She then raised her hands, praying:
> *“O Allāh, let not the state stipend of ʿUmar reach me after this year!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:204).

### Death and Burial
Allāh answered her prayer: Zaynab passed away in Medina in the year 20 AH at the age of fifty-three—the very first of the Mothers of the Believers to pass away after the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, fulfilling the Prophetic prophecy (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:154; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:204).

Caliph ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁ led her funeral prayer with four Takbīrs in the Prophet's Mosque. Her bier was borne into **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]**, and she was lowered into her grave by her nephews Usāmah b. Zayd, Muḥammad b. ʿAbdillāh b. Jaḥsh, and ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Aḥmad b. Jaḥsh (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:154; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:204).
