# Fatḥ Bayt al-Maqdis (فتح بيت المقدس)
*The Peaceful Opening of Jerusalem — The Journey of Caliph ʿUmar, The Sovereign Covenant (*al-ʿUhdah al-ʿUmariyyah*), and the Cleansing of the Holy Sanctuary*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Sources
> Anchored in *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād* by Imām al-Ṣāliḥī (Volume 11, p. 275) and *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 4, pp. 484–486).

---

## 1. Strategic Context & The Prolonged Siege

Following the decisive annihilation of the Byzantine field forces at **[[Ghazwat al-Yarmūk]]** in fifteen AH, the Muslim armies under **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁ advanced into Palestine and laid siege to the fortified holy city of **Bayt al-Maqdis** (Aelia / Jerusalem) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275; *al-Iṣābah*, 4:484).

After months of steadfast encirclement, the inhabitants realized that no Byzantine relief army would arrive. 

The Christian Patriarch of Jerusalem, **Sophronius**, approached the walls and declared to Abū ʿUbaydah:
> *"We agree to surrender the city, but under one condition: we will hand over the keys only to your supreme ruler in person, the one whose physical descriptions are inscribed within our sacred prophetic scriptures!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

Abū ʿUbaydah wrote to Caliph **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ in Medina, reporting the Patriarch's request.

---

## 2. The Historic Journey from Medina to Jerusalem

Caliph ʿUmar assembled the senior consultative council in Medina. **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ advised: *"O Amīr al-Muʾminīn, march forth in person! In your arrival lies the victory of the believers and the peaceful opening of the Holy Land without bloodshed."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484).

ʿUmar departed Medina mounted upon a single reddish camel, accompanied by a single servant and a leather water-skin (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275):
- Demonstrating sublime humility, ʿUmar agreed with his servant to alternate riding: one hour ʿUmar would ride while the servant walked holding the reins, and the next hour the servant would ride while the Caliph walked.
- When they approached the gates of Jerusalem, it was the servant's turn to ride. The servant pleaded: *"O Amīr al-Muʾminīn! The people, commanders, and patriarchs are waiting to look upon you; ride your mount!"* ʿUmar refused, stating: *"No! It is your turn to ride."* 
- ʿUmar entered Jerusalem leading the camel by its halter on foot, his plain woolen garment carrying seventeen patches (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

When **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** and other generals met him dressed in Syrian silk brocade to honor diplomatic customs, ʿUmar threw gravel at them in outrage and uttered his immortal declaration:
> «إِنَّا كُنَّا أَذَلَّ قَوْمٍ، فَأَعَزَّنَا اللَّهُ بِالْإِسْلَامِ، فَمَهْمَا ابْتَغَيْنَا الْعِزَّةَ بِغَيْرِ مَا أَعَزَّنَا اللَّهُ بِهِ أَذَلَّنَا اللَّهُ!»
> *"Indeed, we were the most humiliated and degraded of peoples, then Allāh elevated and honored us through Islam! So whenever we seek honor through anything other than that wherewith Allāh honored us, Allāh shall surely humiliate us!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484).

---

## 3. The Covenant of ʿUmar (*al-ʿUhdah al-ʿUmariyyah*)

Patriarch Sophronius emerged through the city gates; looking upon ʿUmar's patched garments, humility, and commanding dignity, he wept and acknowledged: *"Truly, this is the sovereign described in our ancient books, under whose hand our city was destined to pass!"*

Caliph ʿUmar granted the citizens of Jerusalem the celebrated **Covenant of Protection** (*al-ʿUhdah al-ʿUmariyyah*), an epochal legal charter ensuring:
1. **Sanctity of Life & Property**: Absolute safety for their lives, properties, and ancient churches.
2. **Freedom of Religion**: No church would be demolished, occupied, or diminished, and no cross would be insulted; no citizen would be coerced in matters of religion.
3. **Witnesses**: The charter was signed by ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb and witnessed by **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]**, **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]**, **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]**, and **[[Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

---

## 4. The Church of the Holy Sepulchre & Cleansing the Temple Mount

Sophronius escorted Caliph ʿUmar through the city. While they stood inside the **Church of the Holy Sepulchre** (*Kanīsat al-Qiyāmah*), the time for prayer arrived (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275). 

The Patriarch offered: *"Pray right here inside the church."* ʿUmar courteously declined, saying:
> *"If I pray inside this church, the Muslims who come after me might claim it, saying: 'Here ʿUmar prayed,' and convert it into a mosque."*

ʿUmar stepped outside and performed his prayer on the steps opposite the church, where the Mosque of ʿUmar was subsequently erected (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

### Cleansing the Sacred Rock (*al-Ṣakhrah*)
ʿUmar then asked Sophronius to lead him to the Sanctuary of David (*Miḥrāb Dāwūd* / the site of the Prophet's Night Journey, **[[al-Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj]]**). The Romans had desecrated the Temple Mount, turning the site into a city refuse dump out of spite toward the Jews (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

Looking upon the accumulated filth covering the sacred rock, Caliph ʿUmar tucked up his robe and began sweeping away the debris with his own hands, carrying the refuse in his cloak (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275). 

The Muslim commanders and soldiers immediately joined their Caliph, cleansing the entire plaza until the Foundation Rock was completely cleared (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275). ʿUmar then ordered the construction of a wooden congregational mosque on the southern portion of the enclosure (the original al-Aqsa Mosque).

---

## 5. Bilāl's Adhān in Jerusalem

During this blessed assembly in Jerusalem, the Companions pleaded with Caliph ʿUmar: *"O Amīr al-Muʾminīn, if only you could ask Bilāl to make the call to prayer for us just once!"*

Since the passing of the Prophet ﷺ in eleven AH, **[[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]]** ﵁ had refused to give the Adhān for anyone. But out of respect for ʿUmar and the liberation of the Holy City, Bilāl agreed to ascend and make the Adhān (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:326).

When Bilāl proclaimed: *«اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ! اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ!»* and reached *«أَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ»*, memories of the Prophet's era overwhelmed the assembly. Caliph ʿUmar wept so intensely that his beard was drenched in tears, and the entire gathering of Companions broke down in uncontrollable weeping (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:326).
