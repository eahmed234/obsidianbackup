# Kutub al-Nabī ﷺ ilā al-Mulūk (كتب النبي ﷺ إلى الملوك والأمراء)
*The Prophetic Embassies to World Monarchs — The Universal Call of Islam, The Imperial Audiences, and the Silver Signet Ring*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Sources
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād*, Volume 11, *Jimmāʿ Abwāb Dhikr Rusulihi ﷺ ilā al-Mulūk wa-Naḥwihim wa-Dhikr Baʿḍ Mukātabātihi wa-mā Waqaʿa fī Dhālika min al-Āyāt* (pp. 344–374), cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī. Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the historical records of *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[al-Wāqidī]], and [[Ibn Saʿd]].

---

## 1. The Global Horizon & The Silver Signet Ring

Following the conclusion of **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]** in Dhū al-Qaʿdah six AH, which neutralized hostilities with **[[Quraysh]]**, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ turned the diplomatic focus of the Islamic state toward the global theater (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344). 

At the opening of the year seven AH (or late six AH), the Prophet ﷺ gathered his Companions and announced his divine mandate to address all mankind (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344–346):

> «يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ، إِنَّ اللَّهَ بَعَثَنِي رَحْمَةً وَكَافَّةً، فَلَا تَخْتَلِفُوا عَلَيَّ كَمَا اخْتَلَفَ الْحَوَارِيُّونَ عَلَى عِيسَى ابْنِ مَرْيَمَ!»
> *"O people! Indeed Allāh has sent me as a universal mercy to all mankind; so do not differ concerning me as the disciples differed concerning Jesus son of Mary!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344).

The Muhājirūn and Anṣār rose in absolute devotion, proclaiming: *"O Messenger of Allāh, by Allāh, we will never differ concerning you in anything! Command us and dispatch us wherever you will!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:346).

### The Engraving of the Prophetic Signet (*al-Khātam*)
When the Prophet ﷺ resolved to dispatch formal letters to the monarchs of the non-Arab empires (*mulūk al-ʿAjam*), the Companions advised him: *"O Messenger of Allāh, indeed emperors do not read any letter unless it bears a seal (*makhtūm*)."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

Thereupon, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commissioned a signet ring of pure silver (*khātam min fiḍḍah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344). He had inscribed upon its bezel three lines, reading from bottom to top out of reverence:
- *Muḥammad* (مُحَمَّد) on the bottom line,
- *Rasūl* (رَسُول) on the middle line,
- *Allāh* (اللَّه) on the top line (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344).

The Prophet ﷺ forbade anyone else in the community from ever engraving their ring with that unique inscription (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344).

## 2. The Major Embassies & Their Destinies

The Prophet ﷺ selected elite Companions endowed with physical presence, foreign language fluency, eloquence, and unshakable faith to deliver his royal summons (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344–366):

---

### I. The Embassy to Heraclius (Qayṣar of the Byzantine Empire)
- **Ambassador**: **Diḥyah b. Khalīfah al-Kalbī** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:352–356).
- **Recipient**: Heraclius (*Hiraql*), Emperor of the Eastern Roman Empire, who was then in Jerusalem / Homs walking on foot in thanksgiving for his victory over the Persians.
- **The Letter**:
  > «بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيمِ. مِنْ مُحَمَّدٍ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ إِلَى هِرَقْلَ عَظِيمِ الرُّومِ: سَلَامٌ عَلَى مَنِ اتَّبَعَ الْهُدَى. أَمَّا بَعْدُ: فَإِنِّي أَدْعُوكَ بِدِعَايَةِ الْإِسْلَامِ، أَسْلِمْ تَسْلَمْ، يُؤْتِكَ اللَّهُ أَجْرَكَ مَرَّتَيْنِ، فَإِنْ تَوَلَّيْتَ فَإِنَّ عَلَيْكَ إِثْمَ الْأَرِيسِيِّينَ. وَ﴿يَا أَهْلَ الْكِتَابِ تَعَالَوْا إِلَىٰ كَلِمَةٍ سَوَاءٍ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَكُمْ أَلَّا نَعْبُدَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ وَلَا نُشْرِكَ بِهِ شَيْئًا وَلَا يَتَّخِذَ بَعْضُنَا بَعْضًا أَرْبَابًا مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ فَإِنْ تَوَلَّوْا فَقُولُوا اشْهَدُوا بِأَنَّا مُسْلِمُونَ﴾»
  > *"In the Name of Allāh, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. From Muḥammad, the servant of Allāh and His Messenger, to Heraclius, the sovereign of Rome: Peace be upon him who follows the guidance. To proceed: I invite you with the call of Islam. Embrace Islam and you will be safe; Allāh will bestow upon you your reward twice over. But if you turn away, upon you will rest the sin of the peasants (the common subjects). And: 'O People of the Scripture, come to a word that is equitable between us and you: that we will not worship except Allāh and not associate anything with Him and not take one another as lords instead of Allāh.' But if they turn away, say: 'Bear witness that we are Muslims' [Āl ʿImrān 3:64]."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:353; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).
- **Imperial Reception**: Heraclius summoned Arab merchants then in Gaza, interrogating **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** (then a polytheist) with eleven penetrating questions regarding the Prophet's lineage, followers, truthfulness, and character (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:353–355). Following Abū Sufyān's admissions, Heraclius remarked: *"If what you say is true, he will soon possess the ground beneath these two feet of mine!"* Though convinced of his prophethood, Heraclius concealed his faith when his Roman patriarchs and courtiers violently rebelled against him, choosing his temporal throne over eternal salvation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:356).

---

### II. The Embassy to Chosroes II Parvēz (Kisrā of Sasanian Persia)
- **Ambassador**: **ʿAbdullāh b. Ḥudhāfah al-Sahmī** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:361–362).
- **Recipient**: Chosroes II (*Kisrā Abarwīz*), King of Kings of the Persian Empire at Ctesiphon (*al-Madāʾin*).
- **The Letter**: Summons to Islam bearing the opening: *«مِنْ مُحَمَّدٍ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ إِلَى كِسْرَى عَظِيمِ فَارِسَ...»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:361).
- **Imperial Reception & Prophetic Invocations**: 
  When the letter was translated, Chosroes flew into a rage because the Prophet ﷺ had placed his own name before that of the Emperor. He snatched the letter and tore it to shreds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:361). He then wrote to his vassal governor in Yemen, Bādhān, commanding: *"Send two strong men to bring me this slave from the Ḥijāz!"*
  When news reached Medina that Chosroes had torn his letter, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ supplicated (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:362; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*):
  > «مَزَّقَ اللَّهُ مُلْكَهُ كُلَّ مُمَزَّقٍ!»
  > *"May Allāh tear his empire completely to shreds!"*
  Within months, Chosroes was overthrown and murdered by his own son Shīrūyah (Kavadh II), and the Persian governor of Yemen, Bādhān, embraced Islam, signaling the collapse of Sasanian dominion over southern Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:362).

---

### III. The Embassy to al-Muqawqis (Ruler of Alexandria and Coptic Egypt)
- **Ambassador**: **[[Ḥāṭib b. Abī Baltaʿah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:348–349).
- **Recipient**: Jurayj b. Mīnā (*al-Muqawqis*), ruler of Egypt and patriarch of Alexandria.
- **The Dialogue**: Ḥāṭib delivered a masterclass of diplomatic eloquence, reminding al-Muqawqis of Pharaoh's doom and demonstrating that Islam fulfills the prophetic mission of Christ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:348). Al-Muqawqis acknowledged: *"I knew that a Prophet was yet to arise, but I anticipated he would emerge in the Levant"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:348).
- **The Diplomatic Gifts**: Although he declined to embrace Islam fearing his Greek masters, al-Muqawqis treated Ḥāṭib with royal honor and dispatched magnificent gifts to the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:349):
  1. Two noble Coptic maidens: **Māriyah al-Qibṭiyyah** (who bore the Prophet ﷺ his son **Ibrāhīm**) and her sister Sīrīn (whom the Prophet ﷺ gifted to the poet Ḥassān b. Thābit).
  2. A prized white mule named **Duldul**.
  3. A thousand measures of Egyptian gold, fine linen robes of Alexandria, and precious perfumes (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:349).

---

### IV. The Embassy to al-Najāshī (The Negus of Abyssinia)
- **Ambassador**: **ʿAmr b. Umayyah al-Ḍamrī** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:365–366).
- **Recipient**: The Negus (*Aṣḥamah* or his successor).
- **The Letter & Response**: The Prophet ﷺ invited him to Islam and requested the repatriation of the remaining emigrants of Abyssinia led by **[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:365). 
  The Negus received the letter with supreme veneration, descended from his throne onto the dust, pressed the parchment to his eyes, and affirmed his faith in Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:365). He chartered two ships to carry Jaʿfar and thirty-two surviving Muhājirūn back to Medina, where they arrived during the conquest of Khaybar (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:365).

---

### V. The Embassies to the Arabian Kings (Bahrain & Oman)
- **To Bahrain**: Dispatched **al-ʿAlāʾ b. al-Ḥaḍramī** ﵁ to **al-Mundhir b. Sāwā al-ʿAbdī** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:364). Al-Mundhir embraced Islam immediately alongside the Arab and Persian population of eastern Arabia, while jizyah was instituted for Zoroastrians and Jews (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:364).
- **To Oman**: Dispatched **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁ to the co-kings of Oman, **Jayfar** and **ʿAbd**, sons of al-Julandā of the Azd (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:365; 6:200). Both embraced Islam, granting ʿAmr authority over their charity and legal affairs until the Prophet's passing (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:365).
- **To al-Yamāmah**: Dispatched **Salīṭ b. ʿAmr** ﵁ to **Hawdhah b. ʿAlī al-Ḥanafī**, who demanded a share in prophetic governance; the Prophet ﷺ rejected his condition, and Hawdhah died shortly thereafter (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:357).
- **To the Ghassanid Border King**: Dispatched **Shujāʿ b. Wahb al-Asadī** ﵁ to **al-Ḥārith b. Abī Shamir al-Ghassānī** in the Balqāʾ; al-Ḥārith threatened to invade Medina, but died the year of Fatḥ Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:358).

## 3. Theological & Historical Significance

1. **Universal Scope of the Mission (*Kāffatan lil-Nās*)**: The diplomatic letters permanently transformed Islam from an Arabian movement into a global world religion, fulfilling the Quranic mandate: *«وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ إِلَّا كَافَّةً لِلنَّاسِ بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا»* (Sūrat Sabaʾ 34:28) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:344).
2. **Prophetic Foresight**: Every prophecy uttered regarding these rulers was fulfilled with mathematical precision: Kisrā's empire was shattered to fragments; Caesar's borders were rolled back from the Levant and Egypt; while the realms of Bahrain and Oman became bastions of faith (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:356, 362).
3. **Precursor to the Liberation Campaigns**: These letters established the initial diplomatic contact between Medina and the superpowers of late antiquity, laying the direct legal and diplomatic groundwork for the subsequent campaigns of **[[Ghazwat Muʾtah]]**, **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]**, and the early Caliphal conquests (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:346).
