# Ghazwat Khaybar (غزوة خيبر)
*The Decisive Conquest of the Fortified Oasis of Northern Arabia*

## 1. Strategic Context & Divine Promise
Following the conclusion of [[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]] in Dhū al-Qaʿdah 6 AH, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ returned to Medina and remained there for approximately twenty nights (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:115). Allāh had promised the conquest and spoils of Khaybar to the believers while at Ḥudaybiyah, revealing between Mecca and Medina in **[[Sūrat al-Fatḥ]]**:
> «وَعَدَكُمُ اللَّهُ مَغَانِمَ كَثِيرَةً تَأْخُذُونَهَا فَعَجَّلَ لَكُمْ هَٰذِهِ»
> *"Allāh has promised you abundant spoils that you will capture, and has expedited this one for you..."* (Qurʾān 48:20; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:115).

In Muḥarram 7 AH, the Prophet ﷺ ordered his companions to mobilize for **[[Khaybar]]**, the formidable northern citadel oasis and political sanctuary of Jewish opposition (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:115). To maintain complete spiritual purity of purpose, the Prophet ﷺ explicitly barred those who had lagged behind at Ḥudaybiyah and now wished to join merely for plunder:
> «لَا تَخْرُجُوا مَعِي إِلَّا رَاغِبِينَ فِي الْجِهَادِ، فَأَمَّا الْغَنِيمَةُ فَلَا»
> *"None shall march out with me except those desiring Jihād; as for worldly booty, do not come!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:115).

The Prophet ﷺ appointed **Sibāʿ b. ʿUrfuṭah al-Ghifārī** ﵁ as his deputy over Medina, and was accompanied on the campaign by Umm al-Muʾminīn **[[Umm Salamah]]** ﵂ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:115).

## 2. The March & Vanguard Incidents
The Muslim force consisted strictly of the 1,400 companions who had taken part in [[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]], accompanied by 200 horses (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:142–143). During the march, the Prophet ﷺ sifted the ranks, having **[[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]]** ﵁ announce that whoever rode a weak or recalcitrant mount should return (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:117).

As they advanced by night, the poet **ʿĀmir b. al-Akwaʿ** ﵁ chanted camel-driving verses (*ḥudāʾ*):
> «اللَّهُمَّ لَوْلَا أَنْتَ مَا اهْتَدَيْنَا ... وَلَا تَصَدَّقْنَا وَلَا صَلَّيْنَا»
> «فَاغْفِرْ فِدَاءً لَكَ مَا اتَّقَيْنَا ... وَأَلْقِيَنْ سَكِينَةً عَلَيْنَا»
> «وَثَبِّتِ الْأَقْدَامَ إِنْ لَاقَيْنَا ... إِنَّا إِذَا صِيحَ بِنَا أَتَيْنَا»
> *"O Allāh, were it not for You, we would not have been guided, nor given in charity, nor prayed! Forgive us, and cast tranquility upon us, and make our feet firm when we meet the enemy!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:116).

Hearing him, the Prophet ﷺ inquired: *“Who is this driver?”* Upon being informed, he supplicated: *“May Allāh have mercy on him!”* The companions recognized this invocation as an indication of imminent martyrdom (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:116).

Halting at **al-Ṣahbāʾ** on the outskirts of Khaybar, the Prophet ﷺ prayed ʿAṣr and called for provisions; only simple barley-meal (*sawīq*) was brought, which was moistened and eaten collectively. He then led the Maghrib prayer after simply rinsing his mouth, without repeating ablution (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:117). Guided by Ḥusayl b. Khārijah and ʿAbdullāh b. Nuʿaym al-Ashjaʿī, the Prophet ﷺ navigated the valleys to approach Khaybar from the north, cutting off the oasis from Syria and severing their military alliance with Banū Ghaṭafān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:117).

## 3. Topography & Citadel Clusters
Khaybar consisted of heavily fortified volcanic plateaus divided into three primary sectors containing eight major citadels:
1. **Al-Naṭāh (النطاة)**: The foremost operational sector, holding Ḥiṣn Nāʿim, Ḥiṣn al-Ṣaʿb b. Muʿādh, and Qalʿat al-Zubayr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:119, 121–122).
2. **Al-Shiqq (الشق)**: The central fortified sector, containing Ḥiṣn Ubayy and Ḥiṣn al-Barīʾ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:123).
3. **Al-Katībah (الكتيبة)**: The deepest, most formidable redoubt, encompassing Ḥiṣn al-Qamūṣ (citadel of the house of Abū al-Ḥuqayq), Ḥiṣn al-Waṭīḥ, and Ḥiṣn al-Salālim (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:124, 131).

The siege commenced against al-Naṭāh. At Ḥiṣn al-Ṣaʿb b. Muʿādh—the most provisioned citadel in all Khaybar—the Muslims suffered acute hunger until the fort fell, yielding vast stores of grain, dates, livestock, and fat (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:121).

## 4. The Investiture of ʿAlī & Slaying of the Champions
As the siege advanced to the citadels of al-Katībah and its pinnacle, Ḥiṣn al-Qamūṣ, early assaults led by **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ met fierce resistance without achieving a breakthrough (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:124). That evening, the Prophet ﷺ made his momentous announcement:
> «لَأُعْطِيَنَّ الرَّايَةَ غَدًا رَجُلًا يُحِبُّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ، وَيُحِبُّهُ اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ، يَفْتَحُ اللَّهُ عَلَى يَدَيْهِ!»
> *"Tomorrow I shall surely give the banner to a man who loves Allāh and His Messenger, and whom Allāh and His Messenger love; Allāh will grant conquest at his hands!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:124, 128; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 3701; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, no. 2405).

The companions spent the night in anticipation, each hoping to receive the standard. The following morning, the Prophet ﷺ called for **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁, who was suffering from acute ophthalmia (*ramad*) and had to be led by hand. The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ applied his blessed saliva to ʿAlī’s inflamed eyes and supplicated for him; ʿAlī was healed instantly, as if he had suffered no affliction (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:125). Handing him the white banner, the Prophet ﷺ instructed him:
> «انْفُذْ عَلَى رِسْلِكَ حَتَّى تَنْزِلَ بِسَاحَتِهِمْ، ثُمَّ ادْعُهُمْ إِلَى الْإِسْلَامِ ... فَوَاللَّهِ لَأَنْ يَهْدِيَ اللَّهُ بِكَ رَجُلًا وَاحِدًا، خَيْرٌ لَكَ مِنْ أَنْ يَكُونَ لَكَ حُمْرُ النَّعَمِ»
> *"Advance at your ease until you enter their courtyard, then summon them to Islam... For by Allāh, that Allāh guides through you a single man is better for you than possessing the finest red camels!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:125).

### Duels and the Fall of Marḥab
The Jewish garrison emerged led by their champion Marḥab, clad in heavy double-mail and a helmet beneath a stone shield, reciting:
> «قَدْ عَلِمَتْ خَيْبَرُ أَنِّي مَرْحَبُ ... شَاكِي السِّلَاحِ بَطَلٌ مُجَرَّبُ»
> *"Khaybar knows well that I am Marḥab, fully armed, a battle-tested warrior!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:125).

ʿAlī stepped forth to meet him, answering:
> «أَنَا الَّذِي سَمَّتْنِي أُمِّي حَيْدَرَهْ ... كَلَيْثِ غَابَاتٍ كَرِيهِ الْمَنْظَرَهْ ... أُوفِيهِمُ بِالصَّاعِ كَيْلَ السَّنْدَرَهْ»
> *"I am he whom my mother named Ḥaydarah (the lion), like a fierce lion of the forest, paying back in full measure with swift slaughter!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:125).

ʿAlī struck Marḥab on the head, cleaving his helmet and skull down to his molars, slaying him instantly (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:125, 128). In the ensuing combat, other Jewish champions were vanquished: Marḥab's brother al-Ḥārith was slain, **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ slew Yāsir in single combat after his mother **Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib** feared for him, and ʿĀmir was killed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:125–127).

### The Gate of Khaybar
During the storming of the citadel, a combatant struck ʿAlī's hand, knocking his shield away. ʿAlī reached for a heavy door at the entrance of the fortress, ripped it from its hinges, and held it as a shield while fighting fiercely until Allāh granted victory. **Abū Rāfiʿ** ﵁ attested:
> «فَلَقَدْ رَأَيْتُنِي فِي نَفَرٍ سَبْعَةٍ أَنَا ثَامِنُهُمْ، نَجْهَدُ عَلَى أَنْ نَقْلِبَ ذَلِكَ الْبَابَ، فَمَا نَقْلِبُهُ»
> *"I saw myself with a group of seven men—I being the eighth—straining with all our might to turn that door over, and we could not turn it over!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:128).

## 5. Capitulation & The Sharecropping Agreement
With the fall of al-Qamūṣ, the defenders retreated into their final strongholds of **al-Waṭīḥ** and **al-Salālim** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:131). Realizing destruction was certain, they sent emissaries suing for peace.

Under the terms of capitulation, their lives were spared, and they were permitted to leave with their families, surrendering their land, fortresses, gold, silver, horses, and armor (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:131). When Kinānah b. al-Rabīʿ b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq and his brother denied possessing the hidden wealth of Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab brought from Banū al-Naḍīr, the hidden treasure was uncovered, proving their breach of terms (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:131–132).

Lacking agricultural laborers among the companions to cultivate the expansive date orchards, the Prophet ﷺ consented to their plea to remain on the land as tenants, establishing the legal precedent of sharecropping (*musāqāh/muzāraʿah*):
> «نُقِرُّكُمْ فِيهَا عَلَى ذَلِكَ مَا شِئْنَا»
> *"We allow you to remain upon this land for half the fruit and harvest, for as long as we wish [or as Allāh wills]."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:133).

Each season, the Prophet ﷺ dispatched **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]** ﵁ to equitably estimate the date harvest (*kharṣ*), offering the Jewish farmers the choice to take whichever half they preferred, a display of justice that left them marveling (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:133).

## 6. Matrimony with Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyayy
Following the capitulation of al-Qamūṣ, **[[Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyayy]]** ﵂—daughter of the chieftain Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab and widow of Kinānah b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq—was among the captives. Recognizing her status as the chief woman of her people, the Prophet ﷺ granted her freedom and married her, establishing her emancipation as her dowry (*ṣadāquhā ʿitquhā*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:132; 11:212).

Her wedding feast (*walīmah*) was celebrated during the return journey at Sadd al-Ṣahbāʾ, where dates, clarified butter, and dried curd (*ḥays*) were served to the army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:132).

## 7. The Poisoned Sheep & Martyrdom of Bishr
Following the pacification of Khaybar, Zaynab bint al-Ḥārith (wife of Sallām b. Mishkam and niece of Marḥab) gifted the Prophet ﷺ a roasted sheep. Having inquired what cut the Prophet ﷺ preferred and being told the shoulder (*al-dhirāʿ*), she saturated it with lethal poison (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:134).

The Prophet ﷺ took a bite from the shoulder alongside **[[Bishr b. al-Barāʾ]]** ﵁. The Prophet ﷺ immediately spat out his bite, exclaiming that the bone itself had spoken to inform him it was poisoned (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:134). Bishr, having already swallowed his mouthful (*uklah*), succumbed to the toxin and died a martyr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:134–135). When questioned, the woman confessed: *“I reasoned: if you are a mere king, we would be rid of you; but if you are a true Prophet, you would surely be informed”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:135).

## 8. Joyous Arrivals & Legal Injunctions
### Return of Jaʿfar and the Arrival of Abū Hurayrah
Khaybar was crowned with the return of **[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ and the Abyssinian emigrants, alongside the Ashʿarites led by **Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:135–136). Embracing Jaʿfar and kissing him between the eyes, the Prophet ﷺ declared:
> «مَا أَدْرِي بِأَيِّهِمَا أَنَا أَفْرَحُ: بِفَتْحِ خَيْبَرَ، أَمْ بِقُدُومِ جَعْفَرٍ؟»
> *"I know not with which I am more overjoyed: the conquest of Khaybar, or the arrival of Jaʿfar?!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:135).

Simultaneously, **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁ arrived with eighty households of the Daws tribe, joining the Prophet ﷺ at Khaybar and receiving a share of the expedition's proceeds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:136–137, 143).

### Crucial Legal Injunctions
At Khaybar, the Prophet ﷺ instituted several permanent legal prohibitions:
1. **Domestic Donkeys**: When famine drove some soldiers to slaughter domestic donkeys (*al-ḥumur al-ahliyyah*), the Prophet's ﷺ herald ordered the cooking pots overturned and forbade eating donkey flesh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:130).
2. **Mutʿah (Temporary Marriage)**: Temporary marriage (*mutʿat al-nisāʾ*) was permanently prohibited on the day of Khaybar (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:130).
3. **Beasts of Prey**: The consumption of any wild predatory animal possessing canine fangs (*kull dhī nāb min al-sibāʿ*) was outlawed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:130).

## 9. Distribution of Spoils & Casualties
The spoils of Khaybar were divided into 1,800 shares: 1,400 for the foot soldiers and 400 for the cavalry (allotting two shares per horse and one for its rider) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:142–143). **[[Zayd b. Thābit]]** ﵁ and **Jabbār b. Ṣakhr al-Anṣārī** ﵁ were appointed as official assessors and distributors (*ḥāsibayn qāsimayn*), while **Farwah b. ʿAmr al-Bayāḍī** ﵁ had custody of the war booty (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:142–143). **[[Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh]]** ﵁, the sole veteran of Ḥudaybiyah unable to attend Khaybar, was granted an equal share by the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:142).

Between 16 and 18 Muslims were martyred throughout the campaign, including ʿĀmir b. al-Akwaʿ, Maḥmūd b. Maslamah, and Bishr b. al-Barāʾ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:144). Enemy leadership suffered total defeat, shattering northern tribal opposition and paving the way for the peaceful Pilgrimage of Fulfillment (*ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ*).
