# Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr (مصعب بن عمير العبدري) ﵁
*Abū ʿAbdillāh al-ʿAbdarī al-Qurashī — Muṣʿab al-Khayr, The First Ambassador of Islam, Standard-Bearer of Uḥud*

## 1. Lineage & Early Life in Mecca
He is Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr b. Hāshim b. ʿAbd Manāf b. ʿAbd al-Dār al-Qurashī al-ʿAbdarī (kunya: Abū ʿAbdillāh; titled by the Prophet ﷺ: *Muṣʿab al-Khayr*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185; 4:92). In pre-Islamic Mecca, he was the most handsome, pampered, and elegantly dressed youth in the city, adorned in exquisite Yemeni silken garments and perfumed with the finest Arabian musk (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185).

Upon hearing of the Daʿwah, he entered **[[Dār al-Arqam]]** in secret and embraced Islam. When his mother, Khunās bint Mālik, discovered his conversion, she imprisoned him in their home until he managed to escape, joining the early believers in **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185). He returned to Mecca altered in outward appearance, wearing coarse, patched skins, completely detached from worldly luxury.

## 2. The First Ambassador of Islam (12th Year of Nubūwah)
Following **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā]]**, the twelve delegates from Yathrib requested a teacher to instruct them in the Qurʾān. The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commissioned Muṣʿab as the first official ambassador of Islam (*Safīr al-Islām*) to Yathrib, residing as a guest in the home of **[[Asʿad b. Zurārah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185).

Known as *al-Muqriʾ* ("The Reciter"), Muṣʿab preached with immense gentleness, eloquence, and wisdom. When the armed chieftains **[[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]]** ﵁ and **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁ confronted him with drawn weapons to halt his preaching, Muṣʿab replied with calm serenity:
> *"Will you not sit and listen? If you are pleased with our message, accept it; and if you dislike it, we shall remove from you that which you despise."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:186).
Both noble chieftains sat, heard his recitation of the Qurʾān, embraced Islam, and brought the entire tribe of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal into the faith, laying the ground for **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:186–188).

## 3. Standard-Bearer & Martyrdom at Uḥud
- **Standard-Bearer at Badr (2 AH)**: Awarded the supreme white banner of the Muhājirūn at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, fighting in the front rank of the victors (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:40, 92).
- **The Ultimate Stand at Uḥud (3 AH)**: Entrusted again with the supreme standard of the Muslim army at **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:183, 206). When the polytheist counterattack encircled the army and panic spread, Muṣʿab stood firm as a lone beacon, planting his feet and raising the banner high to draw enemy fire away from the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206).
- The polytheist horseman Ibn Qamīʾah charged him, severing Muṣʿab's right hand; Muṣʿab grasped the standard with his left hand, reciting:
  > «وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِنْ قَبْلِهِ الرُّسُلُ»
  > *"Muḥammad is not but a messenger; other messengers have passed on before him!"* (Qurʾān 3:144; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206).
  Ibn Qamīʾah then severed his left hand; Muṣʿab threw his chest against the standard, clamping it with his severed arms until Ibn Qamīʾah pierced his torso with a lance, felling him as a martyr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206–207).

## 4. The Shroud of Grass & Legacy
Following the battle, the Prophet ﷺ walked the field and stood weeping over Muṣʿab’s body (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:230). They possessed only a single short striped cloak (*burdah*) to shroud him: if they covered his head, his feet were exposed, and if they covered his feet, his head remained bare. The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ wept and commanded:
> «غَطُّوا بِهَا رَأْسَهُ، وَاجْعَلُوا عَلَى رِجْلَيْهِ الْإِذْخِرَ!»
> *"Cover his head with it, and place sweet-scented desert grass (idhkhir) over his feet!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:230; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 1276).

Looking upon his humble shroud, the Prophet ﷺ recited:
> «مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ رِجَالٌ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوا اللَّهَ عَلَيْهِ ۖ فَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ قَضَىٰ نَحْبَهُ وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَنْتَظِرُ ۖ وَمَا بَدَّلُوا تَبْدِيلًا»
> *"Among the believers are men true to what they promised Allāh: among them is he who has fulfilled his vow, and among them is he who awaits, and they have not altered in the least!"* (Qurʾān 33:23; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:230).
