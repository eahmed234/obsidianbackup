# Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr (مصعب بن عمير العبدري) ﵁
*Abū ʿAbdillāh al-ʿAbdarī al-Qurashī — Muṣʿab al-Khayr, Safīr al-Islām (The First Ambassador of Islam), Standard-Bearer of Badr and Uḥud*

> [!NOTE] Classical Scholarly Provenance
> This biography is anchored in ***[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād]]*** by **[[al-Ṣāliḥī]]** (Vol. 3, pp. 185–188; Vol. 4, pp. 40, 92, 183, 206–207, 230) and ***[[al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah]]*** by **[[Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī]]** (Vol. 6, p. 98, entry no. 8020; Vol. 8, p. 163).

---

## 1. Lineage, Youth, and Dramatic Conversion

### Patrilineal and Maternal Descent
- **Full Name**: Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr b. Hāshim b. ʿAbd Manāf b. ʿAbd al-Dār b. Quṣayy b. Kilāb al-Qurashī al-ʿAbdarī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185; 4:92; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98).
- **Kunya**: Abū ʿAbdillāh (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:98).
- **Clan**: [[Banū ʿAbd al-Dār]], the noble Quraysh clan traditionally charged with the guardianship of the Kaʿbah keys (*sidānah*) and custody of the battle standard (*liwāʾ*).
- **Mother**: Khunās bint Mālik b. al-Maḍrab al-ʿAmiriyyah, a woman of extraordinary affluence and forceful authority (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185).

### The Pampered Youth of Mecca
In pre-Islamic Makkah, Muṣʿab was universally celebrated as the most handsome, pampered, and elegantly attired youth in the city (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:98). Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ ﵁ recounted:
> *"Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr was the most indulged youth in Makkah, and the most generously provisioned by his parents."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:98).

His mother adorned him in luxurious Yemeni garments, scented him with the most exquisite Arabian fragrances, and gave him footwear of the finest leather (*Haḍramī* sandals). His scent lingered in the streets of Makkah long after he had passed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185).

### Secret Islam and Imprisonment
Upon learning of the Prophetic message, Muṣʿab slipped secretly into **[[Dār al-Arqam]]**, listened to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and embraced Islam (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:98). He concealed his conversion fearing the wrath of his mother and clan, praying in private. 

However, ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah al-ʿAbdarī caught sight of him praying and informed his mother and kin. They seized him and locked him in shackles inside their home (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:98). He remained imprisoned until the first migration to Abyssinia, when he managed to slip past his guards and joined the believers across the Red Sea in **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98). 

When he eventually returned to Makkah, the opulent garments and perfumes were gone; he wore only coarse, patched sheepskins. Seeing his transformation from royal luxury to ascetic hardship for the sake of Allāh, the Prophet ﷺ wept, and conferred upon him the noble title **«مُصْعَبُ الْخَيْرِ»** (*Muṣʿab al-Khayr* — Muṣʿab the Good) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98).

## 2. Marital Network and Kinship

- **Marriage to Ḥamnah bint Jaḥsh**: Muṣʿab married **Ḥamnah bint Jaḥsh** al-Asadiyyah, daughter of Umaymah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib (paternal aunt of the Prophet ﷺ) and sister of the Mother of the Believers Zaynab bint Jaḥsh and ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh.
- **Daughter**: She bore him **Zaynab bint Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr** (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:163).
- After Muṣʿab’s martyrdom at Uḥud, Ḥamnah bint Jaḥsh was married to **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁, bearing him Muḥammad b. Ṭalḥah (al-Sajjād) and ʿImrān b. Ṭalḥah.

## 3. The First Ambassador of Islam to Yathrib

Following **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā]]** (the First Pledge of al-ʿAqabah, 12th year of Prophethood), the twelve representatives from Yathrib requested an instructor to teach them the recitation of the Qurʾān and the tenets of Islam. The Prophet ﷺ dispatched Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr as the **First Ambassador of Islam** (*Safīr al-Islām*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98). In Yathrib, he was revered by the early Muslims as **«الْمُقْرِئُ»** (*al-Muqriʾ* — The Quranic Reciter).

### Residence with Asʿad b. Zurārah and the First Jumuʿah
Muṣʿab took up residence in the home of **[[Asʿad b. Zurārah]]** ﵁, chieftain of Banū al-Najjār (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185). Under the guidance of the Prophet's instructions, Muṣʿab and Asʿad established the very first Friday communal prayer (**Jumuʿah**) in Islamic history at Naqīʿ al-Khaḍamāt in the territory of Banū Bayāḍah, gathering forty believers before the Prophet's migration (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:185).

### Conversion of Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr and Saʿd b. Muʿādh
As the message spread, the paramount chieftains of the Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal clan of the Aws, **[[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]]** ﵁ and **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁, became alarmed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:186–188). 

Usayd seized his spear, confronted Muṣʿab and Asʿad in an orchard, and threatened them fiercely: *"What brings you to our quarter to seduce our weak-minded folk? Leave us if you value your lives!"* 

Muṣʿab responded with legendary composure, gentleness, and wisdom:
> «أَوَ تَقْعُدُ فَتَسْمَعَ؟ فَإِنْ رَضِيتَ أَمْرًا قَبِلْتَهُ، وَإِنْ كَرِهْتَهُ كُفَّ عَنْكَ مَا تَكْرَهُ»
> *"Will you not sit and listen? If you are pleased with our matter, accept it; and if you dislike it, we shall withhold from you that which you despise!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:186).

Usayd planted his spear, sat, and listened as Muṣʿab recited the Qurʾān. His countenance illuminated with faith, and he embraced Islam immediately. Usayd then sent Saʿd b. Muʿādh under a pretext; Muṣʿab repeated the same gentle invitation, reciting the opening of Sūrat al-Zukhruf. Saʿd's heart opened to faith; he strode to his clan and proclaimed: *"O Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal! How do you know me among you?"* They replied: *"Our master and our superior in judgement!"* He declared: *"Then the speech of your men and women is forbidden to me until you believe in Allāh and His Messenger!"* Before nightfall, every household of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal embraced Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:187–188).

Through Muṣʿab's wisdom, Yathrib was completely opened without a single drop of blood, preparing the city for **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**.

## 4. Standard-Bearer of the Armies of Islam

As a scion of [[Banū ʿAbd al-Dār]]—the traditional custodians of the war standard in Quraysh—the Prophet ﷺ honored Muṣʿab by bestowing upon him the supreme military banner of the Muslims in the early engagements:
- **At Badr (2 AH)**: Entrusted with the grand white standard (*al-liwāʾ al-aʿẓam*) of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, leading the Muhājirūn into victory (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:40, 92; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98).
- **At Uḥud (3 AH)**: Entrusted again with the supreme standard of the Muslim army at **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:183, 206; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98).

---

## 5. Heroic Last Stand & Martyrdom at Uḥud (3 AH)

When the Muslim archers abandoned their posts and the Meccan cavalry under Khālid b. al-Walīd encircled the Muslim rear, chaos ensued, and the rumor spread that the Prophet ﷺ had been killed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206). 

Muṣʿab stood firm like an unyielding mountain, planting his feet and hoisting the banner high into the air to rally the believers and draw the entire fury of the enemy upon himself, thereby shielding the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206).

### The Severing of Hands and the Quranic Anthem
The polytheist knight **Ibn Qamīʾah** charged Muṣʿab on horseback, striking off his right hand. Muṣʿab immediately seized the standard with his left hand, defiantly proclaiming:
> «وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِنْ قَبْلِهِ الرُّسُلُ»
> *"Muḥammad is not but a messenger; other messengers have passed away before him!"* (Qurʾān 3:144; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206).

Ibn Qamīʾah struck again, severing his left hand. Muṣʿab knelt and clamped the standard against his chest with his bleeding upper arms, continuing to recite the same divine verse, until Ibn Qamīʾah pierced his breast with a lance, martyring him upon the field (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206–207). 

When Muṣʿab fell, an angel descended in the likeness of Muṣʿab, took up the standard, and fought until the end of the day (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:207).

## 6. The Shroud of Idhkhir & Prophetic Tears

Following the cessation of combat, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ searched the field and found Muṣʿab lying face down in the blood and dust (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:230; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98). 

The Muslims found nothing to shroud him in except a single short, coarse mantle (*namirah* or *burdah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:230; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98). When they pulled the cloth over his head, his feet remained exposed; and when they pulled it over his feet, his head remained bare. 

The Prophet ﷺ wept until his tears flowed upon the dust, recalling Muṣʿab’s days of incomparable elegance in Makkah, and instructed:
> «غَطُّوا بِهَا رَأْسَهُ، وَاجْعَلُوا عَلَى رِجْلَيْهِ شَيْئًا مِنَ الْإِذْخِرِ»
> *"Cover his head with the cloth, and place over his feet some sweet-rush grass (idhkhir)!"* (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 1276; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:230; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:98).

Standing over the body of his beloved ambassador, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ recited the divine tribute:
> «مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ رِجَالٌ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوا اللَّهَ عَلَيْهِ ۖ فَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ قَضَىٰ نَحْبَهُ وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَنْتَظِرُ ۖ وَمَا بَدَّلُوا تَبْدِيلًا»
> *"Among the believers are men true to what they promised Allāh: among them is he who has fulfilled his vow, and among them is he who awaits, and they have not altered in the least!"* (Qurʾān 33:23; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:230).

The Prophet ﷺ then turned to the Companions and declared:
> *"The Messenger of Allāh bears witness that you are martyrs in the sight of Allāh on the Day of Resurrection! Visit them, greet them with peace, for by Him in Whose Hand is my soul, no one greets them with peace until the Day of Resurrection except that they return the greeting!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:230).
