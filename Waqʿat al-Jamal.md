# Waqʿat al-Jamal (وقعة الجمل)
*The Battle of the Camel: The Misunderstood Confrontation Outside Baṣrah, the Martyrdom of Ṭalḥah and al-Zubayr, and ʿAlī's Noble Reconciliation (Jumādā al-Ākhirah 36 AH)*

## 1. Strategic Context & Divergent Ijtihād
Following the assassination of Caliph **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ in Medina during the tragic siege (*Yawm al-Dār*), the Muslim world was shaken by severe internal discord (*al-fitnah*). When **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ assumed the Caliphate, he prioritized stabilizing the central administration, securing the provincial garrison cities, and establishing control over the state before identifying and prosecuting the individual killers of ʿUthmān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:148; *al-Iṣābah*, 4:468).

Conversely, a distinguished group of Companions including the Mother of the Believers **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂, **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁, and **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ held that immediate retributive justice (*qiṣāṣ*) against the murderers was an absolute legal obligation that could not be delayed. Departing for Baṣrah to rally righteous public support for communal reform (*iṣlāḥ*), their mission was explicitly peaceful, intended to unite the believers upon justice (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:149).

During the march toward Baṣrah, when passing the watering place of al-Ḥawʾab, desert dogs barked at ʿĀʾishah's carriage. Remembering the solemn prophecy of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ:
> «أَيَّتُكُنَّ تَنْبَحُ عَلَيْهَا كِلَابُ الْحَوْأَبِ؟»
> *"Which of you is it that the dogs of al-Ḥawʾab shall bark at?!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:149),
she was stricken with deep remorse and intended to turn back immediately, but was persuaded that her presence would bring reconciliation between Muslims (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:149).

---

## 2. Diplomatic Overtures & Sabotage by the Regicides
Arriving at Baṣrah, Amīr al-Muʾminīn ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib dispatched al-Qaʿqāʿ b. ʿAmr al-Tamīmī as his diplomatic emissary to meet with ʿĀʾishah, Ṭalḥah, and al-Zubayr. 

The negotiations were an overwhelming success: both parties reached full mutual consensus to avoid hostilities, conclude a binding peace pact, and unite the community under ʿAlī's authority to methodically apprehend the conspirators.

However, the extremist agitators and regicides who had taken part in the siege of ʿUthmān realized that a peaceful concord between ʿAlī and the Companions meant their certain execution under the law. Under the cover of night, rogue conspirators launched clandestine skirmishes against both camps simultaneously, shouting that the other party had betrayed the truce. Waking to clashes in the dark, both sides believed they had been treacherously attacked.

---

## 3. The Engagement Outside Baṣrah
On Thursday, in Jumādā al-Ākhirah 36 AH, combat erupted on the outskirts of Baṣrah:
- The conflict centered around the armored camel-litter (*al-Hawdaj*) bearing **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂, which gave the battle its historical name (*Waqʿat al-Jamal*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:149).
- Devoted warriors of the Banū Dabbah and Azd rallied around the camel, seizing its bridle in succession to protect the Mother of the Believers from stray missiles.
- The judge of Baṣrah, Kaʿb b. Sūr ﵁, raised a copy of the Holy Qurʾān between the ranks, crying: *“I adjure you by Allāh and Islam to cease bloodshed!”* before being struck down by a stray arrow (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:461).

### The Departure and Martyrdom of al-Zubayr
Before the clash escalated, ʿAlī called forth **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ between the lines, reminding him:
> *“Do you remember the day when the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ asked you: 'Do you love ʿAlī?' and you said: 'Why should I not love my maternal cousin?' whereupon he said: 'Indeed, you shall fight him while you are in the wrong!'”*
Remembering the Prophetic warning, al-Zubayr swore never to take up arms against ʿAlī, turned his horse away from the field, and withdrew toward Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:461). While praying at Wādī al-Sibāʿ, he was ambushed and assassinated from behind by ʿAmr b. Jurmūz (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:461). When Ibn Jurmūz brought al-Zubayr's sword to ʿAlī expecting a reward, ʿAlī wept, kissed the blade, and pronounced the Prophetic verdict:
> «بَشِّرْ قَاتِلَ ابْنِ صَفِيَّةَ بِالنَّارِ!»
> *"Give glad tidings of the Fire to the killer of the son of Ṣafiyyah!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:461).

### The Martyrdom of Ṭalḥah
**[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁ was struck in the knee by an arrow fired by Marwān b. al-Ḥakam during the confusion of the melee (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:432). The wound bled profusely (*fāḍa al-dam*); he was carried from the field into Baṣrah, where he succumbed to martyrdom at sixty-four years of age in Jumādā al-Ākhirah 36 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:432).

---

## 4. Climax & ʿAlī's Noble Reconciliation
Recognizing that the fighting would not cease so long as the camel stood as a rallying banner, ʿAlī ordered his men to hamstring the mount so it would kneel without injuring ʿĀʾishah. The litter was gently set upon the ground, and the fighting immediately collapsed.

ʿAlī dispatched ʿĀʾishah's brother, **Muḥammad b. Abī Bakr**, to verify her safety and tend to her. ʿAlī approached her carriage, inquiring with filial devotion: *“How are you, O mother?”* She replied: *“Well, may Allāh forgive you.”* ʿAlī responded: *“And may Allāh forgive you.”*

### Post-Battle Conduct & General Amnesty
ʿAlī strictly prohibited the pursuit of fleeing combatants, the execution of the wounded, or the seizure of any property. Inspecting the fallen, ʿAlī wept over the dead, personally leading the funeral prayers over the fallen on both sides and burying them with full Islamic honors. Standing over Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh, ʿAlī gently wiped the dust from his face, praying:
> *“Would that I had died twenty years before this day!”*

ʿAlī prepared a royal escort of forty noble women of Baṣrah, commanded by her brother Muḥammad b. Abī Bakr, to accompany ʿĀʾishah with consummate honor and dignity back to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:149).
