# Ghazwat Muʾtah (غزوة مؤتة)
*The Epic Clash of the Northern Marches — The Three Martyred Commanders and the Stratagem of Sayfullāh*

## 1. The Provocation & Casus Belli
In early 8 AH, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ sent his diplomatic ambassador **[[al-Ḥārith b. ʿUmayr al-Azdī]]** ﵁ carrying a royal epistle addressed to the Byzantine vassal ruler, the King of Buṣrā in southern Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:144).

Upon reaching the village of Muʾtah in the Balqāʾ region, al-Ḥārith was intercepted by **Shuraḥbīl b. ʿAmr al-Ghassānī**, the regional governor on behalf of the Byzantine Empire (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:682; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:144). Shuraḥbīl interrogated him, bound him in ropes, and brutally executed him (*ḍuribat ʿunuquhu ṣabran*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:682; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:144). 

Al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar affirms the historical singularity of this outrage: *«Never was any diplomatic emissary of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ slain other than him»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:682). Under ancient international statecraft, the assassination of an accredited herald was an explicit declaration of war. Deeply grieved, the Prophet ﷺ mobilized the largest expeditionary force ever dispatched north from Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:144).

## 2. Mobilization & The Three Appointed Commanders
In Jumādā al-Ūlā 8 AH, three thousand Muslim warriors gathered at the staging camp of **al-Jurf** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:145). 

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ established an unprecedented, pre-designated succession of supreme command:
> «أَمِيرُ النَّاسِ زَيْدُ بْنُ حَارِثَةَ، فَإِنْ قُتِلَ زَيْدٌ فَجَعْفَرُ بْنُ أَبِي طَالِبٍ، فَإِنْ قُتِلَ جَعْفَرٌ فَعَبْدُ اللهِ بْنُ رَوَاحَةَ، فَإِنْ قُتِلَ عَبْدُ اللهِ بْنُ رَوَاحَةَ فَلْيَرْتَضِ الْمُسْلِمُونَ بَيْنَهُمْ رَجُلًا فَلْيَجْعَلُوهُ عَلَيْهِمْ»
> *"The commander of the people is [[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]; if Zayd is slain, then [[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]; if Jaʿfar is slain, then [[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]; and if ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah is slain, let the Muslims agree among themselves upon a man and appoint him over themselves!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:144).

### Prophetic Farewell & Rules of Engagement
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and the Muslims marched alongside the army as far as **Thaniyyat al-Wadāʿ** to bid them farewell (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:145). The Prophet ﷺ gave the troops their strict ethical charter:
> «اغْزُوا بِاسْمِ اللهِ، فِي سَبِيلِ اللهِ، قَاتِلُوا مَنْ كَفَرَ بِاللهِ، اغْزُوا وَلَا تَغُلُّوا، وَلَا تَغْدِرُوا، وَلَا تَمْثُلُوا، وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا وَلِيدًا، وَلَا امْرَأَةً، وَلَا كَبِيرًا فَانِيًا، وَلَا مُنْعَزِلًا بِصَوْمَعَةٍ...»
> *"March in the Name of Allāh, in the way of Allāh! Fight those who disbelieve in Allāh. March forth, but do not embezzle booty, do not breach treaties, do not mutilate the dead, do not slay a child, nor a woman, nor a decrepit elder, nor a monk secluded in his hermitage!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:145).

## 3. The March to Maʿān & The Council of War
The army advanced into southern Jordan and encamped at **Maʿān** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:147). There, military intelligence delivered staggering news: Emperor Heraclius himself was encamped at Maʾāb in the Balqāʾ leading one hundred thousand Roman legionnaires, reinforced by an additional one hundred thousand Christian Arab auxiliary tribesmen from Lakhm, Judhām, Balī, Bahrāʾ, and Ghassān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:147).

Confronted by an enemy coalition numbering two hundred thousand troops against three thousand, the Muslims held an anxious council of war at Maʿān lasting two nights (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:147). Some proposed: *«Let us write to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ informing him of the enemy's numbers; he will either send reinforcements or dispatch his command.»*

At that decisive juncture, **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]** ﵁ rose and delivered an electric address:
> «يَا قَوْمِ! وَاللهِ إِنَّ الَّتِي تَكْرَهُونَ لَلَّتِي خَرَجْتُمْ تَطْلُبُونَ: الشَّهَادَةَ! وَمَا نُقَاتِلُ النَّاسَ بِعَدَدٍ وَلَا قُوَّةٍ وَلَا كَثْرَةٍ، مَا نُقَاتِلُهُمْ إِلَّا بِهَذَا الدِّينِ الَّذِي أَكْرَمَنَا اللهُ بِهِ! فَانْطَلِقُوا فَإِنَّمَا هِيَ إِحْدَى الْحُسْنَيَيْنِ: إِمَّا ظُهُورٌ وَإِمَّا شَهَادَةٌ!»
> *"O my people! By Allāh, that very thing which you dread is the very objective you marched out to seek: martyrdom! We do not fight mankind through numbers, nor strength, nor multitude; we fight them only with this religion wherewith Allāh has honored us! So advance forth: it is but one of the two glorious outcomes—either victory or martyrdom!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:147).

The army was galvanized and cried: *«By Allāh, Ibn Rawāḥah has spoken the truth!»* The force broke camp and advanced (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:147).

## 4. Tactical Deployment at Muʾtah
The Muslim army drew battle lines at the village of **Muʾtah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148):
- **Commander-in-Chief**: **[[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]** ﵁ bearing the white army standard (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).
- **Right Flank (*Maymanah*)**: **[[Quṭbah b. Qatādah al-ʿUdhrī]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).
- **Left Flank (*Maysarah*)**: ʿAbāyah (or Rāfiʿ) b. Mālik al-Anṣārī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).

Early in the engagement, Quṭbah b. Qatādah charged the commander of the Christian Arab confederacy, **Mālik b. Zāfilah**, and slew him in single combat, steadying the Muslim right flank against massive enemy pressure (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).

## 5. The Succession of the Three Martyrs

### 1. Zayd b. Ḥārithah
**[[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]** ﵁ advanced carrying the Prophet's standard, plunging deep into the dense thicket of Byzantine lances until he was pierced through by multiple spears and fell as the first supreme commander martyred (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).

### 2. Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib (Dhū al-Janāḥayn)
**[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ leaped forward and caught the falling standard (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148). Dismounting from his blond mare, he hamstrung her legs (*ʿaqarahā*) to demonstrate to both armies that retreat was inconceivable (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148). Raising the standard, he fought ferociously while chanting:
> يَا حَبَّذَا الْجَنَّةُ وَاقْتِرَابُهَا ... طَيِّبَةً وَبَارِدًا شَرَابُهَا  
> وَالرُّومُ رُومٌ قَدْ دَنَا عَذَابُهَا ... كَافِرَةٌ بَعِيدَةٌ أَنْسَابُهَا ... عَلَيَّ إِذْ لَاقَيْتُهَا ضِرَابُهَا  
> *"O how delightful is Paradise and its nearness—sweet and cool is its water! And the Byzantines are approaching their doom: infidels, alien of lineage; it is upon me when I meet them to strike them down!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).

During the melee, a Roman soldier severed Jaʿfar's right arm. He instantly grasped the standard with his left. A second blow severed his left arm. Jaʿfar collapsed onto the shaft, clamping the banner to his chest with his upper arms and chin until a Byzantine soldier struck him down (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:149). When his body was recovered, his companions counted over ninety distinct wounds from swords, spears, and arrows across his chest and face—not a single strike upon his back (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:149).

### 3. ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah
**[[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]** ﵁ rushed forward on horseback and seized the standard (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:149). For a fleeting second, his mortal soul hesitated before the wall of Roman steel; he instantly rebuked himself in famous verse:
> أَقْسَمْتُ يَا نَفْسُ لَتَنْزِلِنَّهْ ... كَارِهَةً أَوْ لَتُطَاوِعِنَّهْ  
> إِنْ أَجْلَبَ النَّاسُ وَشَدُّوا الرَّنَّهْ ... مَا لِي أَرَاكِ تَكْرَهِينَ الْجَنَّهْ؟!  
> *"I have sworn, O my soul, that you shall surely descend into the fray, willing or unwilling! If the hosts shout and intensify the battle cries, why do I see you shrinking from Paradise?!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:149).

Dismounting from his horse, his cousin brought him a roasted bone of meat saying: *«Strengthen yourself with this; you have tasted nothing for days.»* He took a bite, then heard the roar of combat swell, threw the meat aside, drew his sword, and plunged into the center of the enemy lines until martyred (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:149).

## 6. The Miraculous Transmission in Medina
Eight hundred kilometers away in Medina, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ sat in his mosque surrounded by his Companions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:150). Suddenly, a Divine trance overtook him; the veil was lifted from his sight, and he witnessed the battlefield of Muʾtah unfolded before him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:150).

With tears streaming down his blessed face, the Prophet ﷺ delivered a real-time eulogy to the weeping congregation:
> «أَخَذَ الرَّايَةَ زَيْدٌ فَأُصِيبَ، ثُمَّ أَخَذَهَا جَعْفَرٌ فَأُصِيبَ، ثُمَّ أَخَذَهَا ابْنُ رَوَاحَةَ فَأُصِيبَ—وَإِنَّ عَيْنَيْهِ لَتَذْرِفَانِ—حَتَّى أَخَذَ الرَّايَةَ سَيْفٌ مِنْ سُيُوفِ اللهِ حَتَّى فَتَحَ اللهُ عَلَيْهِمْ»
> *"Zayd took the standard and was struck down; then Jaʿfar took it and was struck down; then Ibn Rawāḥah took it and was struck down—while his tears flowed freely—until the standard was taken by a sword from the swords of Allāh (*sayfun min suyūfillāh*), until Allāh granted victory through him!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:150).

Of Jaʿfar, the Prophet ﷺ announced his immortal honor:
> «أَبْدَلَهُ اللهُ بِيَدَيْهِ جَنَاحَيْنِ يَطِيرُ بِهِمَا فِي الْجَنَّةِ مَعَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ حَيْثُ شَاءَ»
> *"Allāh has replaced his two hands with two wings wherewith he flies through Paradise alongside the angels wherever he wills!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:150).

## 7. The Elevation of Khālid b. al-Walīd & Field Maneuvers
When Ibn Rawāḥah fell, the standard tumbled to the ground and the army faced imminent collapse (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:150). 

The Badri veteran **[[Thābit b. Aqram]]** ﵁ leaped into the fray, lifted the banner, and called for an election. When the soldiers selected him, Thābit renounced command, rushed to **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁, and thrust the standard into his hands: *«Take this, O Abū Sulaymān, for you are far more skilled in warfare than I!»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:500; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:150).

Assuming supreme command, Khālid rallied the shattered front. In ferocious hand-to-hand combat, Khālid broke nine swords in his hand, leaving only a broad Yemeni blade intact (*ṣafīḥah yamāniyyah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:151).

### The Overnight Stratagem
Recognizing that a head-on battle against two hundred thousand soldiers meant annihilation, Khālid devised a brilliant psychological deception overnight (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:151):
1. **Flank Inversion**: He transferred the right flank to the left, and the left to the right.
2. **Line Rotation**: He moved the vanguard (*muqaddamah*) to the rear (*sāqah*), and the rear to the vanguard.
3. **Simulated Reinforcements**: At daybreak, the Byzantines faced completely unfamiliar faces, standards, and equipment across every sector of the line. Behind the front, Khālid sent cavalry detachments to gallop across the hills, kicking up massive dust plumes while chanting Takbīr.

Convinced that massive reinforcements from Medina had arrived during the night, the Byzantine commanders panicked (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:151). Exploiting their hesitation, Khālid ordered an orderly, step-by-step retreat in tight defensive formations. Fearing an elaborate encirclement ambush, the Byzantines declined pursuit and halted (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:151). Khālid successfully disengaged and extracted the entire Muslim army intact.

## 9. Reception in Medina & The Vindication: *al-Karrār*
When the army approached Medina, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and the townspeople rode out to meet them at al-Jurf (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:155). Some townspeople and children threw dust upon the soldiers, crying: *«O you who fled! Have you fled from the path of Allāh?!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:155).

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ intervened forcefully, rebuking the crowd and vindicating his warriors:
> «لَيْسُوا بِالْفُرَّارِ، وَلَكِنَّهُمُ الْكَرَّارُ إِنْ شَاءَ اللهُ تَعَالَى!»
> *"They are not runaways! Rather, they are the persistent charging warriors (*al-karrār*), if Allāh wills!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:155).

The Prophet ﷺ visited the household of Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib, took Jaʿfar's small sons—ʿAbdullāh, ʿAwn, and Muḥammad—in his arms, weeping and caressing their heads (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:156). He then issued his enduring sunnah to the community:
> «اصْنَعُوا لِآلِ جَعْفَرٍ طَعَامًا، فَإِنَّهُ قَدْ أَتَاهُمْ مَا يَشْغَلُهُمْ!»
> *"Prepare food for the family of Jaʿfar, for there has indeed come upon them that which occupies them!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:156).
