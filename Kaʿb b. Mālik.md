# Kaʿb b. Mālik (كعب بن مالك الأنصاري) ﵁
*Shāʿir al-Islām (The Poet of Islam) and the Sincere Penitent of Tabūk*

## 1. Lineage & Early Islam
He is Kaʿb b. Mālik b. ʿAmr b. al-Qayn al-Salmī al-Khazrajī (kunya: Abū ʿAbdillāh or Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān), from the Banū Salimah clan of the Khazraj (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:214; 4:213). He embraced Islam early and pledged fealty at [[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:214).

Alongside **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]** ﵁ and Ḥassān b. Thābit ﵁, Kaʿb was commissioned by the Prophet ﷺ to defend the honor of Islam and answer the invectives of Quraysh through poetry. The Prophet ﷺ said of their poetry: *“By Him in Whose hand is my soul, it stings them worse than the flight of arrows!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:278).

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones
- **Valor at [[Ghazwat Uḥud]] (3 AH)**: Fought fiercely in the ranks of the defenders when the reversal struck, sustaining seventeen wounds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206, 213). He was the first companion to recognize the Prophet ﷺ alive through the slits of his helmet, crying out at the top of his voice: *“O Muslims, rejoice! Here is the Messenger of Allāh!”* whereupon the Prophet ﷺ gestured for him to remain silent (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:206).
- **The Trial and Repentance of Tabūk (9 AH)**: At **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]**, despite having two riding camels and abundant provisions, procrastination led him to remain behind without valid excuse. Unlike the hypocrites who fabricated false justifications, Kaʿb spoke absolute truth to the Prophet ﷺ: *“By Allāh, I had never been stronger or more prosperous than when I stayed behind.”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:475–476).
- The Prophet ﷺ ordered the community to boycott him and his two truthful companions (Murārah b. al-Rabīʿ and Hilāl b. Umayyah) for fifty nights, until the earth seemed constrained for them despite its vastness. Their profound repentance (*Tawbah*) was accepted by divine revelation in **[[Sūrat al-Tawbah]]** (9:118) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:479–480).

## 3. Demise & Legacy
Kaʿb lived on in Medina, revered for his uncompromising truthfulness and literary mastery, losing his eyesight in his later years. He passed away in Medina around 50 AH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:480).
