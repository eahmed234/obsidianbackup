# Sariyyat al-Ghamr (سرية الغمر)
*ʿUkkāshah b. Miḥṣan's raid on Ghamr Marzūq*

The **expedition to al-Ghamr** was a raid led by [[ʿUkkāshah b. Miḥṣan]] ﵁ in Rabīʿ al-Awwal of the year 6 AH against Ghamr Marzūq, a water belonging to [[Banū Asad]].[^s6.77] The tribe learned of the approach and scattered before the Muslims arrived, but a captured lookout led the raiders to a herd belonging to his own kinsmen. The Muslims drove off two hundred camels and returned to Medina without loss and without having fought.[^s6.77][^s6.78]

## The expedition

The Prophet ﷺ sent ʿUkkāshah with forty men.[^s6.77] Among them were [[Thābit b. Aqram]] — whom one report names as the commander — Shujāʿ b. Wahb and Yazīd b. Ruqaysh; another account adds Laqīṭ b. Aʿṣam, of the tribe of Baliyy and an ally of Banū ʿAmr.[^s6.77][^laqit]

ʿUkkāshah set out quickly and pressed the pace, but the tribe was warned of the force, abandoned its pastures and withdrew to the high ground of its country. When the Muslims reached the water they found the camp deserted. ʿUkkāshah sent Shujāʿ b. Wahb ahead to scout for news or tracks, and Shujāʿ came back having found the tracks of livestock close by.[^s6.77]

## The captured lookout

Following the tracks, the Muslims surprised one of the tribe's lookouts, who had kept watch all night and was asleep when they took him in the morning. Asked where the people and their herds were, he said both had gone up to the high ground. One of the Muslims struck him with a whip, and he offered a bargain: if they spared his life, he would lead them to livestock belonging to cousins of his who knew nothing of the raid.[^s6.77]

They gave him safe-conduct, and he led them so far that they began to suspect a trap, and threatened to kill him unless he told the truth. He told them they would see the herds from the next low rise — and there they found the livestock grazing freely.[^s6.77]

## The raid

> «فأغاروا عليها وأصابوها وهربت الأعراب في كل وجه، ونهى عكّاشة عن الطلب. واستاقوا مائتي بعير»
> *They fell upon the herd and took it; the Bedouin fled in every direction, and ʿUkkāshah forbade pursuit. They drove off two hundred camels.*[^s6.77]

They brought the camels down to Medina, released their prisoner, and came back to the Prophet ﷺ with no one harmed, having met no fighting.[^s6.77][^kayd]

## Points of disagreement

Some claimed that Thābit b. Aqram was killed on this expedition, but al-Ṣāliḥī rejects this outright: Thābit was killed later, in the [[Ḥurūb al-Riddah|Riddah wars]].[^s6.77] One copy of al-Ḥākim's *al-Iklīl* gives the scout's name as "Sibāʿ b. Wahb"; the other copies have Shujāʿ b. Wahb, and no Companion named Sibāʿ b. Wahb is known.[^s6.78]
