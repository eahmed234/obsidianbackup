# Daws (دوس)
*The Valiant Azdite Tribe of the Southern Highlands, Bearers of the Light and the Seventy Households of Khaybar*

## 1. Lineage and Geographic Territory
Daws is a celebrated and formidable branch of the great southern Arab confederation of **[[al-Azd]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417). Classical genealogists trace their lineage to:
**Daws b. ʿUdnān b. ʿAbdillāh b. Zahr b. Kaʿb b. al-Ḥārith b. Kaʿb b. ʿAbdillāh b. Mālik b. Naṣr b. al-Azd** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417).

They inhabited the rugged, precipitous elevations of the Sarāt mountain range bordering southern Arabia, renowned for their formidable military valor, fierce independence, and fortified mountain strongholds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417).

## 2. The Conversion of al-Ṭufayl b. ʿAmr (Dhū al-Nūr)
During the Makkan period, the paramount chieftain and acclaimed poet of Daws, **[[al-Ṭufayl b. ʿAmr al-Dawsī]]** ﵁, arrived in Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417). Fearing his eloquence, the magnates of Quraysh warned him incessantly against listening to the Prophet ﷺ, alleging that his words acted like sorcery that divided father from son and husband from wife (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417). So terrified was al-Ṭufayl that he stuffed raw cotton (*kursuf*) into his ears upon entering **[[al-Masjid al-Ḥarām]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417).

However, while standing near the Kaʿbah, Allāh caused words of the Prophetic recitation to reach his hearing (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417). Recognizing the supreme beauty and truth of the message, al-Ṭufayl removed the cotton, followed the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ to his home, and requested to hear the message (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417). The Prophet ﷺ recited **[[Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ]]** and the Muʿawwidhatayn, whereupon al-Ṭufayl declared his faith:
> «فَلَا وَاللَّهِ مَا سَمِعْتُ قَوْلًا قَطُّ أَحْسَنَ مِنْهُ، وَلَا أَمْرًا أَعْدَلَ مِنْهُ، فَأَسْلَمْتُ!»
> *"By Allāh, I had never heard any speech more beautiful than this, nor any decree more just! So I submitted to Islam!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417).

Al-Ṭufayl asked for a divine sign (*āyah*) to assist him in calling his fierce tribe to Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417). A luminous beacon like a lantern (*miṣbāḥ*) appeared between his eyes; when he supplicated that it not be upon his face lest the people mistake it for a blemish (*muthlah*), the light transferred miraculously to the tip of his riding whip, earning him the perpetual honorific **Dhū al-Nūr** (*The Possessor of the Light*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:417).

## 3. The Prophetic Invocation and the Delegation at Khaybar
Upon returning to the highlands, al-Ṭufayl brought his father and his wife to Islam, but the broader clans of Daws initially resisted his preaching (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:418). Discouraged by their moral stubbornness, al-Ṭufayl returned to Makkah and petitioned: *«O Prophet of Allāh! Daws has overcome me by clinging to sin; invoke Allāh against them!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:418).

Instead of cursing them, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ raised his hands to the heavens and supplicated with boundless mercy:
> «اللَّهُمَّ اهْدِ دَوْسًا وَائْتِ بِهِمْ! ارْجِعْ إِلَى قَوْمِكَ وَارْفُقْ بِهِمْ»
> *"O Allāh, guide Daws and bring them! Return to your people and treat them with gentleness!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:418).

Al-Ṭufayl returned patiently to the mountains of Daws and continued calling them to Islam throughout the migrations and the campaigns of Badr, Uḥud, and the Trench (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:418). 

Finally, in 7 AH while the Prophet ﷺ was besieging the fortresses of **[[Khaybar]]**, al-Ṭufayl marched into Medina and joined the Prophet ﷺ at Khaybar accompanied by seventy or eighty households (*sabʿīna aw thamānīna baytan*) of Daws who had embraced Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:418). Among this historic contingent was the paramount narrator of Hadith, **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:418). The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ welcomed the warriors of Daws warmly and assigned them equal shares from the spoils of Khaybar alongside the original expeditionary force (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:418).

## 4. Destruction of Dhū al-Khaffayn and Military Service
Following **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**, the Prophet ﷺ dispatched al-Ṭufayl b. ʿAmr back to the territory of Daws to demolish the pagan wooden idol **Dhū al-Kaffayn** (the false deity of ʿAmr b. Ḥumamah al-Dawsī). Al-Ṭufayl set the idol ablaze while declaring poetic verses renouncing polytheism, completing the eradication of paganism from the territory of Daws. Warriors of Daws went on to serve with distinction across the Ridda wars and the early Islamic conquests.
