# al-Rawḥāʾ (الروحاء)
*The Blessed Waystation of the Prophets and Pivot of Strategic Deterrence*

## 1. Topography & Sacred Antiquity
*Wādī al-Rawḥāʾ* is an expansive valley and famous historical oasis located approximately forty to forty-five miles (around seventy-three kilometers) southwest of Medina, serving as the crucial junction where the road from Medina branches toward Badr, Yanbuʿ, and Mecca (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:211).

The valley holds distinguished spiritual renown in Prophetic tradition as the sacred pathway traversed by past messengers. The Prophet ﷺ related:
> «لَقَدْ مَرَّ بِفَجِّ الرَّوْحَاءِ سَبْعُونَ نَبِيًّا حُفَاةً عَلَيْهِمُ الْعَبَاءُ، يَؤُمُّونَ بَيْتَ اللَّهِ الْعَتِيقَ!»
> *"Seventy Prophets have surely traversed the pass of al-Rawḥāʾ, barefoot, clad in woolen cloaks, directing themselves toward the Ancient House of Allāh!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:211–212).

The Prophet ﷺ drank from its well (*Biʾr al-Rawḥāʾ*) and prayed at its prayer stations during multiple journeys.

## 2. Strategic Role in the Campaigns
- **En Route to [[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]] (2 AH)**: The Muslim army halted at al-Rawḥāʾ on their outward march from Medina, praying there and dispatching advance scouts (Basbas b. ʿAmr and ʿAdī b. Abī al-Zaghbāʾ) forward to gather intelligence at Badr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:25).
- **Return from Badr & Execution of the Antagonists**: Upon returning victorious from Badr with the prisoners of war, the army encamped at al-Rawḥāʾ. Here, delegations of Muslims from Medina met the Prophet ﷺ to congratulate him on the victory, and it was at al-Rawḥāʾ that the criminal instigator al-Naḍr b. al-Ḥārith was executed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:136).
- **The Deterrence of [[Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad]] (3 AH)**: Following the crisis of Uḥud, the victorious polytheist army under **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** encamped at al-Rawḥāʾ, debating whether to wheel around and launch an assault to annihilate Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:309). It was at al-Rawḥāʾ that the allied chieftain **[[Maʿbad b. Abī Maʿbad al-Khuzāʿī]]** met Abū Sufyān, terrifically exaggerating the size and fury of the pursuing Muslim army, which induced panic in the Meccans and forced their hasty retreat back to Mecca (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:309–310).
