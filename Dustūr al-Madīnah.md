# Dustūr al-Madīnah (موادعة النبي ﷺ لليهود / صحيفة المدينة)
*The Constitutional Covenant Between the Emigrants, the Helpers, and the Jewish Tribes of Medina*

## 1. Historical & Strategic Genesis
Upon establishing the sanctuary of the Mosque (**[[Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]**) and cementing the bond of fraternal solidarity (**[[al-Muʾākhāh]]**), the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ drafted a formal constitutional treaty (*kitāb*) establishing the civil, juridical, and defense framework of the nascent Madinan commonwealth (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:382). 

This pact was enacted during the first year of the Hijrah, prior to the divine permission for armed warfare and the subsequent legislation of the poll-tax (*jizyah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:382). It brought the disparate factions of Yathrib—the exiled [[al-Muhājirūn|Muhājirūn]], the agrarian [[al-Anṣār|Anṣār]] of [[al-Aws]] and [[al-Khazraj]], and the entrenched Jewish clans inhabiting the fortresses (*āṭām*) and palm groves—into a unified socio-political compact.

Al-Ṣāliḥī documents the foundational core of the covenant:
> «وَكَتَبَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ كِتَابًا بَيْنَ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ وَالْأَنْصَارِ، وَادَعَ فِيهِ يَهُودَ وَعَاهَدَهُمْ، وَأَقَرَّهُمْ عَلَى دِينِهِمْ وَأَمْوَالِهِمْ، وَاشْتَرَطَ عَلَيْهِمْ وَشَرَطَ لَهُمْ»
> *"And the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ drafted a written covenant between the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār in which he made peace with the Jews, concluded a treaty with them, affirmed them upon their religion and their properties, and stipulated mutual obligations upon them and for them."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:382, citing Ibn Isḥāq and Abū ʿUbayd in *Kitāb al-Amwāl* via an authentic transmission from al-Zuhrī).

## 2. Core Principles & Structural Obligations
As transmitted across classical biographical and administrative compendia, the charter codified revolutionary governance norms (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:382–384):

### 1. The Unity of the Commonwealth (*Ummah Wāḥidah*)
The signatories formed an integrated civic entity distinct from outside tribes:
- The believers from Quraysh and Yathrib, and those who join them, attach to them, and strive alongside them, constitute a single community (*ummah wāḥidah*) to the exclusion of external adversaries.
- Ancient blood-feud indemnities (*diyāt*) and captive redemptions (*fidāʾ*) were regularized within each clan according to equity and recognized custom (*al-maʿrūf*), removing the arbitrary disparity that had historically favored Banū al-Naḍīr over Banū Qurayẓah.

### 2. Religious Freedom & Sanctity of Property
- The treaty recognized absolute liberty of conscience: the Jews retain their faith, scriptures, and synagogues unhindered, and the Muslims retain their faith (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:382).
- Full legal protection was accorded to the persons, fortresses, orchards, and commerce of the Jewish signatories, provided they maintained peace and did not conspire against the commonwealth.

### 3. Mutual Defense & Wartime Expenditure
- The internal security of the Yathrib basin was declared sacred (*ḥarām*): aggression against any signatory was treated as an attack against all.
- In the event of military mobilization to defend Medina against outside invaders, the Jewish tribes were obligated to contribute their proportionate share of wartime expenditure alongside the Muslims for the duration of the campaign (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:382).
- Neither faction was permitted to grant refuge, trade passage, or aid to the polytheists of **[[Quraysh]]** or their commercial allies, Quraysh having declared open hostilities against the Prophet ﷺ and expelled the Muhājirūn.

### 4. Ultimate Judicial Sovereignty
- While intra-tribal domestic affairs remained governed by traditional custom and Torah law, any severe discord, treason, or dispute threatening the peace of the oasis was subject to ultimate appeal:
> «وَإِنَّهُ مَا كَانَ بَيْنَ أَهْلِ هَذِهِ الصَّحِيفَةِ مِنْ حَدَثٍ أَوْ اشْتِجَارٍ يُخَافُ فَسَادُهُ، فَإِنَّ مَرَدَّهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ، وَإِلَى مُحَمَّدٍ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ»
> *"And whatever dispute or quarrel arises among the people of this document, from which harm is feared, its ultimate referral is to Allāh, Mighty and Majestic, and to Muḥammad, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ."*

## 3. The Jewish Leadership & Tribal Dynamics
Al-Ṣāliḥī details the prominent chieftains and religious authorities (*aḥbār*) of the three dominant Jewish tribes residing in Medina at the ratification of the charter (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:383–384):

### [[Banū Qaynuqāʿ]] (The Goldsmiths & Merchants)
- Inhabited the dense market quarters within the urban center of Medina.
- Prominent figures included:
  - **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Salām]]** ﵁: The chief rabbi and foremost scholar who embraced Islam immediately upon the Prophet's arrival in Medina.
  - Saʿd b. Ḥunayf, Finḥāṣ, and Rifāʿah b. Zayd b. al-Tābūt.

### [[Banū al-Naḍīr]] (The Landowners of al-ʿĀliyah)
- Controlled the fertile southern palm oases and fortified keeps.
- Prominent chieftains included:
  - **Abū Yāsir b. Akhṭab**: The first Jewish leader to visit the Prophet ﷺ upon his arrival; he recognized his prophetic signs and urged his people to obey him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:383).
  - **Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab**: Abū Yāsir's intransigent brother who overpowered his counsel, openly declaring eternal enmity against the Prophet ﷺ out of bitter ethnic jealousy that prophethood had arisen among the descendants of Ismāʿīl ﵊ rather than Isḥāq ﵊ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:383–384).
  - **Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf**: Wealthy poet and conspirator.
  - **Sallām b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq (Abū Rāfiʿ)**: Prominent financier and caravan leader.

### [[Banū Qurayẓah]] (The Southern Bastion)
- Guarded the southeastern approaches to Medina.
- Prominent leaders included:
  - **Kaʿb b. Asad**: The paramount chieftain and keeper of the covenant for Banū Qurayẓah, who initially honored the treaty (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:384).
  - **al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā**: Senior sage and jurist.
  - Shamwīl b. Zayd.

## 4. The Breakdown & Breach of the Pacts
Despite the comprehensive civil and religious safeguards codified in the charter, each of the three major Jewish tribes subsequently violated their covenants in succession, precipitating military confrontation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:384):
1. **[[Ghazwat Banī Qaynuqāʿ]] (Shawwāl 2 AH)**: Following the Muslim victory at Badr, Banū Qaynuqāʿ initiated public provocations in their marketplace, broke their pact, and were besieged and expelled from Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:384; 4:160–174).
2. **[[Ghazwat Banī al-Naḍīr]] (Rabīʿ al-Awwal 4 AH)**: Banū al-Naḍīr conspired to assassinate the Prophet ﷺ by dropping a millstone from a fortress roof during diplomatic negotiations regarding blood-money, resulting in their siege and expulsion to Khaybar and Syria, as chronicled in **[[Sūrat al-Ḥashr]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:384; 5:11–32).
3. **[[Ghazwat Banī Qurayẓah]] (Dhū al-Qaʿdah 5 AH)**: During the Confederate siege of **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**, Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab persuaded Kaʿb b. Asad to tear up the charter and launch a rear assault against the undefended families of Medina. Upon the lifting of the siege, Banū Qurayẓah were besieged and submitted to the arbitration of **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁, as documented in **[[Sūrat al-Aḥzāb]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:384; 5:80–110).
