# Banū al-Naḍīr (بنو النضير)
*The Chieftains of al-ʿĀliyah — The Treachery of the Millstone and the Expulsion to Khaybar*

## 1. Lineage & Territory
Banū al-Naḍīr was one of the two preeminent Jewish priestly tribes (*al-Kāhinān*, alongside Banū Qurayẓah) residing in Yathrib prior to the Hijrah, claiming descent from Prophet Hārūn b. ʿImrān ﵊ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:378). They occupied heavily fortified stone castles (*āṭām*) and rich date-palm plantations in the fertile southern highland (*al-ʿĀliyah*) of **[[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:317).

Prior to Islam, they held superior political and military prestige over their neighbors, concluding mutual defense treaties with **[[al-Khazraj]]** and maintaining immense wealth through agricultural monopolies and high-interest financing (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:378, 384).

## 2. Pacts & Early Hostilities in Medina
Upon the Prophet's arrival in Medina, Banū al-Naḍīr entered into the mutual civic pact of **[[Dustūr al-Madīnah|The Constitution of Medina]]**, recognizing reciprocal defense and freedom of religion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:384).

However, their leadership harbored deep theological envy and rancor:
- **Collusion at Ghazwat al-Sawīq (2 AH)**: When **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** led a nighttime raid on Medina following Badr, he was received and secretly hosted inside the fortress of Sallām b. Mishkam, chief and treasurer of Banū al-Naḍīr, who provided him with food, drink, and military intelligence regarding Muslim positions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:174).

## 3. The Treachery of the Millstone & The Expulsion (4 AH)
In Rabīʿ al-Awwal 4 AH, the Prophet ﷺ visited the tribal settlement of Banū al-Naḍīr accompanied by **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, and **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ to seek their customary financial contribution toward the blood-money (*diyah*) of two slain men of Banū ʿĀmir (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:317).

While the Prophet ﷺ sat resting against the wall of one of their fortresses:
1. The chieftains conspired to assassinate him; **ʿAmr b. Jiḥāsh b. Kaʿb** ascended to the roof to cast a massive millstone (*raḥā*) down upon his head (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:317).
2. Divine revelation descended through Jibrīl ﵊ warning the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, who rose immediately and returned to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:318).
3. The Prophet ﷺ dispatched Muḥammad b. Maslamah delivering an ultimatum: they were granted ten days to evacuate Medina due to their treacherous breach of covenant (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:319).
4. The hypocrite leader **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** sent word urging them to refuse the order, falsely pledging two thousand fighters to die in their defense (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:321).
5. The Prophet ﷺ laid siege to their fortresses for six nights, cutting and burning their non-fruitful date palms (*al-līnah*) to deny them cover (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:322–324).
6. Banū al-Naḍīr surrendered on terms of safe passage into exile, carrying away only what their camels could bear excluding weapons of war (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324). They departed on six hundred camels, proudly accompanied by music and tambourines, dismantling the doorframes and timber of their own homes (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324–325).

Their evacuated agricultural lands and date groves were designated as *Fayʾ* granted exclusively to the Prophet ﷺ, who distributed them among the destitute Muhājirūn and two impoverished Anṣārī veterans (**[[Sahl b. Ḥunayf]]** and Abū Dujānah) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:326). This historic siege was memorialized by Allāh in the revelation of **[[Sūrat al-Ḥashr]]** (*Sūrat al-Naḍīr*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).

## 4. Instigation of the Confederates & Final Subjugation
The exiled chiefs of Banū al-Naḍīr, led by **Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab** and Sallām b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq, resettled in the massive citadel of **[[Khaybar]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:325). From Khaybar, they traveled to Makkah and Najd to assemble the ten-thousand-man confederate army during **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]** (5 AH), and Ḥuyayy personally infiltrated the fortress of Kaʿb b. Asad to orchestrate the treason of **[[Banū Qurayẓah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:363, 373).

Their final power was shattered during **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]** (7 AH), where their chief warriors were slain, culminating in the marriage of the Prophet ﷺ to **[[Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyayy]]** ﵂ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:115–151).
