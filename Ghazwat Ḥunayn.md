# Ghazwat Ḥunayn (غزوة حنين)
*The Battle of the Valley of Ḥunayn — The Spiritual Purge of Pride, the Lone Charge on the White Mule, and the Divine Descent of Tranquility*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from ***[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād|Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād fī Sīrat Khayr al-ʿIbād]]*** by **[[al-Ṣāliḥī|Imām Shams al-Dīn Muḥammad b. Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī al-Shāmī]]** (d. 942 AH), Volume 5, *al-Bāb al-Thāmin wal-ʿIshrūn fī Ghazwat Ḥunayn* (pp. 310–381). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the primary accounts of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[Mūsā b. ʿUqbah]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], [[al-Bukhārī]], and [[Muslim]].

---

## 1. Strategic Context & Mobilization of Hawāzin
Following the rapid, peaceful liberation of Makkah in Ramaḍān 8 AH, the proud pagan confederations of **Hawāzin** (Naṣr, Jusham, Saʿd b. Bakr) and **Thaqīf** of al-Ṭāʾif refused to submit (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:310).

Their supreme commander, the fiery thirty-year-old chieftain **Mālik b. ʿAwf al-Naṣrī**, decreed an unprecedented tactical gamble: he commanded every warrior to march out accompanied by their wives, children, camels, sheep, and herds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:311). His rationale was to deny his soldiers any possibility of retreat, forcing them to fight to the death to protect their families and wealth.

The aged, blind military sage **Durayd b. al-Ṣimmah al-Jushamī** (over a century old, carried in a litter) condemned the deployment furiously:
> *"The pasture of a goat! Does anything turn back a fleeing warrior? If victory is yours, only men with swords profit you; but if you are defeated, you will have disgraced your women and handed your wealth to your foe!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:311–312).
Mālik b. ʿAwf rejected Durayd's counsel, threatening to fall upon his sword if the tribes did not obey him. The confederates massed in the winding, steep defiles of **Awtās** and the **Valley of Ḥunayn** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:312).

---

## 2. The Muslim Host & The Sin of Overconfidence
On 6 Shawwāl 8 AH, the Prophet ﷺ marched out from Makkah at the head of **twelve thousand warriors**: the ten thousand veterans of the Conquest of Makkah, reinforced by two thousand newly converted Meccans (*al-Ṭulaqāʾ*) led by **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:313).
- **The Borrowed Armor**: To equip his troops, the Prophet ﷺ borrowed one hundred coats of mail and armaments from the non-Muslim merchant Ṣafwān b. Umayyah as a guaranteed loan (*ʿāriyah muʾaddāh*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:313).
- **The Trial of Numbers**: Viewing this unprecedented, glittering armada of armor and cavalry, a sentiment of self-reliance crept into the hearts of some soldiers, who remarked:
  > «لَنْ نُغْلَبَ الْيَوْمَ مِنْ قِلَّةٍ!»
  > *"We shall never be defeated today on account of small numbers!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:313).
  This phrase pained the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, for Allāh had granted them victory at Badr when they were few, and now decreed that they should taste the bitter futility of numbers severed from divine reliance (fulfilling Qurʾān 9:25: *«When your great numbers pleased you, but they availed you nothing»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:313–314).

---

## 3. The Ambush at Dawn in the Ravines of Ḥunayn
Before dawn on 10 Shawwāl 8 AH, the Muslim vanguard entered the narrow, precipitous gorge of Ḥunayn (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:318).
- Unknown to the Muslims, Mālik b. ʿAwf had positioned his marksmen, ambush parties, and cavalry in the narrow mountain crevices and caves overlooking the track (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:318).
- Led by the cavalry of Banū Sulaym under **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁, the vanguard descended into the gorge in the morning twilight. Suddenly, Hawāzin unleashed a coordinated, blizzard-like volley of arrows (*katībah shaḍwāʾ*) and charged down the slopes (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:318–319).
- The horses of Banū Sulaym panicked, bucked, and stampeded backward through the narrow defile, crashing into the forward ranks of the Muhājirūn and Anṣār. Blind panic engulfed the army; thousands of men turned and fled down the valley toward Makkah, leaving their mounts and supplies behind (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:319).
- Newly converted Meccans and hypocrites began gloating; Abū Sufyān muttered: *"Their flight will not stop until they reach the sea!"* while Shaybah b. ʿUthmān sought to assassinate the Prophet ﷺ in the confusion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:319, 359).

---

## 4. The Steadfastness of the Prophet ﷺ & The Lone Charge
Amidst the catastrophic collapse of twelve thousand men, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ remained rooted like a massive mountain upon his white mule, **al-Duldul** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:322).
- Rather than retreating, the Prophet ﷺ spurred his mule **forward directly into the teeth of the enemy vanguard**, his uncle **[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ grasping its bridle to restrain it from hurtling into the enemy spears, and **Abū Sufyān b. al-Ḥārith b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib** ﵁ holding its stirrup (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:322).
- The Prophet ﷺ raised his majestic voice above the clash of iron, shouting his immortal royal defiance:
  > «أَنَا النَّبِيُّ لَا كَذِبْ ... أَنَا ابْنُ عَبْدِ الْمُطَّلِبْ!»
  > *"I am the Prophet without falsehood! I am the son of ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib!"* (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 4315; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:322).

---

## 5. The Ring of Steel: The Stalwarts Who Stood Firm
Around the Prophet's mule, only a tiny handful of unwavering believers formed a defensive wall of steel against the polytheist onslaught (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:329):
1. **[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁
2. **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁
3. **[[al-Faḍl b. al-ʿAbbās]]** ﵁
4. **[[Abū Sufyān b. al-Ḥārith]]** ﵁
5. **[[Rabīʿah b. al-Ḥārith]]** ﵁
6. **[[Usāmah b. Zayd]]** ﵁
7. **[[Ayman b. ʿUbayd]]** ﵁ (the son of Umm Ayman, who fought heroically until martyred beside the Prophet's stirrup)
8. **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁
9. **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:329).
- **The Valor of the Women**: Standing steadfast among the handful was **Umm Sulaym bint Milḥān** ﵂ (mother of Anas b. Mālik), holding a dagger strapped to her waist. When the Prophet ﷺ asked about it, she answered: *"I took it so that if any polytheist draws near me, I will rip open his belly!"* and **[[Nusaybah bint Kaʿb]]** ﵂ (Umm ʿUmārah) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:330).

---

## 6. The Call of al-ʿAbbās & The Counter-Assault
Turning to his uncle al-ʿAbbās—who possessed a booming voice that could carry for miles—the Prophet ﷺ commanded:
> «يَا عَبَّاسُ، نَادِ: يَا مَعْشَرَ الْأَنْصَارِ! يَا أَصْحَابَ السَّمُرَةِ! يَا أَصْحَابَ سُورَةِ الْبَقَرَةِ!»
> *"O ʿAbbās, call out: 'O assembly of the Anṣār! O companions of the Acacia Tree [of al-Riḍwān]! O people of Sūrat al-Baqarah!'"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:323).
- Al-ʿAbbās shouted with the full power of his voice across the valley. The call struck the hearts of the fleeing believers like a thunderbolt.
- Warriors attempting to turn their fleeing camels—unable to control them in the narrow gorge—leaped from their saddles, cast off their heavy cloaks, and ran on foot with shields and drawn swords shouting:
  > **«لَبَّيْكَ! لَبَّيْكَ!»**
  > *"Here we are at your service! Here we are!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:323).
- Within moments, one hundred veteran Anṣār formed an impenetrable phalanx around the Prophet ﷺ, followed by hundreds more. The clash was terrifying; viewing the sparks flying from colliding swords, the Prophet ﷺ remarked:
  > «الْآنَ حَمِيَ الْوَطِيسُ!»
  > *"Now the furnace has grown red hot!"* (*Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:324).

---

## 7. The Handful of Dust & Descent of the Angels
The Prophet ﷺ dismounted his mule, took a handful of desert pebbles and dust, hurled it into the faces of the massed enemy lines, and proclaimed:
> «شَاهَتِ الْوُجُوهُ!»
> *"May the faces be deformed!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:324).
- By divine miracle, not an enemy warrior escaped having his eyes, mouth, and nostrils filled with blinding dust (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:324).
- Divine tranquility (*al-sakīnah*) descended upon the believers, accompanied by legions of angels descending in white robes upon black-and-white mounts, striking terror into Hawāzin (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:327–328).
- The enemy line snapped. Hawāzin broke into a catastrophic rout, fleeing in complete disorder and abandoning their wives, children, and millions in livestock (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:331).

---

## 8. Slaying of the Pagan Champions & Casualties
- **Durayd b. al-Ṣimmah**: Fleeing in his litter, the centenarian commander was overtaken and slain by Rabīʿah b. Rufayʿ al-Sulamī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:333).
- **The Slaying of 70 Champions**: Approximately seventy polytheist champions were slain on the field (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:333).
- **Muslim Casualties**: Only a small number of Muslims fell, foremost being **[[Ayman b. ʿUbayd]]** ﵁ (the brother of Usāmah b. Zayd) and Saraqah b. al-Ḥārith (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:329).
- **Abū Qatādah al-Anṣārī**: The champion **[[Abū Qatādah al-Anṣārī]]** ﵁ slew an enemy knight who was overpowering a Muslim, and the Prophet ﷺ awarded him the knight's prized armor pursuant to the royal ruling: *«Whoever slays an enemy combatant, for him is his spoils (*salab*)!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:336–337).

---

## 9. The Enormous Spoils of al-Jiʿrānah
The booty captured at Ḥunayn was the largest gathered in the history of Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:338):
- **6,000 captives** (women and children).
- **24,000 camels**.
- **Over 40,000 sheep and goats**.
- **4,000 silver uqiyyahs** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:338).
The Prophet ﷺ ordered the entire booty transferred to **al-Jiʿrānah** under the custody of Masʿūd b. ʿAmr al-Ghifārī while he marched onward to besiege Thaqīf at **Ghazwat al-Ṭāʾif** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:338).
