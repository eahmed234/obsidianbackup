# Mabʿath al-Nabī ﷺ (مبعث النبي ﷺ وبدء الوحي)
*The Commencement of Prophethood — True Dreams, the Cave, and the Silence That Followed*

## 1. His Age, the Day, and the Disputed Month
**Age forty** is, in al-Nawawī's words, *"the correct view the scholars have settled upon."*
Al-Suhaylī concurs it is what the biographers and traditionists hold; a lone report from Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄
and Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab gives forty-three, which the book judges an error (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:225).

It came on a **Monday**, by day. The **month** is genuinely disputed: most hold **Ramaḍān** — Ibn Ḥajar
judges this preponderant, since it was the month he was in retreat at Ḥirāʾ when the angel came — with
reported dates of the 17th, 7th, or 14th within it. Others report **Rajab** (the 17th) or
**Rabīʿ al-Awwal** (the 1st or 8th). Abū Dāwūd al-Ṭayālisī's report suggests the very end of Ramaḍān
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:225). A tradition via Wāthilah b. al-Asqaʿ ﵁ places the Qurʾān's descent specifically
on the **24th of Ramaḍān**, alongside the earlier scriptures each said to have descended on their own
dates within the same month (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:227).

## 2. What Came First: True Dreams
**ʿĀʾishah** ﵂ narrates: *"the first thing the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was given of revelation was the
true vision in sleep — he never saw a vision but it came true like the break of dawn"* (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:228). Scholars explain this as a mercy: prophethood was not to fall upon him all at once and
overwhelm him, but to begin gently, so his soul would be prepared for what awaited
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:229).

## 3. Stone and Tree Greeting Him
**Barrah bint Abī Tijrāh** reports that once Allāh began to honour him, he would walk far into the
ravines and valley floors alone, and *"he never passed a stone or a tree but it said, 'peace be upon
you, O Messenger of Allāh' — and he would look right, left, and behind him, and see no one"*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:228). Al-Suhaylī identifies the stone in one such report as **the Black Stone
itself**.

Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄ is reported to have divided this period: **fifteen years at Makkah** before the Hijrah —
seven in which he saw the light and heard the voice, before revelation, and eight in which he was
already receiving revelation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:229).

## 4. Isrāfīl, Before Jibrīl
Al-ʿĀmir al-Shaʿbī reports, in a chain Aḥmad graded sound: *"prophethood descended upon him at forty,
and **Isrāfīl** was paired with him for three years — teaching him a word, a thing, but the Qurʾān was
not yet sent down upon his tongue. When three years had passed, **Jibrīl** was paired with him, and
the Qurʾān was sent down upon his tongue for twenty years — ten at Makkah and ten at Medina — and he
died at sixty-three"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:230). **Al-Wāqidī rejected this report outright**, holding
that no angel but Jibrīl was ever paired with him. Al-Ṣāliḥī records both positions without forcing a
resolution, noting Abū Shāmah's attempt at reconciling them — that the true dreams and stone/tree
greetings of the earlier period may be what is meant by Isrāfīl's pairing, occurring in the same
interval ʿĀʾishah's account simply does not mention (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:230).

## 5. The Night in the Cave
Multiple companions transmit the central event — Khadījah ﵂, ʿĀʾishah ﵂, and several others named by
al-Ṣāliḥī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:232). Before it, he had already dreamed of men approaching who said
*"huwa huwa, wa-lam yaʾni lahu baʿd"* — "it is him, but his time has not yet come" — and of his own
chest being opened by two who examined his heart and pronounced it *"the heart of a good man,"*
washed and restored it, and of a ladder of silver descending from a gap opened in the roof of his own
house (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:232–233).

Then, at Ḥirāʾ, where he had taken to spending the month of Ramaḍān each year in devotion
(*al-taḥannuth*), feeding whatever poor came to him:

> «فَغَتَّهُ حَتَّىٰ بَلَغَ مِنْهُ الْجَهْدَ … ثُمَّ أَرْسَلَهُ فَقَالَ: اقْرَأْ. قَالَ: مَا أَنَا بِقَارِئٍ»

Jibrīl pressed him three times, each time to the point of exhaustion, before he recited:

> ﴿اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ ۝ خَلَقَ الْإِنسَانَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ﴾
> *"Recite in the name of your Lord who created — created man from a clinging clot."* (96:1–2)
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:233)

In al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib's ﵁ fuller telling, **both Jibrīl and Mīkāʾīl** came; one asked the other, *"is it
him?"* — *"it is him"* — then had him weighed against one man, then ten, then a hundred, then a
thousand of his own nation, outweighing them each time, until the scale's contents fell away and
Mīkāʾīl said, *"his nation will follow him, by the Lord of the Kaʿbah!"* He was seated upon a cloth set
with ruby and pearl, his chest opened, and *"the place Shayṭān touches, and the clot of blood,"*
removed, washed, and stitched closed — before the recitation itself was given
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:235).

## 6. Home, Trembling
He returned to Khadījah ﵂ saying, *"cover me! cover me!"* (*zammilūnī, zammilūnī*), until his fear had
passed, then told her all that had happened, adding: *"I feared for myself."* She answered:

> «كَلَّا، وَاللَّهِ لَا يُخْزِيكَ اللَّهُ أَبَدًا، إِنَّكَ لَتَصِلُ الرَّحِمَ، وَتَحْمِلُ الْكَلَّ، وَتَكْسِبُ الْمَعْدُومَ، وَتَقْرِي الضَّيْفَ، وَتُعِينُ عَلَىٰ نَوَائِبِ الْحَقِّ»
> *"Never! By Allāh, Allāh will never disgrace you — you uphold ties of kinship, bear the burdens of
> the weak, provide for the destitute, honour the guest, and aid in the calamities of truth."*

## 7. Waraqah's Testimony, and His Death
Khadījah ﵂ took him to her cousin **Waraqah b. Nawfal**, by then old and blind, a Christian who copied
from the Gospel. Hearing the account, he said:

> «أَبْشِرْ، فَأَنَا أَشْهَدُ أَنَّكَ الَّذِي بُشِّرَ بِهِ ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ، هَٰذَا النَّامُوسُ الَّذِي أُنْزِلَ عَلَىٰ مُوسَىٰ، وَإِنَّكَ لَنَبِيٌّ مُرْسَلٌ، وَسَتُؤْمَرُ بِالْجِهَادِ بَعْدَ يَوْمِكَ هَٰذَا، وَلَئِنْ أَدْرَكَنِي ذَٰلِكَ لَأُجَاهِدَنَّ مَعَكَ»
> *"Rejoice! I bear witness that you are the one foretold by the son of Maryam — this is the same
> Nāmūs [angel of revelation] sent down upon Mūsā. You are truly a Messenger sent, and you will be
> commanded to struggle after this day of yours — and if I live to see it, I will struggle at your
> side."*

He then asked, extraordinarily: *"will they really expel me?"* — *"Yes,"* said Waraqah. *"No one has
ever brought what you have brought without being opposed. If I live to that day, I will support you
with all my strength."* He then kissed the crown of the Prophet's ﷺ head. **Waraqah died not long
after**, and *"the revelation grew faint"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:236). He left verses expressing his hope
to live to see the mission fulfilled (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:236–237).

## 8. The Pause in Revelation (*Fatrat al-Waḥy*)
After Ḥirāʾ, days passed with no further sign of Jibrīl. His grief was severe — Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄ and
al-Zuhrī both transmit that **he went repeatedly toward the mountain peaks of Thabīr and Ḥirāʾ meaning
to throw himself down**, and each time, as he reached the summit, Jibrīl appeared to him and said:

> «يَا مُحَمَّدُ، أَنْتَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ حَقًّا»
> *"O Muḥammad, you are truly the Messenger of Allāh"*

— at which his heart would still, and he would turn back. This recurred whenever the pause grew long
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:271).

**Jābir b. ʿAbdullāh** ﵁ narrates the resolution: *"I had kept retreat at Ḥirāʾ for a month; when my
retreat was over I descended and went down into the valley — and I heard a voice. I looked right, saw
nothing; looked left, saw nothing … then I raised my head, and there he was — [Jibrīl] on a throne
between heaven and earth. I was terrified and went home and said, 'cover me, cover me!' — and Allāh
sent down** *«يَا أَيُّهَا الْمُدَّثِّرُ ۝ قُمْ فَأَنذِرْ ۝ وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ ۝ وَثِيَابَكَ
فَطَهِّرْ ۝ وَالرُّجْزَ فَاهْجُرْ»* *"O you wrapped in your cloak! Arise and warn! Your Lord, magnify!
Your garments, purify! And uncleanliness, avoid!"* (74:1–5) — after that, revelation came in strength
and did not cease again"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:271–272).

### How Long Did the Pause Last?
A report via al-Shaʿbī gives **three years**, corresponding to the disputed Isrāfīl-pairing account
above; al-Wāqidī rejects it. **Ibn Kathīr** favours *"close to two years, or two and a half"* as the
apparent (*ẓāhir*) position, cautioning that this need not contradict Jibrīl's own initial visit with
*Iqraʾ* — the pause being a later, separate interval before *al-Muddaththir* came
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:272–273).

### Sūrat al-Ḍuḥā — Not What Some Have Claimed
Two popular explanations for the occasion of **Sūrat al-Ḍuḥā** (93) are addressed and set aside: that a
woman mocked him for two or three nights' absence from night prayer during an illness, taunting that
*"your shayṭān has abandoned you"* (this account itself Ibn Ḥajar notes is not firmly established); and
a separate, weaker report blaming Jibrīl's delay on a puppy that had wandered unnoticed beneath his
bed. Al-Ṣāliḥī states plainly that whatever conflicts with the authenticated report in
*al-Ṣaḥīḥ* — via **Jundub b. Sufyān al-Bajalī** ﵁, on his illness of two or three nights — **is not
established** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:275–276).

## 9. What Followed
From here, the concealed call to Islam began — see **[[al-Daʿwah al-Sirriyyah wal-Jahriyyah]]** for
the earliest believers and the years at **[[Dār al-Arqam]]**.
