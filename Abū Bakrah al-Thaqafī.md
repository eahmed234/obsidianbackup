# Abū Bakrah al-Thaqafī (أبو بكرة نفيع بن الحارث الثقفي)
*The Freed Slave of the Prophet ﷺ — The Descender from the Ramparts of al-Ṭāʾif*

## 1. Lineage & Background
His name was **Nufayʿ b. al-Ḥārith**, or **Nufayʿ b. Masrūḥ** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369). His mother was **Sumayyah** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369). He was widely known across Islamic scholarship by his celebrated *kunyah* **Abū Bakrah** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369).

When the Umayyad state under Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān claimed his half-brother Ziyād as a legitimate son of Abū Sufyān, Abū Bakrah adamantly disassociated himself from any lineage claims, proudly maintaining his bond to the Prophet ﷺ:
> «أَنَا مَوْلَى رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ، فَإِنْ أَبَى النَّاسُ إِلَّا أَنْ يَنْسُبُونِي فَأَنَا نُفَيْعُ بْنُ مَسْرُوحٍ»
> *"I am the freedman of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ! And if the people insist upon assigning me a genealogy, then I am Nufayʿ b. Masrūḥ!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369).

## 2. Emancipation at the Siege of al-Ṭāʾif (8 AH)
In Shawwāl 8 AH, during **[[Ghazwat al-Ṭāʾif]]**, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ laid siege to the formidable stone fortress of **Thaqīf** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:382). 

To demoralize the defenders and grant freedom to the oppressed, the Prophet's herald proclaimed beneath the battlements:
> «أَيُّمَا عَبْدٍ نَزَلَ مِنَ الْحِصْنِ وَخَرَجَ إِلَيْنَا فَهُوَ حُرٌّ!»
> *"Whosoever among the enslaved servants descends from the ramparts of the fortress and comes out to us is unconditionally a free man!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:384).

Hearing this proclamation, Nufayʿ tied a rope to a water-wheel pulley (*bakrah*), climbed over the high stone wall, and descended smoothly down the sheer face of the citadel into the Muslim lines (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:384). 

He embraced Islam before the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, who immediately emancipated him from bondage and affectionately gave him the permanent agnomen:
> «أَنْتَ أَبُو بَكْرَةَ!»
> *"You are Abū Bakrah (Father of the Pulley)!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:384).

He was counted among the approximately twenty-three servants who attained their freedom through that prophetic decree (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:384). When Thaqīf later accepted Islam and requested that their former slaves be returned to servitude, the Prophet ﷺ firmly refused, declaring: *«They are the freedmen of Allāh and the freedmen of His Messenger!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:384).

## 3. Settlement in Basra & Strict Neutrality
During the Caliphate of [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]] ﵁, Abū Bakrah migrated to Iraq and settled in the newly established garrison city of **Basra**, where he constructed a large residence and raised a distinguished, scholarly family (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369).

When civil strife (*al-Fitnah*) erupted during the later years of the early Caliphate, Abū Bakrah adhered to a stance of uncompromising neutrality. Remembering the prophetic warnings against intra-Muslim combat, he refused to bear arms at either the Battle of the Camel or the Battle of Ṣiffīn, retiring into devout seclusion and counseling believers to avoid bloodshed (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369).

He was a prolific narrator of Prophetic ḥadīth, preserved in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, and all major collections, transmitted through his sons ʿUbaydullāh, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, and Muslim, as well as masters such as al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Ibn Sīrīn, and Abū ʿUthmān al-Nahdī (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369).

## 4. Death & Burial
Abū Bakrah lived in Basra until his passing in the year fifty-one AH (671 CE) (or fifty-two AH) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:409; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:369). The senior companion **[[Abū Barzah al-Aslamī]]** ﵁ led the funeral prayer over him, and he was buried in Basra (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:369).
