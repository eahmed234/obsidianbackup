# ʿĀm al-Ḥuzn (عام الحزن)
*The Year of Sorrow: The Deaths of Abū Ṭālib and Khadījah, and the Persecution That Followed*

## 1. The Dating
Ibn Kathīr holds that Abū Ṭālib died **before** Khadījah ﵂, both **in the same year, three years
before the Hijrah**. Ṣāʿid's *al-Fuṣūṣ* dates Abū Ṭālib's death to **twenty-eight days after** Banū
Hāshim left the Shiʿb; Ibn Ḥazm gives **mid-Shawwāl**. Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām and Thaʿlabah b. Ṣuʿayr report
that only **a month and five days** separated the two deaths; al-Ḥākim gives **three days**
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:428).

## 2. Quraysh's Last Approach to Abū Ṭālib
As Abū Ṭālib's final illness became known, Quraysh — alarmed that **Ḥamzah** and **ʿUmar** had
accepted Islam and that word of Muḥammad ﷺ had spread through every clan — sent their chiefs to him:
**ʿUtbah and Shaybah, sons of Rabīʿah**, **[[Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)|Abū Jahl]]**, **Umayyah b.
Khalaf**, and **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]**, among other nobles. They asked him to summon his nephew and
broker a truce — each side to leave the other's religion alone. The Prophet ﷺ was sent for and sat
near his uncle; fearing he might yield ground to Abū Ṭālib directly, Abū Jahl interrupted, and the
Prophet ﷺ instead offered them **one word** by which, he said, *"you would rule the Arabs and the
non-Arabs would pay you tribute"* — meaning *"lā ilāha illā Allāh."* They refused in astonishment, and
Allāh revealed the opening of **Sūrat Ṣād** concerning them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:428–429).

## 3. The Deathbed Plea
Abū Ṭālib remarked to the Prophet ﷺ, *"By Allāh, nephew, I have not seen you ask them for something
so remote [before]"* — meaning the request seemed to him a small thing to refuse. Sensing hope, the
Prophet ﷺ pressed him: *"Uncle, only say it, and I will plead for you by it on the Day of Judgement."*
Abū Ṭālib answered: *"Were it not for the fear of reproach upon you and your father's sons after me,
and that Quraysh would think I said it only from terror of death, I would say it — solely to gladden
you"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:429).

Ibn al-Kalbī transmits that, as death came upon him, Abū Ṭālib gathered the nobles of Quraysh and
charged them: *"O assembly of Quraysh, you are the elect of Allāh among His creation… I charge you to
hold this Kaʿbah in reverence… maintain your ties of kinship and do not sever them… and I charge you
well concerning Muḥammad, for he is the trustworthy one of Quraysh and the truthful one of the
Arabs — he combines everything I have charged you with. By Allāh, it is as if I see the destitute of
the Arabs and the oppressed among mankind, from every quarter, answering his call, believing his word,
and magnifying his cause, until through them the great men of Quraysh are brought low…"* (*Subul
al-Hudā*, 2:429).

## 4. He Died Without the Word
Allāh revealed concerning him:

> «إِنَّكَ لَا تَهْدِي مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يَهْدِي مَنْ يَشَاءُ»
> *"Indeed, you do not guide whom you like, but Allāh guides whom He wills"* (Qurʾān 28:56)
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:430).

**Al-ʿAbbās** ﵁ asked the Prophet ﷺ whether his protection and championing of the Prophet ﷺ would
benefit Abū Ṭālib at all. The Prophet ﷺ answered: *"Yes — I found him in the depths of the Fire, and
brought him out to its shallowest part (*ḍaḥḍaḥ*)"* — in another wording, *"had it not been for me, he
would be in the Fire's lowest depth."* Elsewhere the Prophet ﷺ described this mercy in full: *"Perhaps
my intercession will benefit him, so that he is placed in a shallow part of the Fire reaching his
ankles, from which his brain boils"* — and, by a further wording, named him explicitly: *"the least
punished of the people of the Fire is Abū Ṭālib, wearing two sandals [of fire] from which his brain
boils"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:430).

> [!NOTE] The Book's Own Verdict
> Al-Ṣāliḥī states plainly that these authentic ḥadīths refute a report attributed to al-ʿAbbās that
> Abū Ṭālib spoke the testimony of faith, and equally refutes the claim — which he attributes to *"some
> extremist Rāfiḍah"* and which he says Ibn Ḥajar rejects in *al-Iṣābah* on the strength of weak
> reports — that Abū Ṭālib accepted Islam. Al-Ṣāliḥī also transmits **ʿAlī** ﵁'s own report: coming to
> the Prophet ﷺ to say *"your erring uncle has died,"* he was told simply, *"Go and bury him,"* and
> afterward, *"Wash yourself"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:430–431). This is the same position recorded in
> **[[Abū Ṭālib]]**'s own note.

## 5. The Death of Khadījah
Reports vary on the interval: ʿUrwah places her death **before** the Prophet's ﷺ own departure for
Madīnah; al-Balādhurī, **about two years** before the Hijrah (calling the report of five years a
mistake). Muḥammad b. ʿUmar al-Aslamī gives **the tenth of Ramaḍān**, at the age of **sixty-five**.
Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām dates her death to **the tenth year of the mission, after Banū Hāshim's exit from the
Shiʿb**; she was buried at **al-Ḥajūn**, and the Prophet ﷺ descended into her grave himself — *"the
funeral prayer had not yet been legislated."* ʿĀʾishah ﵂ herself is quoted: *"Khadījah died before the
prayer was made obligatory."* Al-Ṣāliḥī closes: *"She was a truthful counsellor to the Prophet ﷺ in
Islam, and he found repose in her; in the Jāhiliyyah she was called al-Ṭāhirah (the Pure)"* — her
fuller biography and virtues are reserved for the chapters on the Prophet's ﷺ wives (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:434). See **[[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid]]** ﵂.

## 6. Quraysh's Escalation After Abū Ṭālib's Death
Ibn Isḥāq states plainly: *"When Abū Ṭālib died, Quraysh inflicted upon the Prophet ﷺ what they had
not dared attempt in his lifetime."* A foolish man of Quraysh threw dust on the Prophet's ﷺ head; one
of his daughters wept as she washed it away, and the Prophet ﷺ told her, *"Do not weep, daughter, for
Allāh will protect your father,"* adding, *"Quraysh never inflicted on me anything I hated, until Abū
Ṭālib died."* Abū Hurayrah ﵁ transmits that they turned openly hostile to his face the moment Abū
Ṭālib died, and the Prophet ﷺ said, *"O uncle, how quickly I have felt your loss"* (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:435).

**Abū Lahab** briefly offered his own protection — *"Go on as you intended; by al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā,
none shall reach you while I live"* — until **ʿUqbah b. Abī Muʿayṭ** and **Abū Jahl** came to him and
asked the Prophet ﷺ, through him, where his own father ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib had gone after death. Told
*"with his people,"* they twisted this into a claim the Prophet ﷺ had placed ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib in the
Fire — and when confronted, the Prophet ﷺ confirmed plainly: *"Yes; and whoever dies as ʿAbd
al-Muṭṭalib died enters the Fire."* Abū Lahab withdrew his protection on the spot: *"I will be your
enemy forever, since you claim ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib is in the Fire"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:436).

Ibn Isḥāq names those who now openly molested the Prophet ﷺ in his own home as neighbours: **Abū
Lahab**, **al-Ḥakam b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ b. Umayyah**, **ʿUqbah b. Abī Muʿayṭ**, **ʿAdī b. al-Ḥamrāʾ**, and
**Ibn al-Aṣdāʾ al-Hudhalī** — none of whom, save al-Ḥakam, ever accepted Islam. They would throw a
sheep's entrails upon him as he prayed, or into his cooking-pot when it was set out, until he took to
using a stone as a screen while praying. ʿUrwah relates, on ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ's own authority, the worst
he ever witnessed: while the Prophet ﷺ prayed in the shelter of the Kaʿbah, **ʿUqbah b. Abī Muʿayṭ**
came and wound his own garment around the Prophet's ﷺ neck, choking him violently, until **Abū Bakr**
seized ʿUqbah by the shoulder, threw him off, and cried:

> «أَتَقْتُلُونَ رَجُلًا أَنْ يَقُولَ رَبِّيَ اللَّهُ وَقَدْ جَاءَكُمْ بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ»
> *"Would you kill a man for saying, 'My Lord is Allāh,' when he has brought you clear proofs from your
> Lord?"* (Qurʾān 40:28) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:435–436).

## 7. What Follows
With his uncle's protection gone and Khadījah's counsel lost, the Prophet ﷺ sought support outside
Makkah at **[[Riḥlat al-Ṭāʾif]]**, and was granted the consolation of **[[al-Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj]]**.
