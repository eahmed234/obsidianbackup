# Ghazwat Banī Liḥyān (غزوة بني لحيان)
*The Punitive March to Baṭn Ghurān & ʿUsfān — Avenging the Martyrs of al-Rajīʿ and the Deterrence of Quraysh*

## 1. Strategic Context & Provocation
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ grieved with profound intensity over the tragic martyrdom and betrayal of the reconnaissance party of al-Rajīʿ, where his noble Companions **[[ʿĀṣim b. Thābit]]** ﵁, **[[Khubayb b. ʿAdī]]** ﵁, and their comrades were ambushed in cold blood by the bedouin tribe of Banū Liḥyān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). Banū Liḥyān belonged to the ferocious clan of **Hudhayl** (Banū Liḥyān b. Hudhayl b. Mudrikah b. Ilyās b. Muḍar), dwelling in the rugged wilderness bordering the sacred territory of Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:31).

For two years following the incident, geopolitical exigencies—including the Confederate Siege of Medina (**[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**) and the subjection of **[[Ghazwat Banī Qurayẓah|Banū Qurayẓah]]**—precluded a distant southward expedition (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). Once internal security in Medina was firmly consolidated in 6 AH, the Prophet ﷺ mobilized a rapid punitive force to hold Banū Liḥyān accountable and demonstrate that Muslim blood could never be spilled with impunity (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

## 2. Feigned Route & Swift March
To catch the vigilant desert tribe off guard (*li-yuṣība min al-qawmi ghirrah*), the Prophet ﷺ employed operational deception (*tawriyah*). He openly proclaimed that his target was Syria to the north, marching his men out of Medina and establishing camp at **al-Jurf** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). 

He mobilized a light, highly mobile strike force of two hundred (200) seasoned warriors accompanied by twenty (20) warhorses (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). He appointed **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Umm Maktūm]]** ﵁ (or Numaylah b. ʿAbdillāh al-Laythī) as deputy governor over Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

Once in motion, the Prophet ﷺ abruptly turned westward and southward, navigating complex desert tracks across Mount Ghurāb, Muḥayṣ, and al-Batrāʾ, swinging left through the ravine of Yayan, and traversing Ṣukhayrāt al-Thumām before joining the coastal pilgrim route at **al-Sayyālah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). The force accelerated at full march until they reached **Baṭn Ghurān**—a desolate valley five miles north of **ʿUsfān** where the blood of the martyrs of al-Rajīʿ had been spilled (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

## 3. Halting at al-Rajīʿ & Supplication for the Martyrs
Arriving at the very rocks where ʿĀṣim b. Thābit and his comrades had made their heroic last stand, the Prophet ﷺ halted the entire army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). He stood over the battlefield, invoked divine mercy upon the souls of the martyrs, and supplicated extensively for them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

Alerted to the rapid approach of the Muslim cavalry, the scouts of Banū Liḥyān sounded the alarm. The tribesmen evacuated their villages and fled into the rugged, inaccessible peaks of the surrounding mountains (*ruʾūs al-jibāl*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). The Prophet ﷺ encamped in the valley for one or two days, dispatching search detachments (*sarāyā*) into every mountain pass, but the enemy remained completely concealed in the cliffs (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

## 4. Demonstration of Strength at ʿUsfān & Kurāʿ al-Ghamīm
Having denied Banū Liḥyān any operational footing and demonstrated that the Muslim army could penetrate their territorial sanctuary at will, the Prophet ﷺ resolved to deliver a powerful psychological shock to Quraysh in Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). He declared to his companions:
> «لَوْ أَنَّا هَبَطْنَا عُسْفَانَ لَرَأَى أَهْلُ مَكَّةَ أَنَّا قَدْ جِئْنَا مَكَّةَ»
> *"If we descend into ʿUsfān, the people of Makkah will see that we have marched to the very gates of Makkah!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

The Prophet ﷺ marched his force southward and took up position at **ʿUsfān** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). From there, he dispatched **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ at the head of a cavalry vanguard of ten horsemen to ride forward into **Kurāʿ al-Ghamīm**—the northern boundary of the Makkan outer perimeter (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). 

The sudden arrival of Muslim cavalry at Kurāʿ al-Ghamīm struck immense terror into Quraysh, demonstrating that the Prophet ﷺ held undisputed military initiative throughout the Ḥijāz. Having achieved this psychological deterrence, the vanguard rejoined the Prophet ﷺ at ʿUsfān, and the army began its orderly return to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

## 5. The Sunnah of Return Supplication (*Duʿāʾ al-Qufūl*)
During the return march from this campaign, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ instituted the foundational Sunnah invocation recited upon returning from travel and military campaigns (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30). **[[Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh]]** ﵄ narrated:
> «آئِبُونَ تَائِبُونَ، إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى، لِرَبِّنَا حَامِدُونَ، لِرَبِّنَا عَابِدُونَ، أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنْ وَعْثَاءِ السَّفَرِ، وَكَآبَةِ الْمُنْقَلَبِ، وَسُوءِ الْمَنْظَرِ فِي الْأَهْلِ وَالْمَالِ»
> *"We return, repentant—if Allāh wills—to our Lord praising, to our Lord worshiping! I seek refuge in Allāh from the hardships of travel, the sadness of return, and an unfortunate sight in family and wealth."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).
Muḥammad b. ʿUmar al-Wāqidī affirmed: *“This was the very first occasion on which the Prophet ﷺ uttered this renowned supplication.”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

The Prophet's ﷺ absence from Medina lasted fourteen (14) nights (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).

## 6. Classical Commemorative Poetry
The renowned poet of Islam **[[Kaʿb b. Mālik]]** ﵁ commemorated the panic of Banū Liḥyān and the might of the Prophetic army in verse (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30):
> «لَوْ أَنَّ بَنِي لِحْيَانَ كَانُوا تَنَاظَرُوا ... لَقُوا عُصَبًا فِي دَارِهِمْ ذَاتَ مَصْدَقِ»
> «لَقُوا سُرْعَانَ يَمْلَأُ السَّرْبَ رَوْعُهُ ... أَمَامَ طَحُونٍ كَالْمَجَرَّةِ فَيْلَقِ»
> «وَلَكِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا وَبَارًا تَتَبَّعَتْ ... شِعَابَ حِجَانٍ غَيْرِ ذِي مُتَنَفَّقِ»
> *"Had Banū Liḥyān dared to stand and face us, they would have met formidable cohorts in their homeland of steadfast resolve;*
> *They would have met an advance vanguard whose awe fills the horizon, ahead of a crushing battalion like the vast Milky Way!*
> *Instead, they were like cowardly rock hyraxes, fleeing into the twisting, dead-end ravines of the mountains!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:30).
