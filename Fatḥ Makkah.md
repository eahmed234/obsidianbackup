# Fatḥ Makkah (فتح مكة)
*The conquest of Makkah in Ramaḍān of the year 8*

The **Conquest of Makkah**, which al-Ṣāliḥī calls *al-Fatḥ al-Aʿẓam*, "the Greatest Conquest", took place in Ramaḍān of the eighth year after the Hijrah.[^s5.200][^bukh4275] Its cause was a night raid by Banū Bakr, helped in secret by men of [[Quraysh]], on [[Banū Khuzāʿah]], who had entered into the Prophet's ﷺ side of the [[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah|truce of al-Ḥudaybiyah]].[^s5.200][^s5.201] When Khuzāʿah came to Medina asking for help, the Prophet ﷺ promised it; [[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb|Abū Sufyān]] came to renew the truce and went home with nothing.[^s5.203][^s5.206][^s5.207] The Prophet ﷺ prepared in secret, asking Allāh to keep the news from Quraysh, and marched with ten thousand men.[^s5.209][^s5.266] At Marr al-Ẓahrān Abū Sufyān was brought to him by [[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib|al-ʿAbbās]] and became a Muslim, and the Prophet ﷺ declared that whoever entered Abū Sufyān's house, shut his own door or entered the Mosque was safe.[^s5.214][^s5.217][^s5.218] The army entered Makkah from several sides with orders to fight only those who fought them; resistance came at al-Khandamah, where [[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]'s force was attacked and drove the attackers off.[^s5.227][^s5.228] The Prophet ﷺ circled the Kaʿbah, brought down the idols around it, prayed inside it, and, standing at its door, told Quraysh: «اذهبوا فأنتم الطّلقاء», *"Go, for you are free."*[^s5.234][^s5.239][^s5.242] He stayed in Makkah about nineteen days.[^s5.261][^s5.273]

## The alliance of Khuzāʿah

The feud between Khuzāʿah and Banū Bakr went back to the Jāhiliyyah. Khuzāʿah had killed and robbed a merchant of Banū al-Ḥaḍramī, Mālik b. ʿAbbād, who was under the protection of al-Aswad b. Razn; Banū al-Dīl killed a man of Khuzāʿah in return; and Khuzāʿah then killed three sons of al-Aswad b. Razn—Dhuʾayb, Salmā and Kulthūm—at ʿArafah by the boundary stones of the sanctuary. Islam came between them and held the two sides apart, though the enmity stayed.[^s5.200]

When the truce of al-Ḥudaybiyah was made, one of its terms let any tribe join either side: whoever wished could enter the Prophet's ﷺ compact, and whoever wished could enter that of Quraysh. Khuzāʿah entered the Prophet's ﷺ compact.[^s5.200] They had been allies of [[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib|ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib b. Hāshim]], and they brought the Prophet ﷺ the document of that alliance, which [[Ubayy b. Kaʿb]] read to him. The Prophet ﷺ told them that every alliance of the Jāhiliyyah was only made stronger by Islam, but that there was to be no new alliance in Islam.[^s5.200]

## The raid at al-Watīr

In Shaʿbān, twenty-two months after the truce, Banū Nufāthah and Banū Bakr asked the nobles of Quraysh to help them with men and weapons against Khuzāʿah, reminding them of the men Khuzāʿah had killed among them. Quraysh agreed readily. Abū Sufyān was not consulted and did not know of it; it is also said that they put it to him and he refused. Quraysh gave the help in secret so that Khuzāʿah would not be on their guard.[^s5.201]

They met at al-Watīr, a place below Makkah where Khuzāʿah had their homes. Among them, disguised and veiled, were leading men of Quraysh: [[Ṣafwān b. Umayyah]], [[ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl]], Ḥuwayṭib b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, Shaybah b. ʿUthmān and Mikraz b. Ḥafṣ, with their slaves; the head of Banū Bakr was Nawfal b. Muʿāwiyah al-Duʾalī. Several of these men later became Muslims.[^s5.201] They fell on Khuzāʿah by night, while they felt safe because of the truce; most of those they found were children, women and weak men. They kept killing them as far as the boundary stones of the sanctuary. When Nawfal's men warned him that he had entered the sanctuary, he answered that he had no god that day, and urged Banū Bakr to take their revenge.[^s5.201] The survivors took refuge in the houses of [[Budayl b. Warqāʾ al-Khuzāʿī|Budayl b. Warqāʾ]] and of a *mawlā* of theirs named Rāfiʿ.[^s5.201]

[[Suhayl b. ʿAmr]] told Nawfal that they had done enough and would not follow him in killing the rest, and Nawfal let them go. Quraysh then regretted what they had done and knew it was a breach of their covenant with the Prophet ﷺ; [[al-Ḥārith b. Hishām]] and ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Rabīʿah went to Ṣafwān, Suhayl and ʿIkrimah and blamed them for it.[^s5.201]

## Khuzāʿah's appeal

Al-Wāqidī reports that on the morning after the raid the Prophet ﷺ told [[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr|ʿĀʾishah]] that something had happened to Khuzāʿah. When she asked whether Quraysh would really dare to break the truce, he said that they would break it for a matter Allāh intended, and that it would be for the good.[^s5.201][^s5.202] In a report of al-Ṭabarānī, [[Maymūnah bint al-Ḥārith|Maymūnah]] heard him say in the place where he made his ablutions, *"Labbayk"* three times and *"You are helped"* three times. He told her it was the poet of Banū Kaʿb calling on him for help; three days later she heard the poet recite his verses after the dawn prayer.[^s5.202]

[[ʿAmr b. Sālim al-Khuzāʿī]] came to Medina with forty riders of Khuzāʿah and told the Prophet ﷺ, as he sat in the Mosque among the people, what had happened to them and how Quraysh had helped their enemies. Then ʿAmr stood and recited:[^s5.202]

> «يا رب إنّي ناشد محمّدا ... حلف أبينا وأبيه الأتلدا»
> *"O Lord, I call on Muḥammad by the old alliance of our father and his."*

His verses go on to say that Quraysh had broken the Prophet's ﷺ covenant, and that they had fallen on Khuzāʿah at al-Watīr while they slept and killed them as they bowed and prostrated.[^s5.202][^s5.203] The Prophet ﷺ answered, «نصرت يا عمرو بن سالم», *"You are helped, ʿAmr b. Sālim."* A cloud passed over and thundered, and he said that it was breaking with the help of Banū Kaʿb.[^s5.203] ʿĀʾishah said she had not seen him so angry for a long time.[^s5.203] In al-Wāqidī's report from Ibn ʿAbbās he rose dragging his cloak and said:[^s5.203]

> «لا نصرت إن لم أنصر بني كعب ممّا أنصر منه نفسي»
> *"May I not be helped if I do not help Banū Kaʿb as I would help myself."*

He told ʿAmr and his companions to go back and scatter through the valleys.[^s5.203] They also told him that Anas b. Zunaym had lampooned him, and he declared his blood forfeit.[^s5.204]

### What Quraysh were offered

A further report, which al-Ṣāliḥī gives under the heading "what is said", has Quraysh regretting their help to Banū Nufāthah. ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Sarḥ, then an apostate living among them, predicted that the Prophet ﷺ would not attack without first giving them a choice: to pay the blood-money for the twenty-three dead of Khuzāʿah, to renounce their alliance with Banū Nufāthah, or to have the truce thrown back at them openly.[^s5.204] In Ibn ʿUmar's version the Prophet ﷺ did send a man named Ḍamrah with these three choices, and Qurẓah b. ʿAbd ʿAmr answered for Quraysh that they would neither pay nor renounce Nufāthah, but would accept the end of the truce. Quraysh then regretted turning his envoy away and sent Abū Sufyān.[^s5.204][^s5.205]

## Abū Sufyān in Medina

The Prophet ﷺ told his Companions that Abū Sufyān would come asking to renew the covenant and lengthen the truce, and would go home angry.[^s5.205] In Makkah, al-Ḥārith b. Hishām and ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Rabīʿah urged Abū Sufyān that the matter had to be mended. He told them of a dream his wife [[Hind bint ʿUtbah]] had had: blood came flowing from al-Ḥajūn and stood for a while at al-Khandamah, then vanished. He said he had neither been consulted nor wanted what had been done, but that the blame would fall on him, and he set out with a *mawlā* of his.[^s5.205] At ʿUsfān he met Budayl b. Warqāʾ, who said he had only been settling a dispute along the coast; but when Budayl had gone, Abū Sufyān broke open his camels' dung, found the stones of Medina dates in it, and swore that they had been to Muḥammad.[^s5.205][^s5.206]

In Medina he went first to his daughter [[Umm Ḥabībah]], the wife of the Prophet ﷺ. When he went to sit on the Prophet's ﷺ bedding she folded it away from him; asked whether she thought the bedding too good for him or him too good for it, she told him it was the bedding of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and that he was an unclean idolater.[^s5.206] He then asked the Prophet ﷺ in the Mosque to strengthen the covenant and lengthen the term, saying he had been absent at al-Ḥudaybiyah. The Prophet ﷺ asked whether anything had happened on their side; Abū Sufyān said Allāh forbid, they kept to their truce. *"Then we keep to our term and our truce,"* the Prophet ﷺ answered, and when Abū Sufyān repeated his request he made no reply.[^s5.206]

He went in turn to [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq|Abū Bakr]], [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb|ʿUmar]], [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān|ʿUthmān]], [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib|ʿAlī]] and [[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]], and to the leading men of Quraysh and the Anṣār. Each told him that his protection lay within the protection of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and that no one could grant protection against him. ʿUmar answered that if he had nothing but ants he would fight them with those.[^s5.206][^s5.207] He asked [[Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ|Fāṭimah]], with the young [[al-Ḥasan b. ʿAlī|al-Ḥasan]] crawling in front of her, to grant protection, or to have her son do it; she refused.[^s5.207] At last ʿAlī told him that he knew of nothing that would help him, but that he was the chief of Banū Kinānah and might stand up and declare protection between the people himself. Abū Sufyān did so in the Mosque and told the Prophet ﷺ, who said only, *"You say that, Abū Ḥanẓalah?"* He rode home.[^s5.207]

He had been away so long that Quraysh suspected him of having secretly become a Muslim. Hind kicked him in the chest when she heard his news. The next morning he shaved his head at Isāf and Nāʾilah, sacrificed to them and wiped their heads with the blood, to clear himself of the suspicion. When he told Quraysh what he had done, they said Muḥammad had merely played with him, and that his declaration of protection was worthless.[^s5.207][^s5.208]

## Preparations

In a report of Ibn Abī Shaybah, the Prophet ﷺ called Abū Bakr and then ʿUmar and spoke with each of them privately about the campaign. Abū Bakr spoke for leniency, reminding him that they were his own people; ʿUmar spoke against Quraysh, recalling everything they had said of him. The Prophet ﷺ compared Abū Bakr to Ibrāhīm, who was gentler for Allāh than soft oil, and ʿUmar to Nūḥ, who was harder for Allāh than stone, and said that the decision was ʿUmar's, telling the people to make ready.[^s5.208]

He told ʿĀʾishah to prepare his things and keep it hidden, and prayed: *"O Allāh, take hold of their hearing and their sight, so that they see us only suddenly and hear of us only unexpectedly."* He set men to guard the mountain passes, and ʿUmar went round them telling them to turn back anyone they did not know.[^s5.209] To mislead anyone watching, he sent [[Abū Qatādah al-Anṣārī|Abū Qatādah b. Rabʿī]] to Baṭn Iḍam, so that people would think he was heading that way. Then he made it known that he was marching on Quraysh, and sent word to the Bedouin and the Muslims around Medina that whoever believed in Allāh and the Last Day should be in Medina in Ramaḍān.[^s5.211] [[Ḥassān b. Thābit]] urged the people on with verses mourning the men of Khuzāʿah, killed by men who had not drawn their own swords.[^s5.211][^s5.212]

### The letter of Ḥāṭib

When the Prophet ﷺ had resolved on the march, [[Ḥāṭib b. Abī Baltaʿah]] wrote to Quraysh to warn them, and gave the letter to a woman for a fee. The reports differ over who she was: a woman of Muzaynah, according to Muḥammad b. Jaʿfar; Kanūd, according to al-Wāqidī; or Sārah, a *mawlāh* of Banū al-Muṭṭalib, according to others whom Ibn Isḥāq heard. She hid the letter in her hair, twisting her braids over it, and avoided the main road.[^s5.209] The wording of the letter is reported in several ways.[^s5.209][^s5.210]

News of it came to the Prophet ﷺ from heaven. He sent ʿAlī and [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām|al-Zubayr]]—with [[al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr|al-Miqdād b. al-Aswad]] as a third in Abū Rāfiʿ's report from ʿAlī, and Abū Marthad in Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī's—telling them to go to Rawḍat Khākh, where they would find a woman carrying a letter.[^s5.210][^s5.266] When they searched her baggage and found nothing, ʿAlī swore that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had not lied and told her she would bring out the letter or be searched; she undid her braids and handed it over.[^s5.210]

Ḥāṭib told the Prophet ﷺ that he had not changed his faith: he had no clan in Makkah to protect his family there, as the other Muhājirūn had, and he had wanted a favour among Quraysh to protect his kin. The Prophet ﷺ said that he had told the truth. ʿUmar asked to strike off his head as a hypocrite, and the Prophet ﷺ answered that ʿUmar could not know that Allāh had not looked upon the people of Badr and said:[^s5.210][^s5.211]

> «اعملوا ما شئتم فقد غفرت لكم»
> *"Do what you will, for I have forgiven you."*

ʿUmar's eyes filled with tears. Allāh revealed the opening verses of [[Sūrat al-Mumtaḥanah]], forbidding the believers to take His enemies and theirs as allies.[^s5.211]

## The march

The Prophet ﷺ left [[Abū Ruhm al-Ghifārī|Abū Ruhm Kulthūm b. Ḥuṣayn al-Ghifārī]] in charge of Medina. It is also said that he left [[ʿAbdullāh b. Umm Maktūm|Ibn Umm Maktūm]], as Ibn Saʿd and al-Balādhurī give it; al-Ṣāliḥī calls the first the sound view.[^s5.212]

According to al-Wāqidī he set out on a Wednesday after the afternoon prayer, on the tenth of Ramaḍān, with the Muhājirūn, the Anṣār and groups of the Arabs; his crier announced that whoever wished to fast could fast and whoever wished to break the fast could break it. He sent al-Zubayr ahead with two hundred men.[^s5.212] At al-ʿArj, while fasting, he poured water over his head because of the thirst and heat.[^s5.212] Between al-ʿArj and al-Ṭalūb he saw a bitch growling over the puppies she was suckling, and set Jumayl b. Surāqah to stand by her so that no one in the army would disturb her or them.[^s5.212] A spy of Hawāzin was brought in and told him that Hawāzin were gathering against him; he said, *"Allāh is sufficient for us, and He is the best disposer of affairs,"* and had Khālid hold the man so that he could not give warning.[^s5.212] At Qudayd [[Banū Sulaym]] joined him, and there he tied the banners and gave them out to the tribes.[^s5.212]

He had fasted, and the people with him, until he reached al-Kadīd, the water between Qudayd and ʿUsfān. Told that the fast was hard on the people and that they were watching what he did, he called for a vessel after the afternoon prayer, raised it on his mount so that they could see it, and drank. Of those who still fasted he said, *"Those are the disobedient."* He did not fast again until the month was over.[^bukh4275][^s5.213] Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī reports that he first told them that breaking the fast would make them stronger now that they were close to the enemy, which was a permission, and at the next halt told them to break it, which was a command.[^s5.213] One narration places al-Kadīd between ʿUsfān and Amaj, and Jābir's report names Kurāʿ al-Ghamīm; al-Ṣāliḥī explains these as places near one another on the same journey.[^s5.213][^s5.267]

Al-ʿAbbās came to meet him as a Muslim; Ibn Hishām says they met at [[al-Juḥfah]], and al-ʿAbbās sent his baggage on to Medina and went on with the army.[^s5.213] [[Abū Sufyān b. al-Ḥārith]], the Prophet's ﷺ cousin, and ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Umayyah met him at Niq al-ʿUqāb.[^s5.213]

## Marr al-Ẓahrān

The army camped at Marr al-Ẓahrān at nightfall, and the Prophet ﷺ had his men light ten thousand fires; he put ʿUmar in charge of the guard. Quraysh had heard nothing at all of the march.[^s5.214] In ʿUrwah's report in al-Bukhārī, Abū Sufyān, [[Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām]] and Budayl b. Warqāʾ came out looking for news and reached Marr al-Ẓahrān to find fires like the fires of ʿArafah. Budayl said they were the fires of Banū ʿAmr—Khuzāʿah—and Abū Sufyān answered that ʿAmr were too few for that. Men of the Prophet's ﷺ guard caught them and brought them to him, and Abū Sufyān became a Muslim.[^bukh4280][^s5.214]

Al-ʿAbbās tells the story at length in Ibn ʿAbbās's report. Fearing that Quraysh would be destroyed if the Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah by force before they came to ask for safety, he rode out on the Prophet's ﷺ grey mule to find someone who could carry a warning to Makkah. At al-Arāk he heard Abū Sufyān and Budayl arguing over the fires, called out to Abū Sufyān, and told him that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was there with ten thousand men. He took Abū Sufyān up behind him to ask for his safety. At each fire the Muslims let them pass on seeing the Prophet's ﷺ mule, until they reached ʿUmar's fire. ʿUmar ran to the Prophet's ﷺ tent to ask leave to kill the enemy of Allāh now that he was in their hands with no covenant, but the mule got there first, and al-ʿAbbās told the Prophet ﷺ that he had given Abū Sufyān his protection.[^s5.215][^s5.216] In Ibn ʿAbbās's report Abū Sufyān's two companions went back; Ibn ʿUqbah and al-Wāqidī, in another place, say al-ʿAbbās brought all three to the Prophet ﷺ.[^s5.216]

In that account the three spent most of the night with the Prophet ﷺ. Budayl and Ḥakīm bore witness, but Abū Sufyān said there was still something in his heart about the Prophet's ﷺ messengership.[^s5.216] The Prophet ﷺ told al-ʿAbbās to take him to his tent and bring him back in the morning.[^s5.217] At dawn Abū Sufyān was alarmed by the call to prayer, and when he watched the army bow and prostrate behind the Prophet ﷺ he said that neither Persia nor Byzantium were as obedient to their kings. When he told al-ʿAbbās that his nephew's kingdom had become great, al-ʿAbbās answered:[^s5.217]

> «إنه ليس بملك، ولكنها النبوة»
> *"It is not kingship; it is prophethood."*

The Prophet ﷺ asked him whether the time had not come for him to know that there is no god but Allāh. Abū Sufyān answered that if there were another god with Allāh it would have helped him by now; but when asked whether he did not know that Muḥammad was the Messenger of Allāh, he said there was still something in his heart about that. Al-ʿAbbās told him to accept Islam before his head was struck off, and he bore witness.[^s5.217] Ibn ʿUqbah and al-Wāqidī, in another place, imply that he bore witness without anyone putting it to him.[^s5.217]

Abū Sufyān and Ḥakīm complained that he had brought a rabble against his own people. The Prophet ﷺ answered that they were the wrongdoers, having betrayed the covenant of al-Ḥudaybiyah and helped against Banū Kaʿb in the sanctuary. When they suggested he would have done better to turn his strength on Hawāzin, he said he hoped his Lord would give him all of it: the conquest of Makkah, the honour of Islam through it, the defeat of Hawāzin and their wealth and families as spoil.[^s5.217][^s5.218]

### The promise of safety

They asked him for an amnesty: if Quraysh kept out of the way and held back their hands, would they be safe? He said yes.[^s5.218] Al-ʿAbbās—or, in Ibn Abī Shaybah's report, Abū Bakr—told him that Abū Sufyān was a man who loved honour, and asked that he be given something.[^s5.218] The Prophet ﷺ said:

> «من دخل دار أبي سفيان فهو آمن»
> *"Whoever enters the house of Abū Sufyān is safe."*

He added that whoever entered the Mosque or shut his door was safe; Ibn ʿUqbah adds whoever entered the house of Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām. Abū Sufyān's house was in upper Makkah and Ḥakīm's in lower Makkah.[^s5.218]

The Prophet ﷺ said that night that there were four men of Quraysh in Makkah whom he held above idolatry and wished to see in Islam: [[ʿAttāb b. Asīd]], [[Jubayr b. Muṭʿim]], Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām and Suhayl b. ʿAmr. Ibn ʿAsākir reports it from ʿAṭāʾ, who thought it went back to Ibn ʿAbbās.[^s5.219]

## The review of the army

When Abū Sufyān set off to go back to his people, al-ʿAbbās feared he might abandon his Islam, and the Prophet ﷺ had him held at the narrows of the valley to watch the army pass. *"Treachery, Banū Hāshim?"* Abū Sufyān asked; al-ʿAbbās answered that the people of prophethood did not betray, and kept him there until morning.[^s5.218] The Prophet ﷺ had a crier tell every tribe to be mounted at dawn and stand with its leader by its banner, showing its arms and equipment.[^s5.219] Al-Wāqidī gives the order in which the tribes passed Abū Sufyān. Each called out "Allāhu akbar" three times as it went by, and each time Abū Sufyān asked al-ʿAbbās who they were.[^s5.219][^s5.220]

| 1 | [[Banū Sulaym]], under [[Khālid b. al-Walīd]] | 1,000; it is also said 900 | two *liwāʾ*s, borne by al-ʿAbbās b. Mirdās and Khufāf b. Nudbah; a *rāyah* borne by al-Ḥajjāj b. ʿIlāṭ |
| 2 | [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]], with Muhājirūn and men of various Arab tribes | 500 | a black *rāyah* |
| 3 | [[Banū Ghifār]] | 300 | borne by [[Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī|Abū Dharr]]; it is also said Īmāʾ b. Raḥaḍah |
| 4 | Aslam | 400 | two *liwāʾ*s, borne by [[Buraydah b. al-Ḥuṣayb]] and Nājiyah b. al-Aʿjam |
| 5 | Banū Kaʿb b. ʿAmr | 500 | borne by [[Bisr b. Sufyān al-Khuzāʿī\|Busr b. Sufyān]] |
| 6 | Muzaynah | 1,000, with 100 horses | three *liwāʾ*s, borne by al-Nuʿmān b. Muqarrin, ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. ʿAwf and Bilāl b. al-Ḥārith |
| 7 | Juhaynah | 800 | four *liwāʾ*s, borne by Abū Rawʿah Maʿbad b. Khālid, Suwayd b. Ṣakhr, Rāfiʿ b. Makīth and ʿAbdullāh b. Badr |

At Banū Bakr Abū Sufyān said they were people of ill omen, on whose account Muḥammad had come against them; al-ʿAbbās answered that Allāh had chosen good for them in it, since they were now safe and would enter Islam. At Ashjaʿ he said they had been the Arabs most bitter against Muḥammad.[^s5.220] Last came the Prophet's ﷺ own battalion, the Green Battalion of the Muhājirūn and Anṣār, with a banner for every clan of the Anṣār, so covered in iron that only their eyes could be seen. ʿUmar's voice could be heard marshalling them, telling the front to wait for the rear. It is said that there were two thousand men in mail in it.[^s5.220] The Prophet ﷺ rode al-Qaṣwāʾ between Abū Bakr and [[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]], talking with them.[^s5.220][^s5.221]

In ʿUrwah's report in al-Bukhārī the order is Ghifār, Juhaynah, Saʿd b. Hudhaym and Sulaym, then a battalion such as had never been seen: the Anṣār, under Saʿd b. ʿUbādah with the banner. Then came the smallest battalion, in which were the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and his Companions, with the Prophet's ﷺ banner carried by al-Zubayr.[^bukh4280] Al-Ṣāliḥī notes that al-Ḥumaydī's text reads "the most majestic" for "the smallest", and that "smallest" means only fewest in number.[^s5.221][^s5.267]

### "The day of slaughter"

As Saʿd b. ʿUbādah passed with the banner he called out to Abū Sufyān: «اليوم يوم الملحمة، اليوم تستحل الكعبة», *"Today is the day of slaughter; today the Kaʿbah is made lawful."*[^bukh4280] When the Prophet ﷺ came by, Abū Sufyān told him what Saʿd had said, and the Prophet ﷺ answered:[^bukh4280]

> «كذب سعد، ولكن هذا يوم يعظم الله فيه الكعبة، ويوم تكسى فيه الكعبة»
> *"Saʿd is wrong. This is a day on which Allāh will honour the Kaʿbah, a day on which the Kaʿbah will be clothed."*

In the fuller account al-Ṣāliḥī gives, Saʿd also said that Allāh had humbled Quraysh that day, and the Prophet ﷺ answered that it was the day of mercy and the day on which Allāh had honoured Quraysh.[^s5.220][^s5.221] Ibn Isḥāq says that one of the Muhājirūn, whom Ibn Hishām names as ʿUmar, warned the Prophet ﷺ that Saʿd might attack Quraysh; Ibn Ḥajar thought it unlikely to have been ʿUmar. Al-Wāqidī says it was [[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]] and ʿUthmān.[^s5.221] Ḍirār b. al-Khaṭṭāb al-Fihrī, hearing Saʿd's words, composed verses pleading with the Prophet ﷺ for the people of Makkah; in the reports of Ibn Isḥāq and of Jābir, a woman of Quraysh recited them to him.[^s5.221][^s5.222]

The Prophet ﷺ took the banner from Saʿd and gave it to his son [[Qays b. Saʿd b. ʿUbādah|Qays]], so that it did not leave Saʿd's family. Al-Wāqidī says Saʿd would not hand it over until the Prophet ﷺ sent his turban as a sign. It is also said that ʿAlī took it and carried it into Makkah, and Abū Yaʿlā reports from al-Zubayr that it was given to him, which Ibn ʿUqbah held to.[^s5.222] Ibn Ḥajar reconciles the reports: ʿAlī was sent to take it, then it was given to Qays to spare Saʿd's feelings, and then Saʿd, afraid that his son would do something the Prophet ﷺ disliked, asked that it be taken from him, and al-Zubayr received it. He cites al-Bazzār's report from Anas that Qays was in the vanguard and Saʿd asked the Prophet ﷺ to move him for that reason.[^s5.222][^s5.223]

### Abū Sufyān warns Makkah

In the report of Ibn Abī Shaybah and al-Ṭabarānī, al-ʿAbbās asked leave to go ahead to the people of Makkah and offer them safety. The Prophet ﷺ had him called back, fearing that Quraysh would do to him what Thaqīf had done to ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd; in that report al-ʿAbbās then went and called on Makkah to accept Islam and be safe.[^s5.223] Al-ʿAbbās told Abū Sufyān to go and reach his people before the Prophet ﷺ did. Abū Sufyān rode ahead into Makkah by way of Kadāʾ and shouted that Muḥammad had come with what they could not stand against, and repeated the terms of the amnesty. Hind seized him by the moustache and called on the people to kill him; he told them not to be deceived by her.[^s5.223]

## Those excluded from the amnesty

The Prophet ﷺ named a number of people who were to be killed on the day of the conquest and were not covered by the amnesty. Some were killed and others became Muslims and were pardoned.[^s5.223]

- **ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. Khaṭal**, whom the Prophet ﷺ had renamed ʿAbdullāh. He had become a Muslim and was sent to collect the *ṣadaqah* with a man of Khuzāʿah to serve him; when the man fell asleep without preparing his food, Ibn Khaṭal killed him, apostatized and fled to Makkah, where he composed lampoons against the Prophet ﷺ and had his two singing girls sing them.[^s5.223][^s5.224] On the day of the conquest he rode out armed, but turned back in terror at al-Khandamah and took refuge under the coverings of the Kaʿbah.[^s5.224] Anas reports that when the Prophet ﷺ took off his helmet he was told that Ibn Khaṭal was clinging to the coverings of the Kaʿbah, and said, *"Kill him."*[^s5.224] Ibn Ḥajar calls the report that [[Abū Barzah al-Aslamī]] killed him the soundest on the point; other reports add or name Saʿīd b. Ḥurayth, al-Zubayr, or Sharīk b. ʿAbdah al-ʿAjlānī.[^s5.268]
- **ʿAbdullāh b. Saʿd b. Abī Sarḥ**, who had become a Muslim and apostatized. ʿUthmān interceded for him on the day of the conquest; he became a Muslim again and was accepted.[^s5.224]
- **ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl**, who became a Muslim and was accepted.[^s5.224]
- **al-Ḥuwayrith b. Nuqaydir**, who had harmed the Prophet ﷺ and goaded the camel of his daughter Zaynab when she emigrated. ʿAlī killed him as he slipped out of his house to flee.[^s5.224] Ibn Hishām says he had also goaded the camel carrying Fāṭimah and Umm Kulthūm when al-ʿAbbās was taking them to Medina, throwing them to the ground.[^s5.225]
- **Miqyas b. Ṣubābah**, who had become a Muslim and taken the blood-money for his brother Hishām, killed in error by a man of the Anṣār at Dhū Qarad, and had then killed the Anṣārī and apostatized. [[Numaylah b. ʿAbdillāh al-Laythī|Numaylah b. ʿAbdullāh]] killed him on the day of the conquest.[^s5.225]
- **Habbār b. al-Aswad**, who had goaded Zaynab's camel so that she miscarried. When he learned that his blood had been declared forfeit, he declared his Islam and the Prophet ﷺ accepted it and pardoned him.[^s5.225]
- **al-Ḥuwayrith b. al-Ṭalāṭil al-Khuzāʿī**, whom ʿAlī killed, according to Abū Maʿshar.[^s5.225]
- **Kaʿb b. Zuhayr**, according to al-Ḥākim; he came later, became a Muslim and praised the Prophet ﷺ.[^s5.225]
- **[[Waḥshī b. Ḥarb]]**, who fled to al-Ṭāʾif and became a Muslim when its people did.[^s5.225]
- **Sārah**, the *mawlāh* of ʿAmr b. Hāshim b. al-Muṭṭalib, a singer in Makkah who sang Ibn Khaṭal's lampoons. Al-Ṣāliḥī identifies her as the woman found carrying Ḥāṭib's letter. She became a Muslim and lived into the caliphate of ʿUmar.[^s5.225]
- **[[Hind bint ʿUtbah]]**, who had cut open the liver of [[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib|Ḥamzah]]; she became a Muslim and was pardoned.[^s5.225]
- **Ibn Khaṭal's two singing girls**, named Fartanā and Qarībah (or Arnab). One was given safety and became a Muslim and the other was killed; Ibn Isḥāq says Fartanā became a Muslim and Qarībah was killed. Ibn Isḥāq also names an Umm Saʿd who was killed.[^s5.225][^s5.226]

## The entry into Makkah

The Muslims halted at Dhū Ṭuwā to wait for the Prophet ﷺ, who came up in his Green Battalion on al-Qaṣwāʾ.[^s5.226] Anas reports that when the people looked up at him he lowered his head to his saddle in humility, and Abū Hurayrah that his beard almost touched the middle of his saddle, in humility to Allāh at the conquest He had given; he said, *"O Allāh, the true life is the life of the Hereafter."*[^s5.226] He wore a black turban and entered without *iḥrām*; ʿAmr b. Ḥurayth saw its end hanging between his shoulders.[^s5.226] Reports differ on the colours: Abū Hurayrah says his turban, *rāyah* and *liwāʾ* were all black; Jābir that his *liwāʾ* was white; and ʿĀʾishah that the *liwāʾ* was white and the *rāyah* black, the one called al-ʿUqāb.[^s5.226][^s5.227] ʿĀʾishah reports that he entered from Kadāʾ, in upper Makkah.[^s5.226]

Seeing the women of Makkah slap the horses' faces with their veils, he smiled at Abū Bakr and asked how Ḥassān's verse went; Abū Bakr recited the lines in which Ḥassān had foretold the horses raising dust at Kadāʾ, and the Prophet ﷺ told them to enter where Ḥassān had said.[^s5.227]

### The columns

The Prophet ﷺ told al-Zubayr to enter from Kadāʾ in upper Makkah, plant his banner at al-Ḥajūn and wait there for him.[^s5.227] He put Khālid b. al-Walīd over the right wing—Aslam, Sulaym, Ghifār, Muzaynah, Juhaynah and other Arab tribes—with orders to enter from al-Līṭ in lower Makkah and plant his banner at the first houses.[^s5.227] [[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]] commanded the men without armour, in Aḥmad's report, and the foot, in Muslim's; Ibn Isḥāq says he led the line of Muslims pouring into Makkah ahead of the Prophet ﷺ.[^s5.227] The commanders were told to hold back their hands and fight only those who fought them.[^s5.227]

ʿUrwah's report in al-Bukhārī has Khālid entering from upper Makkah at Kadāʾ and the Prophet ﷺ from Kudā.[^bukh4280] Al-Ṣāliḥī points out that this conflicts with the sound reports, in which Khālid entered from lower Makkah and the Prophet ﷺ from upper Makkah, as Ibn ʿUqbah and Ibn Isḥāq also state.[^s5.267]

### Al-Khandamah

Ṣafwān b. Umayyah, ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl and Suhayl b. ʿAmr called men to fight and gathered a band at al-Khandamah, joined by men of Quraysh, Banū Bakr and Hudhayl, who swore that Muḥammad would never enter by force.[^s5.227][^s5.228] One of them, Jammāsh b. Qays of Banū al-Dīl, had been mending his weapons and promising his wife a captive to serve her.[^s5.228] When Khālid entered where he had been ordered, they barred his way, drew their weapons and shot arrows at him. He called to his men and fought them. In one account twenty-four men of Quraysh and four of Hudhayl were killed; Ibn Isḥāq says about twelve or thirteen of the idolaters. The rest fled in disorder, some being killed at al-Ḥazwarah and some escaping over the hilltops with the Muslims behind them.[^s5.228] Abū Sufyān and Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām went about calling on Quraysh not to throw away their lives, and people ran into their houses, shut their doors and threw their weapons into the streets.[^s5.228] Jammāsh ran home and told his wife to shut the door on him, with verses on how Ṣafwān and ʿIkrimah had fled.[^s5.228][^s5.229]

Two Muslims were killed. In ʿUrwah's report in al-Bukhārī they were of Khālid's horsemen: Ḥubaysh b. al-Ashʿar and [[Kurz b. Jābir al-Fihrī]].[^bukh4280] Al-Ṣāliḥī's account makes them two of al-Zubayr's men who missed the road and took another, and gives the second name as [[Ḥubaysh b. Khālid al-Khuzāʿī|Ḥubaysh b. Khālid b. Rabīʿah b. al-Ashʿar al-Kaʿbī]].[^s5.229]

From Adhākhir the Prophet ﷺ saw the glint of swords and asked whether he had not forbidden fighting. He was told that Khālid had been attacked and had fought only because of that, and he said, *"Allāh's decree is best."*[^s5.229] In a report of al-Ṭabarānī, a man sent to tell Khālid to stop told him instead to kill whoever he could, and Khālid killed seventy; when the Prophet ﷺ asked the man why, he said he had meant one thing and Allāh another, and the Prophet ﷺ said nothing.[^s5.229] In Abū Hurayrah's report, Quraysh had gathered a rabble of followers, meaning to join them if they succeeded and to accept terms if they failed. The Prophet ﷺ had Abū Hurayrah call the Anṣār to him alone and told them to cut down Quraysh and their rabble; no one resisted them. Abū Sufyān came and said that the mass of Quraysh had been destroyed, and the Prophet ﷺ repeated that whoever entered Abū Sufyān's house or laid down his weapon was safe, and the people threw down their weapons.[^s5.229][^s5.230]

Later Khālid came to the Prophet ﷺ, who asked why he had fought when fighting had been forbidden. Khālid said that they had started it, shot at them and attacked them; he had held back as long as he could and called them to Islam, and fought only when he had no choice. The Prophet ﷺ told him to stop the pursuit and said, *"Allāh's decree is best."*[^s5.233] He then told the Muslims to lay down their arms, except Khuzāʿah against Banū Bakr until the afternoon prayer. They fell on them for an hour, the hour that was made lawful for the Prophet ﷺ and for no one before him.[^s5.233][^s5.234] Al-Ṣāliḥī cites ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr, in Aḥmad's report, that the hour ran from the morning of the conquest until the afternoon prayer.[^s5.268]

## The Prophet's camp

ʿAbdullāh b. Mughaffal saw the Prophet ﷺ on his camel on the day of the conquest reciting [[Sūrat al-Fatḥ]], drawing out his voice.[^s5.230] Abū Saʿīd reports that he said, *"This is what my Lord promised me,"* and recited, *"When the help of Allāh comes, and the victory"* ([[Sūrat al-Naṣr]] 110:1).[^s5.230]

When he looked down from Adhākhir on the houses of Makkah, he praised Allāh and, pointing to where his tent stood, told [[Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh|Jābir]] that this was where Quraysh had sworn to one another against them in their unbelief.[^s5.230] He had said in Medina that their camp, when Allāh gave them Makkah, would be at the Khayf of Banū Kinānah, where Quraysh had sworn to one another on unbelief—that is, al-Muḥaṣṣab, where Quraysh and Kinānah had sworn to boycott Banū Hāshim and Banū al-Muṭṭalib.[^s5.231] Abū Rāfiʿ had pitched a leather tent for him at al-Ḥajūn, and he came to it with his wives Umm Salamah and Maymūnah.[^s5.230] [[Usāmah b. Zayd]] asked whether he would stay in his own house, and he answered, *"Has ʿAqīl left us any property or house?"* ʿAqīl and Ṭālib had inherited from Abū Ṭālib while still unbelievers, and ʿAqīl had sold the houses.[^s5.230][^s5.231] He would enter no house, and stayed at al-Ḥajūn, coming to the Mosque from there for each prayer.[^s5.231] Al-Ṣāliḥī explains the choice of the place as a reminder of the hardship once suffered there, for thanks and for forgiving those who had done wrong.[^s5.267]

[[Umm Hāniʾ bint Abī Ṭālib|Umm Hāniʾ]] had given refuge to two of her in-laws of Banū Makhzūm whom ʿAlī wanted to kill. She went to the Prophet ﷺ in upper Makkah, and he said, *"We grant refuge to whomever you have granted refuge."* He then bathed, screened by Fāṭimah, and prayed eight *rakʿahs* of the forenoon prayer.[^s5.231] In al-Bukhārī's report from her, he bathed in her house and prayed eight *rakʿahs*, lighter than any prayer she had seen him pray, though he completed their bowings and prostrations.[^s5.231][^s5.232] Al-Ṣāliḥī reconciles the two by supposing it happened more than once, or that she came from another house and found him bathing.[^s5.268] Later he asked her for food; she had only dry bread and some vinegar, and he ate and said, *"What a fine condiment vinegar is."*[^s5.235]

Ibn ʿAbbās reports that when Makkah was conquered Iblīs gave a great cry and told his offspring that they could not hope to turn the community of Muḥammad back to idolatry after that day.[^s5.232]

### Abū Quḥāfah

[[Abū Quḥāfah]], Abū Bakr's father, had lost his sight. As the army approached he had his youngest daughter take him up Abū Qubays; when she told him the dark mass was spreading out, he knew the horses had been loosed and had her hurry him home. The horsemen met her on the way and someone snatched a silver necklace from her neck.[^s5.232][^s5.233] When the Prophet ﷺ had entered the Mosque, Abū Bakr led his father in. The Prophet ﷺ asked why he had not left the old man at home so that he could go to him, and Abū Bakr answered that his father had more right to walk to him. The Prophet ﷺ wiped his chest and told him to accept Islam, and he did.[^s5.233] His hair and beard were white as the *thaghāmah* plant, and the Prophet ﷺ told them to change the colour but to avoid black.[^s5.233] Abū Bakr asked three times, by Allāh and by Islam, for his sister's necklace, and when no one brought it told her to count it as lost, since there was little honesty among people that day.[^s5.233]

## The Kaʿbah

### The circuit and the idols

After resting in his tent until the people had settled, the Prophet ﷺ bathed, put on his armour and helmet again, and rode al-Qaṣwāʾ to the Kaʿbah with Abū Bakr beside him.[^s5.234] At the Kaʿbah he touched the Corner with his staff and said "Allāhu akbar", and the Muslims took it up until Makkah shook, while the idolaters watched from the hills. He went round the House on his camel, which [[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]] led by its halter.[^s5.234]

Around the Kaʿbah were three hundred and sixty idols fixed with lead. Hubal, the greatest, faced the Kaʿbah, and Isāf and Nāʾilah stood where the sacrifices were made. With a bow in his hand, the Prophet ﷺ pointed at each idol as he passed it and struck at its eye, reciting:[^s5.234]

> «جَاءَ الْحَقُّ وَزَهَقَ الْبَاطِلُ إِنَّ الْبَاطِلَ كَانَ زَهُوقًا»
> *"Truth has come and falsehood has vanished. Falsehood is ever bound to vanish."* ([[Sūrat al-Isrāʾ]] 17:81)

Every idol he pointed at fell on its face—or, in another wording, on its back—without his touching it.[^s5.234] He completed seven circuits on his camel, touching the Black Corner with his staff at each, then dismounted and prayed two *rakʿahs* at the Maqām, still in his armour and helmet.[^s5.235] At Zamzam he said that if he did not fear that Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib would be crowded out of their office he would draw a bucket himself; al-ʿAbbās—it is also said al-Ḥārith b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib—drew one for him, and he drank and made his ablutions while the Muslims hurried to catch the water.[^s5.235] Hubal was broken as he stood over it. Al-Zubayr reminded Abū Sufyān of how he had boasted of Hubal at Uḥud, and Abū Sufyān told him to leave it, for if there had been another god with Muḥammad's things would have gone otherwise.[^s5.235]

Ibn Hishām tells of Faḍālah b. ʿUmayr al-Laythī, who meant to kill the Prophet ﷺ during the circuit. The Prophet ﷺ asked him what he had been telling himself, laughed when he said he had been remembering Allāh, told him to ask forgiveness, and put his hand on his chest; Faḍālah said that before he lifted it no one was dearer to him.[^s5.235][^s5.236] ʿAlī reports that the Prophet ﷺ climbed on his shoulders and, finding ʿAlī too weak to rise under him, had ʿAlī climb on his own shoulders instead, so that he could reach the roof of the Kaʿbah and work loose the greatest idol of Quraysh, made of brass and fixed down with iron pegs.[^s5.236]

### The key and the images

[[ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah]] had come to Medina as a Muslim before the conquest, with Khālid b. al-Walīd and [[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]].[^s5.236] After the circuit the Prophet ﷺ sent [[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ|Bilāl]] to him for the key of the Kaʿbah. His mother Sulāfah refused to give it up, swearing by al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā, until ʿUthmān told her that if she did not he and his brother would be killed. Hearing the voices of Abū Bakr and ʿUmar at the door, she gave it to him, saying she would rather he took it than men of Taym and ʿAdī.[^s5.237] In al-Zuhrī's report the Prophet ﷺ waited, sweating, until ʿUthmān came, and when ʿUthmān stumbled and dropped the key the Prophet ﷺ went to it and bent over it with his garment.[^s5.237]

From al-Baṭḥāʾ the Prophet ﷺ had sent ʿUmar to wipe out every image in the Kaʿbah, and he would not enter until it was done. ʿUmar had left an image of Ibrāhīm, and when the Prophet ﷺ saw it he said: *"May Allāh fight them! They made him an old man casting lots with arrows."*[^s5.237][^s5.238] In Ibn ʿAbbās's report he refused to enter while the idols were inside and had them brought out, among them images of Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl holding divining arrows, and said that they knew the two had never cast lots with them.[^s5.238] Ibn Abī Shaybah reports from Ibn ʿUmar that the Muslims stripped to their waist-wraps and washed the Kaʿbah inside and out with water from Zamzam until no trace of the idolaters was left.[^s5.238]

### Inside the Kaʿbah

In Ibn ʿUmar's report in al-Bukhārī, the Prophet ﷺ came from upper Makkah on his camel with Usāmah b. Zayd riding behind him, and with him Bilāl and ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah, until he made the camel kneel in the Mosque. He told ʿUthmān to bring the key, and went in with Usāmah, Bilāl and ʿUthmān. He stayed a long time, and when he came out the people raced to go in. Ibn ʿUmar was the first, and found Bilāl standing behind the door; he asked where the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had prayed, and Bilāl showed him the place. *"I forgot to ask him how much he prayed,"* Ibn ʿUmar said.[^bukh4289][^s5.238][^s5.239] The door was shut on them.[^s5.239] He broke a wooden dove he found inside, and in Jābir's report he smeared saffron over the images of Ibrāhīm, Ismāʿīl and Isḥāq that he found there.[^s5.239] He said "Allāhu akbar" in its corners and praised Allāh.[^s5.239]

Bilāl told Ibn ʿUmar that he had prayed between the two front pillars, the Yemeni ones; the reports differ on how many pillars were to his right and left, and say the House then stood on six pillars in two rows and that he prayed with the door behind him and about three cubits between him and the wall.[^s5.240] Several reports say he prayed two *rakʿahs*.[^s5.241] Al-Ṣāliḥī spends several *tanbīhāt* reconciling Ibn ʿUmar's "I forgot to ask" with these reports, and Bilāl's affirmation that he prayed there with a report from Usāmah denying it: Ibn Ḥajar prefers Bilāl's report as the one affirming what was seen, while others suggest Usāmah was occupied with supplication, or had been sent to fetch water to wipe out the images.[^s5.269][^s5.270][^s5.272] On leaving the House he is reported to have prayed two *rakʿahs* facing the Kaʿbah and said, *"This is the qiblah."*[^s5.241]

### The address at the door

He came out with the key in his hand, while Khālid kept the people back from the door.[^s5.242] The people had sat down around the Kaʿbah, and he stood at its door and said:[^s5.242]

> «لا إله إلا الله وحده لا شريك له، صدق وعده، ونصر عبده، وهزم الأحزاب وحده»
> *"There is no god but Allāh alone, without partner. He has kept His promise, helped His servant, and alone defeated the Confederates."*[^s5.242]

He asked Quraysh what they thought he would do. They answered that they thought well: *"A noble Prophet, a noble brother and the son of a noble brother, and you have the power."* He told them he would say what his brother Yūsuf had said: *"No blame upon you today. Allāh will forgive you, and He is the most merciful of the merciful"* (Yūsuf 12:92). Then he said, «اذهبوا فأنتم الطّلقاء», *"Go, for you are free."* They went out as if raised from their graves, and entered Islam.[^s5.242]

In the same address he declared that every claim of usury, blood, distinction or property from the Jāhiliyyah was beneath his feet, the first blood he set aside being that of Rabīʿah b. al-Ḥārith, except the custody of the House and the giving of water to the pilgrims. He fixed the blood-money for a killing by stick or whip, the semblance of a deliberate killing, at a hundred camels, forty of them pregnant. He said that Allāh had removed from them the pride of the Jāhiliyyah and its boasting of ancestors, for all of them were from Ādam and Ādam was from dust, and he recited *"O mankind, We created you from a male and a female…"* (al-Ḥujurāt 49:13).[^s5.242] He declared that Allāh had made Makkah sacred on the day He created the heavens and the earth: it had been lawful to no one before him and would be lawful to no one after, and to him only for an hour of a day; its game was not to be startled, its trees not cut, and its lost property not picked up except by one who would announce it. At al-ʿAbbās's request he excepted the *idhkhir* grass.[^s5.242][^s5.243] The address as al-Ṣāliḥī assembles it continues with a series of rulings—no bequest to an heir, the child belongs to the bed, a Muslim not killed for a disbeliever, a woman not to travel three days without a *maḥram*, and others—and ends with the Prophet ﷺ telling them to write it out for Abū Shāh.[^s5.243]

### The key returned

He sat by the place of the water-giving, holding the key; al-Wāqidī's teachers say he had taken the key of the water-giving from al-ʿAbbās and that of the House from ʿUthmān.[^s5.243] ʿAlī—in another report al-ʿAbbās—asked him to give their family the custody of the House together with the water-giving.[^s5.244][^s5.245] Instead he called ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah and gave the key back to him, saying, *"Today is a day of goodness and keeping faith,"* and telling him to take it forever, never to be taken from them except by a wrongdoer.[^s5.244]

ʿUthmān recalled that before the Hijrah, when he had refused the Prophet ﷺ entry to the Kaʿbah and spoken harshly to him, the Prophet ﷺ had said that perhaps one day he would see the key in his hand to put where he wished. When the key was returned the Prophet ﷺ called him back and asked whether it had not happened as he had said, and ʿUthmān bore witness that he was the Messenger of Allāh.[^s5.244] In reports of Ibn Jurayj, Mujāhid and Jābir, the verse *"Allāh commands you to render trusts to those entitled to them"* ([[Sūrat al-Nisāʾ]] 4:58) was revealed about this, and the Prophet ﷺ came out of the Kaʿbah reciting it; ʿUmar said he had never heard him recite it before.[^s5.244][^s5.245]

## The Anṣār, Abū Sufyān and Hind

After his circuit the Prophet ﷺ went up al-Ṣafā until he could see the House, raised his hands, and praised Allāh and supplicated. Below him some of the Anṣār said to one another that the man had been overtaken by longing for his town and tenderness for his clan. Revelation came, and when it was over he told them what they had said, and asked:[^s5.246]

> «فما أسمي إذن!! كلا، إني عبد الله ورسوله، هاجرت إلى الله وإليكم، المحيا محياكم والممات مماتكم»
> *"What would my name be then? No: I am the servant of Allāh and His Messenger. I emigrated to Allāh and to you; my life is your life and my death is your death."*[^s5.246]

They came to him weeping, saying they had spoken only out of jealousy for Allāh and His Messenger, and he told them that Allāh and His Messenger accepted their excuse and believed them.[^s5.246]

Several reports tell of Abū Sufyān's thoughts being answered. As he watched people following at the Prophet's ﷺ heels, he wondered to himself whether to raise an army against him again; the Prophet ﷺ came and struck him on the chest and said, *"Then Allāh would disgrace you."*[^s5.246] Another time he wondered how Muḥammad overcame them, and the Prophet ﷺ struck his chest and said, *"By Allāh we overcome you."*[^s5.247] In Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab's report, he asked Hind during the night whether she thought all this came from Allāh, and she said yes; the next morning the Prophet ﷺ repeated their words to him, and Abū Sufyān bore witness, swearing that no one had heard them but Allāh and Hind.[^s5.246][^s5.247]

## The pledge of allegiance

Al-Aswad b. Khalaf saw the Prophet ﷺ sit at Qarn Masfalah and take the pledge of the people—old and young, men and women—on faith in Allāh and the testimony that there is no god but Allāh and that Muḥammad is His servant and Messenger.[^s5.247] Ibn Jarīr says he was told that the Prophet ﷺ sat on al-Ṣafā, with ʿUmar below him, and took from the men the pledge to hear and obey Allāh and His Messenger as far as they could.[^s5.247]

Then the women pledged, among them Hind bint ʿUtbah, veiled and disguised for fear that he would hold her to account for what she had done to Ḥamzah. She answered each condition as he gave it. To *"you will not steal"* she said she used to take a little of Abū Sufyān's wealth now and then, and Abū Sufyān, who was present, released her from what was past. To *"you will not commit adultery"* she asked whether a free woman would do such a thing. To *"you will not kill your children"* she said they had raised them small and he had killed them grown, and the Prophet ﷺ and ʿUmar laughed.[^s5.247][^s5.248] He told ʿUmar to take their pledge and ask forgiveness for them. The Prophet ﷺ did not shake hands with women; ʿĀʾishah reports, in al-Bukhārī and Muslim, that his hand never touched a woman's hand, and that he took their pledge only in words.[^s5.248]

A crier announced in Makkah that whoever believed in Allāh and the Last Day was to leave no idol in his house unbroken.[^s5.248]

## The adhān of Bilāl

When the time of the noon prayer came, the Prophet ﷺ told Bilāl to give the call to prayer from the top of the Kaʿbah, to vex the idolaters, while Quraysh watched from the hilltops and some of their leaders had fled and hidden.[^s5.248] Abū Sufyān, ʿAttāb—or, in Ibn Abī Shaybah's wording, Khālid—b. Asīd, and al-Ḥārith b. Hishām were sitting in the courtyard of the Kaʿbah. ʿAttāb said that Allāh had honoured his father Asīd by not letting him hear this; al-Ḥārith said that if he knew it to be true he would follow it; Abū Sufyān said he would say nothing, for the very pebbles would report him.[^s5.248][^s5.249] Others present, among them al-Ḥakam b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ, spoke with contempt of the slave of Banū Jumaḥ shouting on the House. Jibrīl told the Prophet ﷺ what they had said, and when he came out and told them he knew it, al-Ḥārith and ʿAttāb bore witness that he was the Messenger of Allāh, since no one had been with them to tell him.[^s5.249]

The Prophet ﷺ also sent Tamīm b. Asad al-Khuzāʿī to renew the boundary stones of the sanctuary. In the reports al-Ṣāliḥī gives, these had first been set up by Ibrāhīm, shown the places by Jibrīl, and were renewed by Ismāʿīl and by Quṣayy b. Kilāb.[^s5.249]

## Those who became Muslims

**Al-Sāʾib b. ʿAbdullāh al-Makhzūmī**, who had been the Prophet's ﷺ partner in trade before Islam, was greeted as *"my brother and my partner, who never wrangled or quarrelled."*[^s5.249]

**[[al-Ḥārith b. Hishām]]** had taken refuge with ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Rabīʿah in Umm Hāniʾ's house, and after two days returned home, fearing ʿUmar, who only greeted him and passed on. Ashamed to face the Prophet ﷺ after opposing him everywhere, he met him at the Mosque, was received with a smile, and bore witness; the Prophet ﷺ said that one like him should not have been ignorant of Islam.[^s5.249][^s5.250]

**[[Suhayl b. ʿAmr]]** shut himself in his house and sent his son ʿAbdullāh to ask for protection. The Prophet ﷺ granted it and told those around him not to stare at Suhayl, for he had intelligence and honour. Suhayl said that the Prophet ﷺ had been dutiful as a child and as a man. He went about safely, went out to Ḥunayn with the Prophet ﷺ while still an idolater, and became a Muslim at al-Jiʿrānah.[^s5.250]

**ʿUtbah and Muʿattib, the sons of Abū Lahab**, had withdrawn with other idolaters of Quraysh. At the Prophet's ﷺ request al-ʿAbbās brought them from ʿUranah; they became Muslims, and the Prophet ﷺ took them by the hand to the Multazam and prayed. He said he had asked his Lord for these two cousins of his, and He had given them to him.[^s5.250]

**[[ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zibaʿrā]]** had fled to Najrān. After Ḥassān sent him verses of reproach he came to the Prophet ﷺ, who said, *"This is Ibn al-Zibaʿrā, and with him a face in which is the light of Islam."* He confessed his long enmity, and the Prophet ﷺ told him that Islam wipes out what came before it. He composed verses of apology.[^s5.250][^s5.251]

**[[ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl]]** had been with a band in lower Makkah when Khālid fell on them, and fled, meaning to throw himself into the sea rather than accept Islam.[^s5.252] His wife [[Umm Ḥakīm bint al-Ḥārith]], who had become a Muslim, told the Prophet ﷺ that he had fled toward Yemen, and the Prophet ﷺ granted him safety.[^s5.252] In Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ's report, a storm struck the ship he had boarded and the sailors told the passengers to call on Allāh alone; ʿIkrimah vowed that if only sincerity could save him at sea, nothing else could save him on land, and that he would put his hand in Muḥammad's.[^s5.252] In al-Zuhrī's report Umm Ḥakīm went after him and reached him at the shore. The Prophet ﷺ told his Companions that ʿIkrimah was coming as a believer and an emigrant, and that they were not to insult his father, since insulting the dead hurts the living and does not reach the dead.[^s5.252][^s5.253] He leapt up to meet him. ʿIkrimah asked what he was calling to, and when the Prophet ﷺ listed the duties of Islam he bore witness.[^s5.253]

**[[Ṣafwān b. Umayyah]]** set out for Jeddah to sail to Yemen. [[ʿUmayr b. Wahb]] asked the Prophet ﷺ to grant safety to the chief of his people, and went after him. Ṣafwān refused to come back without a sign, and the Prophet ﷺ sent the turban he had worn on entering Makkah.[^s5.253][^s5.254] Ṣafwān came to the Prophet ﷺ as he finished the afternoon prayer and asked for two months to decide; the Prophet ﷺ gave him four. At the division of the spoils of Hawāzin, seeing him gaze at a valley full of livestock, the Prophet ﷺ gave it to him with all it held, and Ṣafwān said that no soul would give like that but a prophet's, and became a Muslim on the spot.[^s5.254]

**[[Hind bint ʿUtbah]]**, in a report from al-Wāqidī, dreamed on three nights after the conquest of the Prophet ﷺ calling her out of darkness, then onto the road, then pulling her back from the brink of the Fire; she went and broke up the idol in her house. She came to the Prophet ﷺ at al-Abṭaḥ, became a Muslim and uncovered her face.[^s5.254][^s5.255] In ʿĀʾishah's report in al-Bukhārī and Muslim, she told him that there had been no household on earth she more wished to see humbled than his, and now none she more wished to see honoured.[^s5.254]

## The second day

On the day after the conquest Khuzāʿah killed a man of Hudhayl, an idolater: Junaydib b. al-Adlaʿ, who had killed Aḥmar Baʾsan of Aslam in the Jāhiliyyah. Khirāsh b. Umayyah al-Kaʿbī struck him down as he stood among the people. The Prophet ﷺ told Khuzāʿah to stop killing, said that he would pay the blood-money for the man they had killed, and spoke against Khirāsh.[^s5.255][^s5.256] After the noon prayer he stood with his back to the Kaʿbah and declared again that Makkah had been made sacred by Allāh, not by people, that it had been lawful for him only for that hour, and that its sanctity had returned that day as it had been the day before. He told them that whoever was killed after that, his family could choose between the blood-money and retaliation, and he paid the blood-money for the man of Hudhayl; Ibn Hishām says it was a hundred camels and that he heard it was the first blood-money the Prophet ﷺ paid.[^s5.256][^s5.257]

## Rulings and sayings of the conquest

He said that no Qurashī would be killed bound after that day until the Day of Resurrection—al-Wāqidī explains, for unbelief—and that Makkah would not be invaded after that day.[^s5.257][^s5.260] He took no spoils from Makkah, but sent raiding parties outside the sanctuary, among them Khālid b. al-Walīd to destroy al-ʿUzzā and Saʿd b. Zayd al-Ashhalī to Manāt.[^s5.260] He borrowed money from men of Quraysh—from Ṣafwān b. Umayyah, ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Rabīʿah and Ḥuwayṭib b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, in Abū Ḥuṣayn's report a hundred and thirty thousand dirhams in all—and divided it among the poorer of his Companions. Ibn Abī Rabīʿah's loan he repaid when Allāh gave him the wealth of Hawāzin, saying that the reward for a loan is thanks and repayment.[^s5.257][^s5.258]

Among the other rulings al-Ṣāliḥī collects from the days of the conquest:

- The sale of wine, swine, carrion and idols was forbidden.[^s5.258]
- Hind was told she might feed her children from Abū Sufyān's wealth in a fair measure.[^s5.258]
- In the dispute over the son of Zamʿah's slave woman, the Prophet ﷺ ruled that *"the child belongs to the bed"*.[^s5.258][^s5.259]
- When Usāmah interceded for a woman who had stolen, the Prophet ﷺ rebuked him and said that if Fāṭimah bint Muḥammad stole he would cut off her hand.[^s5.259]
- A man who had vowed to pray in Jerusalem if Makkah was conquered was told to pray in Makkah.[^s5.259]
- *"There is no hijrah after the conquest, but jihād and intention"*: Makkah had become a land of Islam, and the duty to emigrate from it ended.[^s5.260]

## Length of the stay

Ibn ʿAbbās reports in al-Bukhārī that the Prophet ﷺ stayed in Makkah nineteen days, shortening the prayer; Abū Dāwūd's text has seventeen. ʿImrān b. Ḥuṣayn gives eighteen nights, and another report from Ibn ʿAbbās fifteen.[^s5.261] Al-Bayhaqī reconciled the figures by whether the days of arrival and departure are counted, and Ibn Ḥajar judged nineteen the strongest. Anas's report of ten days, he says, refers to the Farewell Pilgrimage.[^s5.273]

## The date and the numbers

There is no disagreement that the conquest took place in Ramaḍān.[^s5.265] Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, in a report Aḥmad gives with a sound chain, says they left Medina on the second of Ramaḍān; al-Zuhrī, in a report al-Bayhaqī gives with a sound chain, that the Prophet ﷺ reached Makkah on the morning of the thirteenth, which would make twelve days on the road. Ibn Ḥajar thought al-Wāqidī's date of the tenth for the departure weak; al-Ṣāliḥī notes that Ibn Isḥāq agrees with al-Wāqidī and that Isḥāq b. Rāhawayh has it from Ibn ʿAbbās with a sound chain. Other reports put the entry on the sixteenth (Muslim), the eighteenth or the twelfth (Aḥmad), the nineteenth, the seventeenth, or the twentieth.[^s5.265][^s5.266] A report in the *Ṣaḥīḥ* dates the campaign eight and a half years after the arrival in Medina; Ibn Ḥajar calls this a mistake for seven and a half.[^s5.266][^s5.267]

Ibn Isḥāq states that ten thousand Muslims were present at the conquest, as al-Bukhārī reports from ʿUrwah and Isḥāq b. Rāhawayh from Ibn ʿAbbās. ʿUrwah, al-Zuhrī and Ibn ʿUqbah also give twelve thousand, and the two figures are reconciled as ten thousand who left Medina and two thousand who joined them.[^s5.266]
