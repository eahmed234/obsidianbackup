# Sariyyat al-ʿUraniyyīn (سرية العرنيين)
*The expedition sent after the men of ʿUkl and ʿUraynah who killed the Prophet's ﷺ herdsman and drove off the camels*[^bukh3018][^s6.115]

Eight men of ʿUkl and ʿUraynah came to the Prophet ﷺ in Madīnah, professed Islam and fell ill; he sent them out to the camels to drink their milk and urine, and when they recovered they killed the herdsman, drove off the camels and left Islam. The Prophet ﷺ sent twenty horsemen after them, with [[Kurz b. Jābir al-Fihrī]] ﵁ as their commander according to Ibn Isḥāq and most; the men were captured and brought back, and punished. The verse of *muḥārabah* (al-Māʾidah 5:33) was revealed concerning them.[^s6.115][^s6.116][^s6.117] Al-Ṣāliḥī gives the expedition a chapter titled "the expedition of Kurz b. Jābir or Saʿīd b. Zayd to the ʿUraniyyīn".[^s6.115]

## The men and their illness

Anas ﵁ says that eight men of ʿUkl came to the Prophet ﷺ and found Madīnah unsuitable (*ijtawaw*).[^bukh3018] In another report they pledged allegiance to him on Islam, and the land was unhealthy to them so that their bodies fell ill.[^bukh6899] Abū ʿAwānah's version has them severely emaciated and yellow, with swollen bellies. They asked to be sheltered and fed, and stayed in the Ṣuffah — a shaded place at the north end of the Prophet's ﷺ mosque, where strangers with no shelter and no family stayed.[^s6.115][^s6.120] Their tribes are disputed (see below). When they had recovered they found Madīnah unsuitable; one wording says *istawkhamū* (its air did not suit their bodies), and Ibn Isḥāq says they found it unhealthy and grew weak and thin.[^s6.115][^s6.120]

They said: "We were people of the udder, not of cultivated land, so seek milk for us." He answered: "I find nothing for you except that you join the camels."[^bukh3018] In one report he told them to join the herdsmen at Fayfāʾ al-Khabār, and permitted them to go to the camels of charity and drink their milk and urine; in another, he asked whether they would not go out with "our herdsman" among his camels and take of their milk and urine, and they agreed.[^s6.115][^bukh6899][^musl1671]

## The killing of Yasār

They went out, drank the milk and urine until they were healthy and fat, killed the herdsman, drove off the camels and disbelieved after their Islam.[^bukh3018] Muslim's report has them turning on the herdsmen and killing them.[^musl1671]

The herdsman was Yasār, whom the Prophet ﷺ had obtained in the expedition against Banū Muḥārib and Banū Thaʿlabah; seeing that he prayed well, he freed him and sent him with milch camels of his pasturing in al-Ḥimā.[^s6.115] Al-Ṣāliḥī relates that Yasār overtook them with some men and fought them; they cut off his hands and feet and thrust thorns into his tongue and eyes until he died.[^s6.115] Ibn Ḥajar gives the same details in a report from al-Wāqidī on the authority of Yazīd b. Rūmān, in which eight men of ʿUraynah came and became Muslim.[^i5.434][^i5.435] According to Muḥammad b. ʿUmar, a woman of Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf passed Yasār under a tree; she returned to her people and told them, and they brought him dead to Qubāʾ.[^s6.115][^s6.116] The camels were fifteen milch camels, according to Muḥammad b. ʿUmar and Ibn Saʿd.[^s6.117]

## The pursuit

The crier came to the Prophet ﷺ and he sent the pursuit.[^bukh3018] Muslim's report says that there were with him young men of the Anṣār, about twenty, and he sent them; another report says he sent twenty horsemen in their tracks.[^s6.116][^musl1671] Al-Ṣāliḥī, following Muḥammad b. ʿUmar, names among them [[Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ]], [[Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī]] and Abū Ruhm, [[Buraydah b. al-Ḥuṣayb]], Rāfiʿ b. Makīth and his brother Jundab, Bilāl b. al-Ḥārith and ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. ʿAwf of Muzaynah, Jiʿāl b. Surāqah and Suwayd b. Ṣakhr al-Juhanī ﵃, and says that these are of the Muhājirūn.[^s6.116] He appointed Kurz b. Jābir al-Fihrī over them.[^s6.116][^i5.435] He also sent a tracker (*qāʾif*) with them, and supplicated against the men: "Blind the road to them and make it narrower upon them than a camel's skin."[^s6.116][^musl1671]

Allāh blinded the paths to them, and they were overtaken that day and seized.[^s6.116] Muḥammad b. ʿUmar gives the search in detail: Kurz and his companions searched until night overtook them and they spent the night at al-Ḥarrah; in the morning they did not know which way the men had gone, and met a woman carrying a camel's shoulder. She told them she had passed a people who had slaughtered a camel and given her the shoulder, and that they were in a wilderness where the smoke would be seen when they reached it.[^s6.116][^i5.435] The horsemen came upon them when they had finished their meal and asked them to surrender; all surrendered and none escaped.[^s6.116] They bound them, mounted them behind on the horses and brought them to Madīnah, where they found the Prophet ﷺ at al-Rughābah, land adjoining al-Jurf.[^s6.116][^s6.122]

## The punishment

Al-Bukhārī's report says that he cut off their hands and feet, then ordered nails to be heated and applied them to their eyes like kohl, and threw them in al-Ḥarrah, where they asked for water and were not given it until they died.[^bukh3018] Another report says he threw them in the sun until they died.[^bukh6899] Muslim's wording is that he put out their eyes and left them in al-Ḥarrah until they died.[^musl1671] Anas ﵁ said: "I saw one of them biting the ground with his mouth from thirst", and Muslim records that he did not cauterise them.[^s6.116][^musl1671] According to Anas, "the Prophet ﷺ put out the eyes of those only because they had put out the eyes of the herdsmen."[^musl1671] Abū Qilābah said of them: "they killed, stole, disbelieved after their Islam and made war on Allāh and His Messenger."[^s6.116] Al-Ṣāliḥī says they were thrown in al-Ḥarrah because it was the closest land to where they had done what they did.[^s6.121][^s6.122]

Then Allāh revealed: "The recompense of those who make war on Allāh and His Messenger and strive in the land for corruption …" (al-Māʾidah 5:33). According to the report, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ did not afterwards put out an eye, cut a tongue or go beyond cutting the hand and foot, and never afterwards sent out a force without forbidding it *al-muthlah* (mutilation); he used to urge charity and forbid mutilation.[^s6.117][^s6.122] The question of whether this is a matter of abrogation is taken up below.

## Disputed points

**Their tribes.** Anas's reports have "eight of ʿUkl", "ʿUkl and ʿUraynah" or "ʿUkl or ʿUraynah"; in Muslim one report has "people of ʿUraynah".[^bukh3018][^s6.115][^musl1671] Ibn Jarīr and Abū ʿAwānah say four were of ʿUraynah and three of ʿUkl, and the eighth was of neither and was given no lineage.[^s6.115] Al-Ṣāliḥī says "ʿUkl *and* ʿUraynah" with the conjunction is without doubt, and quotes al-Ḥāfiẓ that this is correct and is al-Bukhārī's report in *al-Maghāzī*, though others have "or". Ibn al-Tīn, following al-Dāwūdī, claimed that ʿUraynah are ʿUkl; al-Ḥāfiẓ calls this a mistake: ʿUkl is a tribe of Taym al-Ribāb, of ʿAdnān, and ʿUraynah is of Qaḥṭān, among Bajīlah and Quḍāʿah; the one meant here is ʿUraynah b. Nadhīr b. Qasr b. ʿAbqar, and ʿAbqar's mother was Bajīlah. A report in ʿAbd al-Razzāq making them of Banū Fazārah has a worthless chain and is a mistake.[^s6.117]

**The date.** Ibn Isḥāq says their coming was after the expedition of [[Ghazwat Dhī Qarad]], in Jumādā al-Ākhirah of 6 AH; al-Bukhārī mentions it after [[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]], which was in Dhū l-Qaʿdah of that year; Muḥammad b. ʿUmar says Shawwāl, and Ibn Saʿd, Ibn Ḥibbān and others followed him.[^s6.117] Ibn Sīrīn said this story was before the *ḥudūd* were revealed.[^s6.117]

**The commander.** Ibn Isḥāq and most say Kurz b. Jābir al-Fihrī.[^s6.117] Mūsā b. ʿUqbah says Saʿīd, which others give as [[Saʿd b. Zayd al-Ashhalī|Saʿd b. Zayd]] al-Anṣārī al-Ashhalī; al-Ḥāfiẓ allows that he was head of the Anṣār while Kurz commanded the whole group.[^s6.117] Al-Ṭabarānī and others report from Jarīr b. ʿAbdillāh al-Bajalī that the Prophet ﷺ sent him in their tracks, but the chain is weak and the well-known view is that Jarīr came about four years later.[^s6.116][^s6.117] The Anṣār-and-Muhājirūn discrepancy is explained as "Anṣār" having been said by predominance, or all being called Anṣār in the wider sense; it is also said that those Muḥammad b. ʿUmar did not name may have been of the Anṣār.[^s6.116][^s6.119]

**Whose camels, and how many herdsmen.** Some reports suggest the milch camels belonged to the Prophet ﷺ, and al-Bukhārī says so explicitly in *al-Muḥāribīn*; in others they are the camels of charity. The reconciliation offered is that the camels of charity pastured outside Madīnah, and the sending of the Prophet's ﷺ own milch camels to the pasture coincided with these men asking to go out to the desert for camel milk; he ordered them to go out with a herdsman. The same passage adds that the truth of the Prophet's ﷺ saying that Madīnah expels its impurity was borne out.[^s6.118] Al-Bukhārī's reports agree that the one killed was the Prophet's ﷺ herdsman, in the singular, while others say herdsmen; it is possible that the camels of charity had herdsmen and some were killed with the herdsman of the milch camels, or that some narrators used the plural loosely, and al-Ḥāfiẓ prefers the latter, since none of the authorities on the campaigns affirmed that they killed anyone but Yasār.[^s6.119]

**The manner of punishment.** One report has *sammara* (nailed) their eyes with a shaddah on the mīm and another without; all of al-Bukhārī's reports have the rāʾ, while Muslim has *sammala*. Al-Khaṭṭābī says *saml* is putting out the eye by whatever means, *samr* is a variant of it, and it may be from *al-mismār* (nail); it is then explained that their eyes were applied with heated rods, as in the Ṣaḥīḥ report "he applied it to them as kohl", which does not contradict the report of *saml*.[^s6.119][^s6.120] A report in Ibn ʿAwānah, from Ibn ʿAqīl from Anas, has him crucifying two and putting out the eyes of two; al-Ḥāfiẓ remarks that it mentions only six and that, if preserved, their punishment was distributed among them.[^s6.117] Al-Qāḍī found their not being given water problematic, given the consensus that one on whom execution is due is not refused water; he answered that this did not happen by the Prophet's ﷺ order, nor did he forbid giving them water; al-Ḥāfiẓ calls that very weak, since the Prophet ﷺ knew of it and his silence established the ruling, and al-Nawawī answered that the apostate *muḥārib* has no sanctity regarding water or anything else. It is also said the wisdom was that they had disbelieved in the favour of the camel milk they had been given to drink.[^s6.119]

**Abrogation.** Quoting al-Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ, the commentary printed with Muslim's report says the scholars differed: some of the Salaf said this was before the revelation of the *ḥudūd*, the verse of *muḥārabah* and the prohibition of mutilation, and so is abrogated; it is also said it is not abrogated and that the verse was revealed concerning them.[^musl1671b]

**The urine.** Those who hold the urine of animals whose meat is eaten to be pure, Mālik and Aḥmad, argue from the Prophet's ﷺ ordering these men to drink the camels' milk and urine; al-Shāfiʿī and the majority hold all urine and dung impure, of animals eaten or not.[^s6.118] Al-Ṣāliḥī also cites Ibn ʿAbbās that the Prophet ﷺ said: "In the urine of camels is healing for those whose stomachs are sick."[^s6.119]
