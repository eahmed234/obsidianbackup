# Wafd Thaqīf (وفد ثقيف — إسلام أهل الطائف)
*The Delegation of Thaqīf — The Submission of al-Ṭāʾif, the Eradication of al-Lāt, and the Inviolability of Prayer*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 6, *al-Bāb al-Thālith wal-ʿIshrūn fī Wafd Thaqīf ilayhi ﷺ* (pp. 296–302), compiling the detailed accounts of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[al-Wāqidī]], and [[Ibn Saʿd]], cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah*, 4:452 (entry for ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ).

---

## 1. Context & The Martyrdom of ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd ﵁

Following the lifting of the Siege of al-Ṭāʾif (**[[Ghazwat al-Ṭāʾif]]**) in 8 AH, the supreme chieftain of **[[Banū Thaqīf|Thaqīf]]**, **ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd al-Thaqafī** ﵁, pursued the Prophet ﷺ to Medina and embraced Islam with radiant conviction (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296). 

ʿUrwah returned to his people in al-Ṭāʾif hoping to guide them to faith, confident in their deep ancestral love for him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296). When he ascended the balcony of his home and openly proclaimed the *shahādah*, calling his tribe to abandon idolatry, the folk of Thaqīf were seized with fury and shot volleys of arrows at him, mortally wounding him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296). As he lay dying, ʿUrwah praised Allāh for granting him martyrdom beside the martyrs who fell with the Prophet ﷺ at the walls of al-Ṭāʾif (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296).

Following ʿUrwah's martyrdom, Thaqīf endured several months surrounded by Muslim allied tribes who cut off their commerce and caravans (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296). Realizing that all of Arabia had submitted and that they stood isolated, their senior leaders convened in late 9 AH and decided to dispatch a high-level diplomatic embassy to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296).

---

## 2. Arrival in Medina & Negotiations

The delegation was headed by **ʿAbd Yālīl b. ʿAmr b. ʿUmayr**, accompanied by two leaders from the confederates (*al-Aḥlāf*) and three from Banū Mālik, including the youngest delegate, **[[ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296).

Upon reaching the outskirts of Medina, they were met by **[[al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah]]** ﵁ (himself a Thaqafī nobleman), who raced to give the glad tidings to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296). **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ pleaded with al-Mughīrah to allow him the honor of announcing their arrival to the Prophet ﷺ, to which al-Mughīrah graciously consented (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296).

The Prophet ﷺ pitched tents for the delegation directly inside the courtyard of **[[al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]** so they could listen to the recitation of the Qurʾān during night prayers and witness the humility and devotion of the Muslim community (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296). **[[Khālid b. Saʿīd b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁ served as the diplomatic mediator and drew up the documentation between the parties (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296).

---

## 3. The Uncompromising Foundations of Faith

The delegates engaged in prolonged negotiations, attempting to secure exemptions that would ease their transition from Jāhiliyyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297):
1. **Preservation of al-Lāt**: They pleaded that their chief idol, **[[al-Lāt]]** (*al-Rabbah*), be left untouched for three years, so that their women, children, and simple folk would not be distressed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297). The Prophet ﷺ flatly refused. They successively bargained for two years, then one year, then a single month. The Prophet ﷺ rejected every delay, insisting that idolatry had to be abolished immediately and completely (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297).
2. **Exemption from Smashing It Themselves**: Seeing his resolve, they requested: "Relieve us from breaking it with our own hands." To this, the Prophet ﷺ showed mercy and consented: «أَمَّا كَسْرُ أَصْنَامِكُمْ بِأَيْدِيكُمْ فَسَنُعْفِيكُمْ مِنْهُ!» (*"As for breaking your idols with your own hands, we shall excuse you from that!"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297).
3. **Exemption from Prayer, Zakāt, and Military Service**: They requested that they be exempt from paying zakāt, participating in expeditions, and performing the ritual prayer (*Ṣalāh*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297). 

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ laid down the eternal foundation of Islamic worship, proclaiming:
> «لَا خَيْرَ فِي دِينٍ لَا رُكُوعَ فِيهِ!»
> *"There is no good in a religion that has no bowing (in prayer)!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297).

Recognizing the Prophet's uncompromising firmness upon the truth, the delegation accepted all conditions and surrendered unconditionally to Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297).

---

## 4. The Excellence of ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ ﵁

While the older chieftains were occupied with diplomatic haggling or resting in their pavilion, the youngest delegate, **[[ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁, would quietly slip away to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ whenever the delegation rested (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297). 

He memorized the Qurʾān directly from the Prophet ﷺ and sought instruction from **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ in the understanding of the Sunnah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297). 

When the treaty was concluded and the delegation prepared to return, they asked: "O Messenger of Allāh! Appoint over us an Imām to lead our prayer." 

The Prophet ﷺ chose **[[ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁, despite his youth, saying to him:
> «أَنْتَ إِمَامُهُمْ، وَاقْتَدِ بِأَضْعَفِهِمْ، وَاتَّخِذْ مُؤَذِّنًا لَا يَأْخُذُ عَلَى أَذَانِهِ أَجْرًا!»
> *"You are their Imām! Cater to the weakest among them (in the length of prayer), and appoint a muezzin who takes no wage for his adhān!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297).

Decades later, during the Apostasy Wars (**[[Ḥurūb al-Riddah]]**), it was this very same ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ ﵁ who stood before Thaqīf and kept them firm upon Islam, telling them: "O Thaqīf! You were the last of the Arabs to enter Islam; do not be the first of them to apostatize!" (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:452).

---

## 5. The Demolition of al-Lāt (*al-Rabbah*)

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ dispatched a detachment to al-Ṭāʾif to demolish the idol sanctuary of al-Lāt, placing it under the joint command of **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** ﵁ and **[[al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298).

When the Muslims approached the shrine with pickaxes, the women of Thaqīf came out weeping, bareheaded, mourning their fallen idol (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298). 

Al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah ﵁ advanced with his axe. Playing a jest on the fearful crowd, he struck the stone idol once and deliberately threw himself backward groaning, pretending the idol had smitten him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298). The pagan crowd cheered in jubilation, shouting: "May Allāh shatter al-Mughīrah! The Rabbah has slain him!" Al-Mughīrah then sprang to his feet laughing, saying:
> «قَبَّحَكُمُ اللَّهُ! إِنَّمَا هِيَ لِخَافٌ وَحِجَارَةٌ!»
> *"May Allāh deform you! It is nothing but worthless stone and slab!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298).

Al-Mughīrah and Abū Sufyān systematically leveled the temple to the ground, dug up its foundation stones, and extracted all the gold, silver, and ornamental jewelry preserved in its treasury (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298). The treasure was utilized to pay off the substantial debts of ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd and his brother al-Aswad b. Masʿūd according to the Prophet's instructions, and the site of the idol was converted into the city's central mosque (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298).
