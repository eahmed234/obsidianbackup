# Ḥarb al-Fijār (حرب الفجار)
*The War That Broke the Sacred Month — Fought While He Watched, Aged Fourteen to Twenty*

## 1. Cause: A Killing in the Sacred Month
**ʿUrwah al-Raḥḥāl b. ʿUtbah** had granted safe passage to a trading caravan (*laṭīmah*) bound for
**al-Nuʿmān b. al-Mundhir**. **Al-Barrāḍ b. Qays**, of Banū Ḍamrah, asked him: *"do you grant it
safe passage even against Kinānah?"* — *"Yes,"* ʿUrwah answered, *"against all creation."* Al-Barrāḍ
set out watching for his chance, and at **Taymin dhī Ṭalāl**, in a moment ʿUrwah was unguarded,
struck him down — **in the sacred month**. For this the war took its name: ***al-Fijār***, "the
transgression" (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:152).

## 2. Escalation
Word reached Quraysh that al-Barrāḍ had killed ʿUrwah while both were still within the sacred month at
**ʿUkāẓ**. They set out at once, with Hawāzin unaware; when Hawāzin learned of it they pursued and
overtook Quraysh before they could reach the sanctuary. Fighting continued until nightfall, when
Quraysh entered the Ḥaram and Hawāzin held back. The two sides met again over **six named days**:
Shamẓah and Yawm al-ʿAblāʾ (both near ʿUkāẓ); **Yawm al-Sharb**, the greatest of them, in which
**Abū Sufyān** and **Umayyah** had Ḥarb's own sons bound in place so they could not flee — earning
them the name **al-ʿAnābis** ("the lion cubs"); and **Yawm al-Ḥarīrah** at Nakhlah, where Quraysh was
routed except for **Banū Naṣr**, who held firm (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:152).

## 3. The Prophet's ﷺ Own Account of His Part in It
He was present at some of these days, brought along by his uncles. Ibn Saʿd transmits his own words:

> «قَدْ حَضَرْتُهُ- يَعْنِي حَرْبَ الْفِجَارِ- مَعَ عُمُومَتِي، وَرَمَيْتُ فِيهِ بِأَسْهُمٍ، وَمَا أُحِبُّ أَنِّي لَمْ أَكُنْ فَعَلْتُهُ، وَكُنْتُ أَنْبُلُ عَلَىٰ أَعْمَامِي»
> *"I was present at it — meaning the War of al-Fijār — with my uncles, and I shot arrows in it, and I
> do not wish that I had not done so, and I was more skilled than my uncles."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:152)

## 4. The Peace
The following year, Hawāzin and Kinānah had agreed to meet again at ʿUkāẓ. **Ḥarb b. Umayyah**, chief
of Quraysh and Kinānah, held his orphaned ward **ʿUtbah b. Rabīʿah** back from going out with him,
fearing for him — so ʿUtbah went without leave, and appeared on his camel between the two ranks
calling: *"O Muḍar, why do you destroy yourselves?"* He proposed peace: Quraysh and Kinānah would pay
blood-money for Hawāzin's dead in exchange for pardon of their own. Asked for surety, he offered
himself as a hostage; asked who he was, he answered, *"ʿUtbah b. Rabīʿah b. ʿAbd Shams."* Both sides
agreed, and Quraysh and Kinānah handed over **forty hostages**, among them **Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām**. When
Hawāzin saw the hostages secured, they forgave the bloodshed and released them, and the war ended
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:152).

Al-Ṣāliḥī closes with a saying remembered of the peace: *"none of Quraysh rose to leadership while
poor, save ʿUtbah and [[Abū Ṭālib]] — for the two of them rose to it without wealth."*
