# Ghazwat al-Qādisiyyah (معركة القادسية)
*The Decisive Epoch of Mesopotamia — The Four Epic Days of Combat, The Slaying of Rostam, and The Fall of the Sasanian Empire*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Sources
> Anchored in *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 3, pp. 62–63, entry no. 3202 of **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁) and *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād* by Imām al-Ṣāliḥī (Volume 11, pp. 275, 316).

---

## 1. Strategic Context & The Mandate of Caliph ʿUmar

In the year fifteen AH, as the Sasanian Persian Empire mobilized its final, colossal imperial host under its military regent **Rostam Farrokhzād** to purge the Muslims from Iraq, Caliph **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ initially resolved to personally lead the army from Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

The senior consultative council—led by **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** and **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁—firmly advised the Caliph to remain in Medina to preserve the political center of gravity, stating: *"Send a man of iron will and proven martial genius."* ʿAbd al-Raḥmān suggested: *«قَدْ وَجَدْتُهُ: الْأَسَدُ فِي عَرِينِهِ، سَعْدُ بْنُ مَالِكٍ الزُّهْرِيُّ!»* (*"I have found him: the lion in his lair, Saʿd b. Mālik al-Zuhrī!"*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

ʿUmar appointed **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁ as supreme commander over the armies of Iraq (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

---

## 2. Diplomatic Summons & The Prophetic Vision

Before the commencement of hostilities at al-Qādisiyyah, Saʿd dispatched delegations of noble Companions—including al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah, Ribʿī b. ʿĀmir, and Ḥudhayfah b. Miḥṣan—to the royal pavilion of Rostam (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62). 

Entering the jeweled pavilion dressed in coarse garments and barefoot, Ribʿī b. ʿĀmir articulated the eternal mission of Islam:
> «اللَّهُ ابْتَعَثَنَا لِنُخْرِجَ مَنْ شَاءَ مِنْ عِبَادَةِ الْعِبَادِ إِلَى عِبَادَةِ اللَّهِ، وَمِنْ ضِيقِ الدُّنْيَا إِلَى سِعَتِهَا، وَمِنْ جَوْرِ الْأَدْيَانِ إِلَى عَدْلِ الْإِسْلَامِ!»
> *"Allāh has sent us to deliver whoever wills from the servitude of creation to the servitude of Allāh alone, and from the constrictions of this worldly life to its vastness, and from the oppression of false religions to the absolute justice of Islam!"*

Rostam rejected the call to faith and chose war.

---

## 3. The Four Epic Days of Combat

During the battle, Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ was severely afflicted with painful boils and sciatica (*ʿirq al-nasā*), rendering him unable to ride a horse or sit upright. He directed the battle from the upper balcony of the fortress of Qudays, resting upon a pillow on his chest and throwing tactical directives down to his deputies (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

The battle raged across four intense phases:
1. **Yawm Armath (The First Day)**: The Persians deployed thirty-three armored war elephants that terrified the Arab horses and broke the lines of Banū Bajīlah; Saʿd dispatched Banū Asad and skilled archers who shot at the elephants' trunks and unseated the towers.
2. **Yawm Aghwāth (The Second Day)**: Elite reinforcements from Syria arrived under **al-Qaʿqāʿ b. ʿAmr al-Tamīmī**; al-Qaʿqāʿ dressed camels in wooden frames and hanging fabrics to look like terrifying monstrosities, spooking the Persian horses and evening the battlefield.
3. **Yawm ʿImās (The Third Day)**: The Persian elephants returned, led by an enormous white beast; al-Qaʿqāʿ and ʿĀṣim b. ʿAmr blinded the lead elephants with long spears and severed their trunks, causing the panicked beasts to trample their own Persian infantry.
4. **Laylat al-Harīr (The Night of Clashing Arms)**: A nocturnal battle of hand-to-hand combat where neither side could hear commands above the clashing of swords and armored shields.

---

## 4. The Final Rout & Death of Rostam (Yawm al-Qādisiyyah)

On the fourth morning, a fierce desert sandstorm arose blowing directly into the faces of the Persian lines. 

Al-Qaʿqāʿ led a decisive cavalry breakthrough straight into the imperial throne pavilion. Rostam fled his collapsed pavilion, shed his armor, and jumped into the river ʿAtīq, but was pursued and killed by Hilāl b. ʿUllafah al-Taymī (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

Hilāl ascended Rostam's imperial throne and shouted across the plains:
> «قَتَلْتُ رُسْتُمَ وَرَبِّ الْكَعْبَةِ! إِلَيَّ! إِلَيَّ!»
> *"By the Lord of the Kaʿbah, I have slain Rostam! Come to me! Come to me!"*

Hearing this, panic dissolved the Persian ranks; their army broke into wild flight. Thousands drowned in the canals, while the jewel-encrusted Persian national standard (*Derafsh Kaviani*) was captured (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

---

## 5. Strategic Aftermath & The Fall of Ctesiphon

The victory at al-Qādisiyyah was the decisive death blow to Sasanian Mesopotamia (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62):
- Saʿd marched his army directly to the Persian capital of **Ctesiphon (*al-Madāʾin*)**, miraculously crossed the flooded Tigris River on horseback, and entered the White Palace of Kisrā (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).
- The battle led directly to the founding of the metropolitan garrison of **Kūfah** in 17 AH under Saʿd's direction (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).
