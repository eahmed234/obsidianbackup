# ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr (عبد الله بن الزبير)
*Firstborn of the Emigrants in Medina, The Knight of the Kaʿbah, and Lion of Quraysh*

## 1. Lineage, Parents, and Auspicious Birth (1 AH)

ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām b. Khuwaylid b. Asad b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. Quṣayy al-Asadī al-Qurashī, Abū Bakr and Abū Khubayb (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:78). 

His father was the Hawārī of the Prophet ﷺ, [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]] ﵁; his mother was Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr (*Dhāt al-Niṭāqayn*); his maternal grandfather was [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]] ﵁; and his maternal aunt was Mother of the Believers [[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]] ﵂ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:78).

He held the singular historical honor of being the very first child born to the [[al-Muhājirūn|Muhājirūn]] in Medina following the Hijrah (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:79). 

The Jews of Medina had spread rumors that they had cast spells of sorcery upon the Muslim emigrants so that they would never bear children in Medina. When Asmāʾ reached [[Qubāʾ]] and gave birth to ʿAbdullāh, the entire Muslim community erupted into joyous shouts of *Takbīr* (*Allāhu Akbar*) that reverberated across Medina, shattering the superstitious claims (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:79–80).

## 2. Tahnik and Prophetic Blessing

Immediately after birth, his mother brought him to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in the mosque and placed the infant into his lap (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:79). 

The Prophet ﷺ called for a date, chewed it until it became soft, and placed a morsel of it into the infant's mouth. Thus, the very first substance that entered ʿAbdullāh's stomach was the blessed saliva of the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:79). 

The Prophet ﷺ stroked his head, supplicated for abundant blessings upon him, and named him ʿAbdullāh after his grandfather Abū Bakr, giving him his grandfather's kunya Abū Bakr (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:79).

## 3. Oath of Allegiance in Boyhood

When ʿAbdullāh was about seven or eight years of age, his father al-Zubayr brought him to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to swear allegiance (*bayʿah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:79–80). 

When a group of young Qurashī boys gathered before the Prophet ﷺ and momentarily hesitated, ʿAbdullāh leaped forward boldly without fear to grasp the Prophet's hand. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ smiled warmly at his intrepidity and remarked:
> «إنه ابن أبيه!»
> *"Indeed, he is the true son of his father!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:80).

ʿAbdullāh was nine years old when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ passed away, having memorized and retained numerous direct teachings and hadiths from the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:80).

## 4. Renowned Courage and Devotion

ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr became celebrated across Islamic history as one of the four renowned *ʿAbādilah* (scholarly ʿAbdullāhs), recognized for profound juridical acumen, unyielding courage on the battlefield, and legendary absorption in prayer (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:78). 

He distinguished himself in the major conquests of North Africa under ʿAbdullāh b. Saʿd b. Abī Sarḥ, where he personally charged through the Byzantine guard and slew the Byzantine patrician Gregory (Jurjayr), turning the tide of the campaign.

## 5. Later Caliphate and Martyrdom (73 AH)

Following the death of Yazīd b. Muʿāwiyah in 64 AH, the people of the Ḥijāz, Iraq, Yemen, and majority of the Islamic lands pledged allegiance to ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr as Caliph (*Amīr al-Muʾminīn*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:79). He governed from [[Makkah al-Mukarramah|Makkah]] with piety, rebuilding the sacred Kaʿbah upon the foundational stones of Ibrāhīm ﵊.

In 73 AH, during the siege of Makkah by the Umayyad general al-Ḥajjāj b. Yūsuf, ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr fought heroically around the sanctuary until he was martyred beside the Kaʿbah at the age of seventy-two.
