# ʿAdī b. Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī (عدي بن حاتم الطائي)
*Abū Ṭarīf al-Ṭāʾī — Paramount chieftain of Ṭayyiʾ, son of the legendary Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī, recipient of prophetic prophecies regarding the security of the pilgrim caravan and the treasures of Chosroes, and champion of the conquest of Iraq*

## 1. Lineage & Background

**ʿAdī b. Ḥātim b. ʿAbdillāh b. Saʿd b. al-Ḥashraj b. Imriʾ al-Qays b. ʿAdī al-Ṭāʾī**, known by his kunya **Abū Ṭarīf**, was the son of **Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī**, the most renowned figure of hospitality and generosity in pre-Islamic Arabian history (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388). 

Before Islam, ʿAdī inherited the chieftainship of his tribe, adhered to the Christian faith (*kāna naṣrāniyyan*), and received the customary fourth-share of tribal spoils (**al-mirbāʿ**) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388, 389). When Islam spread, ʿAdī held deep aversion to the new faith and fled to the Byzantine borderlands in Syria when **[[Sariyyat al-Fuls]]** arrived at Jabal Ṭayyiʾ in 9 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388–389; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).

## 2. Emancipation of His Sister & Acceptance of Islam

Following the demolition of the idol of al-Fuls, the Prophet ﷺ honorably emancipated ʿAdī's captive sister, **Saffānah bint Ḥātim**, providing her camel transport to rejoin ʿAdī in Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). 

Moved by her vivid report of the Prophet's unparalleled mercy and modesty, ʿAdī journeyed without safe-conduct or treaty (*bi-ghayri amānin wa-lā kitāb*) directly into the presence of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ in Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388, 389).

When ʿAdī entered the Prophet's Mosque, the Prophet ﷺ rose, took ʿAdī warmly by the hand, and began walking him to his home (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:389). Along the path, an elderly woman and an impoverished child approached the Prophet ﷺ asking for assistance; the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ patiently stood with them until he fulfilled their need (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:389). ʿAdī reflected to himself: *"By Allāh, this is not the manner of an earthly king!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:389).

Upon reaching his humble dwelling, the Prophet ﷺ offered ʿAdī a leather cushion stuffed with palm fiber to sit upon while he himself sat upon the bare floor (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:389). The Prophet ﷺ then addressed ʿAdī's unvoiced spiritual reservations:
> «يا عديّ، أسلم تسلم... أنا أعلم بدينك منك، ألست ترأس قومك؟ ألست تأكل المرباع؟ فإنّ ذلك لا يحلّ لك في دينك»
> *"O ʿAdī, submit in Islam and you shall be safe... I know your religion better than you do! Are you not the chieftain of your people? Do you not consume the fourth of tribal spoils (al-mirbāʿ)? Verily, that is not permissible for you according to your own Christian faith!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:389).

## 3. The Three Great Prophetic Portents

The Prophet ﷺ observed that ʿAdī was hesitant because the Muslims appeared impoverished and surrounded by hostile nations (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:389). The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ said:
> «يوشك أن تخرج الظّعينة من الحيرة بغير جواز حتّى تطوف بالبيت، ولتفتحنّ علينا كنوز كسرى بن هرمز، وليفيضنّ المال حتّى يهمّ الرّجل من يقبل صدقته»
> *"Soon shall come a day when a woman traveler will ride out alone from al-Ḥīrah in Iraq without any armed escort until she circumambulates the Kaʿbah in total peace; and the treasures of Chosroes son of Hormizd shall surely be opened to us; and wealth shall overflow until a man will be anxious to find anyone willing to accept his charity!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:389).

Hearing these miraculous disclosures, ʿAdī accepted Islam wholeheartedly (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388, 389). In his old age in Kūfah, ʿAdī used to recount:
> «فرأيت اثنتين: الظعينة، وكنت في أول خيل أغارت على كنوز كسرى، وأحلف باللَّه لتجيئنّ الثالثة!»
> *"I lived to witness two of them with my own eyes: the solitary woman riding from al-Ḥīrah to Makkah in absolute safety, and I myself rode in the very first cavalry squadron that stormed the treasures of Chosroes; and I swear by Allāh, the third prophecy shall surely come to pass!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:389).

## 4. Steadfastness during the Riddah & Conquest of Iraq

When the apostasy erupted across Arabia after the death of the Prophet ﷺ, ʿAdī b. Ḥātim stood firm upon the Sunnah (*thabata ʿalā Islāmihi fī al-riddah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388). He gathered the full zakāt of Ṭayyiʾ and personally brought it to [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]] ﵁ in Medina, being the very first tribal chieftain to present the zakāt of his people during the crisis (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388).

When ʿAdī later visited **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ during his Caliphate, ʿUmar honored him lavishly, proclaiming before the assembly:
> «نعم، آمنت إذ كفروا، وعرفت إذ أنكروا، ووفيت إذ غدروا، وأقبلت إذ أدبروا! إنّ أول صدقة بيضت وجوه أصحاب رسول اللَّه صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم صدقة طيِّئ!»
> *"Indeed, you believed when others disbelieved; you recognized the truth when others denied it; you fulfilled your covenant when others betrayed; and you advanced when others turned their backs! Verily, the first charity that brightened the faces of the Companions of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was the zakāt of Ṭayyiʾ!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388).

ʿAdī participated as a leading commander throughout the conquest of Iraq under [[Khālid b. al-Walīd]] ﵁ and [[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]] ﵁, fighting with valor at al-Qādisiyyah and al-Madāʾin (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388). He subsequently settled in al-Kūfah, stood steadfast with **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ at the Battle of Ṣiffīn, and lived to a venerable age of over 120 years, dying in al-Kūfah around 68 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388). ʿAdī was renowned for his profound devotion to prayer, stating: *"Never was the prayer established since I accepted Islam except that I was already in a state of ritual purity (*wuḍūʾ*)"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:388).
