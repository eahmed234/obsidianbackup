# ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr (عائشة بنت أبي بكر الصديق) ﵂
*Umm al-Muʾminīn — al-Ṣiddīqah bint al-Ṣiddīq, Ḥabībat Rasūlillāh, Foremost Jurist of the Ummah*

## 1. Lineage & Matrimony
She is ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUthmān) b. ʿĀmir al-Taymiyyah al-Qurashiyyah (kunya: Umm ʿAbdillāh; honorifics: *al-Ṣiddīqah bint al-Ṣiddīq*, *Ḥabībat Rasūlillāh*, *al-Muwarraʿah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:164). Reared from infancy in the monotheistic household of her father **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, she never knew a day where Islam was not practiced in her home (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:164).

Before the marriage, Jibrīl ﵊ presented her likeness to the Prophet ﷺ in a silken cloth in dreams, saying: *“This is your wife in this world and the Hereafter!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:165). She was the only virgin wedded to the Prophet ﷺ. Contracted in marriage in Mecca in Shawwāl, the marriage was consummated in Shawwāl 1 AH in Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:164).

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones
- **Service at Uḥud (3 AH)**: During the crisis of **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, ʿĀʾishah and Umm Sulaym tied up their garments and carried heavy leather waterskins on their backs, pouring water into the mouths of the wounded soldiers and rushing back to the wells to refill them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:207; 11:168).
- **The Great Slander & Divine Vindication (*Ḥadīth al-Ifk*) (5 AH)**: Returning from Ghazwat Banī al-Muṣṭaliq (al-Muraysīʿ), her necklace snapped and she lagged behind to search for it, accidentally being left behind by the camel-drivers who hoisted her covered howdah assuming she was inside (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:65; 11:170). She was brought safely to Medina by **Ṣafwān b. al-Muʿaṭṭal al-Sulamī** ﵁. The chief hypocrite **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** seized upon the incident to invent a malicious slander against her purity (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:65–68).
- ʿĀʾishah endured a month of agonizing illness and sorrow until Allāh personally descended ten timeless verses of divine revelation in **Sūrat al-Nūr** (24:11–20), exonerating her forever and establishing severe punishment for slandering chaste women (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:70–72; 11:170).
- **The Blessed Chamber of the Prophet's Passing (11 AH)**: When the Prophet's terminal illness began, he gently inquired: *“Where am I tomorrow? Where am I tomorrow?”* seeking the dwelling of ʿĀʾishah. His other wives consented to his remaining in her room (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:174; 12:215). The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ passed away with his blessed head resting against her chest, his last earthly taste being the moistened miswāk softened by her mouth (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:174; 12:215). He was buried in the very chamber where he died, beneath her floor.

## 3. Intellectual Preeminence & Legacy
ʿĀʾishah was universally acknowledged by the senior Companions as a supreme scholar, jurist, and master of Arabic oratory, poetry, medicine, and genealogies (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:175). Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī ﵁ attested:
> *"Whenever we, the companions of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, found any hadith difficult to comprehend, we would ask ʿĀʾishah and always find that she possessed deep knowledge concerning it!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:175; *Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī*, no. 3883).
She transmitted 2,210 Prophetic traditions, preserving the private, domestic, and legal sunnah of the household.

## 4. Demise & Burial
She lived four decades after the Prophet's passing, educating scholars from across the expanding Islamic empire. She passed away in Medina on Tuesday, 17 Ramaḍān 57 AH (or 58 AH) at the age of sixty-six. Her funeral prayer was led by **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁, and she was laid to rest at night by torchlight in **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:183).
