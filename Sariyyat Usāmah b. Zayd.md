# Sariyyat Usāmah b. Zayd (سرية أسامة بن زيد إلى أبنى)
*The Expedition of Usāmah b. Zayd to Ubnā — The Final Deployment of the Prophet ﷺ and the First Imperial Triumph of the Caliphate*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 6, *al-Bāb al-Thamānūn fī Sariyyat Usāmah b. Zayd b. Ḥārithah ilā Ubnā* (pp. 248–254), cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah*, 1:202–203; *al-Iṣābah*, 2:497. Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the accounts of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[al-Wāqidī]], and [[Ibn Saʿd]].

---

## 1. Context & Strategic Objectives

Following the Farewell Pilgrimage (**[[Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ]]**) in Dhū al-Ḥijjah 10 AH, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ returned to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248). Throughout the winter months of 11 AH, the memory of the heroic martyrdom of his adopted son **[[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]** ﵁, **[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁, and **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]** ﵁ at the Battle of Muʾtah (**[[Ghazwat Muʾtah]]**, 8 AH) weighed heavily upon his noble heart (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248).

On Monday, four nights remaining in Ṣafar of the year eleven AH, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ ordered the mobilization of a major military expedition against the Byzantine Empire (*al-Rūm*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248). On the following day (Tuesday), the Prophet ﷺ summoned **[[Usāmah b. Zayd]]** ﵁—then a youth of eighteen or twenty years—and formally invested him with supreme command:

> «يَا أُسَامَةُ، سِرْ عَلَى اسْمِ اللَّهِ وَبَرَكَتِهِ حَتَّى تَنْتَهِيَ إِلَى مَقْتَلِ أَبِيكَ فَأَوْطِئْهُمُ الْخَيْلَ! فَقَدْ وَلَّيْتُكَ هَٰذَا الْجَيْشَ، فَأَغِرْ صَبَاحًا عَلَى أَهْلِ أُبْنَى وَحَرِّقْ عَلَيْهِمْ، وَأَسْرِعِ السَّيْرَ تَسْبِقِ الْأَخْبَارَ، فَإِنْ أَظْفَرَكَ اللَّهُ فَأَقْلِلِ اللُّبْثَ فِيهِمْ، وَخُذْ مَعَكَ الْأَدِلَّاءَ، وَقَدِّمِ الْعُيُونَ وَالطَّلَائِعَ أَمَامَكَ!»
> *"O Usāmah! March upon the Name of Allāh and His blessing until you reach the place where your father was slain, and trample them with cavalry! For I have placed you in command of this army. Launch a morning assault upon the folk of Ubnā, set fire to their positions, and hasten your march so as to outstrip the news. If Allāh grants you victory, shorten your stay among them, take reliable guides with you, and send forward scouts and vanguards before you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248).

## 2. The Binding of the Banner & The Rules of Just Engagement

On Wednesday, two nights remaining in Ṣafar, the Prophet's fatal illness began with severe fever and headache (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248). 

Despite his suffering, on Thursday morning the Prophet ﷺ tied the great military banner with his own blessed hands and presented it to **[[Usāmah b. Zayd]]** ﵁, delivering his martial charter (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248):
> «اغْزُ بِسْمِ اللَّهِ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، فَقَاتِلْ مَنْ كَفَرَ بِاللَّهِ! اغْزُوا وَلَا تَغْدِرُوا، وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا وَلِيدًا وَلَا امْرَأَةً، وَلَا تَتَمَنَّوْا لِقَاءَ الْعَدُوِّ، فَإِنَّكُمْ لَا تَدْرُونَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُبْتَلَوْنَ بِهِمْ، وَلَٰكِنْ قُولُوا: اللَّهُمَّ اكْفِنَاهُمْ بِمَا شِئْتَ وَاكْفُفْ بَأْسَهُمْ عَنَّا! فَإِنْ لَقُوكُمْ قَدْ جَلَبُوا وَضَجُّوا فَعَلَيْكُمْ بِالسَّكِينَةِ وَالصَّمْتِ، وَلَا تَنَازَعُوا فَتَفْشَلُوا وَتَذْهَبَ رِيحُكُمْ... وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ الْجَنَّةَ تَحْتَ الْبَارِقَةِ!»
> *"March in the Name of Allāh, in the cause of Allāh, and fight those who disbelieve in Allāh! March and do not betray, do not kill a newborn child nor a woman, and do not wish to encounter the enemy, for you do not know if you may be tested through them; rather say: 'O Allāh, suffice us against them however You will, and withhold their might from us!' And if they encounter you shouting and clamoring, maintain serenity and silence, and do not dispute lest you falter and your strength depart... And know that Paradise lies beneath the flash of swords!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248).

Usāmah marched out of Medina and handed the tied standard to **[[Buraydah b. al-Ḥuṣayb]]** al-Aslamī ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248). The army encamped at **[[al-Jurf]]**, three miles north of Medina.

The muster included the foremost elders of the Muhājirūn and Anṣār: **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁, **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁, **[[Saʿīd b. Zayd]]** ﵁, **[[Qatādah b. al-Nuʿmān]]** ﵁, and **Salamah b. Aslam b. Ḥarīsh** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248).

## 3. The Prophetic Defence of Usāmah's Command

Certain individuals expressed hesitation at appointing an unproven youth of eighteen or twenty years over the venerable senior companions of Badr and Ḥudaybiyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:248; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:202).

When news reached the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, he became exceedingly angered. Despite his intense physical weakness, he walked out with a cloth bound tightly around his head, wrapped in a velvet cloak (*qaṭīfah*), ascended the pulpit (*minbar*), and proclaimed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:202):

> «أَمَّا بَعْدُ أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ، فَمَا مَقَالَةٌ بَلَغَتْنِي عَنْ بَعْضِكُمْ فِي تَأْمِيرِي أُسَامَةَ؟! وَلَئِنْ طَعَنْتُمْ فِي إِمَارَتِي أُسَامَةَ لَقَدْ طَعَنْتُمْ فِي إِمَارَتِي أَبَاهُ مِنْ قَبْلِهِ! وَايْمُ اللَّهِ إِنْ كَانَ لَلْإِمَارَةِ لَخَلِيقًا، وَإِنَّ ابْنَهُ مِنْ بَعْدِهِ لَخَلِيقٌ لِلْإِمَارَةِ، وَإِنْ كَانَ لَمِنْ أَحَبِّ النَّاسِ إِلَيَّ، وَإِنَّهُمَا لَمَخِيلَانِ لِكُلِّ خَيْرٍ، فَاسْتَوْصُوا بِهِ خَيْرًا فَإِنَّهُ مِنْ خِيَارِكُمْ!»
> *"As to what follows, O mankind! What is this talk that has reached me from some of you concerning my appointing Usāmah as commander?! If you criticize my appointing Usāmah, you have already criticized my appointing his father before him! And by Allāh, his father was truly worthy of command, and his son after him is truly worthy of command! Indeed, his father was among the most beloved of people to me, and this one is among the most beloved of people to me after him; both are suited for every good, so receive my counsel to treat him well, for he is among the best of you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249).

The Prophet ﷺ descended and entered his house. The Companions continued joining the camp at al-Jurf. When Usāmah's mother **[[Umm Ayman]]** ﵂ pleaded: *"O Messenger of Allāh, if you would only permit Usāmah to remain in camp until you recover, for if he departs in this state he will not profit himself,"* the Prophet ﷺ insisted: **«أنفذوا بعث أسامة»** (*"Dispatch the army of Usāmah!"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249).

---

## 4. The Last Parting & The Passing of the Prophet ﷺ

On Sunday, the illness overcame the Prophet ﷺ. Usāmah walked from al-Jurf into Medina to visit him. The Prophet ﷺ was unable to speak; he raised his hands to the heavens and gently lowered them upon Usāmah in silent supplication (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249).

On Monday morning (12 Rabīʿ al-Awwal 11 AH), the Prophet ﷺ awoke feeling refreshed. Usāmah entered upon him, and the Prophet ﷺ smiled and commanded him:
> «اغْدُ عَلَى بَرَكَةِ اللَّهِ!»
> *"Depart at dawn upon the blessing of Allāh!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249).

Rejoicing at the Prophet's apparent recovery, Usāmah returned to al-Jurf and ordered the 3,000 warriors to mount. Just as he prepared to ride, a courier arrived from Umm Ayman reporting that the Prophet's condition had turned grave and that he was passing away (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249). Usāmah, ʿUmar, and Abū ʿUbaydah rushed into Medina, arriving as the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ breathed his last (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249). 

The troops returned from al-Jurf to Medina, and Buraydah planted the tied banner beside the door of the Prophet's dwelling (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249).

---

## 5. The Decision of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq ﵁

Following the allegiance at the Portico (**[[Saqīfat Banī Sāʿidah]]**), news arrived that Bedouin tribes across the Peninsula were renouncing Islam. The senior Companions approached the new Caliph **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, counseling him:
> *"The Arabs have rebelled against you; do not send away the sword and armor of the Muslims while Medina stands exposed!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249).

Abū Bakr delivered his historic, unshakeable reply (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:249; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:202):
> «وَالَّذِي لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ، لَوْ جَرَّتِ الْكِلَابُ بِأَرْجُلِ أَزْوَاجِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ مَا رَدَدْتُ جَيْشًا وَجَّهَهُ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ، وَلَا حَلَلْتُ لِوَاءً عَقَدَهُ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ!»
> *"By Him besides Whom there is no deity! Even if wild dogs were to drag the feet of the wives of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, I would never recall an army dispatched by the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, nor untie a banner tied by the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ!"*

Abū Bakr ordered Buraydah to carry the banner back to al-Jurf and issued an imperial decree: *"I resolve that not a single man who was enlisted by the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ shall stay behind; whoever tarries shall be made to march on foot!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250). 

Abū Bakr visited Usāmah in his tent and requested permission to retain **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ in Medina to assist him in governing the state, to which Usāmah graciously agreed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250). 

On the first of Rabīʿ al-Ākhar in the year eleven AH, the Caliph walked on foot beside Usāmah's horse to see the army off, telling him: *"I entrust to Allāh your religion, your trust, and the seals of your deeds. Fulfill the command of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, for I neither command you nor forbid you, but merely execute what the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanded!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).

## 6. The Raid on Ubnā & Decisive Victory

Usāmah marched rapidly along the coastal highway through Juhaynah and Quḍāʿah—tribes that had remained steadfast upon Islam—until he reached Wādī al-Qurā, completing the distance to Ubnā in twenty days (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).

- **Intelligence**: A scout from Banū ʿUdhrah named Ḥurayth rode ahead, discovered that the Byzantine garrisons and local tribes were completely unaware of the approaching force, and returned two nights away from Ubnā urging immediate action (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).
- **The Dawn Attack**: Usāmah organized his battle lines and launched a lightning dawn raid upon Ubnā (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250). 
- **Vindication of Zayd b. Ḥārithah**: Riding his father's renowned war-horse *Sabḥah*, Usāmah located and personally executed the man who had slain his father at Muʾtah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).
- **Destruction of Enemy Depots**: The Muslim troops set fire to enemy encampments, captured livestock and prisoners, and spent the remainder of the day collecting the spoils (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250). Usāmah awarded two shares to each horse and one share to each rider, reserving an identical share for himself (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).

---

## 7. Return to Medina & Strategic Impact

Usāmah ordered an immediate withdrawal to prevent Byzantine reinforcements from assembling. He covered the return to Wādī al-Qurā in nine days, dispatched a mounted messenger to announce their safety to Medina, and arrived outside the capital in six further days (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).

- **Triumphal Reception**: Caliph **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, the Muhājirūn, and the citizens of Medina marched out to meet the returning warriors in jubilation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250). 
- Usāmah entered Medina riding *Sabḥah*, with the victorious white banner borne before him by **[[Buraydah b. al-Ḥuṣayb]]** ﵁ until he reached the Prophet's Mosque, where he prayed two units (*rakʿatayn*) of thanksgiving (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).
- Not a single Muslim was slain in the entire campaign (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).
- **Psychological Deterrence**: The Byzantine Emperor Heraclius, residing in Homs, was stunned by the boldness of the expedition, remarking that the Muslims must possess overwhelming domestic strength to launch an offensive force into Syria immediately upon the death of their Prophet (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250). Heraclius ordered permanent garrison guards stationed at al-Balqāʾ, while wavering Bedouin tribes abandoned ideas of attacking Medina, saying: *"Had these people lacked immense power, their army would never have departed!"*
