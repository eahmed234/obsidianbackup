# Waqʿat Ṣiffīn (وقعة صفين)
*The Confrontation at Ṣiffīn: The Standoff on the Euphrates, the Martyrdom of ʿAmmār b. Yāsir, the Elevation of the Muṣḥafs, and the Arbitration (37 AH)*

## 1. Strategic Context & The Standoff on the Euphrates
Following the restoration of order in southern Iraq, Amīr al-Muʾminīn **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ sought to secure the allegiance of Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān, governor of Syria (*al-Shām*). Muʿāwiyah and the Syrian leadership withheld their pledge of allegiance, insisting that the legal retributive punishment (*qiṣāṣ*) against the murderers of **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ must take absolute procedural precedence before accepting the Caliph's authority (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:468).

In the spring of 37 AH, the armies of Iraq led by ʿAlī and the forces of Syria led by Muʿāwiyah converged at **Ṣiffīn**, a plain along the western bank of the Euphrates river near al-Raqqah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:283).

For several months during Dhū al-Ḥijjah 36 AH and Muḥarram 37 AH, ʿAlī halted major military action, dispatching successive diplomatic delegations to avert civil bloodshed and achieve communal unity (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:149). When these diplomatic efforts reached an impasse, skirmishes commenced in late Muḥarram and escalated into full battle in Ṣafar 37 AH.

---

## 2. The General Engagement & Laylat al-Harīr
Intense combat raged across several days in Ṣafar 37 AH:
- The veteran Companion **[[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]]** ﵁, aged ninety-three, fought in the vanguard of ʿAlī's army, inspiring the companions around him with memories of Badr and the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:474).
- The Iraqi vanguard under al-Ashtar al-Nakhaʿī pressed the Syrian defensive lines relentlessly during an epic nocturnal battle celebrated as **Laylat al-Harīr** ("The Night of Clashing Arms"), driving deep into the opposing camp.

---

## 3. Martyrdom of ʿAmmār b. Yāsir & Prophetic Vindication
During the height of the engagement, **[[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]]** ﵁ was struck down and martyred on the battlefield (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:474).

His martyrdom sent profound shockwaves through both armies because of the universally transmitted (*mutawātir*) Prophetic tradition:
> «وَيْحَ عَمَّارٍ! تَقْتُلُهُ الْفِئَةُ الْبَاغِيَةُ، يَدْعُوهُمْ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ وَيَدْعُونَهُ إِلَى النَّارِ!»
> *"Alas for ʿAmmār! The transgressing faction (*al-fiʾah al-bāghiyah*) shall slay him; he invites them to Paradise, and they invite him to the Fire!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:474; *Subul al-Hudā*, 10:151).

The fulfillment of this prophecy removed any theological ambiguity regarding the rectitude of ʿAlī's position, causing distress in the Syrian ranks and prompting senior commanders like **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** to seek an immediate cessation of hostilities (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:474; *Subul al-Hudā*, 10:151).

---

## 4. The Raising of the Muṣḥafs & The Truce
As the Iraqi forces under al-Ashtar prepared a decisive push, the Syrian army, on the counsel of **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]**, raised leaves of the Holy Qurʾān (*al-Maṣāḥif*) upon the tips of their lances, calling out across the lines:
> *“The Book of Allāh is between us and you! Who will protect the frontiers of the Muslims if the men of Syria and Iraq perish?!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:150).

Although ʿAlī perceived this as a tactical maneuver to avoid military defeat, a substantial contingent of reciters (*al-qurrāʾ*) in his army laid down their weapons, insisting upon immediate submission to the arbitration of the Qurʾān. Constrained by internal mutiny, ʿAlī ordered his forces to disengage and agreed to settle the dispute through peaceful arbitration (*Taḥkīm*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:150).

---

## 5. The Arbitration Agreement (al-Taḥkīm)
An armistice was formally concluded:
- Each side agreed to appoint an arbitrator to judge according to the Book of Allāh and the authentic Sunnah.
- The Syrian forces appointed **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁.
- The Iraqi camp selected **[[Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:150).
- Both armies withdrew to their respective garrison capitals (Kūfah and Damascus), agreeing that the two arbitrators would convene at Adhruḥ (or Dūmat al-Jandal) in 38 AH to deliver their judgment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:150).

While the armistice succeeded in halting the immediate slaughter at Ṣiffīn, the compromise provoked severe theological disaffection among a faction of zealots in ʿAlī's army, who seceded from his ranks and formed the violent **Khawārij** sect (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:150; 11:305).
