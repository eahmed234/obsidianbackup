# Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī (أبو ذر الغفاري جندب بن جنادة) ﵁
*The Truthful Ascetic, Lone Desert Walker, and Fearless Proclaimer of Tawḥīd*

## 1. Lineage, Clan & Physical Description
He is Jundub (or Junaydib) b. Junādah b. Sufyān b. ʿUbayd b. Ḥarām b. Ghifār al-Ghifārī (kunya: Abū Dharr) (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106, no. 9875; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:315). He hailed from the Bedouin tribe of Banū Ghifār (a branch of Kinānah), who inhabited the mountainous caravan routes between Mecca and the Levant (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:315). 

His mother was **Ramlah bint al-Waqīʿah**, also of Banū Ghifār (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106). Through his mother, he was the half-brother of the early Companion **ʿAmr b. ʿAbasah** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106). His full brother was **Unays b. Junādah al-Ghifārī**, a renowned poet (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106).

In physical appearance, Abū Dharr was tall (*ṭawīlan*), brown-complexioned (*asmar al-lawn*), gaunt, and lean of build (*maʿrūq ādam naḥīfan*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107; Musnad Yaʿqūb b. Shaybah). He was known to wear a simple striped Qaṭarī cloak (*ḥullah qaṭariyyah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107).

## 2. Monotheistic Instinct & Conversion in Mecca
Even during the pre-Islamic Jāhiliyyah, Abū Dharr had discarded idolatry by natural monotheistic disposition (*fiṭrah*), performing prayer towards wherever Allāh directed him years before the Prophetic mission (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*).

When news reached Banū Ghifār that a man had arisen in Mecca proclaiming divine revelation, Abū Dharr dispatched his brother Unays to investigate (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106). Unays returned reporting: *"I found him enjoining noble character, and uttering speech that is not poetry"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106). Unsatisfied with second-hand reports, Abū Dharr equipped himself with a water-skin and traveled covertly to Mecca (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106).

### The Two Canonical Conversion Accounts
Classical authorities record his encounter with the Prophet ﷺ in two complementary narratives:
1. **The Account of Ibn ʿAbbās (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*)**: Arriving at the Sacred Mosque without knowing the Prophet's appearance, Abū Dharr rested there at nightfall. **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ noticed he was a stranger and took him home as a guest. For three consecutive nights, ʿAlī hosted him without inquiring about his business. On the third day, Abū Dharr disclosed his quest under a pledge of confidentiality. ʿAlī guided him directly behind him to the Prophet's presence, where Abū Dharr listened to his words and immediately embraced Islam as the fourth or fifth believer (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106–107; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:315). Although the Prophet ﷺ instructed him to conceal his faith and return to his clan, Abū Dharr marched straight into the Sacred Sanctuary and shouted the Shahādah aloud. The polytheists of Quraysh swarmed upon him, beating him until **[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** intervened, throwing himself over Abū Dharr and warning Quraysh: *"Woe to you! Do you not know that he is of Ghifār, and your trade caravans to Syria must pass through their lands?!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:106–107; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:315–316). The next day, Abū Dharr repeated his public proclamation and was again rescued by al-ʿAbbās (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107).
2. **The Account of ʿAbdullāh b. al-Ṣāmit (*Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*)**: Inquiring boldly in Mecca about *"the one you call al-Ṣābiʾ"*, the Meccans assaulted him with stones until he bled profusely like a red sacrificial stone (*nuṣub aḥmar*). Abū Dharr concealed himself between the Kaʿbah and its drapes for fifteen days and nights, subsisting entirely upon Zamzam water (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107). One night, the Prophet ﷺ and **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ came to perform circumambulation. Abū Dharr emerged and was the very first person in history to salute the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ with the greeting of Islam: «السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ» (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107). The Prophet ﷺ answered: «وَعَلَيْكَ السَّلَامُ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ»، then inquired of his lineage. When Abū Dharr named Banū Ghifār, Abū Bakr requested the honor of hosting him and fed him handfuls of Taif raisins (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107). Returning to his homeland, Abū Dharr brought his brother Unays, their mother Ramlah, and half of the entire tribe of Banū Ghifār into the fold of Islam (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:317).

## 3. Key Sīrah Milestones
- **Dwellings among Ahl al-Ṣuffah**: He remained among his tribe preaching until after the Battle of the Trench, whereupon he migrated to Medina and resided among the ascetic, impoverished companions of **[[Ahl al-Ṣuffah]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:317).
- **Paragon of Truthfulness**: The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ conferred upon him the supreme testimony of uprightness and sincere speech:
  > «مَا أَظَلَّتِ الْخَضْرَاءُ وَلَا أَقَلَّتِ الْغَبْرَاءُ مِنْ ذِي لَهْجَةٍ أَصْدَقَ مِنْ أَبِي ذَرٍّ»
  > *"No greener sky has shaded, and no dustier earth has carried, one with a tongue more truthful than Abū Dharr!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:452; 10:208; *al-Iṣābah*, 7:108).
- **Prophetic Solitary March at [[Ghazwat Tabūk]] (9 AH)**: During the intense summer expedition to Tabūk, Abū Dharr's emaciated camel collapsed. Rather than turning back, he loaded his provisions onto his own back and marched entirely on foot through the blistering desert. As a solitary figure appeared on the horizon shimmering through the heat mirage, the Prophet ﷺ exclaimed: *“Be Abū Dharr!”* Recognizing him, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ uttered his renowned prophecy (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:109; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:452):
  > «يَرْحَمُ اللَّهُ أَبَا ذَرٍّ: يَمْشِي وَحْدَهُ، وَيَمُوتُ وَحْدَهُ، وَيُبْعَثُ وَحْدَهُ!»
  > *"May Allāh have mercy upon Abū Dharr: he walks alone, he dies alone, and he will be resurrected alone!"*
- **Scholarly Preeminence**: Imām Abū Dāwūd recorded that although he missed Badr while preaching in the desert, Caliph ʿUmar affixed him to the highest stipend category of the Badriyyūn on account of his foremost conversion; he was recognized as equal to **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd]]** ﵁ in profound religious knowledge (*kāna yuwāzī Ibn Masʿūd fī al-ʿilm*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:109). Abū al-Dardāʾ ﵁ narrated that whenever Abū Dharr was present in an assembly, the Prophet ﷺ would initiate conversation with him, and whenever he was absent, the Prophet ﷺ would search for him (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:107).

## 4. Asceticism & Demise at al-Rabadhah
Abū Dharr held passionately to the Prophet's injunction: *«Indeed, the closest of you to me in station on the Day of Resurrection is the one who departs from this world in the identical state in which I left him»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:108; Musnad Aḥmad). 

In his final years, he retired from public life to live in austere, ascetic simplicity at al-Rabadhah, an outpost in the desert east of Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:109; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:453). He passed away there in the year thirty-one AH (or thirty-two AH, according to the majority of authorities) (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:109). At the moment of his death, his wife wept because she had no cloth sufficient to shroud him. Abū Dharr comforted her with the Prophet's prophecy that a group of righteous believers would come across him. Within moments, a caravan arriving from Iraq arrived, bearing **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd]]** ﵁ and a group of companions. Ibn Masʿūd wept over his departed brother, shrouded him, led the funeral prayer, and buried him in the desert sands, before returning to Medina where he died shortly thereafter (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:109; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:453).
