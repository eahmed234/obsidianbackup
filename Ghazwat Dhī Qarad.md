# Ghazwat Dhī Qarad (غزوة ذي قرد)
*The Expedition of the Forest — The Lone Sprint of Ibn al-Akwaʿ and the Chivalry of Sayyid al-Fursān*

## 1. Strategic Context & Provocation
In 6 AH, forty mounted desert raiders belonging to Banū Fazārah and **[[Ghaṭafān]]**, commanded by ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿUyaynah b. Ḥiṣn (or Ḥabīb b. ʿUyaynah), launched a predatory dawn raid against the pasture-lands of Medina at **al-Ghābah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:95).

The raiders ambushed the pasture where twenty milch-camels belonging to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ were grazing. They murdered the Prophet's herdsman—Dhakkār, the son of **[[Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī]]** ﵁—captured his wife Laylā, and drove off the entire herd (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:337; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:95).

## 2. The Lone Foot Pursuit of Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ
At dawn, the young champion runner **[[Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ]]** ﵁ was walking toward the pastures of al-Ghābah with his bow and quiver, accompanying a servant of Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh leading Ṭalḥah's warhorse (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:95).

Discovering the raiders driving off the camels, Salamah instructed the servant: *«Take this horse, gallop to Medina, and alert the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:95). Salamah then ascended the prominent crest of **Thaniyyat al-Wadāʿ**, turned toward Medina, and shouted the ancient alarm of war three times with his booming voice:
> «يَا صَبَاحَاهْ! يَا صَبَاحَاهْ! يَا صَبَاحَاهْ!»
> *"O catastrophe of dawn! Awaken to the raid!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:95).

Without waiting for reinforcements, Salamah sprinted alone on foot across the desert in pursuit of the forty horsemen (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:96). Utilizing his peerless foot-speed—which allowed him to outrun horses on open ground—he used the rocky volcanic ridges (*al-ḥirār*) and acacia trees to cut off their turns (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:127; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:96).

Whenever a horseman wheeled around to attack him, Salamah scaled a boulder and rained arrows upon him, chanting his celebrated anthem:
> خُذْهَا وَأَنَا ابْنُ الأَكْوَعِ ... وَاليَوْمُ يَوْمُ الرُّضَّعِ!
> *"Take this! For I am Ibn al-Akwaʿ! And today is the day of doom for the base!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:96).

His arrows struck their saddles and horses with deadly precision. Whenever their cavalry charged up a slope, he bounded down the rocks; whenever they descended into the gully, he scaled the cliffs (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:96). 

He harassed them so mercilessly that the raiders were forced to abandon all twenty stolen camels (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:96). Continuing his pursuit, Salamah forced them to lighten their mounts by discarding over **thirty cloaks (*burdah*)** and **thirty spears**, planting markers with stones on each item so the advancing Muslims could collect them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:96).

When the exhausted raiders halted at the watering basin of **Dhū Qarad** at midday to drink, Salamah took position on the surrounding crags and showered them with arrows, preventing a single man or horse from tasting a drop of water (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:97).

## 3. The Cavalry Counter-Attack & Martyrdom of Muḥraz
Back in Medina, the alarm sounded across the streets. The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ ordered the horsemen of the city to mobilize immediately (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:97).

The advance vanguard of the cavalry galloped into the thickets of al-Ghābah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:97):
1. **[[Muḥraz b. Naḍlah]]** ﵁ (al-Akhram), mounted upon *al-Janāḥ*, the horse of ʿUkkāshah b. Miḥṣan.
2. **[[Abū Qatādah al-Anṣārī]]** ﵁, the supreme knight of Banū Salimah.
3. **[[al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr]]** ﵁.

As Muḥraz dashed ahead, Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ seized the reins of his horse, pleading: *«O Akhram! Beware of them, lest they encircle and cut you off before the Prophet and the army arrive!»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:582). Muḥraz replied with sublime conviction:
> «يَا سَلَمَةُ! إِنْ كُنْتَ تُؤْمِنُ بِاللهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الآخِرِ، وَتَعْلَمُ أَنَّ الْجَنَّةَ حَقٌّ، وَالنَّارَ حَقٌّ، فَلَا تَحُلْ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَ الشَّهَادَةِ!»
> *"O Salamah! If you believe in Allāh and the Last Day, and know that Paradise is real and the Fire is real, do not come between me and martyrdom!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:582; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:98).

Salamah released his grip. Muḥraz charged directly into the enemy lines and clashed with the raider chieftain, **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿUyaynah al-Fazārī** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:98). Muḥraz struck and hamstrung ʿAbd al-Raḥmān's horse, but ʿAbd al-Raḥmān speared Muḥraz through the chest, and Muḥraz fell as the sole Muslim martyr of the day (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:582; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:98).

Seconds later, **[[Abū Qatādah al-Anṣārī]]** ﵁ rode up, charged ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, and ran his spear through him, slaying the enemy commander on the spot (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:582; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:98). The surviving raiders panicked, abandoned their remaining gear, and fled into the wastes of Najd (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:98).

## 4. Arrival of the Prophet ﷺ & The Elevation of the Champions
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ arrived at the watering place of Dhū Qarad at the head of five hundred warriors, encamping beside the recovered livestock (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:99).

Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ approached the Prophet ﷺ brimming with martial enthusiasm, pleading:
> «يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، خَلِّنِي فِي مِائَةِ رَجُلٍ فَآخُذَ بِكِظَامِهِمْ، فَلَا يَبْقَى مِنْهُمْ مُخْبِرٌ إِلَّا قَتَلْتُهُ!»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh! Release me with one hundred horsemen and I will seize them by their throats, so that not a single soul among them survives to tell the tale!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:99).

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ laughed until his molars were visible (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:99). He placed his hand on Salamah and said with prophetic wisdom:
> «يَا ابْنَ الأَكْوَعِ، مَلَكْتَ فَأَسْجِحْ! إِنَّهُمُ الآنَ لَيُقْرَوْنَ فِي أَرْضِ غَطَفَانَ»
> *"O Ibn al-Akwaʿ! You have attained power, so show clemency (*malakta fa-asjiḥ*)! Indeed, they are being hosted right now in the lands of Ghaṭafān!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:99).

The Prophet ﷺ then assembled the entire army and issued his immortal martial grading:
> «خَيْرُ فُرْسَانِنَا اليَوْمَ أَبُو قَتَادَةَ، وَخَيْرُ رَجَّالَتِنَا سَلَمَةُ»
> *"The best of our cavalrymen today is [[Abū Qatādah al-Anṣārī|Abū Qatādah]], and the best of our infantrymen is [[Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ]]!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:99).

The Prophet awarded Salamah two full shares of the spoils—the share of a mounted knight and the share of a foot soldier (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:100). Upon the army's triumphant return to Medina, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ seated Salamah behind himself upon his own sacred she-camel, **al-ʿAḍbāʾ** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:100).

## 5. The Escape of the Captive Woman & The Vow
During the raid, the captured wife of the slain shepherd had escaped from her captors under cover of night, mounted the Prophet's she-camel *al-ʿAḍbāʾ*, and successfully guided her back to the Muslim camp at Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:101).

Upon arriving safely, the woman declared before the people: *«I made a solemn vow to Allāh that if He delivered me upon this camel, I would slaughter her as a sacrifice of thanksgiving to Him!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:101).

When the matter was brought to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, he smiled gently and rebuked the vow with foundational Islamic jurisprudence:
> «بِئْسَمَا جَزَيْتِهَا! لَا وَفَاءَ لِنَذْرٍ فِي مَعْصِيَةِ اللهِ، وَلَا فِيمَا لَا يَمْلِكُ ابْنُ آدَمَ!»
> *"What an evil reward you have given her! There is no fulfillment of any vow made in disobedience to Allāh, nor in that which the child of Adam does not own!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:101).

The Prophet ﷺ reclaimed his camel and treated the woman with mercy and honor (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:101).
