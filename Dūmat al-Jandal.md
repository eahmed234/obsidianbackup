# Dūmat al-Jandal (دومة الجندل)
*The Northern Oasis Stronghold — The Gateway to Syria and the Imperial Frontier*

## 1. Topography & Strategic Importance
Dūmat al-Jandal is a heavily fortified northern oasis city in the al-Jawf basin, situated approximately fifteen nights' journey north of Medina along the southern fringes of the Syrian desert (*Bādiyat al-Shām*). In the strategic assessment of the Sīrah, it formed the southern gate to the Syrian provinces of the Byzantine Empire:
> *"The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ resolved to approach the closest borders of Syria. It was said to him: 'It is a frontier gateway into Syria; should you approach it, it would strike terror into Caesar [Qayṣar]'..."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342).

The stronghold dominated the commercial transit lines between the Arabian interior, Iraq, and Byzantine Levant, inhabited primarily by the powerful confederacy of **[[Banū Kalb]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342; 6:94).

## 2. Ghazwat Dūmat al-Jandal (Rabīʿ al-Awwal 5 AH)
In Rabīʿ al-Awwal 5 AH, intelligence reached Medina that a hostile gathering at Dūmat al-Jandal was plundering caravans, oppressing travelers, and preparing to march against Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). 

The Prophet ﷺ mobilized one thousand Muslim warriors, marching by night and concealing the force by day under the guidance of a skilled tracker named Madhkūr from Banū ʿUdhrah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). Upon learning of the swift prophetic advance, the polytheist coalition scattered into the surrounding wastes, abandoning their cattle and flocks, establishing early Muslim deterrence on the northern frontiers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342–343).

## 3. The Mission of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf and Khālid's Expedition
Dūmat al-Jandal subsequently witnessed two landmark operations:
- **[[Sariyyat Dūmat al-Jandal]] (Shaʿbān 6 AH)**: The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ appointed **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁ at the head of seven hundred men, personally binding a black turban upon his head and instructing him to invite Banū Kalb to Islam for three days (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:94). On the third day, their Christian monarch al-Aṣbagh b. ʿAmr al-Kalbī embraced Islam alongside large segments of his tribe, cemented by ʿAbd al-Raḥmān's marriage to al-Aṣbagh's daughter Tumāḍir (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:94–95).
- **Subjugation of Ukaydir (Rajab 9 AH)**: During **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]**, the Prophet ﷺ dispatched **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁ with 420 horsemen to seize the fortress of Prince Ukaydir b. ʿAbd al-Malik al-Kindī. Khālid captured Ukaydir while he was hunting wild oryx beneath the moonlit castle walls, bringing him to the Prophet ﷺ to conclude a peace treaty based upon the jizyah poll-tax (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:220–223).
