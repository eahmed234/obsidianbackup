# Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad (غزوة حمراء الأسد)
*The Expedition of Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad — The Pursuit of Moral Deterrence and the Stand of the Wounded*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived exclusively from ***[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād fī Sīrat Khayr al-ʿIbād]]*** by **[[al-Ṣāliḥī|Imām Shams al-Dīn Muḥammad b. Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī al-Shāmī]]** (d. 942 AH), Volume 4, *al-Bāb al-Rābiʿ ʿAshar fī Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad* (pp. 308–316). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the primary accounts of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[Mūsā b. ʿUqbah]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], [[al-Bukhārī]], and [[Muslim]].

---

## 1. Strategic Context & Cause of Departure

On the dawn of Sunday, the 8th of Shawwāl 3 AH — the morning immediately following the traumatic trial of **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]** — the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanded the public caller to summon the believers to arms (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308).

The Prophet's primary strategic objectives were to terrorize the enemy (*murhiban li-l-ʿaduww*), convince Quraysh that the Muslims retained supreme offensive capability and vigor (*anna bihim quwwatan*), and prevent a potential counter-assault upon Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308). To achieve pure psychological deterrence and test spiritual sincerity, the Prophet ﷺ decreed an unbending restriction:
> «لَا يَخْرُجْ مَعَنَا إِلَّا مَنْ شَهِدَ الْقِتَالَ بِالْأَمْسِ!»
> *"No one shall march out with us except those who witnessed combat yesterday!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308).

When the hypocrite chieftain **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** requested permission to join, the Prophet ﷺ flatly refused him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:309). The only companion granted a dispensation was **[[Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh]]** ﵁, who explained that his father **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām]]** ﵁ had ordered him to stay behind at Uḥud to care for his seven sisters; the Prophet ﷺ accepted his excuse and permitted him to march (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308).

---

## 2. The Heroic Mobilization of the Wounded

The Companions responded instantly despite their exhaustion, pain, and grievous injuries (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308–309). Among them:
- **Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr** ﵁: Received seven wounds at Uḥud; when summoned, he exclaimed: *«We hear and obey Allāh and His Messenger!»*, seized his weapon without tending to his wounds, and joined the ranks (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308).
- **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁: Brought thirty provision-camels loaded with dates and slaughtered cattle to sustain the force (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:310).
- **ʿAbdullāh and Rāfiʿ, sons of Sahl** (Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal): Both severely injured, with ʿAbdullāh bearing the heavier wounds; lacking a riding mount, ʿAbdullāh carried Rāfiʿ on his back for a stretch, and they crawled forward together (*yatajārrān*) until they reached the Muslim camp at nightfall (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:310). The Prophet ﷺ prayed for them and commended their fortitude.
- **Leading figures**: The advance vanguard included seventy prominent veterans, among them **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁, **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁, **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁, **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁, **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁, and **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:313–314).

The Prophet ﷺ entrusted the civil governance of Medina during this campaign to the blind companion **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Umm Maktūm]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308).

---

## 3. The Encampment at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad & The Five Hundred Fires

The army advanced eight miles south to **[[Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad]]**, establishing an entrenched encampment on the heights overlooking the road to Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:310).

During the day, the Prophet ﷺ ordered the soldiers to gather firewood across the desert plains. At sunset, he commanded each man to ignite a separate bonfire. Across the plateau, **five hundred fires** blazed each night (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:310). The spectacle was visible across vast distances, broadcasting to every nomadic clan and traveler that the Muslim host was vast, triumphant, and actively hunting the enemy (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:310).

Two Muslim scouts dispatched from the tribe of Aslam — Salīṭ and Nuʿmān, sons of Sufyān b. Ṭalq — overtook the Meccan rear-guard at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, but were surrounded and martyred. When the Prophet ﷺ arrived, he buried the two brothers side by side in a single grave, honoring them as *al-Qarīnayn* (the Joined Pair) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:310).

---

## 4. Psychological Warfare at al-Rawḥāʾ

At **al-Rawḥāʾ** (thirty-six miles south of Medina), the Meccan leadership halted. Discontent had erupted among their chiefs; they began reproaching one another, arguing:
> *"You struck down their champions, shattered their lines, and left their Prophet wounded; yet you turned back before finishing them off or entering their city! Return and let us exterminate the remainder!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311).

Ṣafwān b. Umayyah warned them against this course: *«O my people, do not do this! For the Muslims are now enraged; those who stayed behind will join them, and they will fight with fury! Return while you have the upper hand!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311).

At that critical juncture, **[[Maʿbad b. Abī Maʿbad al-Khuzāʿī]]** of **[[Banū Khuzāʿah]]** arrived at al-Rawḥāʾ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:310–311). Having witnessed the Muslim mobilization at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, Maʿbad confronted **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** with an alarming description:
> *"Muḥammad has marched out seeking you with a host the like of which I have never seen! They are burning with fury against you, and every warrior of the Aws and Khazraj who missed yesterday's battle has joined him in profound regret. By Allāh, I do not believe you will even mount your camels before you see the forelocks of their cavalry!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311).

Maʿbad recited verses describing the ferocious army whose rumblings caused the earth to shake (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311). Terror seized Abū Sufyān and his generals; abandoning all plans to attack Medina, the Meccan army broke camp in panic and fled south to Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311).

To save face, Abū Sufyān hired a passing trading caravan from ʿAbd al-Qays, promising to load their camels with raisins at ʿUkāẓ if they delivered a threatening message to Muḥammad ﷺ: *«Tell him we have assembled our forces to return and exterminate him and his companions»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311). When the riders delivered the threat to the Prophet ﷺ at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, the Muslims responded with unshakable faith:
> **«حَسْبُنَا اللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ»**
> *"Allāh is sufficient for us, and He is the best Disposer of affairs!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311).

## 5. Captures and Executions of Enemy Agents

During the encampment, two prominent polytheist antagonists were captured and brought before the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311–312):

1. **Abū ʿAzzah al-Jumaḥī (ʿAmr b. ʿAbdillāh)**: A poet captured at Badr and released without ransom after swearing never to fight the Muslims again. He broke his oath, inciting the tribes and accompanying the Meccan army to Uḥud (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:312). Captured at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, he pleaded: *«O Messenger of Allāh, forgive me!»* The Prophet ﷺ replied:
   > «وَاللَّهِ لَا تَمْسَحُ عَارِضَيْكَ بِمَكَّةَ وَتَقُولُ: خَدَعْتُ مُحَمَّدًا مَرَّتَيْنِ! إِنَّ الْمُؤْمِنَ لَا يُلْدَغُ مِنْ جُحْرٍ مَرَّتَيْنِ!»
   > *"By Allāh, you shall not wipe your cheeks in Makkah boasting: 'I tricked Muḥammad twice!' Indeed, the believer is not stung from the same hole twice!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:312).
   He commanded **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ (or ʿĀṣim b. Thābit ﵁) to execute him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:312).

2. **Muʿāwiyah b. al-Mughīrah b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ**: A Qurashī spy who had taken refuge in Medina with **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁. ʿUthmān obtained a three-day safe conduct for him from the Prophet ﷺ to depart Medina. He lingered beyond the deadline to spy on Muslim troop movements; the Prophet ﷺ sent **[[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]]** ﵁, who tracked him down and executed him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:311–312).

---

## 6. Divine Revelation & Return to Medina

The Prophet ﷺ remained at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad for three days (Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday), returning victoriously to Medina on Thursday having completely restored Muslim strategic superiority (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:312).

Regarding this expedition, Allāh revealed the immortal praise in **[[Sūrat Āl ʿImrān]]**:
$$\text{الَّذِينَ اسْتَجَابُوا لِلَّهِ وَالرَّسُولِ مِن بَعْدِ مَا أَصَابَهُمُ الْقَرْحُ ۚ لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا مِنْهُمْ وَاتَّقَوْا أَجْرٌ عَظِيمٌ}$$
$$\text{الَّذِينَ قَالَ لَهُمُ النَّاسُ إِنَّ النَّاسَ قَدْ جَمَعُوا لَكُمْ فَاخْشَوْهُمْ فَزَادَهُمْ إِيمَانًا وَقَالُوا حَسْبُنَا اللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ}$$
$$\text{فَانقَلَبُوا بِنِعْمَةٍ مِّنَ اللَّهِ وَفَضْلٍ لَّمْ يَمْسَسْهُمْ سُوءٌ وَاتَّبَعُوا رِضْوَانَ اللَّهِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ ذُو فَضْلٍ عَظِيمٍ}$$
> *"Those who responded to Allāh and the Messenger after injury had afflicted them—for those who did good among them and feared Allāh is a great reward. Those to whom the people said: 'Indeed, the people have gathered against you, so fear them!' But it only increased them in faith, and they said: 'Sufficient for us is Allāh, and He is the best Disposer of affairs!' So they returned with favor from Allāh and bounty, no harm having touched them; and they pursued the pleasure of Allāh, and Allāh is the possessor of great bounty."* (Qurʾān 3:172–174; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:312–313).
