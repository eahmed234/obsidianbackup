# ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ (عثمان بن أبي العاص)
*The Wise Delegate of Thaqīf, Governor of al-Ṭāʾif, and Steely Anchor against Apostasy*

## 1. Lineage and Early Life

ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ b. Bishr b. ʿAbd b. Dahmān b. Dug͟hayth b. Thaʿlabah b. Sayār b. Mālik b. Ḥuṭayṭ b. Jusham b. Qasī b. Munabbih—who is [[Thaqīf]] (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:374). His mother was Fāṭimah bint ʿAbdillāh al-Thaqafiyyah, who was present at the birth of the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:374, 8:275).

## 2. The Delegation of Thaqīf (9 AH)

In Ramaḍān of 9 AH, following the expedition to Tabūk, the delegation of [[Thaqīf]] arrived in [[Medina]] to negotiate their submission (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296). ʿUthmān was the youngest among the delegates. Whenever the elders of the delegation went each morning to confer with the Prophet ﷺ, they left ʿUthmān behind to guard their baggage and mounts (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298).

Once the elders returned and rested during midday, ʿUthmān would proceed quietly to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He questioned the Prophet ﷺ regarding the faith, requested him to recite the Qurʾān, and devoted himself to studying until he acquired deep comprehension and insight into religion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298). The Messenger of Allah ﷺ admired his earnest devotion and loved him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298).

Before departing, ʿUthmān memorized portions of the Qurʾān, including [[Sūrat al-Baqarah]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298, 299). When the delegation asked the Prophet ﷺ to appoint a leader over them, he appointed ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ over them because of his profound zeal for Islam and his mastery of the Qurʾān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298).

## 3. Spiritual Counsel and Prophetic Healing

ʿUthmān reported that he initially struggled with retention, saying to the Prophet ﷺ:
> «يا رسول الله إن القرآن ينفلت مني»
> *"O Messenger of Allah, the Qurʾān slips from me."*

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ placed his hand upon ʿUthmān's chest and supplicated:
> «يا شيطان اخرج من صدر عثمان»
> *"O Satan, come out of the chest of ʿUthmān!"*

ʿUthmān recounted that thereafter he never forgot anything he resolved to retain (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:299). 

In another narration recorded in *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, ʿUthmān complained:
> «يا رسول الله إن الشيطان قد حال بيني وبين صلاتي وقراءتي يلبسها علي»
> *"O Messenger of Allah, Satan intervenes between me and my prayer and my recitation, confounding me."*

The Prophet ﷺ replied:
> «ذاك شيطان يقال له خنزب، فإذا أحسسته فتعوذ بالله منه واتفل على يسارك ثلاثا»
> *"That is a devil called Khanzab. If you sense him, seek refuge in Allah from him and spit dryly to your left three times."*

ʿUthmān performed this instruction, and Allah removed the whisperings from him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:299, 10:244).

The Prophet ﷺ also instructed him to establish the congregational mosque of [[al-Ṭāʾif]] on the very site where their idol, al-Lāt (*al-Ṭāg͟hiyah*), had stood (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:299).

## 4. Firmness During the Riddah Wars

Following the death of the Prophet ﷺ, when waves of apostasy surged across the Arabian Peninsula, elements of [[Thaqīf]] wavered and contemplated renouncing Islam. ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ assembled the people of al-Ṭāʾif and delivered an unforgettable exhortation that anchored their resolve:
> «يا معشر ثقيف، كنتم آخر الناس إسلاما، فلا تكونوا أولهم ارتدادا»
> *"O gathering of Thaqīf! You were the last of mankind to embrace Islam; do not be the first of them to apostatize!"*

His decisive intervention restrained [[Thaqīf]], and they remained steadfast in Islam (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:374).

During the Caliphate of [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]] ﵁, ʿUthmān prepared an expedition from al-Ṭāʾif to combat the apostates, appointing his brother ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ over them to reinforce the Muslim armies (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:270).

## 5. Later Governance and Death

ʿUthmān was confirmed as governor of [[al-Ṭāʾif]] by [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]] ﵁ and subsequently by [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]] ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:374). In 15 AH, [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]] ﵁ appointed him over ʿUmān and al-Baḥrayn (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:374). He subsequently settled in al-Baṣrah (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:374).

He passed away in al-Baṣrah during the caliphate of [[Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān]] ﵁, with 50 AH cited as the year of his death, or shortly thereafter (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:374).
