# Sariyyat al-Mayfaʿah (سرية غالب بن عبد الله الليثي إلى الميفعة)
*The 130-man surprise offensive into Najd commanded by Ghālib b. ʿAbdillāh al-Laythī against Banū ʿUwāl and Banū ʿAbd b. Thaʿlabah*

## 1. Strategic Context & Mandate

Following the conquest of Khaybar and the stabilization of the northern valleys in 7 AH, nomadic clans of Ghaṭafān continued to pose security concerns along the eastern desert approaches to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133). 

In Ramaḍān 7 AH, the freedman of the Prophet ﷺ, **Yasār**, reported precise actionable intelligence regarding a point of vulnerability (*ghirrah*) among the clans of **Banū ʿUwāl** and **Banū ʿAbd b. Thaʿlabah** encamped at **al-Mayfaʿah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133). Al-Mayfaʿah was situated deep within Najd, beyond Baṭn Nakhl toward al-Nuqrah, approximately eight postal stages (*thamāniyat burud*) from Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133).

The Prophet ﷺ commissioned a strike force of **one hundred and thirty (130) men**, placing them under the seasoned veteran commander **[[Ghālib b. ʿAbdillāh al-Laythī]]** ﵁, with Yasār serving as primary desert scout and guide (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133).

## 2. The Tactical Assault & Decisive Engagement

Guided covertly by Yasār across the rugged desert expanses, Ghālib b. ʿAbdillāh advanced undetected until his force was positioned in the immediate vicinity of al-Mayfaʿah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133). 

Executing a unified, simultaneous charge, the 130 Muslims struck the central quarters of the enemy encampments (*waqaʿū fī wasaṭi miḥāllihim*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133). The surprise was total. The Muslims engaged and killed those hostile combatants who came forward to fight (*qatalū man ashrafa lahum*), while the remaining tribesmen scattered into the barren hills (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133). 

Following the strict military regulations of the Prophet ﷺ against taking non-combatants, Ghālib took no captives (*wa-lam yaʾsirū aḥadā*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133).

## 3. Recovery of Flocks & Return to Medina

The detachment secured the pasturing herds of camels and sheep (*al-naʿam wal-shāʾ*) and withdrew steadily along the homeward track to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133). The column arrived safely in Medina without losing a single Muslim warrior (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133).

> [!NOTE]
> Imām al-Ṣāliḥī clarifies a common historiographical confusion: Ibn Saʿd and subsequent chroniclers stated that it was during this expedition that [[Usāmah b. Zayd]] ﵁ killed Nahīk b. Mirdās after the latter had proclaimed *"Lā ilāha illā Allāh"*, prompting the Prophet's famous rebuke: *«Afalā shaqqaqta ʿan qalbihi?»* (*"Did you tear open his heart to know whether he was truthful or lying?"*). Al-Ṣāliḥī demonstrates that this famous event actually occurred during **Sariyyat al-Ḥuraqāt** under Usāmah's command, not at al-Mayfaʿah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133).

---

## 4. Strategic Significance

1. **Inner Desert Deterrence**: Projecting a force of 130 warriors eight postal stages deep into Najd demonstrated that hostile Bedouin clans of Ghaṭafān could no longer organize raids unmolested (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133).
2. **Operational Mastery of Ghālib b. ʿAbdillāh**: This expedition marked the first in a succession of brilliant tactical commands assigned to Ghālib b. ʿAbdillāh al-Laythī in late 7 AH and 8 AH, solidifying his stature as one of the Prophet's most reliable field commanders (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133).
3. **Discipline of Restraint**: By focusing strictly on combatants and securing logistical prize without punitive enslavement of families, the expedition reinforced Islamic ethical principles of engagement (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:133).
