# al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah (الهجرة النبوية الشريفة إلى المدينة المنورة)
*The Prophet's ﷺ emigration from Makkah to Madīnah*

The **Hijrah** was the Prophet's ﷺ emigration from Makkah to Madīnah, in the company of [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]] ﵁.[^s3.238][^s3.239] [[Quraysh]] had agreed to kill him.[^s3.231][^s3.232] His Companions had gone ahead of him in groups, and only [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]] ﵁ and Abū Bakr stayed with him in Makkah, apart from those who were held back or tempted away from their religion.[^s3.227] He spent three nights in a cave on Mount Thawr, travelled by the coastal road, and reached Qubāʾ on a Monday of Rabīʿ al-Awwal in the thirteenth year of the mission.[^s3.243][^s3.253][^bukh3906] From Qubāʾ he rode into the city, where his camel knelt at the site of his mosque and he took up lodging in the house of [[Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī]] ﵁.[^s3.271][^s3.273]

## The Companions go ahead

After the pledge of the seventy at al-ʿAqabah the Prophet's ﷺ heart was at ease, for Allāh had given him a people with strength and defences. The polytheists, who knew what was coming, pressed harder on the Muslims, and the Companions complained and asked leave to emigrate.[^s3.224] He told them that he had been shown the abode of their emigration, a salty land with date palms between two lava fields, and some days later that it was Yathrib, and that whoever wished to go should go.[^s3.224][^bukh3905] In another report he said that he had seen in a dream that he was emigrating from Makkah to a land of palms, and that his own guess had gone to al-Yamāmah or Hajar before it proved to be Yathrib.[^s3.236] Ibn ʿAbbās says that he stayed thirteen years in Makkah; his reports on this differ, and al-Ṣāliḥī defers their treatment to his chapter on the Prophet's death.[^s3.236]

The Companions prepared, travelled together in company and slipped away unannounced.[^s3.224] Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Asad was the first of them to reach Madīnah; Ibn Isḥāq says he emigrated a year before the pledge of al-ʿAqabah. His wife Umm Salamah was kept in Makkah for about a year by the Banū al-Mughīrah, and then they let her go; she set out alone and was met at al-Tanʿīm by ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah, then still a polytheist, who accompanied her as far as Qubāʾ and turned back. She used to say that she had never seen a more generous companion.[^s3.224] ʿĀmir b. Rabīʿah came next with his wife Laylā bint Abī Ḥathmah, who is said to have been the first woman in a howdah to enter Madīnah.[^s3.225] All of Banū Jaḥsh emigrated with their families, and Abū Sufyān seized their house; when ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh told the Prophet ﷺ, he asked him whether he would not be content for Allāh to give him a better house in Paradise, and ʿAbdullāh said that he would; the Prophet ﷺ said: «فذلك لك» ("Then it is yours").[^s3.225]

ʿAlī ﵁ is reported to have said that every emigrant left in secret except ʿUmar, who put on his sword, slung his bow, took arrows in his hand and went to the Kaʿbah, where he circled the House seven times, prayed two rakʿahs at the Station, and then went round the circles of Quraysh telling them that whoever wished to be bereaved of his mother, orphan his children or widow his wife should meet him behind the valley. Nobody followed him, except some weak Muslims whom he instructed.[^s3.225] ʿUmar himself told how he, ʿAyyāsh b. Abī Rabīʿah and Hishām b. al-ʿĀṣ agreed to meet at al-Tanāḍub; Hishām's people held him back, and [[Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)|Abū Jahl]] ☠ and al-Ḥārith b. Hishām, who accepted Islam later, came to Madīnah and persuaded ʿAyyāsh with his mother's vow not to comb her hair or sit in the shade until she saw him. ʿAyyāsh went back with them, and on the road they bound him and took him into Makkah, saying that this was how to treat a fool. The Muslims used to say that Allāh would accept no repentance from those who had gone back, until the verses of Sūrat al-Zumar, "Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allāh," were revealed after the Prophet ﷺ reached Madīnah. ʿUmar wrote them out and sent them to Hishām, who understood at Dhū Ṭuwā that they were about him, and joined the Prophet ﷺ.[^s3.225][^s3.226] According to a report given by Ibn Hishām, when the Prophet ﷺ asked in Madīnah who would bring him ʿAyyāsh and Hishām, [[al-Walīd b. al-Walīd]] went to Makkah in secret, found ʿAyyāsh and Hishām imprisoned in a house with no roof, broke their shackles with his sword and brought them to the Prophet ﷺ.[^s3.226][^s3.227]

[[Ṣuhayb b. Sinān]] ﵁ was told by Quraysh that he had come to them poor and grown rich among them and would not leave with his wealth and himself; he gave them his property in exchange for his freedom, and the Prophet ﷺ said when it reached him: «ربح صهيب» ("Ṣuhayb has profited").[^s3.227] [[Sālim Mawlā Abī Ḥudhayfah]] led the emigrants in prayer at Qubāʾ before the Prophet ﷺ arrived.[^s3.227] The Prophet ﷺ remained in Makkah waiting for permission, and Abū Bakr often asked leave to go; he would say to him, «لا تعجل لعل الله يجعل لك صاحبا» ("Do not hurry; perhaps Allāh will give you a companion"), and Abū Bakr hoped to be that companion.[^s3.227]

## The plot at Dār al-Nadwah

When Quraysh saw that the Prophet ﷺ had followers in another town, and that his Companions were leaving to join them, they understood that these had found a home, protection and strength, and they feared that he would follow and had resolved to fight them. They gathered in the Dār al-Nadwah, the house of Quṣayy b. Kilāb in which Quraysh settled every matter, on a day later called the Day of Crowding.[^s3.231] [[Iblīs]] stood at the door in the guise of a venerable shaykh in a coarse cloak, and told them he was from Najd, had heard of their meeting and might offer an opinion; they let him in. Among those present were ʿUtbah and Shaybah sons of Rabīʿah, [[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]] and Jubayr b. Muṭʿim, who both accepted Islam afterwards, Abū Jahl ☠ and [[Umayyah b. Khalaf]] ☠.[^s3.231]

One speaker proposed to confine him in irons and shut a door on him until he died as the poets before him had died; al-Suhaylī, reporting Ibn Sallām, says it was Abū al-Bakhtarī b. Hishām. The shaykh from Najd rejected it, for word would get out to his followers from behind the closed door and they would rise and take him from them. Another, whom al-Suhaylī names as Abū al-Aswad Rabīʿah b. ʿAmr, proposed to expel him, and the shaykh rejected that too: his speech was so sweet that he would win over another tribe and lead it against them.[^s3.231][^s3.232] Then Abū Jahl said that they should take from every tribe a strong young man of good lineage, give each a sharp sword, and have them strike together as one man, so that his blood would be divided among all the tribes and Banū ʿAbd Manāf, unable to fight them all, would accept the blood-money. The shaykh said that this was the opinion, and the meeting broke up on it.[^s3.232]

## The night of the siege

Jibrīl ﵊ came to the Prophet ﷺ and told him not to sleep that night on the bed he usually slept on, informed him of the plot, and told him that Allāh had given him leave to go out.[^s3.232] At nightfall the young men gathered at his door, watching for him to sleep. He said to ʿAlī: «نم على فراشي وتسج ببردي هذا الحضرمي الأخضر فإنه لن يخلص إليك شيء تكرهه منهم» ("Sleep on my bed and wrap yourself in this green Ḥaḍramī cloak of mine; nothing you dislike will reach you from them"), and the Prophet ﷺ used to sleep in that cloak.[^s3.232]

As they stood there Abū Jahl ☠ told them that Muḥammad claimed that if they followed him they would be kings of the Arabs and non-Arabs and, after being raised from death, would have gardens like those of al-Urdunn, while if they did not there would be slaughter among them and then a fire. The Prophet ﷺ came out to them, took a handful of dust, and said: «نعم أنا أقول ذلك وأنت أحدهم» ("Yes, I say that, and you are one of them"). Allāh took away their sight so that they did not see him, and he scattered the dust on their heads while reciting the opening of Sūrat Yā-Sīn up to «فَأَغْشَيْنَاهُمْ فَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ» (36:9); not one of them was left without dust on his head. Then he went on his way.[^s3.232] Someone who had not been with them came and asked what they were waiting for, and when they said Muḥammad, he told them that he had already come out, put dust on every man's head and gone about his business. Each put a hand to his head and found dust. They looked in and saw ʿAlī lying under the Prophet's ﷺ cloak, and kept saying that it was Muḥammad asleep, until morning, when ʿAlī rose from the bed; they said that the one who had told them had spoken the truth.[^s3.233]

In another report from Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī gave himself for the Prophet ﷺ by putting on his garment and sleeping in his place; the polytheists, who had hoped for the Prophet ﷺ, began to pelt ʿAlī, thinking him to be him, and when he got up to perform ablution they saw that it was ʿAlī, and remarked that he writhed when his companion had not.[^s3.233] ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn said that ʿAlī was the first to sell himself seeking the pleasure of Allāh, and ʿAlī is reported to have spoken verses about that night.[^s3.233] It was in connection with that day that «وَإِذْ يَمْكُرُ بِكَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لِيُثْبِتُوكَ أَوْ يَقْتُلُوكَ أَوْ يُخْرِجُوكَ» (8:30) was revealed, though the sūrah is Madinan and the event took place in Makkah before the emigration.[^s3.233]

## Abū Bakr's house

Abū Bakr, who had asked to emigrate, was told «على رسلك فإني أرجو أن يؤذن لي» ("Take your time, for I hope to be given leave"); he held himself back to accompany the Prophet ﷺ and fed two mounts on samur leaves for four months.[^s3.238][^bukh3905] ʿĀʾishah ﵂ related that the Prophet ﷺ came to them every day at both ends of the day, morning and evening; then one day, at the height of noon, someone told Abū Bakr that he was at the door with his head covered, at an hour when he never came, and Abū Bakr said that nothing but a matter of importance could have brought him.[^bukh3905] The Prophet ﷺ asked him to send out whoever was with him, and Abū Bakr said that they were only his family, in Subul's wording his two daughters. He said that Allāh had permitted him to leave and emigrate, and Abū Bakr said: «الصحبة يا رسول الله» ("Companionship, O Messenger of Allāh?"). «نعم» ("Yes"), said the Prophet ﷺ. ʿĀʾishah said that she had not known that anyone could weep for joy until she saw Abū Bakr weep that day.[^s3.239][^bukh3905] Ibn ʿAbbās relates that when the Prophet ﷺ was ordered to emigrate from Makkah, «وَقُلْ رَبِّ أَدْخِلْنِي مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ وَأَخْرِجْنِي مُخْرَجَ صِدْقٍ» (17:80) was revealed, and that the "entrance" is the emigration to Madīnah; Qatādah says that the "entrance" is Madīnah and the "exit" Makkah.[^s3.238]

Abū Bakr offered him one of his two mounts, and he accepted only on payment of the price, saying «لا أركب بعيرا ليس هو لي» ("I will not ride a camel that is not mine"). Al-Wāqidī gives the price as eight hundred dirhams, and the camel is named al-Jadʿāʾ in Bukhārī's chapter on al-Rajīʿ.[^s3.239] They hired a man of Banū al-Dīl, an ally of the family of al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil al-Sahmī and still on the religion of Quraysh, as an expert guide, trusted him with their two mounts, and agreed to meet at Thawr after three nights.[^s3.239][^bukh3905] Elsewhere Subul gives his name as ʿAbdullāh b. al-Arīqiṭ al-Laythī.[^s3.244] They prepared the provisions quickly and put food in a bag; Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr cut her waist-belt (*niṭāq*), tying the mouth of the bag with one piece. In another wording she split it in two and tied the water-skin with the other, and she was called Dhāt al-Niṭāq or Dhāt al-Niṭāqayn.[^s3.239][^bukh3905][^s3.256] Al-Balādhurī reports that the Prophet ﷺ said she would have two belts in Paradise.[^s3.239]

Ibn Isḥāq says that the Prophet ﷺ told ʿAlī of his departure and ordered him to stay behind in Makkah until he had returned the deposits that people had left with him, since anyone in Makkah who had something to fear for left it with him, knowing his truthfulness and trustworthiness.[^s3.239] Some say ʿAlī remained three days.[^s3.267] Ibn Saʿd says that when the Prophet ﷺ left his house he went to Abū Bakr's house and stayed there until night, and then the two went out and entered the cave on Thawr; Ibn Isḥāq and al-Wāqidī say they left by a small door at the back of Abū Bakr's house.[^s3.238][^s3.239] In a report from Abū Nuʿaym he said that the first person he met was Abū Jahl ☠, and that Allāh blinded him to himself and Abū Bakr until they had passed.[^s3.239] Abū Bakr left with his money, which Asmāʾ gives as five thousand dirhams; her blind grandfather Abū Quḥāfah said that he feared Abū Bakr had distressed them by taking his money with him. She put stones in the niche where her father kept his money, covered them with a cloth and had the old man lay his hand on it; he said that if he had left them that he had done well. She added that Abū Bakr had in fact left them nothing, and that she wanted to put the old man at ease.[^s3.239][^s3.240]

## The cave on Thawr

Abū Bakr is reported to have walked now in front of the Prophet ﷺ, now behind, now on his right and now on his left, saying that he thought of the watchers and walked in front and of the pursuers and walked behind. At the mouth of the cave he asked to enter first, so that anything harmful would befall him instead. He felt about with his hand, and every time he found a hole he tore a piece from his garment and stopped it, until all his garment was used and one hole remained, which he blocked with his heels. Snakes stung him and his tears ran down.[^s3.240] In another report he put his feet in the hole, saying that if anything bit or stung, it would be him.[^s3.240] In a report from Anas, the Prophet ﷺ asked in the morning where his garment was, and when Abū Bakr told him, raised his hands and asked Allāh to put Abū Bakr with him in his rank in Paradise.[^s3.240]

Several reports tell how Allāh concealed them. Abū Miṣʿab al-Makkī said that he heard Anas, Zayd b. Arqam and al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah relate that on the night of the cave a tree grew in front of the Prophet ﷺ and covered him, that a spider wove its web between them, and that two wild doves stood at the mouth of the cave. Young men of Quraysh from every clan came with clubs and swords, and when they were forty cubits away, one looked into the cave, saw only the two doves, and went back to tell his companions that there was nobody inside; the Prophet ﷺ heard him, blessed them, and they went down into the Ḥaram.[^s3.240][^s3.241] A report with a good chain from Ibn ʿAbbās says that the polytheists followed his tracks until they reached the mountain, where they lost them; they climbed it, passed the cave and saw the spider's web across its door, and he stayed there three days.[^s3.241] According to al-Balādhurī, Quraysh hired ʿAlqamah b. Kurz al-Khuzāʿī, who accepted Islam in the year of the conquest, to follow the trail to the cave; he said that the trail broke off there. When they reached the mouth, Umayyah b. Khalaf ☠ asked what business they had in the cave, as there was a spider's web on it from before Muḥammad was born, and went off to relieve himself.[^s3.241] A report from ʿUrwah says that Quraysh rode in every direction searching and sent to the people of the watering places promising a great reward, and that they came up on Thawr itself. The Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr heard their voices, and Abū Bakr wept in fear and sorrow.[^s3.241]

Abū Bakr said, "If one of them looked down at his feet, he would see us," and the Prophet ﷺ answered:

> «مَا ظَنُّكَ يَا أَبَا بَكْرٍ بِاثْنَيْنِ اللهُ ثَالِثُهُمَا»
> "What do you think, Abū Bakr, of two whose third is Allāh?"[^bukh3653][^s3.241]

Then he said, «لا تحزن إن الله معنا» ("Do not grieve; Allāh is with us," 9:40), prayed, and Allāh sent down His tranquillity.[^s3.241][^s3.242] Ibn ʿAbbās said that the tranquillity in the verse came down on Abū Bakr, since it had never left the Prophet ﷺ.[^s3.242] Asmāʾ reports that Abū Bakr saw a man facing the cave and said that he could see them; the Prophet ﷺ said that the angels were screening him with their wings, and the man sat down and relieved himself in their direction. The Prophet ﷺ said that if he had seen them he would not have done so.[^s3.242]

### The three nights

They hid in the cave three nights.[^bukh3905][^s3.243] ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Bakr, a shrewd and quick-witted youth, spent the night with them, left before dawn to be among Quraysh in Makkah in the morning as though he had spent the night there, and remembered every plan made against them, bringing it to them when darkness fell. Ibn Isḥāq adds that [[Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr]] ﵂ brought them what they needed of food each evening. [[ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah]], Abū Bakr's freedman, grazed a flock of Abū Bakr's among the shepherds of Makkah and in the early part of the night brought it to them, so that they spent the night on fresh milk and on milk heated with hot stones, until ʿĀmir drove the flock out in the darkness before dawn; he did this on each of the three nights.[^bukh3905][^s3.243] In Makkah, Asmāʾ relates, a party of Quraysh including Abū Jahl ☠ came to her door and asked where her father was; she said she did not know, and Abū Jahl slapped her so hard that her earring flew off. For three days they did not know which way the Prophet ﷺ had gone, until a jinn was heard reciting verses in the lower part of Makkah and out of its upper part, praising the two companions who had stopped at the tents of Umm Maʿbad; Ḥassān b. Thābit replied to him.[^s3.245][^s3.246]

## The road

When the three nights were over and the people had quietened, the guide came with the two mounts. They set out, and ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah went with them, Abū Bakr seating him behind him to serve them on the road. The guide took them by the coastal road below ʿUsfān, then crossed over until they came back to the road at Amaj.[^s3.243][^bukh3905]

### Shade, a shepherd and milk

Abū Bakr told the father of al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib that they travelled that night and the next day until the height of noon, when the road emptied; he found a long rock with shade that the sun had not yet reached, levelled a place with his hand, spread a fleece and told the Prophet ﷺ to sleep while he looked about. He found a shepherd coming with his sheep to the rock for the same reason, and asked whose he was; he was a man from Makkah, in some wordings from "Madīnah," which, al-Ḥāfiẓ says, in this report means Makkah. The shepherd milked a small quantity into a bowl. Abū Bakr poured water over the milk to cool it, and when the Prophet ﷺ woke he drank until Abū Bakr was content. He said, «ألم يأن للرحيل؟» ("Has the time not come to leave?"), and they set out after the sun had passed its zenith.[^bukh3615][^musl2009][^s3.243][^s3.244][^s3.259]

### Umm Maʿbad

Many reports relate that the Prophet ﷺ, Abū Bakr, Abū Bakr's freedman ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah and their guide ʿAbdullāh b. al-Arīqiṭ al-Laythī came to the tent of [[Umm Maʿbad]], of Khuzāʿah, a mature and hardy woman who sat in the courtyard of her tent giving food and drink to travellers. They asked her for meat and dates, which they would buy, but found nothing; the people were in want and a year of drought. The Prophet ﷺ saw a ewe at the side of the tent and asked her about it; she said that weakness had kept it from the flock. He asked whether it had any milk, and she said it was too weak for that. He asked leave to milk it and she told him to do so if he saw any milk in it. He wiped its udder and back with his hand, named Allāh and prayed for it; it spread its legs, let down its milk and chewed the cud. He asked for a vessel big enough to make a company lie down, and milked into it in a stream until froth covered it. He gave her to drink until she was satisfied, then his companions until they were, and drank last himself, saying, «ساقي القوم آخرهم شربا» ("The one who serves the people drinks last"). He milked again, filled the vessel, left it with her and they went on.[^s3.244][^s3.245]

Her husband Abū Maʿbad came back driving lean goats and asked where the milk had come from, as the ewe was far from the pasture and there was no milch animal in the house; she said that a blessed man had passed by, and described him, saying among other things: «إن صمت فعليه الوقار، وإن تكلم سما وعلاه البهاء» ("If he is silent, dignity is upon him; if he speaks, he rises and splendour covers him"). Abū Maʿbad said that this was the man of Quraysh of whom they had heard, and that he had meant to join him if he found a way.[^s3.245] Umm Maʿbad said that the ewe stayed with them until the Year of Ramādah, the eighteenth year of the Hijrah, in the time of ʿUmar, and that they milked it morning and evening when the land had nothing.[^s3.245]

Al-Bayhaqī relates from Abū Bakr a similar episode with a woman in a tent of an Arab encampment, whose son brought goats that the Prophet ﷺ milked one after another; the woman, who called him "the blessed one," was later brought to him in Madīnah by her son and was given gifts. Al-Bayhaqī judges that it resembles the story of Umm Maʿbad and may be the same episode, and he gives Ibn Isḥāq's version of Umm Maʿbad's name, ʿĀtikah bint Khālid.[^s3.246][^s3.247]

### Surāqah b. Mālik

Quraysh's messengers had set the blood-price of each of the two at a hundred camels for whoever killed or captured him. [[Surāqah b. Mālik b. Juʿshum]], sitting with his people of Banū Mudlij, heard a man say he had just seen figures on the coast that he took to be Muḥammad and his companions. Surāqah knew who they were, signalled to the man to be quiet, said that they were someone else, stayed a little, then went into his house, told his girl to take his mare behind a rise, and went out the back with his spear, its head to the ground and its upper end lowered so that its gleam would not show.[^s3.248][^s3.264] When he drew near, the mare stumbled and he fell; he drew the divining arrows to see whether he should harm them, and the one he disliked came out. He disobeyed the arrows and rode on until he could hear the Prophet's ﷺ recitation — the Prophet ﷺ did not look round, while Abū Bakr looked round a great deal — and the mare's forelegs sank into the ground up to the knees. He got her up, and as she stood a column of dust like smoke rose after her forelegs. He drew the arrows again, and again the same came out. Knowing then that he had been held back from them and that their cause would prevail, he called out for safe conduct, and they stopped.[^s3.248][^bukh3906]

He told them that his people had set the price for them and what the people intended, and offered them provisions and goods; they took nothing from him and asked only that he keep the matter hidden. He asked for a document of safety, and the Prophet ﷺ ordered ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah, who wrote it on a piece of leather.[^s3.248][^bukh3906] In the report of al-Barāʾ from Abū Bakr, Surāqah overtook them as they were on hard ground, and Abū Bakr wept, saying not for himself but for the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ said, «لا تحزن إن الله معنا», and prayed against Surāqah, «اللهم اكفناه بما شئت» ("O Allāh, spare us him as You will"); his mare sank to her belly. Surāqah said he knew that this was their doing, asked them to pray for him and promised to turn back the pursuit, and offered an arrow from his quiver and his camels and sheep along their way; the Prophet ﷺ said that they had no need of his camels and sheep, and prayed for him. He went back saying to everyone he met, "You have been spared what is here," and kept his word to them.[^s3.249][^bukh3615][^bukh3908][^musl2009] Ibn Saʿd and al-Balādhurī say that Surāqah met them at Qudayd on a Tuesday.[^s3.249] Anas relates that the Prophet ﷺ prayed «اللهم اصرعه» ("O Allāh, throw him down"), and the horse threw him.[^bukh3911]

Surāqah said that he kept silent about all this until Makkah had been conquered and the Prophet ﷺ had finished with Ḥunayn and al-Ṭāʾif; then he went to meet him at al-Jiʿrānah with the letter, and the Anṣār's squadrons struck him with their spears until he came near. The Prophet ﷺ said, «يوم وفاء وبر، أدنه» ("A day of fidelity and goodness; bring him near"), and Surāqah accepted Islam. He asked whether there was reward in watering stray camels at his troughs, and was told that there was reward in every living creature.[^s3.248][^s3.249] In a report from al-Ḥasan, the Prophet ﷺ once asked Surāqah, «كيف بك إذا لبست سواري كسرى؟» ("How will it be with you when you wear the bracelets of Kisrā?"); when ʿUmar was brought Kisrā's bracelets, belt and crown, he called Surāqah and put them on him — a hairy-armed man — and told him to raise his hands and say that praise belongs to Allāh, who stripped Kisrā b. Hurmuz of them and dressed Surāqah the bedouin in them.[^i3.35]

### Garments, a banner and a guide

ʿUrwah relates that the Prophet ﷺ met al-Zubayr with a party of Muslim traders returning from Syria, and that al-Zubayr gave the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr white garments.[^s3.250][^bukh3906] Mūsā b. ʿUqbah says that [[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]] ﵁, coming back from Syria, met them and gave Abū Bakr garments from Syria, and that they put some of them on.[^s3.250] Anas says that the Prophet ﷺ rode with Abū Bakr behind him; Abū Bakr was an elderly man who was recognized, and the Prophet ﷺ a young man who was not. When someone asked Abū Bakr who the man in front of him was, he said: "This man guides me on the way," and the hearer took the word for the road while he meant the way of good.[^bukh3911][^s3.251] Abū Hurayrah relates that the Prophet ﷺ told Abū Bakr to turn people away from him, since a prophet may not lie.[^s3.251]

As he drew near Madīnah, [[Buraydah b. al-Ḥuṣayb]] ﵁ of Aslam met him with seventy of his people of Banū Sahm. The Prophet ﷺ asked his name and said to Abū Bakr, «برد أمرنا وصلح» ("Our affair has grown cool and sound"); when he said he was of Aslam, «سلمنا» ("We are safe"); and of Banū Sahm, «خرج سهمك» ("Your arrow has come out"). Buraydah asked who he was, and on being told that he was Muḥammad b. ʿAbdullāh, the Messenger of Allāh, he and all those with him accepted Islam. The next morning Buraydah said that he should not enter Madīnah without a banner; he unwound his turban, tied it to a spear and walked in front of him until they entered.[^s3.251][^s3.252]

## Qubāʾ

The Muslims of Madīnah had heard that the Prophet ﷺ had left Makkah, and used to go out each morning after the dawn prayer to the edge of the Ḥarrah to wait for him, until the heat of noon drove them back. On the day that he arrived they had gone indoors, when a Jewish man climbed up on one of their forts for something he wanted to see, saw the Prophet ﷺ and his companions in white, the mirage lifting them, and could not stop himself crying out at the top of his voice: «يا بني قيلة، هذا جدكم قد جاء» ("O sons of Qaylah, here is your good fortune come"). In another wording he said, "O company of the Arabs, here is the one you have been awaiting." The Muslims rushed to their weapons and met the Prophet ﷺ on the back of the Ḥarrah, on a Monday in Rabīʿ al-Awwal. He turned with them to the right and came down in the upper part of Madīnah at Qubāʾ, among Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf.[^s3.266][^bukh3906] Abū Bakr stood up to receive the people while the Prophet ﷺ sat silent, and the Anṣār who had not seen him greeted Abū Bakr, until the sun reached the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr shaded him with his cloak; then the people knew who he was.[^s3.266][^bukh3906]

He lodged, according to one view, with Kulthūm b. al-Hidm, who is said to have been still a polytheist, as Ibn Zabālah affirms; others say with Saʿd b. Khaythamah. Razīn and al-Ḥākim prefer the first, and the reconciliation reported is that he stayed at Kulthūm's house but sat with the people in Saʿd b. Khaythamah's, since Saʿd was unmarried and his house was the house of the unmarried emigrants. Abū Bakr stayed with Khubayb b. Isāf at al-Sunḥ, or with Khārijah b. Zayd.[^s3.266] ʿAlī stayed on in Makkah some days after the Prophet ﷺ left until he had given people back their deposits, and then followed and lodged at Qubāʾ with Kulthūm b. al-Hidm.[^s3.267]

### The mosque

Kulthūm had a drying yard for dates, which the Prophet ﷺ took from him, founded and built as a mosque.[^s3.267] In ʿUrwah's report in the Ṣaḥīḥ he stayed among Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf and the mosque «الَّذِي أُسِّسَ عَلَى التَّقْوَى» ("founded on piety") was founded, and he prayed in it.[^s3.267][^bukh3906] In Ibn ʿĀʾidh's wording, he stayed three nights, made a place for a mosque and prayed there, and Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf then built it. Another report has [[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]] ﵁ say that the Prophet ﷺ must have a place to take shade and pray when he woke, and gather stones and build the mosque at Qubāʾ, which is why he is said to have been the first to build a mosque, meaning for the Muslims in general or for the Prophet ﷺ in Madīnah. Al-Ṣāliḥī adds that in truth it is the first mosque in which he prayed in the open with his Companions, though others had been built before: Jābir says they spent two years in Madīnah before the Prophet ﷺ came building mosques and keeping up prayer, and it is said that the early emigrants and Anṣār at Qubāʾ had built one there. When he came, he led them in prayer there towards Bayt al-Maqdis.[^s3.267] Ibn Shabbah relates that he later set the qiblah where it now is, saying that Jibrīl was leading him towards the House.[^s3.267][^s3.268]

A woman, al-Shumūs bint al-Nuʿmān, saw him lift a stone or rock for the foundation until it bent him over, with the whiteness of dust on his belly; when one of his Companions asked to take it and spare him, he told him to take another like it.[^s3.268] When the people of Qubāʾ asked him to build them a mosque, he asked one man after another to ride his camel: Abū Bakr's and ʿUmar's would not rise, and when ʿAlī put his foot in the stirrup it stood up. He said: «أرخ زمامها وابنوا على مدارها فإنها مأمورة» ("Loosen her rein and build where she turns, for she is commanded").[^s3.268]

### How long he stayed

The reports differ on how long he stayed with Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf. The Ṣaḥīḥ has "a few over ten nights" from ʿUrwah and fourteen nights from Anas; Ibn Isḥāq says five nights; Ibn Ḥibbān, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, leaving on the Friday; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn ʿUqbah, three nights; some of Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf, twenty-two days.[^s3.269]

## Into the city

On the Friday, as several reports relate, the Prophet ﷺ sent to Banū al-Najjār, his maternal kin, since ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib's mother was of them. They came with swords slung and said to him and his Companions: "Ride, safe and obeyed." When the day was well up he called for his mount, and the Muslims armed and gathered. He rode his camel al-Qaṣwāʾ with the people about him on right and left and behind, some walking and some riding.[^s3.271] Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf gathered and asked whether he was leaving them out of weariness or wanted a better abode than theirs; he said: «إني أمرت بقرية تأكل القرى فخلوها فإنها مأمورة» ("I have been commanded to a town that devours towns; let her be, for she is commanded").[^s3.271]

The people came out into the roads and onto their camels, and the servants and children said, "Allāhu akbar, the Messenger of Allāh has come." Anas recalled running with the boys when they cried that Muḥammad had come, and seeing nothing; then the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr took cover behind a wall of Madīnah and sent a Bedouin to tell the Anṣār, and about five hundred of them came and said, "Go on, safe and obeyed." The girls were on the roofs, asking which of them he was.[^s3.271] The Abyssinians played with their spears for joy at his coming.[^s3.271] The women, children and girls said, as ʿĀʾishah relates:

> «طَلَعَ الْبَدْرُ عَلَيْنَا ... مِنْ ثَنِيَّاتِ الْوَدَاعِ»
> "The full moon has risen over us, from the passes of al-Wadāʿ."[^s3.271]

Al-Barāʾ said he had never seen the people of Madīnah rejoice at anything as they rejoiced at the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ.[^s3.272]

As he passed the houses of the Anṣār each clan asked him to alight with them for their numbers, protection and wealth, and he said to each: «خلوا سبيلها فإنها مأمورة» ("Let her way be; she is commanded"), blessing them.[^s3.272] Banū Sālim, with [[ʿUtbān b. Mālik]] ﵁ and Nawfal b. ʿAbdillāh holding her halter, asked him to stay with them, as did [[ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit]] ﵁ and al-ʿAbbās b. ʿUbādah.[^s3.272] At the mosque of Banū Sālim, in the valley of Rānūnāʾ, the Friday prayer came upon him, and he prayed it there; it was the first Friday prayer he prayed in Madīnah, though some say he prayed it at the mosque of Qubāʾ, and Ibn Saʿd says a hundred men prayed with him.[^s3.272] Al-Ṣāliḥī treats it, with the first sermon given in Madīnah, in his next chapter.[^s3.331]

Then he went on by the right of the road to Banū al-Ḥublā. He had thought to lodge with ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy, who was then the chief of the Khazraj, and ʿAbdullāh told him to go to those who had invited him. Saʿd b. ʿUbādah said not to take it to heart, since the Khazraj had been about to make him king over them when Allāh turned that aside through the truth that he had been given and ʿAbdullāh was choking on it; this, he said, was his house.[^s3.272] At Banū Sāʿidah, [[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]] ﵁, al-Mundhir b. ʿAmr and [[Abū Dujānah]] ﵁ asked him to stay with them; [[Saʿd b. al-Rabīʿ]] ﵁, [[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]] ﵁ and [[Bashīr b. Saʿd]] ﵁ asked likewise. Ziyād b. Labīd and Farwah b. ʿAmr of Banū Bayāḍah offered the same.[^s3.272][^s3.273] According to al-Barāʾ's report he said: «أنزل على أخوال عبد المطلب أكرمهم بذلك» ("I will stay with the maternal uncles of ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, to honour them by it").[^s3.273][^musl2009] Abū Salīṭ and Ṣirmah b. Abī Anas of Banū ʿAdī b. al-Najjār told him that they were his maternal uncles and asked him not to pass them by; he gave the same answer.[^s3.273]

### Where the camel knelt

She went on until she came to the houses of Banū ʿAdī b. al-Najjār, whose faces rose to him; then to the door of the mosque, where she knelt. She got up, went a short way with her rein slack, turned, came back to the place where she had first knelt, knelt again, groaned and laid her chest down. Jabbār b. Ṣakhr prodded her in the hope that she would rise and kneel among Banū Salimah, but she did not. The Prophet ﷺ dismounted and said: «هنا المنزل إن شاء الله» ("Here, if Allāh wills, is the dwelling"), and recited «رَبِّ أَنْزِلْنِي مُنْزَلًا مُبَارَكًا وَأَنْتَ خَيْرُ الْمُنْزِلِينَ» (23:29).[^s3.273] In Bukhārī's report the place was a drying yard for dates belonging to Suhayl and Sahl, two orphan boys in the care of Asʿad b. Zurārah, where some Muslims were praying at that time; they offered it as a gift when he asked its price, and he built a mosque on it, carrying the bricks with them.[^bukh3906] In another report Ibn Saʿd says that [[Asʿad b. Zurārah]] ﵁ took her halter and she stayed with him.[^s3.273]

### Abū Ayyūb

Abū Ayyūb came and said that his house was nearest and his saddle was already in it. The Prophet ﷺ asked which of the houses of his people was nearest, and when Abū Ayyūb said it was his, told him to go and prepare a place to rest at midday.[^s3.273] Another report adds that he said to a second man who asked him to alight, «المرء مع رحله حيث كان» ("A man is with his baggage wherever it is"), and that [[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]] ﵁ stayed with him.[^s3.273] Girls of Banū al-Najjār came out with tambourines saying "How good that Muḥammad is a neighbour"; he asked whether they loved him, and when they said yes, said three times that he loved them.[^s3.274] [[ʿAbdullāh b. Salām]] ﵁ came to look at his face and knew that it was not the face of a liar; the first thing he heard him say was: «أيها الناس أفشوا السلام وأطعموا الطعام وصلوا الأرحام وصلوا والناس نيام تدخلون الجنة بسلام» ("O people, spread the greeting of peace, give food, keep the ties of kinship and pray while people sleep; you will enter Paradise in peace").[^s3.274]

Abū Ayyūb related that he and Umm Ayyūb were upstairs; he disliked to be above the Prophet ﷺ, but the Prophet ﷺ said it was easier for himself and his visitors to be on the lower floor. When a jar of water broke, the two of them wiped up the water with their only blanket, for fear that it would drip on him. It is reported that Abū Ayyūb kept pleading with him until he moved upstairs.[^s3.274][^s3.275] Zayd b. Thābit says that the first gift to enter was a bowl his mother sent, and that Saʿd b. ʿUbādah's and Asʿad b. Zurārah's dishes came to the house every night; his stay at Abū Ayyūb's was seven months.[^s3.275]

The Prophet ﷺ then sent Zayd b. Ḥārithah and Abū Rāfiʿ to Makkah with two camels and five hundred dirhams; they brought back his daughters Fāṭimah and Umm Kulthūm, his wife Sawdah bint Zamʿah, and Zayd's wife Umm Ayman with her son Usāmah. ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Bakr brought out Abū Bakr's family, including ʿĀʾishah, Asmāʾ and Umm Rūmān; on arrival they were lodged in the house of Ḥārithah b. al-Nuʿmān. The emigrants followed one after another, until nobody was left in Makkah but those who had been tempted or imprisoned.[^s3.276]

## Variants and disputes

Al-Ṣāliḥī's notes, and the authorities he quotes in them, record the following differences, which the account above has left as they stand.

- **The first emigrant to arrive.** Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn Saʿd name Abū Salamah, and al-Barāʾ says in Bukhārī that the first to reach them was Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr. Al-Ḥāfiẓ reconciles them: Ibn ʿUqbah is positive that Abū Salamah was first, but he left to escape the polytheists and not to settle in Madīnah, while Muṣʿab came intending to stay.[^s3.225][^s3.228]
- **The first woman in a howdah.** Abū ʿUmar is positive that it was Laylā bint Abī Ḥathmah, while Mūsā b. ʿUqbah says Umm Salamah; al-Ṣāliḥī leaves it there, with "Allāh knows best."[^s3.228]
- **Abū Ṭālib at the plot.** One report has Abū Ṭālib ask the Prophet ﷺ whether he knew what they had plotted, and the Prophet ﷺ say that his Lord had told him. *al-Bidāyah*, as quoted by al-Ṣāliḥī, says that the mention of Abū Ṭālib is strange, indeed disavowed, because the story comes three years before his death.[^s3.234]
- **Why the young men did not enter.** Some biographers say that when they were about to go in on ʿAlī a woman in the house cried out, and they said that it would be a disgrace among the Arabs to be said to have climbed over walls on their cousins' daughters; for that reason they stood at the door until morning waiting for him. Another report, that Māriya the Prophet's servant bent down for him to climb a wall that night, has unknown narrators and is weaker than the report that he came out through the door.[^s3.234]
- **The cave opening to the sea.** Some biographers say that the cave opened on the other side to the sea with a ship tied beside it. Ibn Kathīr says this is not impossible given Allāh's power, but that it has not come down with a strong or even a weak chain, and that they affirm only what is sound or good.[^s3.253]
- **Ten-odd days with only barīr to eat.** A report in Aḥmad and al-Ḥākim has the Prophet ﷺ say that he and Abū Bakr spent over ten days with no food but barīr. Al-Ḥāfiẓ says that the mention of the cave was added by one of the narrators, that it cannot be the Hijrah, since ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah brought them milk in the cave and they met the shepherd and Umm Maʿbad, and that it appears to be another occasion.[^s3.253]
- **The day of departure.** The reports have Monday for leaving and Monday for entering; Ibn ʿAbbās says that the Prophet ﷺ was born, left Makkah, entered Madīnah and died on a Monday, and al-Ḥākim says that Monday for both is established by many reports, though al-Khawārizmī says he left Makkah on a Thursday. Al-Ḥāfiẓ reconciles these by having him leave Makkah on Thursday and the cave in the night of Monday, after three nights in it.[^s3.253]
- **The time between the Companions' emigration and his.** Al-Ḥāfiẓ puts the time from the beginning of the Companions' emigration, together with the first and second pledges of al-ʿAqabah, up to his own emigration, at two months and part of a month.[^s3.252]
- **Umm Maʿbad before Surāqah.** *al-ʿUyūn* gives Surāqah's story before Umm Maʿbad's, and *al-Ishārah* gives hers first; al-Ṣāliḥī follows the latter and says it is the sound order, as a number of authorities state.[^s3.254]
- **Ibn Masʿūd's shepherd.** Abū Nuʿaym puts Ibn Masʿūd's account, in which he was a boy grazing sheep for ʿUqbah b. Abī Muʿayṭ, at the Hijrah, but *al-Bidāyah* and *al-Fatḥ*, as quoted by al-Ṣāliḥī, say that the words "they had fled from the polytheists" there do not refer to the Hijrah, because Ibn Masʿūd was an early convert who had already emigrated to Abyssinia.[^s3.254]
- **His host at Qubāʾ.** Kulthūm b. al-Hidm or Saʿd b. Khaythamah, as given above.[^s3.266]
- **Length of stay at Qubāʾ.** The reports are given above.[^s3.269]
- **Date of arrival.** The Ṣaḥīḥ has the Monday. Ibn ʿUqbah dates it to the first of Rabīʿ al-Awwal; Ibn Isḥāq, by Jarīr b. Ḥāzim, to when two nights of the month had passed; by Ibrāhīm b. Saʿd, to the twelfth night; and Abū Saʿīd, from Abū Bakr b. Ḥazm, to the thirteenth. Al-Ṣāliḥī reconciles them by a difference in sighting the new moon.[^s3.269]
- **By night or by day.** Most reports have him arrive by day, and Muslim's has him arrive by night. Al-Ḥāfiẓ combines them: he came at the end of the night and entered by day.[^s3.269][^musl2009]
- **Why he refused to ride Abū Bakr's camel as a gift.** Al-Suhaylī reports from a Maghribī shaykh that, asked why he would not simply take the mount when Abū Bakr had spent his wealth on him, he said that the Prophet ﷺ wanted his emigration to be from his own wealth.[^s3.252]
- **The passes of al-Wadāʿ.** Authorities differ on whether Thaniyyat al-Wadāʿ lies on the Makkah side of Madīnah or on the Syrian side. Al-Sayyid argues that it is on the Syrian side, and that this is not incompatible with the girls' verses having been sung at his arrival, because Banū Sāʿidah's quarter, through which he came into the city, lay on that side.[^s3.277][^s3.278]
