# al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah (الهجرة النبوية الشريفة إلى المدينة المنورة)
*The Grand Prophetic Migration from Makkah to Medina — The Genesis of the Islamic Commonwealth and the Cornerstone of the Hijrī Era*

## 1. Strategic Context & The Conspiracy at Dār al-Nadwah
Following the [[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah|Second Pledge of al-ʿAqabah]], the believers of Makkah began quietly migrating in small detachments to Yathrib, finding safe haven among the [[al-Anṣār|Anṣār]]. Realizing that Medina was becoming a fortified stronghold of Islam, the chieftains of **[[Quraysh]]** convened an emergency council at **Dār al-Nadwah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:238).

Conspiring under the instigation of [[Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)|Abū Jahl]] (with **[[Iblīs]]** attending in the guise of an elder of Najd), Quraysh rejected imprisonment and banishment, settling upon assassination: each clan would contribute a resolute noble youth, so that they might strike the Prophet ﷺ simultaneously with their swords as a single man. In this manner, his blood would be divided among all the clans, rendering [[Banū Hāshim]] and [[Banū al-Muṭṭalib]] incapable of waging war against all of Quraysh and forcing them to accept financial blood-money (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:238).

The Angel Jibrīl ﵊ descended with divine revelation informing the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ of their conspiracy:
> «وَإِذْ يَمْكُرُ بِكَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لِيُثْبِتُوكَ أَوْ يَقْتُلُوكَ أَوْ يُخْرِجُوكَ ۚ وَيَمْكُرُونَ وَيَمْكُرُ اللَّهُ ۖ وَاللَّهُ خَيْرُ الْمَاكِرِينَ»
> *"And [remember] when those who disbelieved plotted against you to restrain you or kill you or evict you. But they plan, and Allāh plans. And Allāh is the best of planners."* (Qurʾān 8:30; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:238).

Allāh granted the Prophet ﷺ explicit permission to migrate, revealing:
> «وَقُل رَّبِّ أَدْخِلْنِي مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ وَأَخْرِجْنِي مُخْرَجَ صِدْقٍ وَاجْعَل لِّي مِن لَّدُنكَ سُلْطَانًا نَّصِيرًا»
> *"And say: 'My Lord, cause me to enter a sound entrance and to exit a sound exit and grant me from Yourself a supporting authority.'"* (Qurʾān 17:80; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:238).

## 2. The Midday Pact with Abū Bakr & The Night of the Siege
At scorching midday (*fī naḥr al-ẓahīrah*), a time he never customarily ventured out, the Prophet ﷺ went veiled to the house of **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:239). The Prophet instructed him: *«أَخْرِجْ مَنْ عِنْدَكَ»* (*"Clear the room of whoever is present"*). Abū Bakr replied: *"They are only your household [my two daughters ʿĀʾishah and Asmāʾ]."* The Prophet announced: *«إِنَّ اللَّهَ قَدْ أَذِنَ لِي فِي الْخُرُوجِ وَالْهِجْرَةِ»* (*"Allāh has granted me permission to depart and migrate"*). Abū Bakr exclaimed in tears of overwhelming joy:
> «الصُّحْبَةَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ؟»
> *"Companionship, O Messenger of Allāh?"* The Prophet ﷺ replied: *«نَعَمْ»* (*"Yes."*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:239; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 3905).

Abū Bakr offered the Prophet ﷺ his finest mount, but the Prophet ﷺ insisted: *«بِالثَّمَنِ، لَا أَرْكَبُ بَعِيرًا لَيْسَ هُوَ لِي»* (*"Only for the market price; I will not ride a mount that does not belong to me"*), purchasing the she-camel (*al-Qaṣwāʾ*) for eight hundred dirhams (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:239).

### ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib in the Prophetic Bed
On the night appointed for the assassination, armed young champions from each clan of Quraysh cordoned off the Prophet's dwelling. The Prophet ﷺ instructed his cousin **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁:
> «نَمْ عَلَى فِرَاشِي، وَتَسَجَّ بِبُرْدِي هَذَا الْحَضْرَمِيِّ الْأَخْضَرِ، فَإِنَّهُ لَنْ يَخْلُصَ إِلَيْكَ شَيْءٌ تَكْرَهُهُ»
> *"Sleep upon my bed and envelop yourself within this green Ḥaḍramī cloak of mine; for indeed, nothing harmful that you detest shall touch you."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:238).

ʿAlī remained behind in Makkah for three days following their departure to return every entrusted deposit (*al-amānāt*) that the polytheists had deposited with the Trustworthy (*al-Amīn*) ﷺ. 

The Prophet ﷺ stepped out from his house directly through the cordon of assassins, scooping up a handful of earth and scattering it upon their bowed heads while reciting the opening verses of **[[Sūrat Yā-Sīn]]**:
> «وَجَعَلْنَا مِن بَيْنِ أَيْدِيهِمْ سَدًّا وَمِنْ خَلْفِهِمْ سَدًّا فَأَغْشَيْنَاهُمْ فَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ»
> *"And We have put before them a barrier and behind them a barrier and covered them, so they do not see."* (Qurʾān 36:9; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:238).
The assassins stood frozen in slumber until morning broke; discovering ʿAlī rising from the bed, they realized their prey had eluded them.

## 3. The Three Nights in Cave Thawr
To divert the inevitable search parties heading north toward Medina, the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr set out south toward the jagged heights of **Mount Thawr** (*Jabal Thawr*), concealing themselves inside **Cave Thawr** (*Ghār Thawr*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:240–243).

Abū Bakr entered the cavern first, tearing pieces of his own mantle to plug every crevice where serpents or scorpions might lurk (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:240).

### The Divine Shields & The Comfort of Qurʾān 9:40
Quraysh declared a colossal bounty of **one hundred camels** for whoever captured or killed either the Prophet ﷺ or Abū Bakr, dispatching expert Bedouin trackers across the desert. Trackers followed the footprints up Mount Thawr to the very mouth of the cave (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:241). 

By divine decree, a wild spider had spun its web across the cavern cleft, and two rock doves had nested upon its threshold. Seeing the undisturbed web, the trackers scoffed: *"Had anyone entered here, this web would have been torn before his birth!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:241).

Hearing their footsteps and hushed speech directly outside the ledge, Abū Bakr whispered in agony:
> «يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، لَوْ أَنَّ أَحَدَهُمْ نَظَرَ إِلَى قَدَمَيْهِ لَأَبْصَرَنَا!»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh! If one of them were to glance down at his feet, he would see us!"*
The Prophet ﷺ replied with serene majesty:
> «مَا ظَنُّكَ يَا أَبَا بَكْرٍ بِاثْنَيْنِ اللَّهُ ثَالِثُهُمَا؟»
> *"What is your thought, O Abū Bakr, of two companions when Allāh is their third?"* (Qurʾān 9:40; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:242; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 3653).

This scene was immortalized in the Qurʾān:
> «إِلَّا تَنصُرُوهُ فَقَدْ نَصَرَهُ اللَّهُ إِذْ أَخْرَجَهُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا ثَانِيَ اثْنَيْنِ إِذْ هُمَا فِي الْغَارِ إِذْ يَقُولُ لِصَاحِبِهِ لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَنَا ۖ فَأَنزَلَ اللَّهُ سَكِينَتَهُ عَلَيْهِ»
> *"If you do not aid the Prophet—Allāh has already aided him when those who disbelieved had driven him out as one of two, when they were in the cave and he said to his companion, 'Do not grieve; indeed Allāh is with us.' And Allāh sent down His tranquility upon him..."* (Qurʾān 9:40).

### The Secret Logistical Network
For three nights, a disciplined familial network sustained the travelers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:243):
- **ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Bakr**: A sharp, intelligent youth who spent the nights in the cave, slipped back into Makkah before dawn, spent the day monitoring Quraysh's plots, and brought full intelligence reports each nightfall.
- **Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr**: Traveled out under cover of night bringing provisions; when she found no rope to tie the leather water skin to the saddle, she tore her waist girdle (*niṭāq*) in two, earning the eternal prophetic title **Dhāt al-Niṭāqayn** ("She of the Two Girdles").
- **[[ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah]]** ﵁: Abū Bakr's freed shepherd who drove a flock of dairy sheep over the rocky trails at dusk to obliterate the footprints of ʿAbdullāh and Asmāʾ, providing the fugitives with fresh milk.

## 4. The Coastal March & Miraculous Encounters
After three nights, when the initial search frenzy subsided, their non-Muslim guide, **ʿAbdullāh b. Urayqiṭ al-Duʾalī**—an experienced desert tracker trusted implicitly—brought their two camels to the foot of Mount Thawr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:243). He guided them away from the conventional caravan paths, leading them west toward the Red Sea coast (*al-Sāḥil*).

### The Sinking of Surāqah b. Mālik
**[[Surāqah b. Mālik b. Juʿshum]]**, eager to claim the 100-camel reward, spotted the riders and galloped after them on his thoroughbred mare (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:246–250). 

As Surāqah drew within javelin range, his horse stumbled and its legs sank deeply into the solid desert rock up to the knees, throwing him to the ground. A dense column of dark smoke like dust erupted from the earth where the hooves struck (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:246). Realizing that a divine power shielded the Prophet ﷺ, Surāqah called out for safe conduct (*amān*).

The Prophet ﷺ granted him a written parchment of protection and delivered a stunning prophecy:
> «كَيْفَ بِكَ إِذَا لَبِسْتَ سِوَارَيْ كِسْرَى وَمِنْطَقَتَهُ وَتَاجَهُ؟»
> *"How will it be with you when you wear the bracelets of Khosrow (the Emperor of Persia), his belt, and his crown?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:248).
Surāqah rode back, turning away every other search party he encountered: *«قَدْ كَفَيْتُكُمْ مَا هَاهُنَا»* (*"I have combed this route, there is nothing here!"*). Two decades later, during the Caliphate of **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, the royal regalia of the Persian Emperor was captured at the conquest of al-Madāʾin, and ʿUmar summoned Surāqah and placed Khosrow's gold bracelets upon his arms, fulfilling the prophecy (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:249).

### The Miracle at the Tent of Umm Maʿbad
Near Qudayd, the travelers stopped at the humble tent of **Umm Maʿbad ʿĀtikah bint Khālid al-Khuzāʿiyyah** seeking provisions, but drought had ravaged the region (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:244–246). 

The Prophet ﷺ noticed a lone, emaciated sheep too weak to keep pace with the herd. He asked: *«هَلْ بِهَا مِنْ لَبَنٍ؟»* (*"Does she yield any milk?"*). She replied: *"She is too exhausted."* The Prophet ﷺ stroked its udder with his hand, pronounced the name of Allāh, and supplicated. Immediately, the sheep's udder filled with abundant milk; the Prophet ﷺ milked a large vessel until foam rose to its brim, quenching Umm Maʿbad and his companions, before drinking last himself (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:245). 

When her husband Abū Maʿbad returned, he marveled at the milk. Umm Maʿbad delivered her celebrated, peerless physical portrait of the Prophet ﷺ:
> «رَأَيْتُ رَجُلًا ظَاهِرَ الْوَضَاءَةِ، أَبْلَجَ الْوَجْهِ، حَسَنَ الْخَلْقِ، لَمْ تَعِبْهُ ثُجْلَةٌ، وَلَمْ تُزْرِ بِهِ صَعْلَةٌ... كَأَنَّ عُنُقَهُ إِبْرِيقُ فِضَّةٍ... إِذَا صَمَتَ عَلَاهُ الْوَقَارُ، وَإِذَا تَكَلَّمَ سَمَاهُ الْبَهَاءُ...»
> *"I saw a man of manifest radiance, luminous of countenance, noble of stature... When silent, dignified majesty enveloped him; when he spoke, splendor exalted him..."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:245).

## 5. Arrival at Qubāʾ & Founding of the First Mosque
On Monday, the 12th of Rabīʿ al-Awwal 1 AH, the Prophet ﷺ entered the palm oasis of **Qubāʾ** on the southern perimeter of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:266). The Jews and Anṣār had waited on the volcanic heights each morning until midday heat drove them indoors; a Jewish lookout spotted the white garments of the travelers glistening in the desert heat and shouted at the top of his voice:
> «يَا بَنِي قَيْلَةَ! هَذَا جَدُّكُمْ قَدْ جَاءَ!»
> *"O sons of Qaylah [the Anṣār]! Here is your sovereign good fortune whom you have awaited!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:266).

The Prophet ﷺ resided in Qubāʾ for several days with Kulthūm b. al-Hidm and Saʿd b. Khaythamah, laying the foundations of **Masjid Qubāʾ**—the first mosque constructed in Islam, celebrated in Qurʾān 9:108: *«لَّمَسْجِدٌ أُسِّسَ عَلَى التَّقْوَىٰ مِنْ أَوَّلِ يَوْمٍ أَحَقُّ أَن تَقُومَ فِيهِ»*. During this halt, **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ arrived on foot, having walked the entire distance from Makkah after returning the trusts, his feet swollen and bleeding (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:269).

## 6. The Triumphal Entry into Yathrib
On Friday, the Prophet ﷺ rode out from Qubāʾ into the inner city of Medina. He prayed the first Friday congregation in Islam in the valley of **Banū Sālim b. ʿAwf** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:331). 

The clans of the Anṣār lined the lanes in full armor, while women, maidens, and children climbed atop rooftops singing the immortal verses of welcome:
> «طَلَعَ الْبَدْرُ عَلَيْنَا ... مِنْ ثَنِيَّاتِ الْوَدَاعِ»
> «وَجَبَ الشُّكْرُ عَلَيْنَا ... مَا دَعَا لِلَّهِ دَاعِ»
> «أَيُّهَا الْمَبْعُوثُ فِينَا ... جِئْتَ بِالْأَمْرِ الْمُطَاعِ»
> *"The full moon has risen upon us from the valley of al-Wadāʿ!*
> *Gratitude is binding upon us so long as a caller calls unto Allāh!*
> *O you who have been dispatched among us, you have brought a command that must be obeyed!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:275).

Every clan leader begged the Prophet ﷺ to lodge with them, grasping the halter of his camel. The Prophet ﷺ gently bade them: *«خَلُّوا سَبِيلَهَا فَإِنَّهَا مَأْمُورَةٌ»* (*"Release her reins, for she is commanded"*). The she-camel knelt at the date-drying yard of Sahl and Suhayl, where **[[Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]** was erected, and the Prophet ﷺ was hosted in the residence of **[[Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:275, 3:335).

## 7. Historical & Civilizational Legacy
The Hijrah marked the fundamental turning point in human history:
- **Territorial Sovereignty**: Transformed Islam from a persecuted faith under Makkan hegemony into an independent, sovereign commonwealth governed by divine law.
- **The Islamic Calendar**: When **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ established the Islamic calendar in 16 AH in consultation with the senior Companions, they unanimously rejected dating the era from the Prophet's birth or revelation, choosing the **Hijrah** as the decisive dividing line between truth and falsehood (*al-Fārūq*).
