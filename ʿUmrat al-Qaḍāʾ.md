# ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ (عمرة القضاء)
*The ʿumrah the Prophet ﷺ made in Dhū al-Qaʿdah of the year 7, the month in which the idolaters had barred him from the House*[^s5.189]

ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ is the ʿumrah the Messenger of Allah ﷺ made at the new moon of Dhū al-Qaʿdah of the year 7, the month in which the idolaters had barred him from the House. The Muslims entered Makkah on the terms of the settlement made with its people: a stay of three days, with no weapons carried in except the sword in its scabbard.[^s5.189][^bukh4251] It has four names, *al-qaḍāʾ*, *al-qaḍiyyah*, *al-qiṣāṣ* and *al-ṣulḥ*.[^s5.196]

## The summons

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ ordered his Companions to make ready for the ʿumrah, and that none who had been present at al-Ḥudaybiyah stay behind. None who had been present did so, except men who had been martyred at Khaybar and men who had died.[^s5.189] Some of the Arabs living near Madinah said they had no provisions and no one to feed them. He ordered the Muslims to spend in the way of Allah, to give charity and not to hold back their hands lest they perish, and when they asked with what they were to give charity when one of them found nothing, he answered: «With whatever there is, even half a date.»[^s5.189]

The sources differ on who was left in charge of Madinah. Muḥammad b. ʿUmar and Ibn Saʿd say he appointed Abū Ruhm al-Ghifārī; Ibn Hishām says ʿUwayf b. al-Aḍbaṭ; al-Balādhurī says Abū Dharr. Al-Ṣāliḥī adds: "and it is said ʿUwayf b. al-Aḍbaṭ — and Allah knows best."[^s5.189]

## The hady, the horses and the weapons

Nājiyah b. Jundub al-Aslamī was put over the sacrificial animals (*hady*), which he drove ahead of the Prophet ﷺ, seeking pasture among the trees, with four youths of Aslam; another report adds Abū Hurayrah.[^s5.190] Sixty *badanah* were driven in the *qaḍiyyah*, and the Prophet ﷺ garlanded his *hady* with his own hand.[^s5.190]

According to a report from Qatādah, the Prophet ﷺ also took weapons, helmets, coats of mail and spears, and led a hundred horses under [[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]]. At Dhū al-Ḥulayfah he sent the horses ahead of him and put [[Bashīr b. Saʿd]] over the weapons.[^s5.190] It was said to him that he had brought weapons although the Quraysh had stipulated that they enter only with the traveller's weapons, swords in their scabbards. He answered that they would not bring them into the Ḥaram upon the people, but that the weapons would be near, so that if any of the people stirred them to war they would be at hand.[^s5.190]

Muḥammad b. Maslamah went ahead with the horses to Marr al-Ẓahrān, where he met some men of Quraysh and told them that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would be at that stopping place tomorrow morning, if Allah willed. They saw the many weapons with Bashīr b. Saʿd and hurried to Quraysh to tell them of the horses and weapons. Quraysh were alarmed and said: "By Allah, we have introduced nothing new; we hold to our document and our term — why then does Muḥammad raid us with his Companions?"[^s5.190]

Ibn ʿUqbah reports that the Prophet ﷺ sent [[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]] ahead of him to Maymūnah bint al-Ḥārith to ask for her hand on his behalf; al-Ṣāliḥī refers the account to her biography.[^s5.190]

## Iḥrām and Yaʾjaj

The Prophet ﷺ entered *iḥrām* at the door of the mosque, because he took the road of al-Farʿ; otherwise he would have begun the *talbiyah* at al-Baydāʾ.[^s5.190][^s5.198] He travelled making the *talbiyah*, with the Muslims doing the same, until he reached Marr al-Ẓahrān.[^s5.190]

There he sent the weapons ahead to Baṭn Yaʾjaj, a valley near Makkah, when he saw the markers of the Ḥaram.[^s5.190][^s5.198] Quraysh sent Mikraz b. Ḥafṣ with a party, who met him at Baṭn Yaʾjaj, the *hady* and weapons having caught up. They said: "Muḥammad, we have not known you, small or great, for treachery — you enter the Ḥaram upon your people with weapons, when you stipulated to them that you enter only with the traveller's weapons, swords in their scabbards!" He answered: «I shall not enter upon them with weapons.» Mikraz replied: "That is what you are known by, righteousness and fidelity," and went back quickly to Makkah to tell Quraysh that Muḥammad would not enter with weapons and held to the condition he had made.[^s5.190][^s5.191]

At Marr al-Ẓahrān word reached the Companions that Quraysh were saying they could not rouse themselves for weakness. They proposed to slaughter some of their mounts, eat the meat and drink the broth, so as to be rested tomorrow when they entered. The Prophet ﷺ told them not to, but to gather their provisions to him; they spread leather mats and ate until they left off, and each filled his bag.[^s5.191]

## Entering Makkah

Ibn ʿAbbās says the Prophet ﷺ came to Makkah on the morning of the fourth of "Dhū al-Ḥijjah", as the page prints it.[^s5.191] When Mikraz brought Quraysh the news, some of the nobles of the idolaters could not bear to look at him, from rage, spite and envy.[^s5.191]

He ordered the *hady* ahead of him until it was halted at Dhū Ṭuwā, and entered on his mount al-Qaṣwāʾ, his Companions around him with swords slung on their shoulders, making the *talbiyah*. At Dhū Ṭuwā he halted on his mount, with the Muslims around him, and then entered by the pass that brings one out over al-Ḥajūn.[^s5.191][^s5.199]

It is reported from Anas and others that he entered Makkah in the year of the *qaḍiyyah* on his she-camel, with [[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]] ﵁ holding her rein and saying:[^s5.191]

> «خلوا بني الكفار عن سبيله ... نحن ضربناكم على تأويله»

ʿUmar ﵁ said to him: "O Ibn Rawāḥah — before the Messenger of Allah, and in the Ḥaram of Allah, you say poetry?" The Prophet ﷺ said: «Leave him, ʿUmar — they are swifter upon them than the shower of arrows.» In another report he said: «ʿUmar, I am listening; so be silent.» He told Ibn Rawāḥah to say: «There is no god but Allah alone; He helped His servant, gave honour to His army, and defeated the confederates alone.» Ibn Rawāḥah said it, and the people said it as he did.[^s5.191][^s5.192]

## The ṭawāf

The idolaters had said that a people whom the fever of Yathrib had weakened would come the next day, and sat on Quʿayqiʿān.[^bukh4256][^musl1266][^s5.192] Allah informed His Prophet ﷺ of what they said. On entering the mosque he did *iḍṭibāʿ* with his cloak, baring his right upper arm, and said: «May Allah have mercy on a man who shows them strength from himself»; in another report, «Show them what they dislike.»[^s5.192] He ordered his Companions to do *raml* for the three circuits and to walk between the two corners, so that the idolaters should see their hardiness. Ibn ʿAbbās says the only thing that kept him from ordering *raml* in all the circuits was leniency towards them.[^bukh4256][^musl1266] The idolaters said: "These are the ones you claimed the fever had weakened? These are hardier than so-and-so" — and, in the same report, that they leapt like gazelles; "and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ vexed them as much as he could."[^s5.192] In another report from Ibn ʿAbbās, he ran and did *raml* around the House only to show the idolaters his strength.[^musl1266]

He did not cease making the *talbiyah* until he touched the corner with his staff (*miḥjan*).[^s5.192] ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Awfā reports that the Companions screened him from the idolaters' boys — in one report, from the fools and the boys — for fear they would harm him.[^bukh4255][^s5.192] Some reports have him circle riding his she-camel and touch the corner with his staff; Hishām and Ibn Saʿd add that this was without any ailment, while the Muslims ran around him and Ibn Rawāḥah recited the rajaz.[^s5.192][^s5.193]

## The House and the saʿy

According to a report from Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab, when he finished his ṭawāf he entered the House and stayed in it until [[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]] ﵁, whom he had ordered to do so, gave the dawn call to prayer from the roof of the Kaʿbah. ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl said that Allah had honoured Abū al-Ḥakam in that he did not hear "this slave" say what he said; [[Ṣafwān b. Umayyah]] thanked Allah for taking his father before he saw it; Khālid b. Asīd said the same of his own father, "when Bilāl, son of Umm Bilāl, stands braying above the Kaʿbah"; and [[Suhayl b. ʿAmr]] and the men with him covered their faces. The page notes of ʿIkrimah, Ṣafwān, Khālid and Suhayl alike that each became a Muslim afterwards.[^s5.193]

This is disputed. Ibn Abī Awfā, asked whether the Messenger of Allah ﷺ entered the Kaʿbah in the *qaḍiyyah*, said: "No." And a report of Muḥammad b. ʿUmar from Dāwūd b. al-Ḥuṣayn says that he did not enter it: he sent to them and they refused, saying it was not in his stipulation.[^s5.193]

He did the *saʿy* between al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah on his mount. At the seventh circuit, at al-Marwah, where the *hady* had been halted, he said: «This is the place of slaughter, and all the passes of Makkah are a place of slaughter», and slaughtered there.[^s5.193] Men who had made ʿumrah with him but had not been present at al-Ḥudaybiyah did not slaughter; those who had been present and went out in the *qaḍiyyah* shared in the *hady*.[^s5.193][^s5.194] When the Muslims had circled the House and done the *saʿy* he ordered two hundred of his Companions to go to the party at Baṭn Yaʾjaj and stay with the weapons, while the others came and performed their rites; they did so.[^s5.194]

## Leaving Makkah

At noon on the fourth day, [[Suhayl b. ʿAmr]] and Ḥuwayṭib b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, both of whom became Muslim afterwards, came to him — Ibn Isḥāq says that Quraysh had charged Ḥuwayṭib with his expulsion — while he was in a gathering of the Anṣār talking with [[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]. They said: "Your term has ended, so leave us." He said: «What would it matter to you if you let me alone, so that I consummated my marriage among you and made food?» They said: "We have no need of your food; leave us. We adjure you by Allah, Muḥammad, and by the covenant between us and you, to leave our land — these three have passed."[^s5.194]

He had not lodged in a house: a leather tent was pitched for him at al-Abṭaḥ, and he was there until he left, never entering under the roof of any of its houses.[^s5.194][^s5.198] Saʿd ﵁ grew angry at the harshness of their speech and told Suhayl: "You lie, may you have no mother! It is not your land nor your father's land. By Allah, he will not leave it except willingly and content." The Messenger of Allah ﷺ smiled and said: «Saʿd, do not hurt a people who have visited us in our camp», and the two men fell silent towards Saʿd.[^s5.194]

In the Ṣaḥīḥ, from al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib: when the term had passed, they came to ʿAlī ﵁ and said, "Tell your companion to leave us, for the term has passed."[^bukh4251][^s5.194] ʿAlī told him, and the Prophet ﷺ ordered Abū Rāfiʿ ﵁ to set out, saying: «Let none of the Muslims spend the evening in it.» He rode until he alighted at Sarif, and left Abū Rāfiʿ behind to bring his wife [[Maymūnah bint al-Ḥārith]] ﵂ when evening came; Abū Rāfiʿ stayed until evening and then went out with her and those with her, and she met hardship from the fools of Makkah. Al-Ṣāliḥī refers the account of his marriage to her to her biography.[^s5.194][^s5.199] In Muslim's version, on the third day they said to ʿAlī: "This is the last day of your companion's term; tell him to leave" — and he said «Yes» and left; the commentary printed with it notes that this exchange belongs to the year after the treaty, that of this ʿumrah.[^musl1783]

## The daughter of Ḥamzah

As the Prophet ﷺ left, the daughter of [[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]] followed him calling "O uncle, O uncle"; ʿAlī took her by the hand and said to Fāṭimah ﵂: "Take your cousin."[^bukh4251][^s5.195] Her name is given as ʿUmārah, and it is said Umāmah — al-Ḥāfiẓ says the latter is the well-known; her mother was Salmā bint ʿUmays.[^s5.194][^s5.195] In one report, ʿAlī had spoken to the Prophet ﷺ in Makkah, saying, "Why do we leave our cousin an orphan among the idolaters?" — he did not forbid him, and ʿAlī took her out.[^s5.195]

[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]] ﵁, [[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]] ﵁ and [[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]] ﵁ disputed over her, after they had come to Madinah. ʿAlī said she was his cousin's daughter and that he had brought her out; Jaʿfar said she was his cousin's daughter and that her maternal aunt, [[Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays]] ﵂, was his wife; Zayd said she was his brother's daughter — Zayd had been Ḥamzah's executor, and the Prophet ﷺ had joined the two in brotherhood.[^bukh4251][^s5.195] The Prophet ﷺ judged her to her maternal aunt and said: «The maternal aunt is in the position of the mother.» To ʿAlī he said: «You are from me and I from you»; to Jaʿfar: «You resemble me in my build and my character»; to Zayd: «You are our brother and our *mawlā*.»[^bukh4251] In Ibn ʿAbbās's report the words are, to ʿAlī, «my brother and my companion», and to Zayd, «the *mawlā* of Allah and His Messenger.»[^s5.195] When he judged her to Jaʿfar, Jaʿfar stood up and hopped around him; asked what it was, he said that when the Negus was pleased with someone he would stand and hop.[^s5.195] ʿAlī asked whether the Prophet ﷺ would not marry her, and he said she was his brother's daughter by suckling.[^bukh4251]

## The return

Ibn Isḥāq says that the Prophet ﷺ then went back in Dhū al-Ḥijjah.[^s5.195] The number of the Muslims, apart from women and boys, was two thousand.[^s5.195] Ibn Hishām says that Allah revealed on this occasion, as Abū ʿUbaydah related to him, the verse that Allah has indeed fulfilled for His Messenger the vision in truth (al-Fatḥ 27), and that the "near conquest" in it means Khaybar.[^s5.195]

## Names and disputes

**The names.** It is called ʿUmrat al-Qiṣāṣ; al-Suhaylī says this name suits it best because of "The sacred month for the sacred month, and for sacred things there is retribution," a verse ʿAbd b. Ḥumayd reports from Mujāhid with a sound chain, and Sulaymān al-Taymī asserted it, as having been revealed concerning it.[^s5.195][^s5.196] It is called ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ, and there is disagreement as to why. Al-Suhaylī says that the Prophet ﷺ made a settlement (*qāḍā*) with Quraysh over it, or that he performed the ʿumrah in which he had been barred — an ʿumrah not invalidated by the barring but complete and accepted, so that when they shaved their hair outside the Ḥaram the wind carried it into the Ḥaram. Al-Qāḍī adds that *qaḍāʾ* means the decisive settlement on which the treaty was concluded, hence also ʿUmrat al-Qaḍiyyah. Others said it was a making-up for the first ʿumrah, with Ḥudaybiyah counted among his ʿumrahs for the reward and not because it was completed; al-Ṣāliḥī notes that this rests on a disagreement over whether one barred from the House owes *qaḍāʾ*, which the majority deny while requiring a *hady*, with Abū Ḥanīfah holding the reverse and Aḥmad two views.[^s5.196] The commentary on Muslim says that *qāḍā* here means "decided and carried out", and that those who said it was named for making up the ʿumrah from which he was barred were mistaken.[^musl1783] Ibn Isḥāq also calls it ʿUmrat al-Ṣulḥ.[^s5.196]

**Whether it was a ghazwah.** It is counted as one because Mūsā b. ʿUqbah, from Ibn Shihāb, says he went out prepared with weapons and fighters, fearing treachery from Quraysh — which does not require that fighting occurred. Ibn al-Athīr says it is not among the *ghazawāt*, and that al-Bukhārī mentioned it among them because it includes the reconciliation with the idolaters.[^s5.196]

**Ibn Rawāḥah's verses.** Ibn Hishām held that "we have killed you over its interpretation" and the lines that follow are ʿAmmār b. Yāsir's, from another day (al-Suhaylī: Ṣiffīn), since Ibn Rawāḥah was addressing idolaters, who did not acknowledge the revelation.[^s5.196] *Al-Bidāyah* finds this open to question: al-Bayhaqī related from Anas, by more than one route, that Ibn Rawāḥah walked before him reciting the verses, as also from Yazīd b. Aslam, and Ibn ʿUqbah and others followed Ibn Isḥāq.[^s5.196][^s5.197] Al-Ḥāfiẓ says that if the report is established there is no bar, the meaning possibly being "we struck you over our interpretation until you enter what we entered"; the wording "so today we strike you over its interpretation" appears to be ʿAmmār's, since there was no fighting in this ʿumrah, and the sound wording is "we struck you over its interpretation, as we struck you over its revelation."[^s5.197]

**Al-Tirmidhī and Kaʿb b. Mālik.** Al-Tirmidhī said that another ḥadīth gives the story to Kaʿb b. Mālik, and that this is sounder since Ibn Rawāḥah was killed at Muʾtah before this ʿumrah. Al-Ḥāfiẓ calls it a gross oversight and a rejected error, because the dispute of Jaʿfar, ʿAlī and Zayd over Ḥamzah's daughter belongs to this ʿumrah, and Jaʿfar, Zayd and Ibn Rawāḥah were killed in one place; he adds that some have al-Tirmidhī's report from Anas as concerning the conquest of Makkah, in which case the objection would stand, but that al-Ṣāliḥī saw the other reading in several copies of the *Jāmiʿ*.[^s5.197]

**The time of the departure.** That Suhayl and Ḥuwayṭib came at midday, al-Ṣāliḥī takes, shows that he entered early in the day, so that the three days were not complete until a like time on the fourth.[^s5.197]

**"O uncle."** The girl's address is explained as veneration of the Prophet ﷺ, or by Ḥamzah's being his brother by suckling as well as his uncle by lineage. ʿAlī's taking her out of Makkah was approved despite the stipulation that none of its people be taken out, because they had not asked for her, and because the believing women were not included in it — the Qurʾān on that came only after their return to Madinah.[^s5.197][^s5.198]
