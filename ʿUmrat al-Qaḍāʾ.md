# ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ (عمرة القضاء)
*The Compensatory Pilgrimage — The Triumphant Return to Makkah, The Demonstration of Physical Might, and the Marriage to Maymūnah*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Sources
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād*, Volume 5, *al-Bāb al-Sādis wal-ʿIshrūn fī ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ* (pp. 189–198), cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 8, pp. 322–324, entry no. 11783). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the canonical transmissions of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], Imām al-Bukhārī, Imām Muslim, Imām Aḥmad, and al-Bayhaqī.

---

## 1. Strategic Context & Religious Mandate

In Dhū al-Qaʿdah of the year seven AH (exactly one year following the Treaty of **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]** in which **[[Quraysh]]** turned the believers back from the Sacred House), the crescent of the sacred month appeared (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189). In accordance with the terms inscribed in the pact of al-Ḥudaybiyah and in fulfillment of the divine declaration:

> ﴿الشَّهْرُ الْحَرَامُ بِالشَّهْرِ الْحَرَامِ وَالْحُرُمَاتُ قِصَاصٌ﴾
> *(The sacred month is for the sacred month, and for the violations is legal retribution — al-Baqarah, 2:194)* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189).

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanded the believers to prepare for the minor pilgrimage (*ʿUmrah*). He issued an explicit directive that **not a single person who had witnessed al-Ḥudaybiyah should stay behind** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189). Every surviving participant of the Pledge of the Tree (**[[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]]**) mobilized, save for those who had been martyred at **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]** or had died in the intervening year (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189). 

### Divine Injunction of Solidarity
When certain poor Bedouins resident in Medina complained of having neither provisions nor means to sustain themselves, the Prophet ﷺ urged the wealthy Muslims to spend generously (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189). When some companions asked: *"With what shall we give charity when we possess nothing?"* the Prophet ﷺ answered: *«بِمَا كَانَ وَلَوْ بِشِقِّ تَمْرَةٍ»* (*"With whatever you possess, even if only half a date!"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189). 

Regarding this communal mobilization, Allāh revealed the famous verse of Sūrat al-Baqarah:
> ﴿وَأَنْفِقُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَلَا تُلْقُوا بِأَيْدِيكُمْ إِلَى التَّهْلُكَةِ﴾
> *(And spend in the way of Allāh, and do not throw [yourselves] with your own hands into destruction — al-Baqarah, 2:195).*
The classical exegetes (Ḥudhayfah, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrimah, and Mujāhid) explained that *al-tahlukah* (destruction) in this verse specifically refers to withholding financial expenditure in the path of Allāh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189).

The Prophet ﷺ appointed **Abū Ruhm al-Ghifārī** ﵁ (or ʿUwayf b. al-Aḍbaṭ) as his deputy governor over Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189).

## 2. Sacrificial Livestock & Military Readiness

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ departed Medina accompanied by approximately two thousand believers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189–190).

### The Sacrificial Beasts (*al-Hady*)
The Prophet ﷺ drove sixty sacrificial camels (*badanāt*) before him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190). He personally garlanded their necks (*qalladahā bi-yadihi*) with leather markers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190). He appointed **Nājiyah b. Jundab al-Aslamī** ﵁ over the herd, escorted by four young men of Aslam and **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁, to graze them in the pasture trees along the route (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190).

### Armed Vigilance at Batn Yaʾjaj
Although the terms of the treaty stipulated that Muslims could only enter Makkah bearing the "weapon of the traveler" (swords sheathed within scabbards, *al-suyūf fī al-qurub*), the Prophet ﷺ took profound defensive precautions against potential Quraysh treachery (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190). 

He loaded complete armaments—mail coats (*durūʿ*), iron helmets (*bayḍ*), and spears (*rimāḥ*)—and led **one hundred warhorses** commanded by the vigilant knight **[[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190). He placed the armory under the command of **Bashīr b. Saʿd** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190).

When companions questioned bringing heavy arms, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ articulated his doctrine of defensive readiness (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190):

> «إِنَّا لَا نَدْخُلُهُ عَلَيْهِمُ الْحَرَمَ، وَلَكِنْ يَكُونُ قَرِيبًا مِنَّا، فَإِنْ هَاجَنَا هَيْجٌ مِنَ الْقَوْمِ كَانَ السِّلَاحُ مِنَّا قَرِيبًا»
> *"We shall not bring the weapons into the Sacred Precinct upon them. Rather, they will remain close at hand; if an attack is launched against us by the enemy, our arms will be near us!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190).

The cavalry rode ahead to **Marr al-Ẓahrān**, alarming the Quraysh outposts. The Prophet ﷺ advanced the weapons cache to **Baṭn Yaʾjaj** (within sight of the boundary stones of the Ḥaram). When **Mikraz b. Ḥafṣ** rode out from Makkah demanding reassurance, the Prophet ﷺ affirmed: *"I shall not enter upon them bearing weapons"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190–191). Reassured by the Prophet's legendary fidelity to covenants (*al-birr wal-wafāʾ*), Mikraz returned to Makkah to calm the populace (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191).

## 3. The Triumphal Entry into Makkah

On the morning of the fourth day of Dhū al-Qaʿdah seven AH, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ entered Makkah mounted upon his noble she-camel, **al-Qaṣwāʾ** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191).

The Companions marched around him in disciplined formation, their swords sheathed and belted across their shoulders (*mutawashshiḥīna al-suyūf*), raising their voices in the universal chant of the Talbiyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191). Entering from the mountain pass overlooking al-Ḥajūn, the Prophet ﷺ advanced directly toward the Sacred Mosque (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191).

### The Poetry of ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah
Walking directly before the Prophet's mount and holding her reins was the poet **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]** ﵁, chanting verses of defiance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191):

> خَلُّوا بَنِي الْكُفَّارِ عَنْ سَبِيلِهِ ... نَحْنُ ضَرَبْنَاكُمْ عَلَى تَأْوِيلِهِ
> ضَرْبًا يُزِيلُ الْهَامَ عَنْ مَقِيلِهِ ... وَيُذْهِلُ الْخَلِيلَ عَنْ خَلِيلِهِ
> قَدْ أَنْزَلَ الرَّحْمَنُ فِي تَنْزِيلِهِ ... فِي صُحُفٍ تُتْلَى عَلَى رَسُولِهِ
> يَا رَبِّ إِنِّي مُؤْمِنٌ بِقِيلِهِ ... إِنِّي رَأَيْتُ الْحَقَّ فِي قَبُولِهِ
> *"Clear the way, O sons of the disbelievers, from his path! Today we strike you over its divine interpretation, just as we struck you over its revelation! A striking that severs the skulls from their resting places, and makes the closest friend oblivious of his friend! The All-Merciful has sent down in His revelation, in scriptures recited unto His Messenger: O Lord, indeed I believe in his word, for I have seen the ultimate truth in accepting him!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191).

When **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ cautioned Ibn Rawāḥah: *"O Ibn Rawāḥah! Reciting poetry in the presence of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and in the Sanctuary of Allāh?"* the Prophet ﷺ intervened:

> «خَلِّ عَنْهُ يَا عُمَرُ! فَلَهِيَ أَسْرَعُ فِيهِمْ مِنْ نَضْحِ النَّبْلِ!»
> *"Leave him, O ʿUmar! For indeed, these verses strike harder against them than flights of arrows!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:192).

The Prophet ﷺ then guided Ibn Rawāḥah to proclaim the sacred formula:
> «لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ، نَصَرَ عَبْدَهُ، وَأَعَزَّ جُنْدَهُ، وَهَزَمَ الْأَحْزَابَ وَحْدَهُ»
> *"There is no deity worthy of worship except Allāh alone; He aided His servant, granted victory to His soldiers, and routed the confederates alone!"* The two thousand Muslims echoed it with thunderous resonance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:192).

## 4. The Rites of Tawaf, Ramal & The Adhan Atop the Kaʿbah

The polytheists of Quraysh—refusing out of immense spite and grief to look upon the Muslims—had evacuated their homes and gathered atop the surrounding heights of **Mount Quʿayqiʿān** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:192). The polytheists mocked among themselves: *"Indeed, a delegation has come to you whom the fever of Yathrib has weakened!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:192).

### The Institution of *Ramal* (Trotting) and *Iḍṭibāʿ*
In response to their whispers, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ instructed the Muslims to bare their right shoulders (*al-iḍṭibāʿ*) and trot vigorously with rapid steps (*al-ramal*) during the first three circuits of Ṭawāf between the two corners (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:192–193):

> «رَحِمَ اللَّهُ امْرَأً أَرَاهُمُ الْيَوْمَ مِنْ نَفْسِهِ قُوَّةً!»
> *"May Allāh show mercy to any man who demonstrates physical might from himself before them this day!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:192).

Seeing the two thousand Muslim men trotting with boundless vitality, the Quraysh spectators on the mountains gasped in shock: *"Are these the ones you claimed were weakened by fever? They are more vigorous than gazelles!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:192). This historic act permanently instituted *Ramal* and *Iḍṭibāʿ* into the universal Sunnah of Ṭawāf (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:192).

### Bilāl's Adhān from the Roof of the Kaʿbah
Upon completing the Ṭawāf and Saʿy, the Prophet ﷺ entered the Kaʿbah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:193). When the dawn prayer (*ṣalāt al-fajr*) arrived, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanded **[[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]]** ﵁ to ascend to the very roof of the **Kaʿbah** to proclaim the Adhān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:193). 

As Bilāl's majestic voice echoed the Takbīr across the valley of Makkah, polytheist nobles on the hilltops shook with rage; ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl remarked: *"Allāh has honored Abū Jahl that he did not live to hear this slave call the prayer upon the Kaʿbah!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:193).

### Rotating the Guard at Baṭn Yaʾjaj
Demonstrating impeccable operational security, the Prophet ﷺ ordered two hundred armed companions who had completed their Ṭawāf and Saʿy to march out to **Baṭn Yaʾjaj** to relieve the detachment guarding the armaments, allowing those sentries to enter Makkah and complete their pilgrimage rites (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:194).

## 5. Marriage to Maymūnah bint al-Ḥārith

During his stay in Makkah, the Prophet ﷺ contracted marriage with the noble lady **[[Maymūnah bint al-Ḥārith]] al-Hilāliyyah** ﵂ (whose birth name was Barrah; the Prophet ﷺ named her *Maymūnah*, the Blessed, because it occurred during this blessed reconciliation) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190; *al-Iṣābah*, 8:322).

She had previously been married to Abū Ruhm b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā and had become a widow (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:322). Her sister, **Umm al-Faḍl Lubābah**, was the wife of the Prophet's paternal uncle **[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:322). 

The Prophet ﷺ dispatched **[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ (or Abū Rāfiʿ) to propose to her on his behalf (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190; *al-Iṣābah*, 8:322–323). Maymūnah entrusted her affair entirely to al-ʿAbbās, who gave her in marriage to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:322). 

The matrimonial alliance forged deep tribal bonds with the powerful confederation of Banū Hilāl of Hawāzin (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:322). ʿĀʾishah ﵂ later eulogized her, stating:
> «أَمَا إِنَّهَا كَانَتْ مِنْ أَتْقَانَا لِلَّهِ وَأَوْصَلِنَا لِلرَّحِمِ»
> *"Indeed, she was among the most fearful of us toward Allāh, and the most diligent of us in maintaining ties of kinship!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:324).

## 6. Departure from Makkah & The Daughter of Ḥamzah

At midday on the fourth day, the three days stipulated in the Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah elapsed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:194). **Suhayl b. ʿAmr** and **Ḥuwayṭib b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā** approached the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ while he was seated with **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁, demanding: *"Your time has expired; depart from our land!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:194).

Saʿd b. ʿUbādah bristled at their tone, but the Prophet ﷺ calmed him and strictly honored the treaty, ordering an immediate evacuation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:194). The Prophet ﷺ offered to host a marriage banquet for Quraysh, but they refused (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:194). The Prophet ﷺ moved his camp outside the Sacred Precinct to **Saraf** (approximately ten miles from Makkah), where he consummated his marriage with Maymūnah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:324).

### The Custody of Umāmah bint Ḥamzah
As the Muslim rearguard departed Makkah, **Umāmah**, the young orphaned daughter of **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁, ran after the caravan crying out: *«يَا عَمِّي! يَا عَمِّي!»* (*"O my uncle! O my uncle!"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:195). **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ took her hand and lifted her to **[[Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ]]** ﵂, saying: *"Take your cousin"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:195).

Upon returning to Medina, a dispute of profound noble altruism arose among three great leaders seeking her custody (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:195):
1. **ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib**: claimed custody because she was the daughter of his paternal uncle, and his wife Fāṭimah was the daughter of the Prophet ﷺ.
2. **Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib**: claimed custody because her maternal aunt (*khālah*), Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays, was his wife.
3. **[[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]**: claimed custody because the Prophet ﷺ had paired him in brotherhood (*muʾākhāh*) with Ḥamzah, making him Ḥamzah's appointed trustee (*waṣiyy*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:195).

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ arbitrated with divine justice:
- To ʿAlī he said: *«أَنْتَ مِنِّي وَأَنَا مِنْكَ»* (*"You are of me and I am of you"*).
- To Jaʿfar he said: *«أَشْبَهْتَ خَلْقِي وَخُلُقِي»* (*"You resemble my appearance and my character"*).
- To Zayd he said: *«أَنْتَ أَخُونَا وَمَوْلَانَا»* (*"You are our brother and our patron"*).

The Prophet ﷺ awarded custody of Umāmah to **Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib**, stating the immortal legal principle (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:195):
> «الْخَالَةُ بِمَنْزِلَةِ الْأُمِّ»
> *"The maternal aunt occupies the status of the mother."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:195; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

## 7. Strategic & Historical Legacy

ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ represented a decisive psychological watershed in the history of the Sīrah:
1. **Diplomatic Integrity**: The Prophet's scrupulous adherence to the three-day limit dismantled Quraysh's accusations of perfidy and won widespread moral admiration throughout the Ḥijāz (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:194).
2. **Catalyst for Major Conversions**: The dignity, harmony, and overwhelming spiritual power of the Muslim pilgrimage profoundly affected the elite of Quraysh. Within months of ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ, the two greatest military strategists of Arabia—**[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** and **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]**—along with the custodian of the Kaʿbah **ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah**, journeyed to Medina to embrace Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:198).
3. **Direct Precursor to the Conquest of Makkah**: The peaceful mobilization of two thousand pilgrims into the heart of Makkah demonstrated that the balance of authority in Arabia had irreversibly shifted, directly preparing the ground for **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]** just one year later (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:196).
