# ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ (عمرة القضاء)
*The Compensatory Umrah of Dhū al-Qaʿdah 7 AH — Fulfilment of the Vision and Triumphant Return to the Sanctuary*

> [!NOTE] Canonical Sourcing
> Primary narrative recorded in *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189–195) and referenced throughout the biographical notices of *al-Iṣābah* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:322).

---

## 1. Strategic Context & Mandate

In Dhū al-Qaʿdah 7 AH—precisely one year after the truce of [[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]] in 6 AH where the Quraysh obstructed the Muslims from the Sacred Mosque—the Prophet [[Muḥammad b. ʻAbdullah|Muḥammad]] ﷺ summoned the Companions to embark upon the compensatory pilgrimage (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189).

- **Scriptural Affirmation**: Revealed concerning this pilgrimage was the divine verdict:
  > ﴿الشَّهْرُ الْحَرامُ بِالشَّهْرِ الْحَرامِ وَالْحُرُماتُ قِصاصٌ﴾
  > *"The sacred month is for the sacred month, and for the prohibited things there is legal retribution."* (Sūrat al-Baqarah: 194; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189).
  Hence, classical authorities also designate it as **ʿUmrat al-Qiṣāṣ** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:195).
- **Participants**: The Prophet ﷺ commanded all veterans who had attended [[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]] and Ḥudaybiyah to mobilize. None stayed behind except those martyred at [[Ghazwat Khaybar]] or deceased in the interim (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189). The company comprised approximately two thousand men, excluding women and children (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:195).
- **Deputy in Madīnah**: The Prophet ﷺ appointed [[Abū Ruhm al-Ghifārī]] (Kulthūm b. al-Ḥuṣayn) as his administrative governor over Madīnah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:189).

---

## 2. March, Weapons at Yaʾjaj & Peaceful Entry

- **Precautionary Arms Deployment**: Conscious of Qurayshite perfidy, the Prophet ﷺ drove sixty sacrificial camels supervised by Nājiyah b. Jundub al-Aslamī, accompanied by a reserve cavalcade of one hundred horses commanded by [[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]], with armor and spears under [[Bashīr b. Saʿd]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190).
- **Stationing Arms at Batn Yaʾjaj**: At the outskirts of the Ḥaram boundary at **Baṭn Yaʾjaj**, the heavy arms and cavalcade were halted outside the sacred perimeter, honoring the treaty term that Muslims would enter bearing only travelers' weapons (sheathed swords) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190–191). Mikraz b. Ḥafṣ of Quraysh met the Prophet ﷺ at Yaʾjaj and verified the faithful observance of the pact (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:190–191).
- **Entry into Makkah**: On the fourth of the month, the Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah atop his she-camel al-Qaṣwāʾ through the pass of al-Ḥajūn (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191). The poet-warrior [[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]] walked ahead gripping the camel's bridle and chanting his famous lines:
  > «خلّوا بني الكفّار عن سبيله ... نحن ضربناكم على تأويله»
  > *"Clear out of his path, you sons of disbelievers! Today we strike you over its interpretation as we once struck you over its revelation!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191).

---

## 3. The Rites, Demonstration of Physical Might & Aftermath

- **Raml and Iḍṭibāʿ**: To counter the Qurayshite mockery that the fever of Yathrib had weakened the Muslims, the Prophet ﷺ commanded the Companions to uncover their right shoulders (*iḍṭibāʿ*) and trot briskly (*raml*) during the first three circuits of Ṭawāf between the Yemeni Corner and the Black Stone (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:191–193). The Quraysh watched from the heights of Mount Qubays in utter bewilderment at their vigor.
- **Custody of ʿUmārah bint Ḥamzah**: Upon concluding the three permitted days, the Prophet ﷺ prepared to depart. ʿUmārah (or Umāmah), the orphaned daughter of [[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]], ran behind calling out to the Prophet ﷺ. [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]], [[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]], and [[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]] contested for her guardianship. The Prophet ﷺ awarded custody to Jaʿfar because her maternal aunt [[Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays]] was his wife, issuing the famous legal maxim:
  > «الخالة بمنزلة الأم»
  > *"The maternal aunt occupies the station of the mother."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:194–195).
- **Alliance and Wedding**: While departing Makkah, the Prophet ﷺ concluded his marriage contract with [[Maymūnah bint al-Ḥārith]], culminating in their marriage feast at Saraf (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:194).
