# al-Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj (الإسراء والمعراج)
*The Night Journey to Jerusalem and the Ascension Through the Heavens*

## 1. The Occasioning Verse
Abū Ḥayyān transmits that this sūrah's opening verse was revealed because Quraysh disbelieved the
Prophet ﷺ when he first spoke of the Isrāʾ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:4).

## 2. Where and When
The book devotes a dedicated chapter to reconciling the several reports of where the journey began:
at **al-Ḥaṭīm** or **al-Ḥijr** (the Prophet's ﷺ own wording, per Qatādah, uncertain between the two —
al-Ḥāfiẓ [Ibn Ḥajar] holds al-Ḥaṭīm here means al-Ḥijr); "the roof of my house was opened while I was
in Makkah" (al-Zuhrī, from Anas); and Umm Hāniʾ's report that he spent that night in her house. Ibn
Ḥajar reconciles these: he had spent the night at Umm Hāniʾ's house, itself by the Shiʿb of Abū Ṭālib,
was roused there, and taken out to the mosque (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:64).

On the date, scholars disagree sharply. Many, including al-Nawawī, hold it was **one year before the
Hijrah**; Ibn Ḥazm claims consensus on this. Al-Qāḍī [ʿIyāḍ] instead argues for **five years before**
the Hijrah, reasoning that Khadījah ﵂ prayed with the Prophet ﷺ after the prayer was made obligatory,
and undisputedly died before the Hijrah. Ibn Daḥyah counters that the "prayer" Khadījah shared with
him before this was the earlier two-rakʿah morning-and-evening prayer, not the five daily prayers
ordained on this night — and ʿĀʾishah ﵂ herself stated that *"Khadījah died before the [five] prayers
were made obligatory,"* which the book treats as decisive: she died **before** the Isrāʾ, not after.
Ibn al-Athīr and al-Nawawī's *Fatāwā* hold the month was **Rabīʿ al-Awwal**, against the commonly-cited
Rajab (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:64–65).

## 3. Body and Soul, or Vision Alone?
The book records real scholarly disagreement on **how** the journey occurred, without picking one
account as certain beyond the majority position: most scholars hold it was **with body and soul
together, in full wakefulness**, from Makkah to Jerusalem to the highest heavens to Sidrat al-Muntahā
— al-Qāḍī [ʿIyāḍ] calls this *"the truth, upon which the verse and the well-attested reports of
ascent to the heavens are unambiguous."* A second position, citing Qurʾān 17:60's wording *"the vision
We showed you,"* holds the Isrāʾ to Jerusalem was bodily but the ascent to heaven was **by spirit
alone**; this is attributed to **Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān** and, by a report Ibn Isḥāq could not
directly verify, to **ʿĀʾishah** ﵂ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:69).

## 4. The Journey Begins
Riding **al-Burāq**, accompanied by **Jibrīl** ﵊, the Prophet ﷺ ascended a ladder (*al-miʿrāj*) "more
beautiful than any creation had seen," with steps of silver and of gold — one report describing it as
fashioned from the pearls of Paradise. Before ascending, he was offered a vessel of wine and a vessel
of milk (or, in another wording, honey); he chose the milk, and Jibrīl told him: *"You have chosen the
natural disposition (*al-fiṭrah*) — had you chosen the wine, your nation would have gone astray, and
had you chosen [only] the water, your nation would have drowned"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:86).

## 5. Through the Seven Heavens
At each heaven's gate, Jibrīl sought entry, was asked who accompanied him, and — on confirming
Muḥammad ﷺ had been sent for — was welcomed: *"Welcome to him; Allāh has brought him as a good
brother and a good successor."* The Prophet ﷺ met, in turn: **Ādam** ﵊ in the first heaven, before
whom the souls of his believing and disbelieving descendants were shown; **ʿĪsā and Yaḥyā** ﵊ in the
second; **Idrīs** ﵊ in the fourth, of whom Allāh had said he was raised to a lofty station; **Hārūn**
﵊ in the fifth; **Mūsā** ﵊ in the sixth, described as tall, ruddy, and thick-haired, who wept for the
multitude of the Prophet's ﷺ nation entering Paradise, exceeding his own; and **Ibrāhīm** ﵊ in the
seventh, seated with his back against **al-Bayt al-Maʿmūr**, resembling the Prophet ﷺ more than any of
his own descendants, who instructed him to convey to his nation the reward of the words *subḥāna
Allāh, wal-ḥamdu lillāh, wa-lā ilāha illā Allāh, wa-Allāhu akbar* as "the planting of Paradise" (*Subul
al-Hudā*, 3:86–89).

Between the heavens he was shown the fates of specific sinners: usurers unable to stand but as one
"struck down by Satan's touch," those who devour orphans' wealth swallowing stones of Hellfire,
adulterous and infanticidal women hung by their breasts or feet, and backbiters forced to eat the
flesh of their own brothers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:87).

## 6. Sidrat al-Muntahā and the Rivers of Paradise
At **Sidrat al-Muntahā**, its leaves the size of elephants' ears, covered in colours beyond
description and transformed into ruby and emerald, four rivers ran at its root — two hidden rivers of
Paradise, and two manifest ones identified as the **Nile** and the **Euphrates**. From a spring called
**al-Salsabīl** flowed **al-Kawthar**, whose water the Prophet ﷺ drank, finding it sweeter than honey
and more fragrant than musk, and a second river of mercy, in which he bathed and was forgiven all his
past and future sins (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:90).

## 7. Paradise, the Fire, and the Vision
Shown Paradise, "what no eye has seen nor ear heard," and then the Fire, "in it Allāh's wrath, rebuke,
and vengeance," he saw those who devour human flesh (backbiters) and the one who hamstrung the she-camel
of Ṣāliḥ ﵊. He was raised further, to where he heard the scratching of the Pens, and saw a man wrapped
in the light of the Throne — a man, Jibrīl told him, whose tongue in this world was ever moist with the
remembrance of Allāh and whose heart was attached to the mosques. There, the Prophet ﷺ fell in
prostration and Allāh spoke to him directly, reciting to him what had been given to Ibrāhīm, Mūsā,
Dāwūd, and ʿĪsā ﵊, and — per each of these prophets' own testimony, gathered together by the Prophet ﷺ
in the heavens above — being told he himself had been given what surpassed them all: mercy to all the
worlds, the Qurʾān, and a nation made "the best nation brought forth for mankind" (*Subul al-Hudā*,
3:85, 91).

## 8. Fifty Prayers Reduced to Five
Allāh ordained **fifty prayers** each day and night upon the Prophet ﷺ and his nation. Passing Mūsā ﵊
on the way down, he was told his people would never bear such a burden and urged to return and ask for
reduction — "I dealt with the Children of Israel before you, and tested them most severely with less
than this, and they grew weak and abandoned it." The Prophet ﷺ returned again and again, each time
Allāh removing five, until — having gone back and forth until, in his own words, "I grew too ashamed
before my Lord" — a voice called out: *"I have confirmed My decree and lightened the burden upon My
servants."* Mūsā ﵊ told him, *"Descend, in the name of Allāh"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:92–93).

## 9. Return to Makkah and Quraysh's Mockery
Passing several caravans of Quraysh on his return — noting one camel's load and its colours, and a
lost camel a group was searching for — he arrived among his companions in Makkah shortly before dawn.
The next morning, aware his people would disbelieve him, he sat grieving until **Abū Jahl** came and,
mockingly, asked if anything had happened. Told he had journeyed by night to Jerusalem and back before
morning, Abū Jahl — not wanting the story lost before he could use it against him — had him repeat it
before an assembly of **Banū Kaʿb b. Luʾayy**. **al-Muṭʿim [b. ʿAdī]** accused him of lying to his own
face; **Abū Bakr**, present, answered: *"Ill you have spoken to your nephew, rebuking and disbelieving
him — as for me, I bear witness he is truthful and trustworthy (*ṣiddīq ṣādiq*)."* Pressed to describe
Bayt al-Maqdis's structure in detail, the Prophet ﷺ described it until the description grew difficult
for him — at which point the mosque was made to appear before his own eyes, and he described it,
counting its very doors, while **Abū Bakr** repeated: *"You have spoken true — I bear witness you are
the Messenger of Allāh."*

> [!NOTE] The Origin of "al-Ṣiddīq"
> Asked whether he too believed the Prophet ﷺ had gone to Jerusalem and returned before morning, Abū
> Bakr answered: *"Yes — I believe him even in matters more remote than that; I believe him in news of
> the heavens, morning and evening."* *"For that, Abū Bakr was named **al-Ṣiddīq**"* (*Subul al-Hudā*,
> 3:94).

## 10. The Caravan Signs Confirmed
Asked about their own caravans, the Prophet ﷺ described one at al-Rawḥāʾ that had lost a camel, and
another entering Makkah from al-Tanʿīm on a specific Wednesday, led by a dun-coloured camel bearing two
black sacks — asking Allāh to delay the day when the caravan seemed not to arrive in time, so that the
sun itself was held back until it entered. Quraysh confirmed every detail, down to a bowl of water one
of them had poured out unseen — and, unable to deny the accuracy, called it sorcery instead, following
**al-Walīd**'s earlier verdict. Allāh then revealed:

> «وَمَا جَعَلْنَا الرُّؤْيَا الَّتِي أَرَيْنَاكَ إِلَّا فِتْنَةً لِلنَّاسِ»
> *"And We did not make the vision which We showed you except as a trial for the people"* (Qurʾān
> 17:60) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:94).

## 11. What Follows
Vindicated before his own companions even as Quraysh mocked him, the Prophet ﷺ continued seeking a
protector for the message outside Makkah, culminating in **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā]]** and
**[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]]**.
