# Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām (حكيم بن حزام)
*The Elder Born in the Kaʿbah, Nephew of Khadījah ﵂, Generous Smuggler of the Shiʿb, and Chieftain of Banū Asad*

## 1. Lineage, Unique Birth, and Pre-Islamic Chieftaincy

Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām b. Khuwaylid b. Asad b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. Quṣayy al-Asadī al-Qurashī, Abū Khālid (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:97). He was the nephew of Mother of the Believers [[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid]] ﵂ and a cousin of [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]] ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:97). His mother was Ṣafiyyah (or Fākhitah) bint Zuhayr b. al-Ḥārith b. Asad (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:97).

He possessed the singular historical distinction of being born inside the very interior of the Kaʿbah, when his mother went inside with a group of women and went into labor unexpectedly (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98). 

He was born thirteen years before the Year of the Elephant (*ʿĀm al-Fīl*), five years before the birth of the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98). In the Jāhiliyyah, he was entrusted with the office of *al-Rifādah* (hospitality for pilgrims), was a close friend of the Prophet ﷺ before the advent of revelation, and owned [[Dār al-Nadwah]], the ancient council house of Quraysh (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98).

## 2. Loyalty during the Boycott in the Shiʿb

During the severe three-year boycott of Banū Hāshim in the Mountain Pass (*Shiʿb Abī Ṭālib*), when Quraysh forbade selling food to the Muslims, Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām risked his life to smuggle provisions to his aunt Khadījah ﵂ and the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:378). 

On one occasion, Abū Jahl intercepted Ḥakīm while a servant was carrying wheat to the Shiʿb, seizing him and swearing: *"Are you taking food to Banū Hāshim? By Allah, you and your food shall not leave until I humiliate you in Makkah!"* Abū al-Bakhtarī intervened and reproached Abū Jahl, striking him with the jawbone of a camel to protect Ḥakīm and allow the grain through (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:378).

## 3. Preservation at Badr and Conversion at the Conquest

Ḥakīm attended [[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā|Badr]] under coercion alongside Quraysh, actively seeking to avert battle beforehand and urging ʿUtbah b. Rabīʿah to pay the blood money for ʿAmr b. al-Ḥaḍramī (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98). When battle erupted and the polytheists broke, Ḥakīm narrowly escaped on foot. For the rest of his life, whenever swearing a solemn oath, he would proclaim:
> «لا والذي نجاني يوم بدر»
> *"No, by the One Who delivered me on the Day of Badr!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98).

On the eve of [[Fatḥ Makkah]] in 8 AH, Ḥakīm embraced Islam alongside Abū Sufyān. The Prophet ﷺ honored his pre-eminent standing by announcing to all Makkah:
> «من دخل دار حكيم بن حزام فهو آمن»
> *"Whoever enters the house of Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām is secure!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98).

## 4. The Lesson of Self-Sufficiency and Prophetic Blessing

Ḥakīm witnessed [[Ghazwat Ḥunayn]] and was granted one hundred camels from the spoils of war at al-Jiʿrānah as one whose heart was to be reconciled (*al-Muʾallafat Qulūbuhum*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:397). He asked for another hundred, and the Prophet ﷺ granted them to him, then imparted a profound spiritual admonition:
> «يا حكيم، إن هذا المال خضرة حلوة، فمن أخذه بسخاوة نفس بورك له فيه، ومن أخذه بإشراف نفس لم يبارك له فيه، وكان كالذي يأكل ولا يشبع، واليد العليا خير من اليد السفلى»
> *"O Ḥakīm! This wealth is green and sweet. Whoever takes it with generosity of soul is blessed in it, but whoever takes it with greedy ambition is not blessed in it, like one who eats and is never satisfied; and the upper hand is better than the lower hand."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:397).

Deeply moved, Ḥakīm vowed: *"By the One Who sent you with the truth, I shall never ask anyone for anything after you, nor take from anyone until I depart this world!"* 

Throughout the Caliphates of [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]] ﵁ and [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]] ﵁, both leaders offered Ḥakīm his rightful annual stipend from the public treasury, but he steadfastly refused to accept any sum (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98). The Prophet ﷺ also supplicated for blessing in Ḥakīm's commerce, such that he never entered any transaction except that it prospered (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:209).

He later sold [[Dār al-Nadwah]] to [[Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān]] ﵁ for one hundred thousand dirhams. When his nephew [[ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr]] ﵁ questioned selling the ancestral relic of Quraysh, Ḥakīm replied: *"O my nephew, with it I have purchased an estate in Paradise!"* and distributed every dirham in charity (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98).

## 5. Longevity and Death

Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām was celebrated as one of the rare individuals who lived to an extraordinary age, passing away at one hundred and twenty years, sixty spent in the Jāhiliyyah and sixty in Islam (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:98, 2:98). 

He passed away in [[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah|Medina]] in 54 AH during the caliphate of Muʿāwiyah (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:98).
