# Banū Hawāzin (بنو هوازن)
*The Great Qaysī Confederation of the Hijaz Uplands and Foster-Kin of the Prophet ﷺ*

## 1. Lineage & Major Branches

**Banū Hawāzin** are a major confederation of northern (ʿAdnānī) Arabs originating from **Hawāzin b. Manṣūr** b. ʿIkrimah b. Khaṣafah b. Qays ʿAylān b. Muḍar (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:292, 378). Through Qays ʿAylān, they represent the premier sister branch of Khindif (Kinānah and [[Quraysh]]) within the Muḍarī trunk.

Their principal sub-tribes (*buṭūn*) played prominent individual roles in the Sīrah:
- **Banū Saʿd b. Bakr b. Hawāzin**: The clan of the Prophet's ﷺ wet-nurse **Ḥalīmah al-Saʿdiyyah** ﵂ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:378).
- **Banū Naṣr b. Muʿāwiyah b. Bakr b. Hawāzin**: The clan of supreme commander **[[Mālik b. ʿAwf al-Naṣrī]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:310).
- **Banū Jusham b. Muʿāwiyah b. Bakr b. Hawāzin**: The clan of the aged poet and strategist **Durayd b. al-Ṣimmah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:311, 352).
- **Banū Hilāl b. ʿĀmir b. Ṣaʿṣaʿah**: Maternal kin of several prominent figures, entering the wider Hawāzin nexus.

---

## 2. Fosterage of the Prophet ﷺ

The purest Arabic speech was preserved among the nomad encampments of Hawāzin. In his infancy, the Prophet ﷺ was entrusted to **Ḥalīmah bint Abī Dhuʾayb al-Saʿdiyyah** of Banū Saʿd b. Bakr b. Hawāzin (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:378). 

During his years amongst Banū Saʿd:
- His breast was cleansed by the angels Jibrīl and Mīkāʾīl in the event of **[[Shaqq al-Ṣadr]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:387–390).
- Abundant blessings (*barakah*) enveloped the flocks and home of Ḥalīmah and her husband al-Ḥārith b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:387).
- His foster-siblings included ʿAbdullāh b. al-Ḥārith, Unaysah, and Khudhāmah (al-Shaymāʾ) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:380–381).

Later in life, the Prophet ﷺ acknowledged this deep cultural and emotional affinity, maintaining lifelong honor and reverence for his foster-mother and foster-relations (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:384).

## 3. The Climax at the Battle of Ḥunayn (8 AH)

Following **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]** in 8 AH, the chieftains of Hawāzin gathered under the leadership of the youthful commander **[[Mālik b. ʿAwf al-Naṣrī]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:310). Refusing to surrender their ancestral autonomy to Islam, they assembled a massive confederate army at Awṭās and the valley of **[[Ḥunayn]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:310–311).

- **The Desperate Gamble**: Mālik commanded that all warriors bring forth their women, children, flocks, and property to prevent flight from the field (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:311).
- **Durayd's Warning**: Durayd b. al-Ṣimmah al-Jushamī severely reproached this tactic, asking: *«Does anything turn back a defeated man? If you win, only men with swords profit you; if you lose, you have exposed your families and wealth!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:311). Mālik dismissed the warning.
- **The Ambush & Muslim Victory**: Hawāzin lay in ambush within the narrow defiles of Ḥunayn, launching a devastating arrow assault that initially routed the Muslim vanguard (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:318). However, the Prophet's ﷺ unwavering firmness rallied the Companions, breaking Hawāzin and driving them from the field (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:323–325).

---

## 4. Repentance and Reconciliation at al-Jiʿrānah

Following the pursuit to Awṭās and the siege of **[[al-Ṭāʾif]]**, the vast spoils of Hawāzin were gathered at **[[Qismat Ghanāʾim Ḥunayn bi-al-Jiʿrānah|al-Jiʿrānah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:338, 388).

Before the spoils were completely apportioned, an official delegation from Hawāzin arrived in Medina/al-Jiʿrānah, having embraced Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:388). Invoking the Prophet's ﷺ childhood fosterage among them, their spokesman Zuhayr b. Ṣurad pleaded:
> «يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، إِنَّمَا فِي هَذِهِ الْحَظَائِرِ عَمَّاتُكَ وَخَالَاتُكَ وَحَوَاضِنُكَ اللَّاتِي كُنَّ يَكْفُلْنَكَ...»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh! Verily within these enclosures are your paternal aunts, maternal aunts, and the foster-nurses who once cared for you..."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:389).

Moved by filial piety and compassion, the Prophet ﷺ renounced his own share and that of Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, encouraging the Muhājirūn and Anṣār to do the same. Six thousand captive women and children were willingly restored to Hawāzin (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:389). Furthermore, Mālik b. ʿAwf embraced Islam, was appointed leader over his people, and received one hundred camels from the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:390).
