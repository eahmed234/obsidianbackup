# Quṭbah b. Qatādah al-ʿUdhrī (قطبة بن قتادة العذري)
*Commander of the Right Flank — Champion of Muʾtah and Slayer of Mālik b. Zāfilah*

## 1. Lineage & Identity
His full name is **Quṭbah b. Qatādah al-ʿUdhrī** (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340). He belonged to the noble northern Arab tribe of **Banū ʿUdhrah** (a branch of Quḍāʿah), famous in pre-Islamic Arabia for their poetic eloquence, chivalry, and mastery of desert navigation (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340).

Ibn Ḥajar notes that Ibn Isḥāq explicitly identified him as a man of Banū ʿUdhrah who held command on the day of Muʾtah (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340).

## 2. Generalship at Ghazwat Muʾtah (8 AH)
In Jumādā al-Ūlā 8 AH, when the Muslim expeditionary force of three thousand warriors deployed against the vast Byzantine-Arab confederacy at **[[Ghazwat Muʾtah]]**, the supreme commanders structured the battle lines into formal wings (*al-taʿbīʾah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148). 

**Quṭbah b. Qatādah al-ʿUdhrī** was entrusted with the supreme command of the Right Flank (**Maymanah**), while ʿAbāyah (or Rāfiʿ) b. Mālik al-Anṣārī commanded the Left Flank (**Maysarah**) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).

### Rallying the Troops and Slaying the Byzantine General
During the climactic combat, as the Muslim front lines were subjected to crushing encirclement by superior enemy numbers and the ranks momentarily buckled, Quṭbah b. Qatādah rode across the lines shouting his resolute battle cry:
> «يَا قَوْمِ، يُقْتَلُ الرَّجُلُ مُقْبِلًا خَيْرٌ مِنْ أَنْ يُقْتَلَ مُدْبِرًا!»
> *"O my people! For a man to be slain charging forward is far nobler than to be slain fleeing backward!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).

Spurring his horse directly toward the enemy command division, Quṭbah locked eyes with the prominent Byzantine/Ghassānid prince and auxiliary commander, **Mālik b. Zāfilah** (or Rāfilah) (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148). 

Quṭbah engaged him in a fierce mounted duel, struck him with his spear, and slew him on the battlefield, throwing the enemy vanguard into disarray (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148). 

He commemorated this duel in his celebrated martial poetry:
> طَعَنْتُ ابْنَ زَافِلَةَ الرُّومِيَّ طَعْنَةً ... فَأَثْبَتَّهُ بِالرَّمْحِ وَالْخَيْلُ تَنْفِرُ  
> وَجَادَلْتُهُ بِالسَّيْفِ حَتَّى عَلَوْتُهُ ... بِأَبْيَضَ صَافٍ كَالْعَقِيقَةِ يَبْتُرُ  
> *"I struck Ibn Zāfilah the Byzantine a piercing thrust, fixing him to the earth with my spear while the warhorses reared; and I duelled him with the sword until I overcame him with a gleaming blade like a flash of lightning that shears through armor!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:340; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:148).

His steadfast defense of the right wing anchored the Muslim lines through the darkest hours of the confrontation, enabling **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁ to successfully orchestrate the tactical withdrawal of the army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:151).
