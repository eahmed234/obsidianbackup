# al-Juḥfah (الجحفة)
*The Historic Waystation and Mīqāt of the Northern Pilgrims*

## 1. Topography & Sanctified Designation
Al-Juḥfah (anciently named *Mahyaʿah*) is a significant geographical waystation situated approximately eighty-two miles north of Makkah, near the coastal settlement of Rābigh along the old commercial route linking the Ḥijāz with Egypt and Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:211). It was designated by the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ as the canonical pilgrimage entry point (**Mīqāt**) for the pilgrims of the Levant, Egypt, and Morocco (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:211).

When fever and malaria afflicted the Muhājirūn upon arriving in Medina, the Prophet ﷺ supplicated:
> «اللَّهُمَّ حَبِّبْ إِلَيْنَا الْمَدِينَةَ كَحُبِّنَا مَكَّةَ أَوْ أَشَدَّ... وَانْقُلْ حُمَّاهَا إِلَى الْجُحْفَةِ»
> *"O Allāh! Endear Medina to us as our love for Makkah or even more... and transfer its fever to al-Juḥfah"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:297).

## 2. The Turning of Banū Zuhrah at Badr (2 AH)
During **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, the Meccan army halted at al-Juḥfah when a rider arrived from **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** informing them that the caravan had safely slipped past the Muslims along the Red Sea coast and urging Quraysh to return to Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:28–29).

While **[[Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)]]** ☠ obstinately refused, insisting on marching to Badr, chieftain al-Akhnas b. Sharīq convinced the entire contingent of **[[Banū Zuhrah]]** (approximately three hundred men) to withdraw from al-Juḥfah, sparing their clan from annihilation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29).
