# Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl (حلف الفضول)
*The League of the Virtuous — The Pre-Islamic Covenant of Universal Justice and Protection of the Oppressed*

## 1. Historical Context & Timing
*Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl* was solemnized in the sacred month of **Dhū al-Qaʿdah**, approximately twenty years prior to the Prophetic Mission (*al-Mabʿath*) and shortly following the cessation of the sacrilegious [[Ḥarb al-Fijār|Fijār War]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154). The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was then **twenty years of age**. 

Celebrated across pre-Islamic Arabia as the noblest, most chivalric confederacy ever ratified, it established an ethical precedent in Makkah where clan partisanship had previously shielded wealthy oppressors from accountability (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154).

## 2. The Inciting Outrage: The Merchant of Zubayd
The immediate catalyst was an act of blatant commercial fraud perpetrated against a foreign merchant from the Yemeni tribe of **Zubayd** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154):
- The Zubaydī merchant arrived in Makkah with valuable merchandise. **Al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil al-Sahmī**—a formidable Qurashī aristocrat of high standing and father of [[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]—purchased the consignment on credit but subsequently locked away the goods and refused to pay the merchant his rightful price.
- The desperate merchant sought arbitration from the allied clans of Quraysh known as *al-Aḥlāf* (comprising ʿAbd al-Dār, Makhzūm, Jumaḥ, and Sahm). Bound by tribal favoritism, they spurned his plea, rebuking and driving him away with threats (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154).
- Finding no judicial recourse, the Zubaydī merchant ascended the summit of **Mount Abū Qubays** at sunrise while the chieftains of Quraysh sat assembled in their customary tribal circles around the Kaʿbah. In a piercing voice, he declaimed:
  > «يَا آلَ فِهْرٍ لِمَظْلُومٍ بِضَاعَتُهُ ... بِبَطْنِ مَكَّةَ نَائِي الدَّارِ وَالنَّفَرِ»
  > «وَمُحْرِمٍ أَشْعَثَ لَمْ يَقْضِ عُمْرَتَهُ ... يَا لِلرِّجَالِ وَبَيْنَ الْحِجْرِ وَالْحَجَرِ»
  > «إِنَّ الْحَرَامَ لِمَنْ تَمَّتْ مَكَارِمُهُ ... وَلَا حَرَامَ لِثَوْبِ الْفَاجِرِ الْغُدَرِ»
  > *"O family of [[Fihr b. Mālik|Fihr]]! Help an oppressed stranger whose goods have been seized in the heart of Makkah, far from home and clan!*
  > *A pilgrim in iḥrām, unkempt, his ʿUmrah yet unfulfilled! O men of chivalry, come forth, here between the Ḥijr and the Black Stone!*
  > *Verily, sanctity belongs only to him whose noble deeds are complete; there is no sanctity for the mantle of the treacherous sinner!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154).

## 3. The Convocation at the House of Ibn Judʿān
Upon hearing the merchant's lament, **[[al-Zubayr b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]**—the paternal uncle of the Prophet ﷺ—rose in outrage and demanded: *«أَلِهَذَا مَتْرَكٌ؟»* (*"Is an outrage like this to be left unchecked?"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154).

Al-Zubayr rallied the leading patrician clans of Quraysh to meet at the residence of **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Judʿān]] al-Taymī**, who was renowned for his open hospitality and immense wealth. Ibn Judʿān prepared a lavish banquet, and the representatives of five foremost lineages convened:
1. **[[Banū Hāshim]]**
2. **[[Banū al-Muṭṭalib]]**
3. **Banū Asad b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā**
4. **[[Banū Zuhrah]]**
5. **Banū Taym**

### The Solemn Oath
Standing together in the sacred month, the delegates swore a binding, perpetual covenant before Allāh:
> «لَيَكُونُنَّ يَدًا وَاحِدَةً مَعَ الْمَظْلُومِ عَلَى الظَّالِمِ حَتَّى يُؤَدَّى إِلَيْهِ حَقُّهُ مَا بَلَّ بَحْرٌ صُوفَةً، وَمَا رَسَا حِرَاءُ وَثَبِيرٌ مَكَانَهُمَا، وَعَلَى التَّآسِي فِي الْمَعَاشِ»
> *"That they would stand as a single hand alongside the oppressed against the oppressor until his full right is restored to him—as long as the ocean wets a tuft of wool, and as long as Mounts [[Ḥirāʾ]] and Thabīr stand steadfast upon their bases; and to practice mutual solace and support in livelihood."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154).

Directly following the ratification, the confederates marched in a body to the palace of al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil al-Sahmī, forcibly confiscated the Zubaydī merchant's property, and restored it to the merchant in full (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154).

## 4. Etymology of the Title (*Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl*)
Scholars cite three classical explanations for the designation *Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154–155):
1. **The Remark of Quraysh**: When rival clans saw the signatories intervention against al-ʿĀṣ, they remarked resentfully: *«لقد دخل هؤلاء في فضول من الأمر»* (*"These people have entered into an extraordinary, surplus jurisdiction beyond tribal precedent"*).
2. **The Ancient Jurhum Precedent**: Centuriess earlier, three elders of the ancient tribe of Jurhum—each named *al-Faḍl* (al-Faḍl b. Faḍālah, al-Faḍl b. Wadāʿah, and al-Faḍl b. al-Ḥārith)—had sworn an identical pact to protect the wronged. Because this pact resembled their deed, it was called *Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl* (the Pact of the Faḍls) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:155).
3. **Restitution of Rights**: It was derived from *radd al-fuḍūl*—compelling the powerful to return surplus illicit takings (*fuḍūl*) to their rightful proprietors (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:155).

## 5. Prophetic Witness & Endorsement in Islam
The Prophet [[Muḥammad b. ʻAbdullah|Muḥammad]] ﷺ was present in person at the assembly alongside his uncles and witnessed the swearing of the oath. Throughout his prophetic mission, he recalled this pact with profound admiration, elevating it into a permanent ethical model:
> «لَقَدْ شَهِدْتُ فِي دَارِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ جُدْعَانَ حِلْفًا مَا أُحِبُّ أَنَّ لِيَ بِهِ حُمْرَ النَّعَمِ، وَلَوْ أُدْعَى بِهِ فِي الْإِسْلَامِ لَأَجَبْتُ»
> *"I witnessed in the house of ʿAbdullāh b. Judʿān an alliance that I would not exchange for red camels; and were I to be summoned to it in Islam, I would surely answer the call!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154; transmitted by Aḥmad and al-Bayhaqī from Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh and Jubayr b. Muṭʿim).

## 6. Juristic Significance (*Aḥkām*)
Muslim jurists derive vital legal maxims from the Prophet's sanction of Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl:
- **Legitimacy of Coalitions for Justice**: The Islamic state and Muslim individuals are fully permitted to form pacts, treaties, and civil rights coalitions with non-Muslim entities to defend human rights, eradicate oppression, and assist the destitute.
- **Supremacy of Justice over Tribalism**: The pact broke the inviolability of clan nepotism, demonstrating that justice (*ʿadl*) is an absolute moral objective transcending ethnic or tribal solidarity.
