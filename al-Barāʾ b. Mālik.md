# al-Barāʾ b. Mālik (البراء بن مالك)
*Hero of the Garden of Death at Yamāmah, Whose Supplication Allah Fulfilled, and Martyr of Tustar*

## 1. Lineage and Character

al-Barāʾ b. Mālik b. al-Naḍr b. Ḍamḍam b. Zayd b. Ḥarām b. Jundab b. ʿĀmir b. Ghanm b. ʿAdī b. al-Najjār al-Anṣārī al-Khazrajī al-Najjārī (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:412). He was the brother of [[Anas b. Mālik]] ﵁ and shared the maternal kinship with Sharīk b. Saḥmāʾ (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:412).

al-Barāʾ was renowned for his melodious chanting voice, serving as the camel-driver (*ḥādī*) chanting poetry for the men on expeditions with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413). On one journey when women were present, the Prophet ﷺ affectionately cautioned him:
> «إياك والقوارير»
> *"Gently with the delicate vessels!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413).

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ declared concerning him in an authentic tradition:
> «كم من ضعيف متضاعف ذي طمرين لو أقسم على الله لأبره، منهم البراء بن مالك»
> *"How many a disheveled, dust-covered soul clad in two tattered garments, overlooked by the people, who if he were to swear by Allah, Allah would surely fulfill his oath—and among them is al-Barāʾ b. Mālik."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414; *Subul al-Hudā*, 10:98).

He witnessed every major campaign with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ after [[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā|Badr]] (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413).

## 2. The Garden of Death at Yamāmah (11 AH)

During the [[Ḥurūb al-Riddah|Riddah Wars]] at the Battle of ʿAqrabāʾ in al-Yamāmah against the false prophet Musaylimah, the polytheists barricaded themselves behind the formidable fortified walls of a stone garden, known thereafter as *Ḥadīqat al-Mawt* ("The Garden of Death") (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413).

When the Muslims were held at bay by the locked gates, al-Barāʾ addressed them:
> «يا معشر المسلمين، ألقوني إليهم»
> *"O gathering of Muslims! Hoist me upon your shields and throw me inside over the wall to them!"*

They raised him until he peered over the parapet, and he hurled himself directly into the midst of the enemy garrison (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413). He engaged them single-handedly, fighting fiercely until he forced open the massive gates from within for the Muslim army. 

The Muslim army poured into the enclosure and defeated the forces of Musaylimah (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413). During the assault, al-Barāʾ received over eighty wounds from arrows, spears, and sword strikes; [[Khālid b. al-Walīd]] ﵁ personally remained beside him for a whole month tending his wounds until he recovered (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413).

During this campaign, al-Barāʾ personally fought and struck down the enemy chieftain Muḥakkam al-Yamāmah, breaking his sword across him (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414). In total, he felled one hundred warriors in single combat, over and above those he slew alongside others (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413).

## 3. The Rescue of Anas b. Mālik

During an assault on an enemy fortress, the besieged hurled down iron chains heated white-hot with iron grappling hooks attached, snaring [[Anas b. Mālik]] ﵁ by his clothes to haul him up into the fortification (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414). 

al-Barāʾ dashed up the ramparts, seized the glowing iron chain with his bare hands, and gripped it unflinchingly until he severed the cord and freed his brother. When he withdrew his hands, the flesh had been scorched away down to the bare bone, saving Anas's life (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414).

## 4. Siege of Tustar and Martyrdom (20 AH)

During the conquest of Khūzistān at the siege of the great citadel of Tustar in 20 AH under [[Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī]] ﵁, the Muslim army encountered fierce resistance from the Persian forces commanded by the Hormuzān (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:413–414, 5:575).

When the battle stalled, the Muslims called upon al-Barāʾ:
> «يا براء، أقسم على ربك!»
> *"O Barāʾ! Swear an oath upon your Lord!"*

al-Barāʾ raised his hands and supplicated:
> «أقسم عليك يا رب لما منحتنا أكتافهم، وألحقتني بنبيك»
> *"I adjure You, O my Lord, that You grant us victory over their shoulders, and that You join me with Your Prophet!"*

He charged immediately into the Persian vanguard. The Persian general of al-Zārah was slain, the Persian lines broke into retreat, and al-Barāʾ achieved martyrdom in the fray, struck down by the Hormuzān (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414, 5:575).
