# Abū Lahab (أبو لهب)
*ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib ☠ — The Uncle Condemned by Name in the Qurʾān*

## 1. Identity
Son of **[[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** and thus paternal uncle of the Prophet ﷺ. He is the singular
exception within **[[Banū Hāshim]]**: where the clan closed ranks around its kinsman during
**[[al-Ḥiṣār fī Shiʿb Abī Ṭālib]]**, Abū Lahab broke with his own house and sided with Quraysh.

His wife **Umm Jamīl** — sister of [[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]] ﵁ — joined him in the hostility, and is
included in the condemnation of Sūrat al-Masad.

## 2. Qurʾānic Condemnation
He is the only named opponent of the Prophet ﷺ condemned by *kunyah* in a whole sūrah:
> ﴿تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ﴾
> *"Perish the hands of Abū Lahab, and perish he."* (111:1)

Revealed in his lifetime, it foretold his end while he still had every opportunity to falsify it —
and he never did.

## 3. Death After Badr
He did not fight at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** — and the book singles him out for it. Describing
the Meccan muster it says they went out *"in nine hundred and fifty fighters, and it is said in a
thousand, and **not one of their nobles stayed behind except Abū Lahab**"*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:22).

Al-Ṣāliḥī
treats the arrival of the news of Badr to the families of the slain together with the destruction of
Abū Lahab in a single section — *dhikr wuṣūl khabar muṣāb ahl Badr ilā ahlīhim wa-mahlak Abī Lahab*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:66). > [!NOTE] What the Book Does Not Say
> An earlier version of this note said he died of *al-ʿadasah*, a pustular affliction Quraysh feared
> as contagious, and that his family left his body untended. **Neither detail is supported here** —
> the book uses the word *ʿadasah* only as a size comparison for the stones cast at the Elephant
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:214). Both claims have been removed; the book's own statement is simply that
> his destruction (*mahlak*) followed the news of Badr.

Al-Ṣāliḥī separately records the answered supplications of the Prophet ﷺ against his sons
[[ʿUtbah b. Abī Lahab]] and Lahab b. Abī Lahab (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:216, 10:220).

## 4. A Note on His Sons
Two of his sons, **ʿUtbah and Muʿattib**, later accepted Islam — al-Ṣāliḥī gives their conversion its
own section around the time of **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:250), where the corpus marks
them with the dual honorific ﵄. The father's condemnation was personal, not hereditary.
