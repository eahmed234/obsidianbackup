# al-Daʿwah al-Sirriyyah wal-Jahriyyah (الدعوة السرية والجهرية)
*Three Years of Secret Preaching, the Public Proclamation on al-Ṣafā, and Quraysh's Response*

## 1. The First Believers
Al-Ṣāliḥī opens with the near-unanimous position of the scholars: **[[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid]]** ﵂
was the very first human being to believe — *"Khadījah is the first of Allāh's creation to accept
Islam, by the consensus of the Muslims; no man or woman preceded her"* (Ibn al-Athīr, endorsed by
al-Dhahabī). The dispute is only over **who was first after her** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:300).

On that question the book transmits several competing chains. Ibn Isḥāq and al-Zuhrī hold that
**[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ was the first male believer, converting while still a boy in the
Prophet's ﷺ own household — Abū Ṭālib having given him into the Prophet's ﷺ care during a year of
famine that struck Quraysh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:301). ʿAlī himself is quoted: *"The first of this
community to accept Islam, after Khadījah, were Abū Bakr and I; I accepted Islam before Abū Bakr,
but concealed my faith from fear of my father, until my father met me and asked, 'Have you accepted
Islam?' I said, 'Yes.' He said, 'Then support your cousin and help him'"* (al-Bayhaqī, via Muḥammad
b. Kaʿb al-Quraẓī; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:302). Other reports, via Salmān, al-Miqdād, **Khabbāb**, Jābir,
Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī and Zayd b. Arqam, agree that ʿAlī was first (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:302). Ibn Isḥāq
adds that **Zayd b. Ḥārithah** ﵁ believed next (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:302).

A separate position, transmitted by Muḥammad b. Kaʿb al-Quraẓī, holds instead that **Abū Bakr** was
the **first to openly profess** his Islam, even if ʿAlī had believed privately before him — reconciling
the two claims: *"Abū Bakr was the first to manifest his Islam, and ʿAlī was the first to believe
after Khadījah"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:304). A further, weaker report names **Waraqah b. Nawfal** as the
first man to believe, on the strength of his recognition of the Prophet's ﷺ mission at the Cave of
Ḥirāʾ — though this is contested, since Waraqah died before the era of open dawah proper (*Subul
al-Hudā*, 2:304).

## 2. Abū Bakr's Converts
Once Abū Bakr ﵁ had openly professed Islam, *"he called to it whomever he trusted among his people
who visited and sat with him"* — being *"a man beloved by his people, easy-natured, the most
knowledgeable of Quraysh in genealogy, in commerce, and in the good and ill among them."* Through him
accepted Islam:

- **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ b. Umayyah b. ʿAbd Shams
- **al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām** b. Khuwaylid b. Asad
- **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf** b. ʿAbd ʿAwf, of Banū Zuhrah
- **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]**, Mālik b. Uhayb, of Banū Zuhrah
- **Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh** b. ʿUthmān b. ʿAmr

(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:305)

## 3. The Story of Abū Dharr and His Brother Unays
Al-Ṣāliḥī gives a dedicated chapter to the conversion of **Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī** ﵁, in Makkah, before
the Hijrah — his brother Unays returning first with news of a man who *"commands virtue and forbids
vice,"* their mother's own acceptance of Islam, and the eventual conversion of half, then all, of
Banū Ghifār, with the Prophet's ﷺ blessing upon them: *"Ghifār — may Allāh forgive them; and Aslam —
may Allāh grant them peace"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:316).

## 4. Dār al-Arqam
As converts multiplied but remained a hunted minority, the Prophet ﷺ took to entering the house of
**[[al-Arqam b. Abī al-Arqam]]** near al-Ṣafā to worship Allāh in secret from his people, and a
company of Muslims gathered there with him *"until the believers reached the number of forty men, the
last of them ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb."* Once the fortieth had joined, they emerged. When **ʿUmar** ﵁
himself had accepted Islam he asked: *"O Messenger of Allāh, why do we conceal our religion when we
are upon the truth and their religion is manifest upon falsehood?"* The Prophet ﷺ answered: *"O ʿUmar,
we are few."* ʿUmar swore: *"By the One who sent you with the truth, there will not remain a single
gathering in which I sat in disbelief without my manifesting belief in it there"* (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:319) — see **[[Dār al-Arqam]]** for the house itself, and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** for the fuller
account of his conversion below.

> [!NOTE] A Second Count
> ʿĀʾishah ﵂ transmits, via Sulaymān b. Khaythamah, that when the Prophet's ﷺ companions numbered
> **thirty-eight**, Abū Bakr pressed him to make the call public; the Prophet ﷺ answered, *"O Abū Bakr,
> we are few,"* until Abū Bakr's insistence prevailed and he emerged (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:319–320). This
> account also places **Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib**'s conversion at the moment Abū Bakr himself was
> struck down and rescued — *"they remained with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ in the house a month, thirty-
> nine men"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:320).

## 5. The Command to Proclaim
Allāh then commanded: *«فَاصْدَعْ بِمَا تُؤْمَرُ»* — *"So proclaim openly what you are commanded"*
(Qurʾān 15:94), and *«وَأَنْذِرْ عَشِيرَتَكَ الْأَقْرَبِينَ»* — *"And warn your nearest kinsfolk"*
(Qurʾān 26:214), meaning **Banū Hāshim and Banū al-Muṭṭalib**, whom he warned openly. Ibn Saʿd
transmits that the Prophet ﷺ was commanded to proclaim what had come to him from Allāh and to call
the people to Him — *"and he had called to Allāh in the first period of his prophethood for three
years in secret, until he was commanded to make the call manifest."* Al-Balādhurī, from ʿĀʾishah ﵂,
instead gives **four years** of secret calling (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:322).

## 6. The Gathering of Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib
When *«وَأَنْذِرْ عَشِيرَتَكَ الْأَقْرَبِينَ»* was revealed, the matter weighed heavily on the Prophet
ﷺ, and he remained in his house about a month until his aunts, thinking him ill, came to visit him. He
told them: *"I have no ailment, but Allāh has commanded me to warn my nearest kinsfolk, and I wish to
gather Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib to call them to Allāh."* They told him to call them, but to leave out
**Abū Lahab**, since he would not answer the call. The next morning he sent for Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib,
and with them a number of Banū ʿAbd Manāf — **forty-five men in all**. Abū Lahab hurried to attend,
supposing his nephew meant to abandon what they disliked; but before the Prophet ﷺ could speak, Abū
Lahab warned the gathering that all of Arabia together could not withstand them if they closed ranks
around him, and that *"I have not seen anyone bring his father's people worse than what you have
brought your people."* The Prophet ﷺ fell silent that day (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:322).

Jibrīl ﵊ then came down and encouraged him to carry out what Allāh had commanded regardless. The
Prophet ﷺ gathered them a second time and, after praising and affirming Allāh, said:

> «إِنَّ الرَّائِدَ لَا يَكْذِبُ أَهْلَهُ، وَاللَّهِ لَوْ كَذَبْتُ النَّاسَ جَمِيعًا مَا كَذَبْتُكُمْ، وَلَوْ غَرَرْتُ النَّاسَ مَا غَرَرْتُكُمْ، وَاللَّهِ الَّذِي لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ إِنِّي لَرَسُولُ اللَّهِ إِلَيْكُمْ خَاصَّةً وَإِلَى النَّاسِ كَافَّةً، وَاللَّهِ لَتَمُوتُنَّ كَمَا تَنَامُونَ وَلَتُبْعَثُنَّ كَمَا تَسْتَيْقِظُونَ، وَلَتُحَاسَبُنَّ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ، وَلَتُجْزَوُنَّ بِالْإِحْسَانِ إِحْسَانًا وَبِالسُّوءِ سُوءًا، وَإِنَّهَا لَلْجَنَّةُ أَبَدًا أَوِ النَّارُ أَبَدًا، وَإِنَّكُمْ لَأَوَّلُ مَنْ أُنْذِرَ، وَمَثَلِي وَمَثَلُكُمْ كَمَثَلِ رَجُلٍ رَأَى الْعَدُوَّ فَانْطَلَقَ يَرْبَأُ أَهْلَهُ فَخَشِيَ أَنْ يَسْبِقُوهُ فَجَعَلَ يَهْتِفُ: يَا صَبَاحَاهْ!»
> *"The scout does not lie to his own people. By Allāh, if I lied to all mankind I would not lie to
> you, and if I deceived all mankind I would not deceive you. By Allāh besides Whom there is no god,
> I am indeed the Messenger of Allāh to you specifically and to mankind generally. By Allāh, you shall
> surely die as you sleep, and be raised as you awaken, and be called to account for what you did, and
> be repaid good for good and evil for evil, and it is Paradise forever or the Fire forever, and you
> are the first to be warned. My likeness and yours is that of a man who saw the enemy and went to
> warn his people, but fearing they would be overtaken before he reached them, cried out: 'O the morning
> [of attack]!'"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:322–323)

**[[Abū Ṭālib]]** answered with support, but confessed he could not bring himself to abandon *"the
religion of ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib"* — the position on his faith recorded in his own note. **Abū Lahab** spoke
harshly: *"O Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, this, by Allāh, is the disgrace — restrain him before others do."*
Abū Ṭālib answered: *"By Allāh, we will protect him as long as we remain."* **Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd
al-Muṭṭalib** rebuked Abū Lahab directly — *"Does it become you, my brother, to abandon your nephew and
his Islam?"* — reminding him that the learned had long said a prophet would arise from ʿAbd
al-Muṭṭalib's line. Abū Lahab dismissed it as *"falsehood and women's talk in the bridal chambers"*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:323).

## 7. The Proclamation on Mount al-Ṣafā
Al-Bukhārī and Muslim (from Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄, Abū Hurayrah ﵁, and Qabīṣah b. al-Mukhāriq ﵁) record that
when *«وَأَنْذِرْ عَشِيرَتَكَ الْأَقْرَبِينَ»* was revealed, the Prophet ﷺ climbed **Mount al-Ṣafā** and
called out: **«يَا صَبَاحَاهْ!»** — the traditional cry of alarm. Quraysh gathered around him, and he
asked them:

> «أَرَأَيْتُمْ إِنْ أَخْبَرْتُكُمْ أَنَّ خَيْلًا تَخْرُجُ مِنْ سَفْحِ هَٰذَا الْجَبَلِ تُرِيدُ أَنْ تُغِيرَ عَلَيْكُمْ، أَكُنْتُمْ مُصَدِّقِيَّ؟»
> *"Tell me — if I informed you that cavalry were coming out from the foot of this mountain to raid
> you, would you believe me?"*

They answered: *"We have never known anything but truthfulness from you (*mā jarrabnā ʿalayka
kadhiban*)."* He then said:

> «يَا مَعْشَرَ قُرَيْشٍ أَنْقِذُوا أَنْفُسَكُمْ مِنَ النَّارِ، فَإِنِّي لَا أُغْنِي عَنْكُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ شَيْئًا، يَا بَنِي عَبْدِ مَنَافٍ … يَا بَنِي عَبْدِ شَمْسٍ … يَا بَنِي كَعْبِ بْنِ لُؤَيٍّ أَنْقِذُوا أَنْفُسَكُمْ مِنَ النَّارِ فَإِنِّي لَا أُغْنِي عَنْكُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ شَيْئًا، يَا عَبَّاسُ عَمَّ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ أَنْقِذْ نَفْسَكَ مِنَ النَّارِ فَإِنِّي لَا أُغْنِي عَنْكَ مِنَ اللَّهِ شَيْئًا، يَا صَفِيَّةُ عَمَّةَ مُحَمَّدٍ، وَيَا فَاطِمَةُ بِنْتَ مُحَمَّدٍ أَنْقِذَا أَنْفُسَكُمَا مِنَ النَّارِ فَإِنِّي لَا أَمْلِكُ لَكُمَا مِنَ اللَّهِ شَيْئًا، غَيْرَ أَنَّ لَكُمَا رَحِمًا سَأَبُلُّهَا بِبَلَالِهَا، إِنِّي لَكُمْ نَذِيرٌ بَيْنَ يَدَيْ عَذَابٍ شَدِيدٍ»
> *"O assembly of Quraysh, save yourselves from the Fire, for I cannot avail you anything before
> Allāh. O Banū ʿAbd Manāf … O Banū ʿAbd Shams … O Banū Kaʿb b. Luʾayy, save yourselves from the Fire,
> for I cannot avail you anything before Allāh. O ʿAbbās, uncle of the Messenger of Allāh, save
> yourself from the Fire, for I cannot avail you anything before Allāh. O Ṣafiyyah, aunt of Muḥammad,
> and O Fāṭimah, daughter of Muḥammad, save yourselves from the Fire, for I do not possess for you
> anything before Allāh — save that you have a kinship-tie which I shall keep moist with its due. I am
> indeed a warner to you before a severe punishment."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:323)

Abū Lahab's outburst — *«تَبًّا لَكَ سَائِرَ الْيَوْمِ، أَلِهَذَا جَمَعْتَنَا؟»* ("May you perish the
whole day — was it for this you gathered us?") — brought down the revelation of
**[[Sūrat al-Masad]]** (111:1–5) upon him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:324). The Prophet ﷺ closed the episode by
telling Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib: *"O Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, by Allāh, I do not know of any young man
among the Arabs who has brought his people anything better than what I have brought you — I have
brought you the affairs of this world and the Hereafter"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:324).

## 8. Quraysh's Approach to Abū Ṭālib
Once the Prophet ﷺ began openly denouncing their idols, Quraysh's opposition hardened. A delegation of
their chiefs went to **[[Abū Ṭālib]]** and told him: *"Your nephew has cursed our gods, reviled our
religion, mocked our reason, and misguided our forefathers — either restrain him, or leave us to deal
with him."* Abū Ṭālib gave them a mild answer and put them off. When the Prophet ﷺ nevertheless
persisted, the delegation returned and made explicit that the two of them could not both continue as
before. Abū Ṭālib, distressed, sent for the Prophet ﷺ and asked him to spare himself and spare his
uncle by not placing on him a burden beyond his strength. The Prophet ﷺ answered:

> «يَا عَمِّ، وَاللَّهِ لَوْ وَضَعُوا الشَّمْسَ فِي يَمِينِي وَالْقَمَرَ فِي شِمَالِي عَلَى أَنْ أَتْرُكَ هَٰذَا الْأَمْرَ حَتَّى يُظْهِرَهُ اللَّهُ أَوْ أَهْلِكَ فِيهِ، مَا تَرَكْتُهُ»
> *"O uncle, by Allāh, if they placed the sun in my right hand and the moon in my left on condition
> that I abandon this cause, until Allāh makes it prevail or I perish in it, I would not abandon it."*

He then wept and turned to leave; Abū Ṭālib called him back: *"Go, nephew, and say what you wish, for
by Allāh I will never give you up for anything, ever"* — and composed the poem beginning *"By Allāh,
they shall not reach you while their numbers stand together, until I am laid to rest beneath the
dust"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:326–327).

## 9. The Torment of Quraysh's Own Kin
Unable to touch the Prophet ﷺ while Abū Ṭālib shielded him, Quraysh turned instead upon **each clan's
own converts** — every tribe seizing and torturing whichever of its members had accepted Islam, *"and
Allāh protected His Messenger ﷺ through his uncle Abū Ṭālib"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:328). This is the
persecution recorded in full at *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:357–362, whose victims include:

- **[[Khabbāb b. al-Aratt]]**, laid on burning coals until, in his own words, the moisture of his
  spine was gone.
- **[[Yāsir b. ʿĀmir]]** and **[[Sumayyah bint Khayyāṭ]]**, tortured together with their sons; Sumayyah
  was killed by **[[Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)]]**, becoming, in al-Iṣābah's words, the first martyr of
  Islam.
- **[[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]]**, tormented alongside his parents, to whom the Prophet ﷺ said as he passed:
  *"Patience, O family of Yāsir! Your appointed meeting is Paradise."*
- **[[ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah]]**, tortured until, it is said, he no longer knew what he was saying, before
  **Abū Bakr** ﵁ purchased and freed him.
- **[[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]]** and **[[Abū Fukayhah]]**, likewise freed by Abū Bakr.
- **[[Zunnīrah al-Rūmiyyah]]** and **[[Lubaybah]]**, tortured by **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb|ʿUmar]]**
  before his own conversion and by Abū Jahl; Zunnīrah was blinded and, Quraysh's mockery
  notwithstanding, her sight was miraculously restored.
- **[[Umm ʿUnays]]**, a slave of Banū Zuhrah tortured by al-Aswad b. ʿAbd Yaghūth, and
  **[[al-Nahdiyyah]]** with her daughter, whose mistress refused to free them until Abū Bakr purchased
  and freed both on the spot.

See each companion's own note for the fuller, dual-sourced account of their trial.

## 10. The Islam of Ḥamzah
**Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib**, the Prophet's ﷺ uncle, was a keen huntsman. Returning one day from the
hunt he learned that **Abū Jahl** had accosted and abused the Prophet ﷺ near the Kaʿbah. Filled with
fury, Ḥamzah went straight to Abū Jahl, struck him on the head with his bow hard enough to wound him,
and declared: *"My religion is the religion of Muḥammad — say what I say, if you dare oppose me!"*
Allāh revealed concerning this: *«إِذْ جَعَلَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الْحَمِيَّةَ»* ("When
the disbelievers set up in their hearts a fierce pride," Qurʾān 48:26), understood to refer to Ḥamzah's
zeal (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:332). That night, troubled at having committed himself so suddenly, Ḥamzah
prayed for guidance and, finding peace in his heart, came to the Prophet ﷺ the next morning to be
instructed and confirmed in the faith (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:332–333).

## 11. ʿUtbah b. Rabīʿah's Offer
As converts multiplied, Quraysh sent their most eloquent man, **ʿUtbah b. Rabīʿah**, to negotiate. He
offered the Prophet ﷺ wealth to be the richest of them, leadership so that no matter would be decided
without him, kingship over them, or — if what afflicted him was a jinn no man could shake off —
medical treatment at their expense. The Prophet ﷺ, once ʿUtbah had finished, recited to him the opening
of **Sūrat Fuṣṣilat**, until ʿUtbah begged him to stop, returning to Quraysh visibly shaken (*Subul
al-Hudā*, 2:335–336).

## 12. Demands for Signs and Wonders
Quraysh's demands escalated to requests for miracles on their own terms — sending down angels,
speaking with the dead, or manifesting everything they asked for gathered before them — none of which
were granted, since Allāh knew such wonders, given on their terms of stubborn defiance rather than
sincere guidance, would not have moved them from their misguidance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:338).

## 13. The Testing Questions from the Jews of Madīnah
**al-Naḍr b. al-Ḥārith** and **ʿUqbah b. Abī Muʿayṭ** were sent by Quraysh to the Jewish scholars of
Madīnah for questions no one but a true prophet could answer. Returning with these questions, they put
them to the Prophet ﷺ, who told them he would answer the next day — without adding *"if Allāh wills."*
Revelation was then withheld from him for a period the book records variously as **fifteen nights**
(per Ibn Isḥāq) or **three days** (per al-Zuhrī and Mūsā b. ʿUqbah), during which the people of Makkah
mocked his failure to answer, until Jibrīl ﵊ finally returned to him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:345–346).

## 14. Abū Jahl's Private Admission
Al-Zuhrī, via Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab, transmits that **Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb**, **Abū Jahl**, and
**al-Akhnas b. Sharīq** each separately went out at night to eavesdrop on the Prophet ﷺ praying in his
house, each unaware of the other's presence, for three successive nights, before agreeing among
themselves never to return lest a foolish kinsman see them and be led astray by what they had
witnessed. Afterward, al-Akhnas confronted Abū Jahl directly and asked him, man to man, what he truly
thought of Muḥammad's claim. Abū Jahl admitted: *"By Allāh, I know that what he says is true, but Banū
Quṣayy have said, 'Ours is the guardianship of the Kaʿbah,' and we agreed; 'Ours is the assembly hall,'
and we agreed; 'Ours is the banner,' and we agreed; 'Ours is the provisioning of pilgrims,' and we
agreed — then, when our camel-troops all but touched theirs, they said, 'A prophet is among us!' By
Allāh, I will never accept that!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:352–353).

## 15. al-Walīd b. al-Mughīrah's Confusion
When **Sūrat Ghāfir** was revealed and recited by the Prophet ﷺ in the mosque, **al-Walīd b.
al-Mughīrah**, known as *"the fragrant flower of Quraysh,"* went straight to Banū Makhzūm and confessed:
*"By Allāh, I have just heard speech from Muḥammad that is not the speech of men, nor of jinn; its
lowest register is rich, its topmost branch fruit-laden; it is truly sweet, truly graced; it overcomes
all, and is overcome by nothing."* Alarmed that Quraysh would think he had converted (*"ṣabaʾa
al-Walīd"*), Abū Jahl pressed him to give Quraysh some formula to discredit it. Al-Walīd, after
reflecting, could find nothing false to call it but conceded: *"the nearest thing to say of it is that
it is sorcery, for it parts a man from his father, from his brother, from his wife, from his clan."*
Allāh revealed concerning him: *«ذَرْنِي وَمَنْ خَلَقْتُ وَحِيدًا … إِنَّهُ كَانَ لِآيَاتِنَا عَنِيدًا»*
("Leave Me with the one I created alone … he was indeed stubbornly opposed to Our signs") (*Subul
al-Hudā*, 2:354–355).

## 16. The Islam of ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb
Al-Ṣāliḥī places ʿUmar's ﵁ conversion **shortly after the First Hijrah to Abyssinia**, giving Dhū
al-Ḥijjah of the sixth year of the mission, when he was twenty-six, though other reports place it a
year earlier, three days after **Ḥamzah**'s (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:370). By this point the Muslims
numbered, per various counts, somewhere between thirty-nine and ninety-nine men and women (*Subul
al-Hudā*, 2:370).

ʿUmar set out, sword in hand, intending to kill the Prophet ﷺ, but was met on the way by **Nuʿaym b.
ʿAbdullāh al-Naḥḥām**, who had himself secretly accepted Islam. Learning of ʿUmar's purpose, Nuʿaym
told him to set his own household in order first: his sister **Fāṭimah bint al-Khaṭṭāb** and her
husband **[[Saʿīd b. Zayd]]** had themselves become Muslims. Enraged, ʿUmar went to them, struck his
brother-in-law, and when his sister rose to shield her husband, struck her too until she bled. Seeing
her blood, and hearing them both declare, *"Yes, we have accepted Islam and believed in Allāh and His
Messenger — do as you wish,"* ʿUmar relented and asked to read the pages of Qurʾān they had been
reciting — **Sūrat Ṭāhā**. His sister, telling him *"you are unclean in your disbelief, and none may
touch it but the pure,"* made him wash first. Reading it, he exclaimed at its beauty and set out at
once for the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:371–372).

A parallel account has ʿUmar seeking out **[[Khabbāb b. al-Aratt]]**, who told him the Prophet ﷺ was in
a house near al-Ṣafā with a group of his companions. When ʿUmar knocked, sword still girded, the
companions inside took fright, but **Ḥamzah** urged that he be let in — *"if he means good, we shall
grant it to him; if he means harm, we shall kill him with his own sword."* The Prophet ﷺ himself seized
ʿUmar by his garment and demanded to know his purpose; ʿUmar answered: *"I have come to believe in
Allāh and His Messenger and in what has come from Allāh."* The Prophet ﷺ praised Allāh, then said,
*"Allāh has guided you, son of al-Khaṭṭāb,"* wiped his chest, and prayed for his steadfastness (*Subul
al-Hudā*, 2:373). See **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** for the fuller biography.

## 17. What Follows
The escalating persecution and Quraysh's inability to dislodge the Prophet ﷺ from Abū Ṭālib's
protection drove a first group of Muslims to seek refuge abroad — see **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]**
— and, when even that failed to check the movement's growth, to impose the collective boycott of
**[[al-Ḥiṣār fī Shiʿb Abī Ṭālib]]**.
