# Ghazwat Dūmat al-Jandal (غزوة دومة الجندل)
*The Northern Imperial Demonstration — The Night Marches of 1,000 Warriors, The Guide Madhkūr, and The Striking of Awe into Caesar*

## 1. Strategic Context & Geopolitical Rationale
In Rabīʿ al-Awwal 5 AH, intelligence reached Medina that a large congregation of hostile tribes had assembled at **Dūmat al-Jandal**—a strategic commercial crossroads five nights south of Damascus, marking the gateway to southern Syria (*adnā al-Shām*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). 

These desert forces were extorting passing merchant caravans, plundering travelers, and actively preparing an advance toward Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). 

Recognizing that northern Arabia was critical to securing the perimeter of the Islamic state, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ resolved upon a grand strategic march. Biographers note that a major motivation was to project power toward the Byzantine sphere:
> «أَرَادَ أَنْ يَدْنُوَ إِلَى أَدْنَى الشَّامِ، وَقِيلَ لَهُ: إِنَّهَا طَرَفٌ مِنْ أَفْوَاهِ الشَّامِ، فَلَوْ دَنَوْتَ لَهَا لَكَانَ ذَلِكَ مِمَّا يُفْزِعُ قَيْصَرَ»
> *"He resolved to advance toward the Syrian frontier, for it was said to him: 'It is a gateway to the portals of Syria, and if you approach it, it will strike terror into Caesar!'”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342).

## 2. Mobilization & Night-March Tactics
The Prophet ﷺ appointed **Sibāʿ b. ʿUrfuṭah al-Ghifārī** ﵁ as his deputy governor over Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). 

He mobilized an army of one thousand (1,000) Muslim warriors (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). To ensure absolute operational surprise across hundreds of miles of open northern desert, the Prophet ﷺ employed rigorous infiltration tactics:
> «فَكَانَ يَسِيرُ اللَّيْلَ وَيَكْمُنُ النَّهَارَ»
> *"He would march by night and conceal his forces by day."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342).

He enlisted an expert desert navigator from Banū ʿUdhrah named **Madhkūr** ﵁—a master tracker skilled in desert ways (*hādikh khirrīt*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). Madhkūr guided the army off the beaten caravan tracks (*nakaba ʿan ṭarīqihim*), marching with maximum speed across untrodden defiles.

## 3. The Raid on Dūmat al-Jandal
As the army closed within striking distance of Dūmat al-Jandal, Madhkūr rode forward alone as a scout (*ṭalīʿah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). He discovered the grazing tracks of the enemy's cattle, sheep, and camels while their owners were unaware (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342).

Madhkūr returned to the Prophet ﷺ with exact coordinates. The Prophet ﷺ launched a rapid dawn assault, capturing their livestock and herdsmen (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). The armed population of Dūmat al-Jandal panicked at the sudden emergence of a Medinan army in their distant northern redoubt and scattered into the deep desert (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342).

The Prophet ﷺ encamped in their main settlement for several days (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). He dispatched search parties (*sarāyā*) in all directions; each returned with herds without meeting military resistance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342). **[[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]]** ﵁ captured a solitary local inhabitant who explained: *“They fled yesterday as soon as they heard you seized their herds!”* The Prophet ﷺ presented Islam to him, and he embraced the faith (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342).

## 4. Truce with Ghaṭafān & Return to Medina
On the return journey, the Prophet ﷺ entered into a diplomatic grazing agreement (*muwādaʿah*) with ʿUyaynah b. Ḥiṣn al-Fazārī, allowing the famine-stricken herds of Banū Fazārah to graze at Taghlamīn down to al-Marāḍ (thirty-six miles from Medina) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342–343).

The Prophet ﷺ reentered Medina on the 20th of Rabīʿ al-Ākhir 5 AH, having completed a massive desert sweep without a single Muslim casualty (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:342).

> [!NOTE]
> **Distinction from Sariyyat Dūmat al-Jandal (6 AH)**:
> This campaign (*Ghazwat Dūmat al-Jandal*, 5 AH) was led by the Prophet ﷺ in person with 1,000 men. It is distinct from the later **[[Sariyyat Dūmat al-Jandal]]** (Shaʿbān 6 AH), which was commanded by **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁, during which the Christian king al-Aṣbagh b. ʿAmr al-Kalbī embraced Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:94).
