# Sariyyat ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib ilā Fadak (سرية علي بن أبي طالب إلى بني سعد بفدك)
*The one-hundred-man preemptive march commanded by ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib against Banū Saʿd b. Bakr to disrupt their military alliance with the Jews of Khaybar*

## 1. Strategic Context & Preemptive Intelligence

In Shaʿbān 6 AH, vital intelligence reached the Prophet ﷺ in Medina that a warlike contingent of **Banū Saʿd b. Bakr** encamped at the oasis of **Fadak** were mobilizing to join forces with the hostile Jewish citadel-confederacy of [[Ghazwat Khaybar|Khaybar]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). 

Banū Saʿd had assembled approximately **two hundred warriors** under the chieftainship of **Wabr b. ʿUlaym** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). They had agreed to place their military forces at Khaybar's disposal in exchange for a substantial share of Khaybar's rich seasonal date harvests (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). 

To sever this coalition before it could threaten Medina, the Prophet ﷺ mobilized a strike force of **one hundred warriors** and placed them under the supreme command of his cousin and son-in-law, **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).

## 2. Nocturnal Desert March & Interception of the Spy

ʿAlī executed classic desert operational security: he marched his force strictly by night and concealed them in the ravines during daylight hours (*sāra ʿAlī al-layla wa-kamana al-nahār*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). 

Advancing along the northern trade tracks, the detachment reached **al-Ghamj**, a brackish watering station situated strategically between Khaybar and Fadak (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). There they apprehended an isolated wanderer who initially claimed to be searching for lost camels (*bāghin*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). 

Upon rigorous interrogation, the man confessed that he was actually an advance scout and diplomatic spy (*ʿayn*) dispatched by Banū Saʿd to Khaybar to finalize the military pact offering auxiliary manpower in return for fruit stipends (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). He revealed:
> «تركتهم قد تجمّع منهم مائتا رجل ورأسهم وبر بن عليم»
> *"I left them having gathered two hundred combatants, and their chieftain is Wabr b. ʿUlaym!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).

## 3. The Raid & Seizure of the Herds

The Muslim force promised the scout safe conduct on condition that he guide them directly to the enemy camp or their grazing herds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). The spy led the column across rough, elevated ground and crags (*fadfad wa-ākām*) until they looked down upon an expansive plateau (*arḍ mustawiyah*) covered with grazing herds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). Pointing them out, the guide announced: *"These are their camels and sheep!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).

ʿAlī led his hundred warriors in an immediate assault. The Bedouin herdsmen scattered into the wilderness, spreading panic back to their main encampment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). Although the guide urged ʿAlī to release him immediately since the Bedouins were scattering, ʿAlī prudently held him in custody until the Muslims reached the enemy encampment itself (*ḥattā nablugha muʿaskarahum*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). 

Arriving at the camp, they found that the entire force of two hundred warriors had dispersed in panic without offering battle (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). True to his word, ʿAlī released the guide unharmed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).

## 4. Booty, Safiyy, & Return to Medina

The Muslims gathered a vast prize of livestock:
- **500 camels** (*khamsu miʾati baʿīr*)
- **2,000 sheep** (*alfā shāh*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).

Before dividing the spoils, ʿAlī set aside as the Prophet's special personal portion (**Ṣafiyy Rasūlillāh**) an exceptional, swift milch-camel named **al-Ḥafadah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97, 98). He then extracted the standard fifth (*khumus*) for the public treasury and divided the remaining four-fifths equitably among the one hundred warriors of his detachment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97). 

The expedition returned to Medina in complete safety without losing a single man (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).

---

## 5. Strategic Significance

1. **Disrupting the Northern Coalition**: By preemptively striking Banū Saʿd at Fadak, ʿAlī successfully isolated Khaybar from potential Arab tribal auxiliaries months before the grand march on [[Ghazwat Khaybar]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).
2. **Exemplary Desert Intelligence**: The interception of the enemy scout at al-Ghamj demonstrated the superior counter-intelligence and patrol capabilities developed by the Madinan state (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).
3. **Scrupulous Fulfillment of Treaties**: ʿAlī's release of the enemy spy after the objective was attained highlighted the moral discipline governing Muslim commanders even when operating behind enemy lines (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:97).
