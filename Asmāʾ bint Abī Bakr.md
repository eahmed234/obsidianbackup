# Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr (أسماء بنت أبي بكر)
*Dhāt al-Niṭāqayn — The Heroine of the Cave of Thawr and Mother of ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr*

## 1. Lineage & Early Islam
**Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr al-Ṣiddīq** ﵂ was the elder daughter of the first Caliph **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and Qutaylah bint ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā of Banū ʿĀmir b. Luʾayy (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13). She was the elder half-sister of the Mother of the Believers **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13).

She embraced Islam very early in Makkah, converted after seventeen persons had entered the faith, and pledged allegiance to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13).

She married the disciple of the Prophet ﷺ, **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁, living with him in noble austerity in Makkah and Medina, tending his horse and carrying date-stones upon her head (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13).

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones

### The Heroism of the Hijrah & *Dhāt al-Niṭāqayn*
During the perilous days of the Hijrah, when the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr ﵁ concealed themselves within the Cave of Thawr, Asmāʾ served as their vital intelligence courier and lifeline (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:239–241). 

When the time arrived for their departure toward Medina, she prepared their travel provisions and waterskin in the home of Abū Bakr. Finding no rope to bind them, she tore her waist girdle (*niṭāq*) into two halves: using one to bind the food-bag (*sufrah*) and the other to secure the waterskin (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:241).

Upon learning of her resourceful devotion, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ pronounced the timeless benediction:
> «أَبْدَلَكِ اللَّهُ بِنِطَاقِكِ هَذَا نِطَاقَيْنِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ!»
> *"May Allāh replace this girdle of yours with two girdles in Paradise!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13).
From that day forth, she was venerated across the Muslim community by the title **Dhāt al-Niṭāqayn** (She of the Two Girdles) (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13).

### Confronting Abū Jahl at the Threshold
Shortly after the departure of the Prophet ﷺ, **[[Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)]]** ☠ arrived with a posse of Quraysh striking the door of Abū Bakr's home. When Asmāʾ answered, Abū Jahl demanded: *"Where is your father?"* She boldly refused to disclose their secret, replying: *"I do not know where my father is."* In fury, Abū Jahl struck her face so violently that her earring fell to the ground, yet she never yielded a syllable of intelligence (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:241).

### The First Born of the Hijrah
Asmāʾ migrated to Medina while heavily pregnant with **[[ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13). 

Upon reaching Qubāʾ, she gave birth to him—the very first child born to the Muhājirūn in Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13). The Muslims carried the newborn in jubilation to the Prophet ﷺ, who chewed a date, placed his saliva in the infant's mouth (the first substance entering his belly), rubbed his palate (*taḥnīk*), and prayed for him, confounding the pagan rumor that the Jews had bewitched the Muslims with barrenness (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:13).

### Longevity, Steadfastness, and Passing
Asmāʾ lived a long and blessed life, reaching one hundred years of age with all her teeth sound and her intellect sharp and undimmed (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:14). 

When her son ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr was besieged in Makkah by al-Ḥajjāj b. Yūsuf, she exhorted him to steadfastness upon truth. Following his martyrdom, she confronted al-Ḥajjāj with unyielding dignity, reciting the Prophetic warning: *«Verily there shall arise from Thaqīf a liar and a destroyer»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:14). She passed away shortly thereafter in Makkah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:14).
