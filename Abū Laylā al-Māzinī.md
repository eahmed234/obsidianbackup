# Abū Laylā al-Māzinī (أبو ليلى عبد الرحمن بن كعب المازني) ﵁
*Hero of Banū Māzin b. al-Najjār, Officer of the Palms at Banū al-Naḍīr, and Weeper of Tabūk*

## 1. Lineage & Family Network
**Abū Laylā al-Māzinī** was a distinguished Companion from the Anṣār whose personal name was **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Kaʿb** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:297).

His full lineage is **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Kaʿb b. ʿAmr b. ʿAwf b. Mabdhūl b. ʿAmr b. Ghanm b. Māzin b. al-Najjār al-Anṣārī al-Khazrajī al-Māzinī** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:297).

### Prominent Kinship Network
Abū Laylā was member of one of the most celebrated and devout households of the Anṣār:
- **Brother of Nusaybah bint Kaʿb**: He was the full brother of the celebrated heroine of Uḥud and al-ʿAqabah, **[[Nusaybah bint Kaʿb]]** (Umm ʿUmārah) ﵂ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:298; 8:441).
- **Brother of ʿAbdullāh b. Kaʿb**: His brother **ʿAbdullāh b. Kaʿb** was a veteran of Badr who was appointed by the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ to oversee the collection and custody of the spoils of Badr (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:187, 298).
- **Father of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Abī Laylā**: He was the father of the eminent early Kufan jurist and elder Tābiʿī, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Abī Laylā al-Anṣārī (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:298).

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones & Military Service
According to Ibn Ḥibbān and other classical authorities, Abū Laylā participated in **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**, and all subsequent campaigns alongside the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:298).

### The Cutting of the Date-Palms of Banū al-Naḍīr
During **[[Ghazwat Banī al-Naḍīr]]**, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ issued orders to fell the adversary's date-palms at al-Buwayrah to break their intransigence and induce surrender (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323). The Prophet ﷺ commissioned two trusted companions to oversee this operation: **Abū Laylā al-Māzinī** and **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Salām]]** ﵄ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:298; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).

The two companions adopted contrasting, thoughtful approaches rooted in sincere conviction:
- Abū Laylā selectively cut down the premium, cherished *ʿajwah* palms in order to afflict the belligerents with profound distress and humiliation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).
- ʿAbdullāh b. Salām selectively cut down the lower grade *lawn* palms, explaining that he knew Allāh would ultimately grant the finest orchards to the Muslims as war booty (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).

When Jewish leaders protested and some Muslims wondered whether felling trees constituted corruption, Allāh revealed divine vindication in **[[Sūrat al-Ḥashr]]** (59:5), approving both actions as undertaken by divine decree to disgrace the transgressors (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).

### One of the Weepers (*al-Bakkāʾūn*) at Tabūk
When the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ prepared the expedition of **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]** during extreme summer heat and economic hardship (*Jaysh al-ʿUsrah*), several impoverished believers approached the Prophet ﷺ pleading for mounts so they could join the campaign (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:438). 

Because no spare mounts remained, the Prophet ﷺ informed them that he could find nothing upon which to carry them. In their sorrow at missing the expedition, tears streamed from their eyes. Allāh immortalized their devotion in the Qurʾān:
> «وَلَا عَلَى الَّذِينَ إِذَا مَا أَتَوْكَ لِتَحْمِلَهُمْ قُلْتَ لَا أَجِدُ مَا أَحْمِلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ تَوَلَّوْا وَأَعْيُنُهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ الدَّمْعِ حَزَنًا أَلَّا يَجِدُوا مَا يُنْفِقُونَ»
> *"Nor [is there blame] upon those who, when they came to you that you might give them mounts, you said: 'I find nothing upon which to carry you.' They turned back while their eyes overflowed with tears out of grief that they could not find wherewithal to spend."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:298; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:438).

The consensus of classical commentators and historians—including Ibn Isḥāq, Ibn Saʿd, and al-Kalbī from Ibn ʿAbbās—identifies **Abū Laylā ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Kaʿb** as one of this elite group of weeping Companions (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:298; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:438).

As Abū Laylā and his companion ʿAbdullāh b. Mughaffal departed weeping, they met **[[Yāmīn b. ʿUmayr]]** al-Naḍrī ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:439). When Yāmīn inquired about their grief, they explained their inability to march with the Prophet ﷺ. Moved by their sincerity, Yāmīn provided them with a camel (*nāḍiḥ*) and provisions of two *ṣāʿ* of dates for each, enabling both companions to participate in the blessed campaign (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:439).

## 3. Demise
Abū Laylā al-Māzinī passed away late in the caliphate of the Commander of the Faithful **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:298).
