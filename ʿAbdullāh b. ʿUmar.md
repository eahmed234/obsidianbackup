# ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar (عبد الله بن عمر) ﵄
*Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-ʿAdawī — The Master of the Sunnah, Scrupulous Jurist of Medina, and Paragon of Prophetic Emulation*

## 1. Lineage, Clan & Household Descent
He is ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb b. Nufayl b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. Riyāḥ b. ʿAbdillāh b. Qurṭ b. Razāḥ b. ʿAdī b. Kaʿb al-Qurashī al-ʿAdawī (kunya: Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:155, no. 4852; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:331). He was the eldest son of the Second Rightly Guided Caliph, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁. See **[[Banū ʿAdī]]**.

His mother was **Zaynab bint Maẓʿūn al-Jumaḥiyyah**, the noble sister of the foremost ascetic Companion ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn ﵁ and Qudāmah b. Maẓʿūn ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:156). See **[[Banū Jumaḥ]]**. Through his mother, he was the full brother of Umm al-Muʾminīn **[[Ḥafṣah bint ʿUmar]]** ﵂, wedded to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿUmar the elder (*al-akbar*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:156).

ʿAbdullāh was born in Mecca in the third year following the commencement of the Prophetic mission (3 BH / ~613 CE) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:156). He embraced Islam as a boy alongside his father and performed the Hijrah to Medina at the age of ten (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:156).

## 2. Military Mobilization & The Age of Majority
- **Inspection at Badr and Uḥud**: Burning with desire to defend the Prophet ﷺ, ʿAbdullāh presented himself for military inspection on the eve of **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** (2 AH) at age thirteen, and again at **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]** (3 AH) at age fourteen; on both occasions, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ inspected him and turned him back due to his youthful age (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:156; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:24, 185).
- **Admitted at the Trench (5 AH)**: When he reached the age of fifteen, he presented himself once more before **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**; this time, the Prophet ﷺ approved him and permitted him to bear arms (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:156; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:185; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*). This milestone established the canonical legal standard (*ḥadd al-bulūgh*) across classical Islamic jurisprudence for adulthood and military readiness.
- **Bayʿat al-Riḍwān (6 AH)**: Swore allegiance under the tree during **[[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]]** at **[[al-Ḥudaybiyah]]**, where his father ʿUmar equipped him with a horse and armaments (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:49).
- **Subsequent Campaigns**: Fought at **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]**, marched in the triumph of **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**, stood firm at **[[Ghazwat Ḥunayn]]**, and took part in **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:115, 208, 430).

## 3. The Prophetic Dream & The Night Vigil
During his youth, ʿAbdullāh resided and slept in al-Masjid al-Nabawī as an unmarried young man. Longing for spiritual revelation, he hoped to see a meaningful vision. In a dream, he saw two angels grasp him and lead him towards Hellfire, which appeared constructed like a well with two casings, containing people he recognized. Terrified, he cried out: *“I seek refuge in Allāh from the Fire!”* An angel then greeted him saying: *“Do not fear!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:157; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

He recounted the vision to his sister Ḥafṣah ﵂, who related it directly to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ responded with the renowned tribute:
> «نِعْمَ الرَّجُلُ عَبْدُ اللَّهِ، لَوْ كَانَ يُصَلِّي مِنَ اللَّيْلِ!»
> *"What an excellent man is ʿAbdullāh, if only he prayed at night!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:157; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

From that hour until his demise over six decades later, Ibn ʿUmar slept only a fraction of the night, arising four or five times each night to perform ablution and stand in prayer before Allāh (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:157, 161).

## 4. Paragon of *Ittibāʿ al-Āthār* (Strict Prophetic Emulation)
Ibn ʿUmar was universally celebrated among the Companions and Tābiʿūn for his absolute, scrupulous devotion to duplicating the precise physical actions, travels, and demeanor of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:332):
- Al-Zubayr b. Bakkār recorded: *"Ibn ʿUmar preserved everything he heard from the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, questioned whoever was present whenever he had been absent, followed his exact footsteps in every single mosque the Prophet prayed in, turned his riding beast in the roadway at the precise points he had seen the Prophet turn his camel, never abandoned the Hajj, and whenever he stood at ʿArafah, stood at the exact station where the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had stood"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:160).
- The eminent jurist Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab stated: *"If I were to testify that anyone is among the people of Paradise, I would testify for Ibn ʿUmar... and when he died, he was the best of those who remained on earth"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:158).
- Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān observed: *"ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb lived in an era when he had peers; Ibn ʿUmar lived in an era when he had no peer"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:158).
- Tāwūs b. Kaysān affirmed: *"I never saw any man more scrupulously pious (*awraʿ*) than Ibn ʿUmar"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:158).

## 5. Scrupulous Jurisprudence, Ascetic Charity & Demise
Despite his towering knowledge, Ibn ʿUmar was extraordinarily cautious in issuing legal verdicts (*fatāwā*). When presented with difficult questions, he would openly say *"I do not know"*, warning the questioners: *“Do you wish to make our backs bridges over Hellfire, so you can go about saying: 'Ibn ʿUmar issued us this fatwā'?”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:160).

During the civil dissensions (*fitnah*) that arose following the martyrdom of **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁, Ibn ʿUmar adopted strict political neutrality, resolutely refusing the caliphate when offered to him and declining to draw his sword against any Muslim (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:158).

He lived in profound detachment from worldly wealth, immediately distributing gifts and coins to the destitute of Medina; whenever he saw a slave from his household praying sincerely, he would set him free for the sake of Allāh (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:159).

ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar passed away in Mecca in 73 AH (or 74 AH) at the venerable age of eighty-four (or eighty-six) years (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:156; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:333). He was buried at Dhū Ṭuwā (or al-Muḥaṣṣab), revered as the final surviving Companion in the holy city of Mecca.
