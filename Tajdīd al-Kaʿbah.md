# Tajdīd al-Kaʿbah (بنيان قريش الكعبة ووضع الحجر الأسود)
*Quraysh Rebuild the Kaʿbah — the Waist-Cloth, the Foundation of Ibrāhīm ﵊, and the Verdict of al-Amīn*

## 1. Why They Rebuilt It
Al-Ṣāliḥī gives three causes: a woman burning incense before the Kaʿbah let a spark catch its
covering, which burned; floodwater had already entered and cracked its walls; and thieves had stolen
its ornaments, including golden gazelle figures, from a well inside the structure — a freedman named
Duwayk, found in possession of the loot, had his hand cut off for it, though Quraysh believed the
actual thieves had planted it on him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:169).

Quraysh resolved to strengthen the building and raise its door so that only those they chose could
enter. A shipwreck at Jeddah, belonging to a Byzantine trader, provided timber and a builder,
**Baqūm**, whom **al-Walīd b. al-Mughīrah** and others brought to Makkah for the work
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:169).

## 2. Only Lawful Wealth
Before building began, a man of standing — some attribute this instead to al-Walīd b. al-Mughīrah —
picked up a stone from the Kaʿbah and found it leapt back from his hand of its own accord. He declared:

> «يَا مَعْشَرَ قُرَيْشٍ، لَا تُدْخِلُوا فِي بُنْيَانِهَا مِنْ كَسْبِكُمْ إِلَّا طَيِّبًا، لَا يَدْخُلُ فِيهَا مَهْرُ بَغِيٍّ وَلَا بَيْعُ رِبًا وَلَا مَظْلِمَةُ أَحَدٍ مِنَ النَّاسِ»
> *"O Quraysh, let nothing enter this building from your earnings save what is pure — let no
> prostitute's wage enter it, nor the proceeds of usury, nor any man's stolen due."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:170)

## 3. Division of the Work Among the Clans
Quraysh divided the structure among themselves: the side of the door to **Banū ʿAbd Manāf and
Zuhrah**; between the Black Corner and the Yemeni Corner to **Banū Makhzūm** and allied clans; the rear
to **Banū Jumaḥ and Banū Sahm**; and the side of **al-Ḥijr** to **Banū ʿAbd al-Dār**, **Banū Asad**,
and **Banū ʿAdiyy** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:170).

## 4. Carrying Stones — and a Sign of Preserved Modesty
The Prophet ﷺ carried stones alongside them. **Jābir b. ʿAbdullāh** ﵁ narrates, in *al-Bukhārī* and
*Muslim*, that he carried stones together with **[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib|al-ʿAbbās]]** ﵁, who
told him: *"put your waist-wrap on your shoulder, to shield you from the stones."* He did so — **and
this was before he was sent as a prophet** — and at once his eyes turned up to the sky and he cried,
*"my waist-cloth!"* and refastened it around himself. One narration adds that **he fell unconscious**,
and — al-Ṣāliḥī notes — **he was never again seen unclothed after that day**
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:170).

## 5. The Foundation of Ibrāhīm ﵊, and the Ancient Inscriptions
Digging down to the foundation Ibrāhīm ﵊ had laid, they reached great green stones locked together
like camels' humps. When a workman drove his pickaxe between two of them to pry one loose, **all of
Makkah shook**, and a flash of light shot out from beneath the stone, nearly blinding him — they left
that foundation undisturbed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:171).

In the Corner they found an inscription in Syriac, which a Jewish man read for them: *"I am Allāh,
possessor of Bakkah — I created it the day I created the heavens and the earth, and formed the sun and
the moon, and surrounded it with seven upright angels; its two mountains will not depart; blessing is
granted its people in water and milk."* At the Maqām they found another: *"Makkah is Allāh's sanctuary
— it is approached from three roads; none may make it lawful [to violate] who is not of its people."*
And a third: *"whoever sows good reaps in gladness; whoever sows evil reaps in regret — do you commit
evil deeds and expect good reward? No — just as grapes are not picked from thorns"*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:171).

## 6. The Dispute Over the Black Stone
When the building reached the place of the Corner, each clan wished to be the one to set the Black
Stone in place, and the dispute grew to the brink of war — **Banū ʿAbd al-Dār** and **Banū ʿAdiyy b.
Kaʿb** brought out a bowl filled with blood and pledged themselves upon it (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:171).

**Iblīs** is reported to have nearly reignited the conflict even after its resolution: when a man of
Najd offered to hand the Prophet ﷺ a stone to help set the Corner, al-ʿAbbās ﵁ stopped him from
receiving it; the Najdī — said to have been Iblīs himself — grew furious, mocking Quraysh for placing
"the youngest of them in years and the least in wealth" above themselves, and swore he would scatter
them into factions. The people were briefly stirred before settling again
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:172).

## 7. The Verdict of al-Amīn
Quraysh agreed to let the next man to enter through the gate of Banū Shaybah arbitrate between them.
**Hubayrah b. Abī Wahb al-Makhzūmī** commemorated the resolution in verse:

> «فَلَمَّا رَأَيْنَا الْأَمْرَ قَدْ جَدَّ جِدُّهُ … وَلَمْ يَبْقَ شَيْءٌ غَيْرَ سَلِّ الْمُهَنَّدِ
> رَضِينَا وَقُلْنَا الْعَدْلُ أَوَّلُ طَالِعٍ … يَجِيءُ مِنَ الْبَطْحَاءِ عَنْ غَيْرِ مَوْعِدِ
> فَلَمْ يَفْجَأْنَا إِلَّا الْأَمِينُ مُحَمَّدٌ … فَقُلْنَا رَضِينَا بِالْأَمِينِ مُحَمَّدِ»
> *"When we saw the matter had grown grave, and nothing remained but to draw the sword, we relented and
> said: let justice be the first to appear, coming from the valley unannounced. And none surprised us
> but al-Amīn Muḥammad — so we said: we are content with al-Amīn Muḥammad."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:172)

The Prophet ﷺ had them each take hold of a corner of his own cloak, upon which the Stone rested; when
raised together to its place, he set it into the wall with his own hands.

## 8. When Exactly?
The book records real disagreement even on his age at the time. **Ibn Isḥāq** holds the rebuilding was
**five years** before the mission — which Ibn Ḥajar judges the more well-known position. **Mujāhid**,
via ʿAbd al-Razzāq, and **Mūsā b. ʿUqbah** both place it **fifteen years** before. On his age, one
report gives **thirty-five**; al-Azraqī records a view that he was still a **boy** at the time. Ibn
Ḥajar suggests reconciling the two by supposing the fire came well before the rebuilding itself began
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:172–173).
