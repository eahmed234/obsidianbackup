# ʿĀm al-Jamāʿah (عام الجماعة)
*The Year of Unity: The Historic Reconciliation at Maskin, al-Ḥasan's Noble Abdication, and the Fulfillment of the Prophetic Portent (41 AH)*

## 1. Context & The Looming Confrontation
Following the martyrdom of Amīr al-Muʾminīn **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ in Ramaḍān 40 AH, more than forty thousand (and according to other reports up to ninety thousand) Muslims in Iraq and the eastern provinces pledged allegiance to his elder son, **[[al-Ḥasan b. ʿAlī]]** ﵄, as the Fifth Caliph (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:67).

Recognizing that the armies of Iraq and Syria remained mobilized after the unresolved arbitration of **[[Waqʿat Ṣiffīn]]**, Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān advanced with the Syrian forces toward Iraq. al-Ḥasan deployed his vanguard to Maskin (in northern Iraq) to prevent an invasion of the civilian population. However, observing factional discord, lingering sectarian grievances, and the imminent prospect of renewed catastrophic bloodshed between Muslims, al-Ḥasan resolved to pursue absolute peace (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:67–68).

---

## 2. Fulfillment of the Sublime Prophecy
Decades earlier, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had mounted the Minbar in al-Masjid al-Nabawī holding the young al-Ḥasan in his arms, gazing out at the Companions and declaring:
> «إِنَّ ابْنِي هَذَا سَيِّدٌ، وَلَعَلَّ اللَّهَ أَنْ يُصْلِحَ بِهِ بَيْنَ فِئَتَيْنِ عَظِيمَتَيْنِ مِنَ الْمُسْلِمِينَ!»
> *"Indeed, this son of mine is a noble master (Sayyid), and perhaps Allāh will bring reconciliation through him between two mighty hosts of Muslims!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:153; 11:67).

Islamic scholars across the centuries noted that this Prophetic statement was an explicit divine testimony to al-Ḥasan's singular nobility and spiritual priority: while worldly leaders seek sovereignty at the expense of lives, al-Ḥasan surrendered an empire solely to protect the lives of believers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:68).

---

## 3. The Treaty of Maskin & Terms of Reconciliation
In late Rabīʿ al-Awwal 41 AH—five days remaining in the month—al-Ḥasan met Muʿāwiyah at Maskin (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:67–68):
- al-Ḥasan had governed as Caliph for six months and five days, perfectly completing the thirty-year cycle of the Prophetic Caliphate foretold by the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ: *«The Caliphate after me shall be thirty years, thereafter it shall become kingdom»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:68).
- al-Ḥasan stipulated foundational conditions designed to safeguard the Ummah:
  1. Governance strictly according to the Book of Allāh and the authentic Sunnah of His Messenger ﷺ.
  2. Absolute legal and personal immunity for the people of Medina, the Ḥijāz, and Iraq regarding all events that transpired during the era of his father ʿAlī ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:67).
  3. That the selection of the Caliph after Muʿāwiyah be returned to consultative Shūrā among the Muslims (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:67).

Muʿāwiyah accepted all conditions, granting al-Ḥasan complete guarantees (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:67–68).

---

## 4. al-Ḥasan's Address to the Assembly
Ascending the Minbar before the assembled hosts of Iraq and Syria, al-Ḥasan delivered a historic oration:
> «أَمَّا بَعْدُ، فَإِنَّ أَكْيَسَ الْكَيْسِ التُّقَى، وَإِنَّ أَحْمَقَ الْحُمْقِ الْفُجُورُ. أَلَا وَإِنَّ هَذِهِ الْأُمُورَ الَّتِي اخْتَلَفْتُ فِيهَا أَنَا وَمُعَاوِيَةُ، إِنَّمَا هُوَ لِأَمْرِي؛ فَإِنْ كَانَ لَهُ أَحَقُّ فَهُوَ بِحَقِّهِ، وَإِنْ كَانَ لِي فَقَدْ تَرَكْتُهُ لَهُ إِرَادَةَ إِصْلَاحِ الْأُمَّةِ وَحَقْنِ دِمَائِهَا: ﴿وَإِنْ أَدْرِي لَعَلَّهُ فِتْنَةٌ لَكُمْ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَى حِينٍ﴾!»
> *"To proceed: The most discerning intelligence is piety, and the most foolish ignorance is transgression. Behold! This matter over which Muʿāwiyah and I disputed was either his rightful due—which he has taken—or my rightful due, which I have willingly yielded to him seeking solely the reconciliation of the Ummah and the sparing of its blood: «And I know not; perhaps it is a trial for you and an enjoyment for a time» [al-Anbiyāʾ 21:111]!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:68).

When certain partisans lamented his abdication, al-Ḥasan responded with consummate fear of Allāh:
> *“I feared that on the Day of Resurrection, seventy thousand—or more or less—would come before Allāh with their jugular veins spurting blood, demanding of me why their lives were extinguished!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:68).

---

## 5. Aftermath & The Legacy of ʿĀm al-Jamāʿah
With the conclusion of the pact, hostilities across the Islamic world ceased instantly. The fragmented garrison towns of Iraq, the Levant, Egypt, Arabia, and Persia were united under a singular administration for the first time since the siege of ʿUthmān. In celebration of this providential unity, the year 41 AH was universally designated across Islamic history as **ʿĀm al-Jamāʿah** (*The Year of Unity*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:153; 11:68).

al-Ḥasan departed Kūfah with his brother **[[al-Ḥusayn b. ʿAlī]]** ﵄ and their household, returning to the sanctuary of Medina, where he resided in worship, learning, and generous charity until his martyrdom by poison in 49 AH (or 50 AH) (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:63; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:64, 68).
