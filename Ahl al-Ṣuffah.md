# Ahl al-Ṣuffah (أهل الصفة)
*The Devoted Scholars of the Mosque Portico and Custodians of Prophetic Wisdom*

## 1. Establishment & Nature of the Ṣuffah
*Al-Ṣuffah* was a shaded, roofed veranda erected in the rear northern courtyard of the Prophetic Mosque (**[[Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]**) in Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:325). It was established as a communal sanctuary and residential academy for the destitute Muhājirūn and visiting tribesmen who possessed neither homes, wealth, wives, nor kinship ties in Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:325; 7:196).

Their numbers fluctuated depending on military campaigns, economic circumstances, and weddings, typically housing around seventy companions at any single time, though over four hundred companions resided there across the Medinan era (*Subul al-Hudā*, 7:196).

## 2. Way of Life & Spiritual Rigor
- **Academic & Spiritual Devotion**: Free from agricultural labor and trade caravans, the companions of the Ṣuffah dedicated their days and nights to memorizing newly revealed Qurʾānic verses, learning legal rulings from the Prophet ﷺ, reciting the Book of Allāh, and praying voluntary night vigils (*Qiyām al-Layl*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 7:196).
- **Ascetic Poverty**: Most possessed only a single tattered garment (*nimrah*) that did not reach their feet, which they held together around their necks while praying so their nakedness would not show (*Subul al-Hudā*, 7:197). At times, severe hunger caused them to swoon during prayer, leading visiting Bedouins to think they were afflicted with madness (*Subul al-Hudā*, 7:197).
- **Prominent Members**:
  - **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁: Foremost preserver of Hadith, who attached himself perpetually to the Prophet's presence to absorb knowledge.
  - **[[Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī]]** ﵁: The standard-bearer of ascetic detachment.
  - **[[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]]** ﵁: Muʾadhdhin of the Prophet ﷺ.
  - **Ḥudhayfah b. al-Yamān** ﵁: Custodian of the Prophet's secrets concerning hypocrites.
  - **Ṣuhayb b. Sinān** ﵁ and ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd ﵁ during their initial days in Medina.

## 3. Prophetic Care & Altruism
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ treated Ahl al-Ṣuffah as his personal household guests (*aḍyāf al-Islām*). Whenever charitable gifts (*ṣadaqah*) were brought to him, he sent it entirely to them without partaking of any; when gifts (*hadiyyah*) arrived, he shared it with them equally (*Subul al-Hudā*, 7:196).

He would sit amidst them in the portico, encouraging them and eating with them from common platters (*Subul al-Hudā*, 7:196; 11:48). When his beloved daughter **[[Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ]]** ﵂ asked for a servant to assist her with the exhausting stone hand-mill, the Prophet ﷺ gently replied:
> «لَا أُعْطِيكِ وَأَدَعُ أَهْلَ الصُّفَّةِ تَلْوَى بُطُونُهُمْ مِنَ الْجُوعِ، لَا أَجِدُ مَا أُنْفِقُ عَلَيْهِمْ، وَلَكِنْ أَبِيعُهُمْ وَأُنْفِقُ عَلَيْهِمْ أَثْمَانَهُمْ!»
> *"I shall not grant you a servant while leaving the stomachs of Ahl al-Ṣuffah churning with hunger, finding nothing to sustain them! Rather, I will provide for them before all else!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:48).
He instead taught Fāṭimah and ʿAlī the evening formulas of Tasbīḥ, Taḥmīd, and Takbīr.

## 4. Martial Vanguard
Far from being passive recluses, Ahl al-Ṣuffah formed the standing military vanguard of Medina. Whenever a raid or expedition was announced, they were the first to mobilize, fighting heroically at Badr, Uḥud, the Trench, Khaybar, and Ḥunayn. Many among them achieved martyrdom, including the seventy reciters slain treacherously at **[[Maʾsāt Biʾr Maʿūnah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:320; 7:198).
