# ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh (عبد الله بن جحش الأسدي)
*al-Mujaddaʿ fī Allāh — First Commander in Islam, Martyred at Uḥud*

## 1. Lineage
Subul al-Hudā's register of the earliest converts gives his fuller nasab: **ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh b. Riʾāb
b. Yaʿmar b. Ṣabirah b. Murrah b. Kabīr b. Ghanm b. Dūdān**, of **Banū Asad**, an ally (*ḥalīf*) of
**Banū ʿAbd Shams** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:310). Al-Iṣābah confirms this placement and adds that he was
**among the earliest converts (al-sābiqīn)** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:31, no. 4601).

## 2. Early Islam and the First Hijrah
Ibn Isḥāq records that he **emigrated to Abyssinia** and later **witnessed Badr** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:31).

## 3. First Commander in Islam
A tradition via Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ ﵁ records the Prophet's ﷺ own words on dispatching an expedition:
*"I shall send over you a man most patient among you in hunger and thirst"* — and he sent **ʿAbdullāh
b. Jaḥsh**, *"so he was the first commander in Islam."* Zirr b. Ḥubaysh likewise transmits: *"The first
banner tied in Islam was for ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh."* Ibn Isḥāq, via ʿUrwah, and al-Ṭabarānī, via Jundab
b. ʿAbdullāh al-Bajalī, both preserve the fuller story of the Prophet ﷺ sending him **to Nakhlah** at
the head of this expedition (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:31–32).

## 4. Brotherhood and Counsel
The Prophet ﷺ paired him in brotherhood (*muʾākhāh*) with **ʿĀṣim b. Thābit** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:31).
He was also among those the Prophet ﷺ consulted, alongside **Abū Bakr** and **ʿUmar**, on the question
of the captives of Badr (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:32).

## 5. The Supplication Before Uḥud
Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ ﵁ narrates that on the day of Uḥud, ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh said to him, *"Shall we not
go apart and supplicate?"* They withdrew together; Saʿd prayed first, asking Allāh that when they met
the enemy the next day, he be matched against a fierce man whom he could fight and then be granted
victory over, to kill him and take his spoils. ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh said *"āmīn"* to this, then prayed
his own supplication:

> *"O Allāh, grant me a fierce man to fight for Your sake, until he seizes me and cuts off my nose and
> my ear — so that when I meet You, You ask me, 'For what were these lost?' and I answer, 'For You and
> for Your Messenger,' and You say, 'You have spoken true.'"*

Saʿd later said: *"ʿAbdullāh's supplication was better than mine — for I saw him, at the end of that
day, with his nose and ear hanging by a thread"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:32).

## 6. "al-ʿUrjūn" — the Palm-Branch Sword
Al-Zubayr [b. Bakkār] records that he was called **al-Mujaddaʿ fī Allāh** ("the one mutilated for
Allāh's sake"). His sword broke on the day of Uḥud, and the Prophet ﷺ gave him a palm-branch
(*ʿurjūn*) in its place, which became a sword in his hand — known ever after as **al-ʿUrjūn**. This
sword, al-Zubayr adds, survived long enough to later be sold to Baghā the Turk for two hundred dinars
(*al-Iṣābah*, 4:32).

## 7. Martyrdom
He was killed at Uḥud by **Abū al-Ḥakam b. al-Akhnas b. Sharīq**, and was **buried in one grave with
[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁. He was, at his death, **a little over forty years old**
(*al-Iṣābah*, 4:33).
