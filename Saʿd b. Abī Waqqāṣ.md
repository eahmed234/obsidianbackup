# Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ (سعد بن أبي وقاص الزهري) ﵁
*Abū Isḥāq al-Zuhrī al-Qurashī — The Archer of Islam, Victor of al-Qādisiyyah, Maternal Uncle of the Prophet ﷺ*

## 1. Lineage & Early Preeminence
He is Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ (Mālik) b. Wuhayb b. ʿAbd Manāf b. Zuhrah al-Qurashī al-Zuhrī (kunya: Abū Isḥāq) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:237, 315). Belonging to **[[Banū Zuhrah]]**—the maternal clan of the Prophet's mother **[[Āminah bint Wahb]]**—the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ honoured him as his maternal uncle, once pointing to him and exclaiming with joy:
> «هَذَا خَالِي، فَلْيُرِنِي امْرُؤٌ خَالَهُ!»
> *"This is my maternal uncle! So let any man show me his uncle!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:315; *Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī*, no. 3752).

He embraced Islam at the age of seventeen through the daʿwah of **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, being the third or seventh person to accept the faith (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:315). When his mother went on a hunger strike threatening suicide unless he apostatized, Saʿd declared: *“O mother, by Allāh, if you possessed one hundred souls and they exited one by one, I would never abandon this religion!”* Upon this, Allāh revealed the verse in Sūrat Luqmān (31:15) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:315).

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones
- **The First Arrow in Islam**: During the early reconnaissance mission of Sariyyat Rābigh (under ʿUbaydah b. al-Ḥārith), Saʿd shot an arrow that dispersed the polytheist riders, becoming the very first person in the history of Islam to shoot an arrow in the cause of Allāh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:10; 11:315).
- **Veteran of Badr (2 AH)**: Fought valiantly at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** alongside his younger brother ʿUmayr b. Abī Waqqāṣ (who was martyred at Badr at age sixteen) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:92).
- **The Sovereign Honor at Uḥud (3 AH)**: At **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, Saʿd took his stand beside the Prophet ﷺ as an unyielding archer, expending approximately one thousand arrows to repel wave after wave of attackers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205; 11:316). The Prophet ﷺ personally handed arrows to him from his own quiver, bestowing upon him the unprecedented honor:
  > «ارْمِ سَعْدُ! فِدَاكَ أَبِي وَأُمِّي!»
  > *"Shoot, Saʿd! May my father and mother be ransomed for you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205; 11:316; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 2905).
  He was the sole companion for whom the Prophet ﷺ combined both his parents in a ransom formula (*fidāʾ*).
- **The Answered Supplication**: The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ made a special supplication for him:
  > «اللَّهُمَّ سَدِّدْ رَمْيَتَهُ، وَأَجِبْ دَعْوَتَهُ!»
  > *"O Allāh, direct his shooting and answer his supplication!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:316).
  Consequently, Saʿd never missed a target in archery, and every prayer he uttered was answered directly.
- **Subsequent Campaigns**: Stood firm during **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**, pledged under the tree at **[[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]]**, and marched at **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]**, **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**, and **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:370; 5:48, 124, 445).

## 3. Demise & Legacy
Under the Caliphate of **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, Saʿd was appointed supreme commander of the Islamic armies in Iraq. He achieved the historic victory against the Sasanian Persian Empire at the Battle of al-Qādisiyyah (15 AH), conquered the Persian capital of al-Madāʾin, and founded the garrison city of Kūfah.

He was the very last of the Ten Promised Paradise (*al-ʿAsharah al-Mubashsharūn*) to pass away, dying at his estate in al-ʿAqīq outside Medina in 55 AH at the age of seventy-four. He was carried to Medina and buried in [[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]] wrapped in the worn woolen cloak in which he had fought at Badr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:317).
