# Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ (سعد بن أبي وقاص الزهري) ﵁
*Abū Isḥāq al-Zuhrī al-Qurashī — The Archer of Islam, Victor of al-Qādisiyyah, Maternal Uncle of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ*

## 1. Lineage, Sacred Kinship & Physical Description
### Patrilineal Descent & Clan Ties
He is **Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ (Mālik) b. Uhayb (Wuhayb) b. ʿAbd Manāf b. Zuhrah b. Kilāb b. Murrah b. Kaʿb al-Qurashī al-Zuhrī** (kunya: **Abū Isḥāq**; honorifics: *Fāris al-Islām*, *Rāmī Rasūlillāh*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:61–62; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:237, 315).

His lineage converges with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at their fifth-generation ancestor **Kilāb b. Murrah** (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:61). Belonging to **[[Banū Zuhrah]]**, he shared the noble clan of the Prophet's mother **[[Āminah bint Wahb]]** (who was Āminah bint Wahb b. ʿAbd Manāf b. Zuhrah). Because Saʿd’s grandfather Uhayb and Āminah’s father Wahb were brothers, Saʿd stood as the maternal cousin of Āminah, leading the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ to honor him with the familial title of maternal uncle (*khāl*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62). Jābir ﵁ narrated that as Saʿd approached an assembly, the Prophet ﷺ beamed and exclaimed:
> «هَذَا خَالِي، فَلْيُرِنِي امْرُؤٌ خَالَهُ!»
> *"This is my maternal uncle! So let any man show me his uncle!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:315).

### Maternal Heritage & Umayyad Link
- **Mother**: **Ḥamnah bint Sufyān b. Umayyah b. ʿAbd Shams al-Qurashiyyah al-Umawiyyah**, first cousin of **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).
- Through his mother, Saʿd was directly linked by blood to the aristocratic leadership of Banū Umayyah. When Saʿd accepted Islam, his mother initiated an extreme hunger strike threatening suicide outside the Kaʿbah; Saʿd addressed her firmly: *"O mother, by Allāh, if you possessed one hundred souls and they departed one after another, I would never abandon this religion!"* Thereupon Allāh revealed Sūrat Luqmān (31:15: *«And if they strive to make you associate with Me that of which you have no knowledge, do not obey them, but accompany them in this world with kindness»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:315).

### Siblings & Early Youth
- **Brothers**: 
  - **ʿUmayr b. Abī Waqqāṣ** ﵁: Younger brother who concealed himself at the inspection of Badr fearing rejection due to his youth; the Prophet ﷺ permitted him, and he was martyred at Badr at age sixteen (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:92).
  - **ʿĀmir b. Abī Waqqāṣ** ﵁: Early convert who migrated to Abyssinia.
  - **ʿUtbah b. Abī Waqqāṣ**: Remained polytheist at Uḥud; father of Hāshim b. ʿUtbah al-Mirqāl.

### Physical Appearance & Formidable Strength
- **Stature & Build**: Short to medium height, dark-complexioned (*ādam*), broad-shouldered and thickset, with thick dark hair and exceptionally powerful arms and chest (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:61–63).
- **One of Four Martial Pillars**: Abu Isḥāq recorded that the four most formidable martial figures among the Ṣaḥābah were **ʿUmar**, **ʿAlī**, **al-Zubayr**, and **Saʿd** (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:63).

## 2. Marriages, Kinship Networks & Progeny
Saʿd contracted marriages connecting him to aristocratic Bedouin houses and Quraysh:

1. **Salmā bint Khaṣfah b. Thaqafiyyah**:
   - Widow of the heroic warrior al-Muthannā b. Ḥārithah al-Shaybānī; married Saʿd prior to the Battle of al-Qādisiyyah.
2. **Māwiyyah bint Qays**:
   - Bore him **ʿUmar b. Saʿd** and **Muḥammad b. Saʿd**.
3. **Zabbāʾ bint al-Ḥārith**:
   - Bore him **ʿĀmir b. Saʿd**.
4. **Khawlah bint ʿAmr**:
   - Bore him **Ibrāhīm b. Saʿd** and **Muṣʿab b. Saʿd**.
5. **Daughters**:
   - Father of **ʿĀʾishah bint Saʿd**, one of the foremost female jurists and hadith scholars of Medina who transmitted traditions to Imām Mālik b. Anas and the scholars of Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

## 3. Key Sīrah Milestones
### Earliest Conversion in Makkah
Saʿd was among the first men on earth to embrace Islam, accepting the faith at seventeen through **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:315). He famously remarked:
> «لَقَدْ مَكَثْتُ سَبْعَةَ أَيَّامٍ وَإِنِّي لَثُلُثُ الْإِسْلَامِ»
> *"I spent seven days when I constituted a third of all Islam!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

### The First Arrow Shot in Islam
During the early reconnaissance expedition of Sariyyat Rābigh under the command of ʿUbaydah b. al-Ḥārith, the patrol encountered a detachment of polytheist cavalry. Saʿd loosed an arrow that drove back their cavalry, securing the historic distinction of being the first human being to shoot an arrow in the cause of Allāh (*awwal man ramā bi-sahmin fī sabīlillāh*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62, 64; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:10; 11:315).

### Guardian of the Prophet in Medina
Upon the Prophet's arrival in Medina, sleepless from danger, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ said: *"Would that a righteous man from my companions guard me tonight!"* Suddenly the clanking of armor sounded. The Prophet ﷺ asked: *"Who is that?"* Saʿd answered: *"It is Saʿd, O Messenger of Allāh; I came to stand guard over you."* The Prophet ﷺ made duʿāʾ for him and slept peacefully (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:63).

### The Sovereign Ransom Formula at Uḥud (3 AH)
At **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, when the Muslim defense collapsed, Saʿd took his position directly beside the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, firing approximately one thousand arrows to beat back enemy assaults (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205; 11:316). The Prophet ﷺ personally withdrew arrows from his own quiver, handing them to Saʿd and conferring upon him the unprecedented honor:
> «ارْمِ سَعْدُ! فِدَاكَ أَبِي وَأُمِّي!»
> *"Shoot, Saʿd! May my father and mother be ransomed for you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205; 11:316).
Scholars emphasize that Saʿd was the only companion for whom the Prophet ﷺ ever combined both his parents in a ransom formula (*fidāʾ*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:316). 

When the polytheist Ḥibbān b. al-ʿAriqah fired an arrow that struck the veil of Umm Ayman as she carried water to the wounded, laughing mockingly, the Prophet ﷺ handed Saʿd a sharp, unfeathered arrow. Saʿd struck Ḥibbān squarely in the throat, causing him to collapse backward; the Prophet ﷺ smiled until his molars showed and remarked: *"Saʿd has avenged her!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:64).

### The Infallible Archer & Answered Prayer
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ invoked for him:
> «اللَّهُمَّ سَدِّدْ رَمْيَتَهُ، وَأَجِبْ دَعْوَتَهُ!»
> *"O Allāh, make true his aim and answer his supplication!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62–63; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:316).
Henceforth, Saʿd never missed a target, and every prayer he uttered against oppressors was answered immediately (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62–63).

## 4. Conquest of Iraq, Retirement & Demise
Under Caliph ʿUmar, Saʿd was appointed supreme commander over the armies of Iraq in 15 AH. He orchestrated the decisive defeat of the Sasanian Persian Empire at **al-Qādisiyyah**, captured the Persian capital **Ctesiphon (*al-Madāʾin*)**, and founded the metropolitan garrison of **Kūfah** (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62–63). Caliph ʿUmar appointed him as one of the six electors of the Shūrā council in the year twenty-three AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62).

During the civil conflicts following the death of ʿUthmān, Saʿd strictly retired from politics to his rural estate at al-ʿAqīq outside Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:63). When his nephew Hāshim b. ʿUtbah urged him to claim the Caliphate with one hundred thousand swords, Saʿd replied: *"From those hundred thousand swords, I desire only one sword that if I strike a believer it cuts nothing, but if I strike a disbeliever it cuts through!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:63).

He was the very last surviving member of the Ten Promised Paradise (*al-ʿAsharah al-Mubashsharūn*), dying at his estate in al-ʿAqīq in 55 AH (or 56/57/58 AH) at the age of seventy-four (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:62–63; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:317). He was carried to Medina, prayed over in the Prophet's Mosque by the governor Marwān b. al-Ḥakam and the Mothers of the Believers, and buried in **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** shrouded in his faded woolen tunic in which he had fought at Badr (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:63; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:317).
