# Ghazwat Uḥud (غزوة أحد)
*The Battle of Uḥud — The Severe Trial, The Heroic Stand, and the Martyrdom of the Elect*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived exclusively from ***[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād|Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād fī Sīrat Khayr al-ʿIbād]]*** by **[[al-Ṣāliḥī|Imām Shams al-Dīn Muḥammad b. Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī al-Shāmī]]** (d. 942 AH), Volume 4, *al-Bāb al-Thālith ʿAshar fī Ghazwat Uḥud* (pp. 182–257). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the primary narrations of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[Mūsā b. ʿUqbah]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], [[al-Bukhārī]], [[Muslim]], [[al-Bayhaqī]], and the classical *maghāzī* masters.

---

## 1. Causes and Meccan War Preparations

Following their defeat at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, the remnants of [[Quraysh]] retreated to [[Makkah al-Mukarramah]] seeking vengeance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182). **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** had safely conducted the great caravan into Makkah and deposited its cargo intact at Dār al-Nadwah.

A delegation of Qurashī aristocrats whose kinsmen had fallen at Badr — including ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl, ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Rabīʿah, al-Ḥārith b. Hishām, Ḥuwayṭib b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, and Ṣafwān b. Umayyah — approached Abū Sufyān and the merchants:
> *"Muḥammad has bereaved you and slain your best men. Aid us with the profits of this caravan to wage war against him, that we may avenge those we lost!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182).

The caravan had consisted of **1,000 camels** carrying goods valued at **50,000 dinars**. The merchants returned the principal capital to the investors and allocated their total profit — **25,000 dinars** (since trade had yielded one dinar of profit for each dinar invested) — to equip the expeditionary army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182). Regarding this expenditure, Allāh revealed:
$$\text{إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمْ لِيَصُدُّوا عَن سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ ۚ فَسَيُنفِقُونَهَا ثُمَّ تَكُونُ عَلَيْهِمْ حَسْرَةً ثُمَّ يُغْلَبُونَ}$$
> *"Indeed, those who disbelieve spend their wealth to avert people from the path of Allāh. They will spend it; then it will be for them a regret, and then they will be overcome..."* (Qurʾān 8:36; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182).

Quraysh mobilized approximately **3,000 warriors**, uniting their own clans with confederate Arab allies and the Ahabīsh mercenaries (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182). Within their ranks were **700 men in coats of mail** (*darʿ*) and **200 horses** (*faras*). To prevent retreat, they brought fifteen noblewomen on camel-litters (*al-ẓuʿun*), led by **Hind bint ʿUtbah** (wife of Abū Sufyān), who beat tambourines (*al-dufūf*) and wailed elegies for the dead of Badr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:183). Jubayr b. Muṭʿim promised his Abyssinian slave, **[[Waḥshī b. Ḥarb]]**, his freedom if he killed **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ to avenge Jubayr's uncle Ṭuʿaymah b. ʿAdī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:183).

## 2. Intelligence and Strategic Consultation (*al-Shūrā*)

The paternal uncle of the Prophet ﷺ, **[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁, dispatched a secret message from Makkah warning of the invasion. The courier reached the Prophet ﷺ at Qubāʾ, where **[[Ubayy b. Kaʿb]]** ﵁ read it privately to him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182). The Prophet ﷺ then confided the report in **[[Saʿd b. al-Rabīʿ]]** ﵁ under oath of secrecy (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182).

The polytheist army departed Makkah on the 5th of Shawwāl and encamped on Wednesday at Mount ʿAynayn in the valley bottom near **[[Jabal Uḥud]]**, letting their mounts graze upon the agricultural fields of al-ʿUrāyḍ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:183). On Thursday night, armed squads of [[al-Aws]] and **[[al-Khazraj]]** guarded the Prophet's door at the Mosque (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:184).

### The Prophetic Vision (*al-Ruʾyā*)
On Friday eve, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ experienced a divine dream, which he recounted to his Companions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:184–185):
1. **The Slaughtered Cattle**: *«رَأَيْتُ بَقَرًا تُذْبَحُ»* — interpreted as a company of his noble Companions who would be martyred.
2. **The Notched Sword**: *«رَأَيْتُ فِي ذُبَابِ سَيْفِي ثَلْمًا»* (or that his sword *Dhū al-Faqār* broke at its tip) — interpreted as injury to his own face, and the slaying of a foremost member of his household, **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁.
3. **The Fortified Armor**: *«رَأَيْتُ أَنِّي فِي دِرْعٍ حَصِينَةٍ»* — interpreted as the fortified oasis of **[[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah]]**.
4. **The Ram**: *«كَأَنِّي مُرْدِفٌ كَبْشًا»* — interpreted as the slaying of the enemy's standard-bearer and military leader (*kabsh al-katībah*), Ṭalḥah b. Abī Ṭalḥah.

### The Consultation at the Mosque
The Prophet ﷺ favoured remaining within Medina: barricading the narrow streets, stationing men at the entryways, and positioning women and children atop the fortified stone towers (*al-āṭām*) to rain stones upon the invaders (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:185). The elder Muhājirūn, Anṣār, and **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** concurred with this defensive posture.

However, a spirited group of younger Muslims who had missed Badr, led by enthusiastic veterans such as **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁, **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁, and al-Nuʿmān b. Mālik ﵁, passionately petitioned to march out into the open plain to meet the enemy:
> *"O Messenger of Allāh, we fear that our enemy will think we remained behind out of cowardice! At Badr you had but three hundred men and Allāh gave you victory; today we are a vast assembly!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:185).

After leading Friday prayer and delivering an exhortative sermon, the Prophet ﷺ entered his quarters accompanied by **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, who fastened his armor and tied his turban. When the people realized they had pressured the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ against his inner preference, they expressed deep remorse. The Prophet ﷺ emerged donning his coat of mail, girt with his sword, and declared decisively:
> «مَا يَنْبَغِي لِنَبِيٍّ إِذَا لَبِسَ لَأْمَتَهُ أَنْ يَضَعَهَا حَتَّى يَحْكُمَ اللَّهُ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَ أَعْدَائِهِ!»
> *"It is not fitting for a Prophet, once he has donned his armor, to lay it aside until Allāh has judged between him and his enemies!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:186).

He then bound three banners: the banner of the Aws to **Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr** ﵁, the banner of the Khazraj to **[[al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir]]** ﵁ (or **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁), and the banner of the Muhājirūn to **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:186).

## 3. The March and the Hypocrites' Defection

The Prophet ﷺ marched forth riding his horse *al-Sakb*, flanked by **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁ and **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁ running in armor ahead of him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:186). At Raʾs al-Thaniyyah, he observed a heavily armed Jewish contingent allied with ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy; upon learning they had not embraced Islam, he dismissed them, stating: *«We do not seek the assistance of polytheists against polytheists»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:186).

### Review of the Army at al-Shaykhayn
At the two fortresses of **al-Shaykhayn**, the Prophet ﷺ halted to inspect the ranks (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:187). He turned back seventeen underage youths who were fourteen years old, including **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar]]**, **[[Zayd b. Thābit]]**, **[[Usāmah b. Zayd]]**, and **[[al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib]]**. However, when Rāfiʿ b. Khadīj was commended for his skill with the bow, the Prophet ﷺ permitted him; hearing this, Samurah b. Jundub pleaded that he could wrestle Rāfiʿ to the ground. The Prophet ﷺ bade them wrestle, and when Samurah threw Rāfiʿ, he permitted him as well (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:187).

### The Treachery of Ibn Ubayy
At the dawn halt at **al-Shawṭ**, the hypocrite leader **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** deserted with **one-third of the army (300 men)**, grumbling:
> *"He obeyed children and those of no counsel, and disobeyed me! Why should we slay ourselves here, O people?!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:188).

**[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām]]** ﵁ pursued the deserters, rebuking them: *«Come, fight in the path of Allāh or defend!»* They retorted hypocritically: *«Had we known there would be fighting, we would have followed you»* (Qurʾān 3:167; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:188). The desertion shook the clans of **Banū Salimah** and **Banū Ḥārithah**, but Allāh steadied their resolve, as memorialized in the Qurʾān:
$$\text{إِذْ هَمَّت طَّائِفَتَانِ مِنكُمْ أَن تَفْشَلَا وَاللَّهُ وَلِيُّهُمَا}$$
> *"When two factions among you were about to lose heart, but Allāh was their protector..."* (Qurʾān 3:122; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:188–189).

The remaining Muslim force numbered **700 fighters** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:189). Guided by Abū Khaythamah through the orchards of the Ḥarrah, they circumvented the enemy camp and occupied the base of **[[Jabal Uḥud]]**.

---

## 4. Tactical Deployment & The Archers of Mount ʿAynayn

The Prophet ﷺ drew up the Muslim army with their backs anchored against the rugged heights of **[[Jabal Uḥud]]**, facing Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191). The Muslim battle-cry (*shiʿār*) was ordained as: **«أَمِتْ أَمِتْ»** (*«Amit, amit!»* — *«Slay, slay!»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191).

To secure the Muslim rear and left flank against Meccan cavalry encirclement through the depression of Wādī Qanāt, the Prophet ﷺ stationed **fifty elite archers** atop the strategic knoll of **[[Jabal al-Rumāh|Mount ʿAynayn (Jabal al-Rumāh)]]**, placing them under the command of **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191, 205). He delivered an emphatic, unequivocal military injunction:
> «احْمُوا ظُهُورَنَا، فَإِنْ رَأَيْتُمُونَا نُقْتَلُ فَلَا تَنْصُرُونَا، وَإِنْ رَأَيْتُمُونَا غَنِمْنَا فَلَا تُشْرِكُونَا! وَإِنْ رَأَيْتُمُونَا تَخْطَفُنَا الطَّيْرُ فَلَا تَبْرَحُوا مِنْ مَكَانِكُمْ هَذَا حَتَّى أُرْسِلَ إِلَيْكُمْ!»
> *"Guard our backs! If you see us being slain, do not come to our aid; and if you see us collecting spoils, do not join us! Even if you see birds snatching us, do not abandon this post until I send for you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205).

Opposite them, the Meccans drew up their lines at al-Sabkhah: **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** commanded the cavalry on the right wing, ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl commanded the cavalry on the left wing, and Ṣafwān b. Umayyah commanded the infantry (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191). The ancestral battle standard of Quraysh was committed to the clan of Banū ʿAbd al-Dār (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191).

## 5. Opening Combat & The Slaying of the Standard-Bearers

Before the melee commenced, the Prophet ﷺ held aloft a renowned sword and inquired: *«Who will take this sword with its right?»* Several prominent Companions reached for it, but the Prophet ﷺ withheld it until **Abū Dujānah Simāk b. Kharashah** ﵁ stepped forward and asked: *«What is its right, O Messenger of Allāh?»* He replied: *«That you strike the enemy with it until it bends!»* Abū Dujānah accepted it, wound his famous red band of death (*ʿiṣābat al-mawt*) around his head, and began strutting proudly between the ranks. Observing his swagger, the Prophet ﷺ remarked:
> «إِنَّهَا لَمِشْيَةٌ يُبْغِضُهَا اللَّهُ إِلَّا فِي هَذَا الْمَوْطِنِ»
> *"Indeed, it is a gait that Allāh despises, except in such a situation as this!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:192).

### Ali and the Slaying of Talhah b. Abi Talhah
The chief standard-bearer of Quraysh, **Ṭalḥah b. Abī Ṭalḥah** (*Kabsh al-Katībah*), rode forth challenging the Muslims to a duel. **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ charged him between the ranks, striking him down with a devastating blow that severed his leg (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:194). When Ṭalḥah fell, exposing his nakedness and pleading for mercy through kinship, ʿAlī magnanimously turned away without delivering the coup de grâce, knowing the wound was mortal. The Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims roared with takbīr, fulfilling the Prophet's dream of the slaughtered ram (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:194).

### Annihilation of Banu Abd al-Dar
Successive warriors of Banū ʿAbd al-Dār rushed forward to lift the fallen standard, only to be cut down one after another (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:194–195):
1. **Abū Shaybah ʿUthmān b. Abī Ṭalḥah**: Slayn by **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁, who cleaved his shoulder down to his waist.
2. **Abū Saʿd b. Abī Ṭalḥah**: Shot fatally in the throat by **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁.
3. **Musāfiʿ b. Ṭalḥah**: Slayn by **ʿĀṣim b. Thābit b. Abī al-Aqlaḥ** ﵁ with an arrow.
4. **Al-Ḥārith b. Ṭalḥah**: Likewise felled by an arrow from **ʿĀṣim b. Thābit** ﵁.
5. **Kilāb b. Ṭalḥah**: Slayn by **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ (or Qizmān).
6. **Al-Julās b. Ṭalḥah**: Slayn by **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁.
7. **Arṭāh b. Shuraḥbīl**: Slayn by **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁.
8. **Shurayḥ b. Qāriẓ**: Cut down in the general melee.
9. **Abū Zayd b. ʿUmayr**: Slayn by Qizmān.
10. **Qāsiṭ b. Shuraḥbīl**: Slayn by Qizmān.
11. **Ṣuʾāb (their Abyssinian slave)**: Took up the standard; when his right hand was severed, he seized it with his left; when that was cut off, he embraced the pole to his chest with his stumps until felled by Qizmān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

With all their champions dead, the Meccan standard lay trampled in the dust until raised briefly by a woman, ʿAmrah bint ʿAlqamah al-Ḥārithiyyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

## 6. The Reversal: Disobedience on Mount ʿAynayn

The initial Muslim assault shattered the polytheist lines. The Meccans broke into disorderly flight, abandoning their tents, baggage, and mounts. The Muslim warriors fell upon the abandoned Meccan camp, gathering the spoils (*al-ghanīmah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

Observing the enemy's rout, the archers stationed atop **[[Jabal al-Rumāh]]** shouted: *«O people, the spoils! Your comrades are victorious, what keeps you here?!»* The commander **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr]]** ﵁ pleaded vehemently, reminding them of the Prophet's absolute command: *«Have you forgotten what the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanded you?!»* Forty archers disregarded his orders and abandoned the knoll, leaving Ibn Jubayr with fewer than ten steadfast men (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

**[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]**, commanding the Meccan right-wing cavalry, immediately recognized the gaping tactical vulnerability. Wheel-charging around the rear with his cavalry and supported by ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl, Khālid charged Mount ʿAynayn, overwhelmed and martyred **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr]]** ﵁ and his tiny garrison, and fell upon the exposed rear of the Muslim army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

Shouting their polytheist slogans (*«Yā li-l-ʿUzzā! Yā li-Hubal!»*), the Meccan cavalry struck the scattered Muslims from behind while the fleeing Meccan infantry rallied and counterattacked from the front. Total panic gripped the Muslim lines; trapped in a devastating pincer encirclement, ranks fractured, men struck each other in the dust, and Satan cried out with a piercing voice: **«أَلَا إِنَّ مُحَمَّدًا قَدْ قُتِلَ!»** (*«Indeed, Muḥammad has been killed!»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:196, 249).

---

## 7. The Crucible: Defense of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ

The army fragmented into three groups: a third fled the field, a third fell wounded, and a third stood firm (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:196). In this supreme crisis, a small cadre of Companions formed an impenetrable living barrier around the Prophet ﷺ.

### The Inner Guard
- **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]] ﵁**: Shielded the Prophet ﷺ with his own body, parrying sword cuts and catching arrows aimed at the Prophet's face until his fingers were severed and his hand permanently paralyzed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:199–200). He received over seventy wounds and was elevated to the status of a *"living martyr walking upon the earth"* (*al-shahīd al-ḥayy*).
- **Abū Dujānah** ﵁: Bent his back over the Prophet ﷺ as a human shield, absorbing dozens of arrows into his back without shifting an inch (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:200).
- **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]] ﵁**: The Prophet ﷺ emptied his quiver for him, saying: *«ارْمِ سَعْدُ! فِدَاكَ أَبِي وَأُمِّي!»* (*«Shoot, Saʿd! May my father and mother be ransomed for you!»*) — an honor never combined for any other companion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:200–201, 245). Saʿd shot roughly a thousand arrows that day.
- **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]] ﵁**: When two iron links of the Prophet's helmet circlets (*ḥalaqatā al-mighfar*) penetrated deeply into his cheek, Abū ʿUbaydah refused to use his fingers lest he cause agony; he seized the links with his incisors and pulled them out, losing both front teeth in the process (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:199–200).
- **Nusaybah bint Kaʿb (Umm ʿUmārah)** ﵂: Stood sword in hand defending the Prophet ﷺ against horsemen and infantry, sustaining twelve wounds, including a deep gash on her shoulder inflicted by Ibn Qamiʾah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:202).
- **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]] ﵁**: Sustained over twenty wounds, was injured in the mouth losing teeth, and suffered a deep wound to his leg that left him with a permanent limp (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:200).
- **Mālik b. Sinān** ﵁ (father of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī): Sucked the arterial blood from the Prophet's cheek and swallowed it; the Prophet ﷺ said of him: *«Whoever's blood touches my blood, the Fire shall not touch him»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:200).

### Injuries Sustained by the Prophet ﷺ
During the assault, ʿUtbah b. Abī Waqqāṣ hurled a stone that chipped the Prophet's lower right tooth (*rubāʿiyyah*) and split his lower lip (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:198). ʿAbdullāh b. Shihāb al-Zuhrī slashed his forehead, and **ʿAbdullāh b. Qamiʾah** struck his cheek with a heavy sword blow that forced two rings of the helmet into his cheekbone and knocked him into one of the concealed pits dug by Abū ʿĀmir al-Fāsiq (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:199). As blood cascaded down his face, the Prophet ﷺ wiped it away, supplicating:
> «اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِقَوْمِي فَإِنَّهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ»
> *"O Allāh, forgive my people, for they do not know!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:198).

## 8. Slaying of the Arch-Enemy Ubayy b. Khalaf

As the Prophet ﷺ was assisted up toward the ravine of the mountain (*al-shiʿb*), the polytheist nobleman **[[Ubayy b. Khalaf]]** ☠ rode up on his stallion *al-ʿAwd*, clad in heavy armor, shouting: *«Where is Muḥammad? May I not survive if he survives!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:208).

When Companions offered to intercept him, the Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Leave him alone!»* Taking a small javelin (*ḥarbah*) from **al-Ḥārith b. al-Ṣimmah** ﵁, the Prophet ﷺ shook himself with such intensity that the Companions flew back from around him like flies off a camel's back (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:208). As Ubayy approached, the Prophet ﷺ thrust the javelin into an exposed crevice of his collarbone (*al-tarquwah*) between his helmet and breastplate. Ubayy tumbled repeatedly from his horse, bellowing like a slaughtered bull. Although his comrades dismissed the wound as a mere scratch, Ubayy cried out: *«By al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā, if this scratch were distributed among all the people of Dhū al-Majāz, it would slay them! He told me in Makkah that he would kill me, and by Allāh, had he but spat upon me he would have killed me!»* Ubayy died on the return march at Sarif (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:208). He was the only human being ever slain directly by the hand of the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:208).

## 9. Notable Martyrdoms of the Elect

The battle witnessed the martyrdom of **seventy noble Muslims**, whose testimonies form an enduring pillar of Islamic heritage:

### 1. Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib ﵁ (*Sayyid al-Shuhadāʾ*)
The Prophet's uncle fought furiously with two swords, felling champions including Sibāʿ b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā. Lurking behind a boulder, **[[Waḥshī b. Ḥarb]]** hurled his Abyssinian spear, striking Ḥamzah in the lower abdomen until it pierced through between his legs (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:216–217). Following his death, Hind bint ʿUtbah ripped open his torso, tore out his liver and chewed it, and severed his nose and ears to fashion grotesque necklaces (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:218, 220).

### 2. Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr ﵁ (The Standard-Bearer)
Holding the Great Banner of Islam, **[[Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr]]** ﵁ stood firm against Ibn Qamiʾah. When his right hand was severed, he seized the banner with his left; when his left was severed, he clasped it with his arms to his chest, repeating: *«وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِن قَبْلِهِ الرُّسُلُ»* (*«Muḥammad is no more than a messenger; messengers have passed away before him»*), until struck down by a spear (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:219). Because he closely resembled the Prophet ﷺ in stature, Ibn Qamiʾah mistook him for the Messenger and shouted that Muḥammad was dead.

### 3. Anas b. al-Naḍr ﵁ (The Aroma of Paradise)
Encountering Companions sitting in despair after the rumor of the Prophet's death, **[[Anas b. al-Naḍr]]** ﵁ declared: *«What will you do with life after him?! Arise and die upon that which the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ died!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:215). Charging toward the enemy, he cried to **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁: *«O Saʿd! The aroma of Paradise! I scent its fragrance from beyond Uḥud!»* He fought until martyred; over eighty wounds (sword strikes, spear thrusts, arrow piercings) were found on his body, and only his sister al-Rabīʿ could identify him by his fingertips (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:215). Regarding him, Allāh revealed:
$$\text{مِّنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ رِجَالٌ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوا اللَّهَ عَلَيْهِ}$$
> *"Among the believers are men true to what they promised Allāh..."* (Qurʾān 33:23; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:215).

### 4. Ḥanẓalah b. Abī ʿĀmir ﵁ (*Ghasīl al-Malāʾikah*)
Married on the eve of the battle to Jamīlah bint ʿAbdillāh b. Ubayy, **[[Ḥanẓalah b. Abī ʿĀmir]]** ﵁ departed for the battlefield in a state of ritual impurity (*janābah*) upon hearing the battle-call (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:213). He nearly slew Abū Sufyān before being speared from behind by Shaddād b. al-Aswad (Ibn Shuʿūb). The Prophet ﷺ announced: *«I saw the angels washing Ḥanẓalah between the heavens and the earth with rainwater in vessels of silver!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:213).

### 5. ʿAmr b. al-Jamūḥ ﵁ and ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām ﵁
**[[ʿAmr b. al-Jamūḥ]]** ﵁, severely lame, had been restrained from Badr by his sons. At Uḥud, he insisted on joining, swearing: *«By Allāh, I hope to tread upon the soil of Paradise with this lame foot of mine!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:214). His brother-in-law **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām]]** ﵁ was the very first Muslim slain on the field (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:215). The Prophet ﷺ ordered them buried together in a single grave, saying: *«Bury these two together, for they were pure and loyal friends in this world»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225).

### 6. Saʿd b. al-Rabīʿ ﵁ (The Final Testament)
Dispatched by the Prophet ﷺ to search the battlefield, Zayd b. Thābit ﵁ found **[[Saʿd b. al-Rabīʿ]]** ﵁ in his dying moments with seventy wounds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:222). Saʿd whispered his final testament:
> *"Convey my greetings of peace to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and say to him: 'Saʿd says to you: May Allāh reward you on our behalf better than any Prophet has been rewarded on behalf of his nation.' And convey my greetings to your people among the Anṣār, and tell them: 'You will have no excuse before Allāh on the Day of Resurrection if any harm reaches your Prophet while a single eye among you yet blinks!'"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:222).

## 10. The Regrouping and the Final Verbal Duel

The Prophet ﷺ and the surviving Muslims consolidated their positions in the rocky ravine of **[[Jabal Uḥud]]**. When the Prophet ﷺ attempted to mount a large boulder to survey the enemy, his physical weakness and the weight of his two coats of mail prevented him; **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁ knelt beneath him and hoisted him up until he stood atop the rock, prompting the Prophet ﷺ to proclaim: *«Awjaba Ṭalḥah»* (*«Ṭalḥah has made Paradise obligatory for himself»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:210).

When a cavalry unit under Khālid b. al-Walīd attempted to scale the crest above the ravine, the Prophet ﷺ supplicated: *«O Allāh, it is not fitting that they should be above us!»* **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ and a squad of Muhājirūn drove them back down with stones and arrows (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:210–211).

Allāh then cast a miraculous slumber (*al-nuʿās*) upon the steadfast believers, causing swords to drop from their hands and dispelling terror from their hearts (Qurʾān 3:154; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:204–205).

### The Dialogue with Abū Sufyān
Before retreating toward Makkah, Abū Sufyān ascended an outcrop facing the ravine and called out (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:220):
- *«Is Muḥammad among the people?»* — The Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Do not answer him.»*
- *«Is Ibn Abī Quḥāfah among the people?»* — The Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Do not answer him.»*
- *«Is Ibn al-Khaṭṭāb among the people?»* — The Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Do not answer him.»*

Abū Sufyān boasted: *«As for these, they have been slain; had they been alive they would have answered!»* Unable to restrain himself, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ shouted back:
> *"You lie, O enemy of Allāh! Those you named are all alive, and Allāh has preserved what will bring you grief!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:220).

Abū Sufyān chanted: **«اعْلُ هُبَلْ!»** (*«Exalt Hubal!»*).  
The Prophet ﷺ told ʿUmar: *«Arise and answer him!»*  
ʿUmar cried: **«اللَّهُ أَعْلَى وَأَجَلُّ!»** (*«Allāh is Higher and Greater!»*).  
Abū Sufyān replied: *«We have al-ʿUzzā, and you have no ʿUzzā!»*  
The Prophet ﷺ instructed: *«Say: Allāh is our Protector, and you have no protector!»* (*«اللَّهُ مَوْلَانَا وَلَا مَوْلَى لَكُمْ»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:220).

Abū Sufyān concluded: *«A day for the day of Badr; warfare is alternate!»* and declared an appointment to meet again at Badr the following year (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:220).

## 11. Burial of the Martyrs & Return to Medina

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ wept bitterly over the mutilated corpse of his uncle Ḥamzah ﵁, draping his own mantle over his head while grass (*al-idhkhir*) was placed over his exposed feet (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:224–225). He forbade mutilation in warfare forever (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:224).

The Prophet ﷺ ordered the martyrs buried directly upon the battlefield where they fell, prohibiting their transfer into the cemeteries of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225–226):
> «ادْفِنُوهُمْ بِدِمَائِهِمْ وَثِيَابِهِمْ، فَإِنَّهُ لَيْسَ جَرِيحٌ يُجْرَحُ فِي اللَّهِ إِلَّا جَاءَ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ يَدْمَى، لَوْنُهُ لَوْنُ الدَّمِ، وَرِيحُهُ رِيحُ الْمِسْكِ!»
> *"Bury them in their blood and garments! For there is no wound suffered in the cause of Allāh except that on the Day of Resurrection it bleeds afresh: its color is the color of blood, and its fragrance is the fragrance of musk!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225).

He grouped two and three martyrs per grave, asking: *«Which of them held more of the Qurʾān?»* and placing the one with greater knowledge of the Qurʾān first into the niche (*al-laḥd*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225). He offered no funeral prayers over them and did not wash them, as martyrs are living with their Lord (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225, 248).

Upon his return to Medina on Saturday evening, the women of the Anṣār were weeping for their dead. Hearing weeping from every house except Ḥamzah's, the Prophet ﷺ remarked sorrowfully: *«But Ḥamzah has no weeping women»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:228). The Anṣār immediately instructed their women to mourn Ḥamzah before weeping for their own kin (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:228).

At dawn the following morning, despite exhausting fatigue and grievous wounds, the Prophet ﷺ summoned the veterans of Uḥud to assemble for **[[Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad]]** (8 Shawwāl 3 AH) to pursue Quraysh and demonstrate decisive military resilience (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308).
