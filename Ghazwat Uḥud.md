# Ghazwat Uḥud (غزوة أحد)
*The battle fought below Mount Uḥud in Shawwāl of the year 3*

The **Battle of Uḥud** was fought in Shawwāl of the third year after the Hijrah, below the mountain of Uḥud outside Medina, between the Muslims under the Prophet ﷺ and an army of Quraysh and their allies led by [[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]].[^s4.182][^s4.242] Quraysh had come to avenge Badr.[^s4.182] The Muslims had the upper hand at first and drove the Meccans from their camp, but when most of the archers posted to guard their rear left their position to take spoils, [[Khālid b. al-Walīd]] led the Meccan cavalry round behind them and the battle turned.[^s4.195] The Prophet ﷺ was wounded and for a time was reported killed; about seventy Muslims died, among them his uncle [[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]].[^s4.198][^s4.256] The Qurʾān addresses the battle at length in [[Sūrat Āl ʿImrān]].[^s4.231]

## Background

After Badr, the caravan that Abū Sufyān had brought home was held untouched in the Dār al-Nadwah. The leading men of Quraysh who had lost fathers, sons and brothers at Badr — among them ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl, al-Ḥārith b. Hishām and Ṣafwān b. Umayyah — asked the merchants to give the profits to the war against the Prophet ﷺ, and Abū Sufyān was the first to agree.[^s4.182] According to al-Balādhurī the caravan had been a thousand camels and fifty thousand dinars, and twenty-five thousand dinars were set aside for the campaign; the verse *"Those who disbelieve spend their wealth to turn people from the way of Allāh…"* (al-Anfāl 8:36) was revealed about it.[^s4.182]

Quraysh sent men out to rouse the tribes, and Abū Sufyān gathered nearly three thousand men from Quraysh, their allies and the Aḥābīsh, seven hundred of them in mail and two hundred on horseback.[^s4.182] The leaders brought their wives with them so that the men would not flee, Hind bint ʿUtbah among them, with women beating drums and lamenting the dead of Badr. [[Jubayr b. Muṭʿim]] promised his Abyssinian slave Waḥshī, a skilled javelin-thrower, his freedom if he killed Ḥamzah.[^s4.183] They left Makkah in Shawwāl, and on the way, at al-Abwāʾ, Hind proposed digging up the grave of the Prophet's mother [[Āminah bint Wahb|Āminah]]; Quraysh refused.[^s4.183]

## Medina prepares

The Prophet's uncle [[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib|al-ʿAbbās]] sent word of the army by a man of Ghifār; the letter was read to the Prophet ﷺ at Qubāʾ by [[Ubayy b. Kaʿb]], who was told to keep it secret.[^s4.182] Scouts confirmed that the Meccans had camped outside Medina and turned their animals loose in the crops, and the leaders of al-Aws and al-Khazraj spent the night armed at the mosque door.[^s4.183][^s4.184b]

The Prophet ﷺ had seen a dream: that he was wearing a strong coat of mail, which he took to be Medina; that the edge of his sword was notched, which he took to mean a man of his family would be killed; and that cattle were being slaughtered, which he took to mean that some of his Companions would fall.[^s4.184] He proposed staying in Medina and fighting in its streets if the enemy came in. This was the view of the senior Muhājirūn and Anṣār, and of [[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl|ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy]]. But a group of Muslims, most of them young men who had missed Badr, pressed to go out and meet the enemy, supported by Ḥamzah, [[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]] and others.[^s4.185] After the Friday prayer the Prophet ﷺ put on his armour. When the Muslims, regretting that they had pressed him, offered to stay, he answered that it was not fitting for a prophet who had put on his armour to take it off until Allāh had judged between him and his enemy.[^s4.186] He gave the banner of al-Aws to [[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]], that of al-Khazraj to [[al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir]] (or Saʿd b. ʿUbādah) and that of the Muhājirūn to [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]], and left [[ʿAbdullāh b. Umm Maktūm|Ibn Umm Maktūm]] to lead the prayer in Medina.[^s4.186]

## The march and Ibn Ubayy's withdrawal

At al-Shaykhayn the Prophet ﷺ reviewed the army and sent home boys he judged too young — among them [[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar]], [[Zayd b. Thābit]], [[Usāmah b. Zayd]] and [[al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib]] — though he let Rāfiʿ b. Khadīj stay because he was an archer, and Samurah b. Jundub after he outwrestled Rāfiʿ.[^s4.187] At al-Shawṭ, ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy turned back with a third of the army, complaining that the Prophet ﷺ had listened to boys and ignored him. ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām went after them pleading with them not to abandon their people, and was told, *"If we knew there would be fighting, we would not leave you."*[^s4.188] Two clans of the Anṣār, Banū Salimah and Banū Ḥārithah, nearly lost heart as well; the verse *"When two parties among you were about to lose courage, and Allāh was their protector"* (Āl ʿImrān 3:122) refers to them.[^s4.188][^s4.189]

Seven hundred men remained, according to the majority, with only the Prophet's horse and one other.[^s4.189][^s4.249] The army camped in the valley with its back to Uḥud, facing Medina.[^s4.189]

## Battle order

The Prophet ﷺ posted fifty archers under [[ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr]] to guard the rear against the Meccan cavalry, with strict orders:[^s4.190]

> «وإن رأيتمونا تخطّفنا الطير فلا تبرحوا، حتى أرسل إليكم»
> *"Even if you see birds snatching us away, do not leave your place until I send for you."*[^s4.190]

The two wings were given to [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]] and al-Mundhir b. ʿAmr. Learning that the Meccan standard was carried by Ṭalḥah b. Abī Ṭalḥah of Banū ʿAbd al-Dār, the Prophet ﷺ said, "We are more entitled to loyalty than they," and took the banner of the Muhājirūn from ʿAlī and gave it to [[Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr]].[^s4.190][^s4.186] He wore two coats of mail, and the Muslims' battle-cry that day was *"Amit, amit!"* — "Kill, kill!"[^s4.191] On the Meccan side Khālid b. al-Walīd commanded the cavalry of the right wing and ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl that of the left.[^s4.191]

## The first phase

The first to fight was Abū ʿĀmir "al-Fāsiq", a man of al-Aws who had gone to Makkah with fifty hypocrites and promised Quraysh that his people would come over to them; when the Aws answered his call with curses, he fought them and then pelted them with stones.[^s4.191] Hind and the women with her beat their drums and sang to urge the men on.[^s4.191] The Prophet ﷺ gave his sword to [[Abū Dujānah]], who went into the enemy with his red headband of death.[^s4.192]

ʿAlī killed Ṭalḥah b. Abī Ṭalḥah in single combat — the "ram" of the Prophet's dream — and the Muslims raised the *takbīr* and charged.[^s4.194] The Meccan standard passed from hand to hand among the men of ʿAbd al-Dār, and one bearer after another was killed: by Ḥamzah, by [[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]], by ʿĀṣim b. Thābit, by al-Zubayr, by [[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]], by ʿAlī and by Quzmān, until it lay in the dust and a woman, ʿAmrah bint ʿAlqamah, raised it again.[^s4.194][^s4.195] Three cavalry charges were driven back by the archers.[^s4.193] Quraysh broke and fled, and the Muslims entered their camp and began to plunder it.[^s4.195]

## The archers leave their post

Seeing the enemy in flight, most of the archers called out *"The spoils, the spoils!"* ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr reminded them of the Prophet's order, but fewer than ten stayed with him. Khālid b. al-Walīd saw how few were left on the hill, wheeled the cavalry round with ʿIkrimah, and killed the remaining archers; ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr fought until he was killed, and his body was mutilated.[^s4.195] The Meccan horsemen fell on the Muslims from behind, crying *"O for al-ʿUzzā! O for Hubal!"*, and the Meccan foot soldiers turned back to fight.[^s4.195]

The Muslim lines dissolved. In the confusion some Muslims struck at one another, a voice cried out that Muḥammad had been killed, and many lost heart; some fled as far as Medina, where [[Umm Ayman]] threw dust in their faces and offered them a spindle in exchange for their swords.[^s4.196]

## The Prophet ﷺ under attack

The Prophet ﷺ did not give ground. He shot with his bow until its string broke and the bow itself splintered, and then threw stones.[^s4.196][^s4.197] ʿUtbah b. Abī Waqqāṣ hit him with a stone that broke one of his teeth and cut his lip; ʿAbdullāh b. Shihāb gashed his forehead; and Ibn Qamiʾah struck him so that two rings of his helmet were driven into his cheek. He fell into one of the pits Abū ʿĀmir had dug, and ʿAlī and Ṭalḥah lifted him out.[^s4.198][^s4.199] [[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]] drew the two rings out with his teeth, losing two of his own front teeth.[^s4.199][^s4.200]

A small band fought around him. Eight pledged themselves to fight to the death — ʿAlī, al-Zubayr and Ṭalḥah of the Muhājirūn, and Abū Dujānah, al-Ḥārith b. al-Ṣimmah, al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir, ʿĀṣim b. Thābit and Sahl b. Ḥunayf of the Anṣār — and none of them was killed.[^s4.198] Ṭalḥah shielded the Prophet ﷺ with his hand until it was crippled.[^s4.203] Abū Dujānah bent over him while the arrows struck his own back.[^s4.200] Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ shot arrow after arrow while the Prophet ﷺ handed him more, saying, *"Shoot — may my father and mother be your ransom!"*[^s4.200][^s4.201] [[Nusaybah bint Kaʿb]], who had come out to give water to the wounded, took up a sword and fought beside him; he said that wherever he turned that day he saw her fighting in his defence.[^s4.201] [[Abū Ṭalḥah al-Anṣārī]] stood before him with his shield, telling him not to raise his head lest an arrow strike him: *"My throat before your throat!"*[^s4.204]

In the midst of it, Allāh sent drowsiness on those who stood firm — Abū Ṭalḥah's sword kept slipping from his hand — as a sign of security, and the hypocrites who were afraid for themselves did not share it (Āl ʿImrān 3:154).[^s4.204][^s4.205]

## Regrouping in the ravine

[[Kaʿb b. Mālik]] was the first to recognise the Prophet ﷺ, who signalled him to keep quiet and exchanged armour with him; Kaʿb took more than twenty wounds from men who thought he was the Prophet ﷺ. When the Muslims did recognise him they rallied round him, and he withdrew with them into a ravine of the mountain.[^s4.207][^s4.208] There [[Ubayy b. Khalaf]], who had boasted after being ransomed at Badr that he would kill the Prophet ﷺ from the back of his horse, rode at him. The Prophet ﷺ took a spear from al-Ḥārith b. al-Ṣimmah and struck him in the neck; the wound looked slight, but Ubayy cried that Muḥammad had killed him, and he died at Baṭn Rābigh on the way home.[^s4.208] ʿAlī brought water in his shield to wash the blood from the Prophet's face.[^s4.209][^s4.210] The Prophet ﷺ prayed the noon prayer sitting because of his wounds, and the Muslims prayed sitting behind him.[^s4.211]

## The martyrs

Ḥamzah fought in front of the Prophet ﷺ until Waḥshī, lying in wait, threw his javelin and killed him.[^s4.216][^s4.217] Hind and the women with her mutilated the dead, cutting off ears and noses.[^s4.220] Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr held the banner until his right hand was cut off, then held it in his left until that too was cut off, then clasped it to his chest with his arms until he was killed.[^s4.219]

[[Anas b. al-Naḍr]], who had missed Badr, found men who had thrown down their weapons because they thought the Prophet ﷺ was dead. "What will you do with life after him? Get up and die as he died," he told them, and charged, saying he could smell the scent of Paradise from beyond Uḥud. He was found with more than eighty wounds, so disfigured that only his sister recognised him, by his fingertips.[^s4.215][^s4.216] [[Ḥanẓalah b. Abī ʿĀmir]] had brought Abū Sufyān down from his horse and was about to kill him when he was speared; the Prophet ﷺ said that he had seen the angels washing him, and his wife explained that he had gone out to battle in a state of ritual impurity on hearing the call.[^s4.213] [[ʿAmr b. al-Jamūḥ]], who was badly lame and whose sons had tried to keep him at home, said he hoped to walk on that lame leg in Paradise, and was killed with his son Khallād.[^s4.213][^s4.214] [[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām]], the father of Jābir, was the first Muslim to be killed.[^s4.215]

When [[Saʿd b. al-Rabīʿ]] was found near death with seventy wounds, he sent the Prophet ﷺ his greeting and told his people that they would have no excuse before Allāh if harm reached the Prophet ﷺ while one eye among them still blinked.[^s4.222]

## Abū Sufyān's challenge and the withdrawal

As the armies drew apart, Abū Sufyān rode up the slope and called out three times: was Muḥammad among them? Ibn Abī Quḥāfah? Ibn al-Khaṭṭāb? The Prophet ﷺ told them not to answer, and Abū Sufyān declared that they were all dead. ʿUmar could not hold back: "You lie, enemy of Allāh — those you named are all alive."[^s4.220]

> «اعل هبل» — «الله أعلى وأجلّ»
> *"Be exalted, Hubal!" — "Allāh is higher and more majestic!"*[^s4.220]

"A day for the day of Badr," said Abū Sufyān; "war goes by turns." At the Prophet's instruction ʿUmar answered: *"Not equal: our dead are in Paradise, and yours are in the Fire."* Before leaving, Abū Sufyān named a rendezvous — Badr, at the same time next year — and the Prophet ﷺ had the challenge accepted.[^s4.220][^s4.221] (See [[Ghazwat Badr al-Mawʿid]].) Fearing an attack on Medina, the Prophet ﷺ sent ʿAlī — or Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ, in other reports — to see whether Quraysh were riding their camels and leading their horses, which would mean they were going home. They were; Ṣafwān b. Umayyah had talked them out of raiding the city.[^s4.221]

## Burial and return

The Prophet ﷺ went looking for Ḥamzah and wept when he found him mutilated.[^s4.222] He forbade reprisals in kind: when some vowed to mutilate the Meccan dead if they ever had the chance, the closing verses of al-Naḥl were revealed, and he said, *"We will be patient and not retaliate."*[^s4.223] Ḥamzah's sister [[Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]] came to see him and bore it with patience.[^s4.224]

The martyrs were buried where they fell, in their blood and clothes, two or three to a grave, with the one who knew most Qurʾān placed first.[^s4.225] Some families had carried their dead back to Medina, and the Prophet ﷺ had them brought back to Uḥud.[^s4.226] Muṣʿab's cloak was too short to cover him; when they covered his head his feet showed, so the Prophet ﷺ had his feet covered with grass.[^s4.226][^s4.227]

Riding back to Medina, the Prophet ﷺ was met by women asking for news of their families.[^s4.227][^s4.228] That evening he heard the women of the Anṣār weeping for Ḥamzah, praised them for their sympathy, and sent them home.[^s4.230] He then called the Muslims out again in pursuit of Quraysh, to [[Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad|Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad]].[^s4.308]

## Date and numbers

The battle took place in Shawwāl of the year 3, as most authorities agree. Ibn Isḥāq places the fighting on a Saturday in the middle of the month; other reports give the 11th, 9th, 8th or 7th of Shawwāl.[^s4.242] The majority give the Muslim army as seven hundred after Ibn Ubayy's withdrawal, though one report from Ibn Shihāb says about four hundred.[^s4.249] The number of Muslim dead is most often given as seventy; another report gives sixty-four of the Anṣār and six of the Muhājirūn, al-Shāfiʿī gives seventy-two, and one list of names reaches ninety-six.[^s4.256]
