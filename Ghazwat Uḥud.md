# Ghazwat Uḥud (غزوة أحد)
*The Battle of Uḥud — The Severe Crucible, The Heroic Defense, and the Martyrdom of the Elect*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Master Structure
> This featured master entry is derived exclusively from ***[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād|Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād fī Sīrat Khayr al-ʿIbād]]*** by **[[al-Ṣāliḥī|Imām Shams al-Dīn Muḥammad b. Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī al-Shāmī]]** (d. 942 AH), Volume 4, *al-Bāb al-Thālith ʿAshar fī Ghazwat Uḥud* (pp. 182–257). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the primary narrations of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[Mūsā b. ʿUqbah]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], [[al-Bukhārī]], [[Muslim]], [[al-Bayhaqī]], and the classical *maghāzī* authorities.

---

## 1. Historical Causes & Meccan War Finance

Following their catastrophic defeat at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** (17 Ramaḍān 2 AH), the surviving remnants of [[Quraysh]] retreated to [[Makkah al-Mukarramah]] burning with desire for vengeance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182). **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** had safely conducted the great caravan into Makkah and secured its entire cargo in Dār al-Nadwah.

A delegation of Qurayshite nobles whose fathers, sons, and brothers had fallen at Badr — including ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl, Ṣafwān b. Umayyah, ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Rabīʿah, and al-Ḥārith b. Hishām — approached Abū Sufyān and the merchant-investors:
> *"O people of Quraysh! Muḥammad has bereaved you and slaughtered the flower of your nobility. Aid us with the profits of this caravan to wage war against him, that we may exact our vengeance for those we lost!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182).

The caravan had consisted of **1,000 camels** carrying merchandise valued at **50,000 dinars**. The investors retained their original principal capital and consecrated their entire net profit — **25,000 dinars** (a 1:1 return) — into a dedicated war fund to finance an overwhelming punitive campaign (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182). Regarding this massive financial mobilization, Allāh revealed:
$$\text{إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمْ لِيَصُدُّوا عَن سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ ۚ فَسَيُنفِقُونَهَا ثُمَّ تَكُونُ عَلَيْهِمْ حَسْرَةً ثُمَّ يُغْلَبُونَ}$$
> *"Indeed, those who disbelieve spend their wealth to avert people from the path of Allāh. They will spend it; then it will be for them a regret, and then they will be overcome..."* (Qurʾān 8:36; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182).

---

## 2. Mobilization of the Confederate Coalition

Quraysh mobilized an expeditionary force of **3,000 warriors**, assembling their core clans alongside allied Bedouin confederates of Kinānah and the Ahabīsh mercenaries (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182). 
- **Armament & Cavalry**: The army fielded **700 men in coats of mail** (*darʿ*) and **200 warhorses** (*faras*), supplemented by **3,000 transport camels** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182).
- **The Women's Encampment**: To preclude any possibility of retreat, fifteen Qurashī noblewomen accompanied the army on camel-litters (*al-ẓuʿun*), led by **Hind bint ʿUtbah** (wife of Abū Sufyān), Sulāfah bint Saʿd, and ʿAmrah bint ʿAlqamah. They beat drums (*al-dufūf*) and wailed elegies across the march, chanting verses of defiance:
  > *«إِنْ تُقْبِلُوا نُعَانِقْ … وَنَفْرِشِ النَّمَارِقْ … أَوْ تُدْبِرُوا نُفَارِقْ … فِرَاقَ غَيْرِ وَامِقْ!»*
  > *"If you advance, we embrace you and spread soft carpets; but if you retreat, we depart—a parting without love!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:183).
- **The Commission of Waḥshī**: Jubayr b. Muṭʿim summoned his Abyssinian slave **[[Waḥshī b. Ḥarb]]**, renowned for his unerring javelin throw, promising him manumission: *"Go forth with the army; if you slay Ḥamzah, the uncle of Muḥammad, in revenge for my uncle Ṭuʿaymah b. ʿAdī, you are a free man"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:183).

## 3. Intelligence, Dreams & The Council of War (*al-Shūrā*)

### The Secret Letter of al-ʿAbbās ﵁
The Prophet's uncle **[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ dispatched a swift courier from Makkah carrying a sealed letter detailing the exact troop numbers, cavalry strength, and route of Quraysh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182). The messenger covered the distance in three days, reaching the Prophet ﷺ while he was at the Mosque of Qubāʾ. **[[Ubayy b. Kaʿb]]** ﵁ read the dispatch in secrecy to the Prophet ﷺ, who sworn him to silence and dispatched scouts to monitor the approaches to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:182).

### The Meccan Encampment
Departing Makkah, the Meccans reached the northern oases of Medina on Wednesday, 5 Shawwāl 3 AH, encamping at Mount ʿAynayn near the depression of Wādī Qanāt at the base of **[[Jabal Uḥud]]**, releasing their mounts to graze upon the cultivated crops of al-ʿUrāyḍ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:183). On Thursday night, armed detachments of **[[al-Aws]]** and **[[al-Khazraj]]**, commanded by **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁, **[[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]]** ﵁, and **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁, kept vigil over the Prophet's ﷺ residence (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:184).

### The Prophetic Vision (*al-Ruʾyā*)
On Friday eve, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ experienced a divine dream, recounting four symbolic visions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:184–185):
1. **Slaughtered Cattle** (*«رَأَيْتُ بَقَرًا تُذْبَحُ»*): Interpreted as a company of his noble Companions who would achieve martyrdom.
2. **The Notched Sword** (*«رَأَيْتُ فِي ذُبَابِ سَيْفِي ثَلْمًا»*): Interpreted as a wound to his own face, and the slaying of a prominent member of his household—**[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁.
3. **The Fortified Armor** (*«رَأَيْتُ أَنِّي فِي دِرْعٍ حَصِينَةٍ»*): Interpreted as the fortified domestic perimeter of **[[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah]]**.
4. **The Ram** (*«كَأَنِّي مُرْدِفٌ كَبْشًا»*): Interpreted as the slaying of the enemy's supreme standard-bearer and champion (*kabsh al-katībah*), Ṭalḥah b. Abī Ṭalḥah.

### The Debate: Inner Defense vs. Open Battle
Convening the consultative assembly (*al-shūrā*), the Prophet ﷺ advised remaining within Medina:
> *"Remain in Medina and leave them where they have halted. If they remain encamped, it is an unprofitable station; and if they enter upon us, we shall battle them through the alleyways, while women and children rain stones upon them from atop the fortress walls (*al-āṭām*)"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:185).

Senior Muhājirūn, elder Anṣār, and the hypocrite **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** concurred with this urban defense. However, younger companions who had missed Badr, led by enthusiastic veterans including **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁, **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁, and al-Nuʿmān b. Mālik ﵁, pleaded passionately:
> *"O Messenger of Allāh! We fear our enemy will think we stayed behind out of cowardice and weakness! At Badr you had three hundred and Allāh granted victory; lead us out into the open plain that they may not think us faint-hearted!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:185).

Following Friday prayer, the Prophet ﷺ entered his quarters accompanied by **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, who assisted him in donning his battle dress: two coats of mail (*al-Saʿdiyyah* over *Fiḍḍah*), his sword *Dhū al-Faqār*, helmet, and shield (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:186). Realizing they had imposed their preference upon the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, the Companions expressed profound contrition. The Prophet ﷺ emerged armed and declared:
> «مَا يَنْبَغِي لِنَبِيٍّ إِذَا لَبِسَ لَأْمَتَهُ أَنْ يَضَعَهَا حَتَّى يَحْكُمَ اللَّهُ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَ أَعْدَائِهِ!»
> *"It is not fitting for a Prophet, once he has donned his armor, to lay it aside until Allāh has judged between him and his enemies!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:186).

## 4. The March, Review at al-Shaykhayn & The Deserters

The Prophet ﷺ set forth riding his horse *al-Sakb*, preceded by Saʿd b. Muʿādh and Saʿd b. ʿUbādah running in full armor ahead of him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:186). He bound three standards:
- The Banner of the Muhājirūn: Entrusted to **[[Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr]]** ﵁ (and **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁).
- The Banner of al-Aws: Entrusted to **[[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]]** ﵁.
- The Banner of al-Khazraj: Entrusted to **[[al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:186).

### Review of the Youth at al-Shaykhayn
At the two towers of **al-Shaykhayn**, the army halted for inspection (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:187). The Prophet ﷺ turned back seventeen youths who were fourteen years of age, deeming them too young: **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar]]**, **[[Zayd b. Thābit]]**, **[[Usāmah b. Zayd]]**, **[[al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib]]**, Zayd b. Arqam, and others. However, when Rāfiʿ b. Khadīj was presented as an exceptional archer, he was permitted; hearing this, Samurah b. Jundub pleaded: *"I can wrestle Rāfiʿ to the ground!"* The Prophet ﷺ bade them wrestle, and when Samurah threw Rāfiʿ, he permitted both (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:187).

### The Defection of Ibn Ubayy at al-Shawṭ
At dawn, halting at **al-Shawṭ** between Medina and Uḥud, **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** executed his long-planned mutiny, withdrawing with **300 hypocrites**—one-third of the total force—complaining:
> *"He obeyed children and youth, and disobeyed me! Why should we slay ourselves here, O people?!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:188).

**[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām]]** ﵁ pursued them, shouting: *«Come, fight in the cause of Allāh or defend!»* They retorted cynically: *«Had we known there would be actual fighting, we would have followed you»* (Qurʾān 3:167; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:188). The desertion caused momentary panic among Banū Salimah (of Khazraj) and Banū Ḥārithah (of Aws), but divine grace steadied their hearts:
$$\text{إِذْ هَمَّت طَّائِفَتَانِ مِنكُمْ أَن تَفْشَلَا وَاللَّهُ وَلِيُّهُمَا ۗ وَعَلَى اللَّهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ}$$
> *"When two factions among you were about to lose heart, but Allāh was their protector; and upon Allāh let the believers rely."* (Qurʾān 3:122; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:188–189).

The loyal Muslim army was reduced to **700 resolute fighters** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:189). Guided skillfully by Abū Khaythamah through the stone-walled orchards of the Ḥarrah, they bypassed the Meccan pickets and secured their positions against the foothills of Uḥud without alerting the enemy.

## 5. Tactical Deployment & Injunction on Mount ʿAynayn

The Prophet ﷺ arrayed his battle lines with their backs firmly anchored against the rocky slopes of **[[Jabal Uḥud]]**, facing southward toward Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191). The sacred battle-cry (*shiʿār*) was decreed as: **«أَمِتْ أَمِتْ»** (*«Amit, amit!»* — *«Slay, slay!»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191).

### Stationing the Archers atop Mount ʿAynayn
Recognizing that the open southern depression along Wādī Qanāt exposed his left flank and rear to the 200 Meccan horsemen, the Prophet ﷺ stationed **fifty elite archers** atop the knoll of **[[Jabal al-Rumāh|Mount ʿAynayn (Jabal al-Rumāh)]]**, appointing the veteran **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr]]** ﵁ as their commander (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191, 205). He issued them the most definitive, unconditional military injunction in the Sīrah:
> «احْمُوا ظُهُورَنَا، فَإِنْ رَأَيْتُمُونَا نُقْتَلُ فَلَا تَنْصُرُونَا، وَإِنْ رَأَيْتُمُونَا غَنِمْنَا فَلَا تُشْرِكُونَا! وَإِنْ رَأَيْتُمُونَا تَخْطَفُنَا الطَّيْرُ فَلَا تَبْرَحُوا مِنْ مَكَانِكُمْ هَذَا حَتَّى أُرْسِلَ إِلَيْكُمْ!»
> *"Shield our backs! If you see us being slaughtered, do not come to our aid; and if you see us collecting spoils, do not join us! Even if you see vultures snatching our flesh from the air, do not abandon this post of yours until I send an emissary to you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205).

Opposite them, Quraysh deployed at al-Sabkhah:
- Supreme Command: **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]**.
- Right-Wing Cavalry: **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** (100 horsemen).
- Left-Wing Cavalry: ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl (100 horsemen).
- Infantry Center: Ṣafwān b. Umayyah.
- Hereditary Battle Standard: Committed to the heroic patrician house of **Banū ʿAbd al-Dār** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:191).

---

## 6. The Challenge of the Sword & The Red Band of Death

Before combat commenced, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ drew forth a formidable sword and called out:
> «مَنْ يَأْخُذُ مِنِّي هَذَا السَّيْفَ بِحَقِّهِ؟»
> *"Who will take this sword from me with its right?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:192).

Several illustrious Companions reached forward—including ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb, ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib, and al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām—yet the Prophet ﷺ withheld it. Then **[[Abū Dujānah]] (Simāk b. Kharashah)** ﵁ stepped forth and asked: *«What is its right, O Messenger of Allāh?»* The Prophet ﷺ answered:
> «أَنْ تَضْرِبَ بِهِ عَدُوَّ اللَّهِ حَتَّى يَنْحَنِيَ!»
> *"That you strike the enemy with it until it bends!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:192).

Abū Dujānah took the blade, produced his famed crimson headband known as the **Band of Death (*ʿiṣābat al-mawt*)**, tied it around his brow, and began strutting proudly with martial swagger between the two opposing hosts. Observing him, the Prophet ﷺ remarked:
> «إِنَّهَا لَمِشْيَةٌ يُبْغِضُهَا اللَّهُ إِلَّا فِي هَذَا الْمَوْطِنِ!»
> *"Indeed, it is a swagger that Allāh despises, except in a crucible such as this!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:192).

---

## 7. Opening Combats: ʿAlī & The Fall of the Standard-Bearers

The supreme champion of Quraysh and bearer of their Great Standard, **Ṭalḥah b. Abī Ṭalḥah** (*Kabsh al-Katībah*), rode forward on a camel between the lines, roaring a challenge to single combat.

### ʿAlī and the Slaying of Kabsh al-Katībah
**[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ dashed forward without hesitation, closing the distance and delivering a ferocious downward blow that severed Ṭalḥah's leg at the thigh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:194). As Ṭalḥah collapsed in agony, his garment parted, exposing his nakedness, and he cried out: *"I adjure you by Allāh and kinship, O cousin!"* Magnanimously turning his face, ʿAlī refrained from striking him again, leaving the wound to prove mortal. The Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims shouted *«Allāhu Akbar!»*, fulfilling the Prophetic dream of the slaughtered ram (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:194).

### The Annihilation of the Eleven Standard-Bearers of Banū ʿAbd al-Dār
One after another, eleven champions of Banū ʿAbd al-Dār sprang forward to raise the falling banner, only to be cut down by the Muslim vanguard (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:194–195):
1. **Abū Shaybah ʿUthmān b. Abī Ṭalḥah**: Cleaved through the shoulder to the waist by **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁.
2. **Abū Saʿd b. Abī Ṭalḥah**: Shot fatally through the throat by **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁.
3. **Musāfiʿ b. Ṭalḥah**: Pierced through the chest by an arrow from **[[ʿĀṣim b. Thābit]]** ﵁.
4. **Al-Ḥārith b. Ṭalḥah**: Shot down by a second arrow from **[[ʿĀṣim b. Thābit]]** ﵁.
5. **Kilāb b. Ṭalḥah**: Cut down by **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁.
6. **Al-Julās b. Ṭalḥah**: Struck down by **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁.
7. **Arṭāh b. Shuraḥbīl**: Slayn by **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁.
8. **Shurayḥ b. Qāriẓ**: Felled in the general melee.
9. **Abū Zayd b. ʿUmayr**: Slayn by Qizmān.
10. **Qāsiṭ b. Shuraḥbīl**: Cut down by Qizmān.
11. **Ṣuʾāb (their Abyssinian slave)**: Rushed to lift the standard; when his right arm was severed, he gripped the banner with his left; when that was cut off, he clasped it against his chest with his stumps until martyred (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

The Meccan standard crashed to the earth and lay trampled in the mud until lifted briefly by a woman, ʿAmrah bint ʿAlqamah al-Ḥārithiyyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

---

## 8. Initial Triumph & The Rout of Quraysh

Following the destruction of the standard-bearers, the Muslim assault struck with crushing power. **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ fought with two swords like an unchained torrent; **[[Abū Dujānah]]** ﵁ cut his way straight through the Meccan center, slaying every foe until he stood over a figure urging the enemy on with screeching cries; raising his sword to strike, the person screamed, revealing herself to be Hind bint ʿUtbah. Abū Dujānah stayed his blade, remarking later: *«I honored the sword of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ too greatly to strike a woman with it!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:192).

The polytheist infantry shattered into complete disarray. The Meccans abandoned their encampment, casting aside their armor, baggage, and tents, fleeing frantically across the plain while the noblewomen fled on foot clutching their robes (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195). The Muslim frontline warriors swept through the vacant camp, collecting the vast spoils of war (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

---

## 9. The Disaster on Mount ʿAynayn & Khālid's Pincer Maneuver

From their vantage point atop **[[Jabal al-Rumāh]]**, the archers saw the enemy running in panic and their comrades gathering the spoils. The cry rippled through their ranks:
> *«O people! The spoils! Your companions are victorious; what keeps us here?!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

Their commander, **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr]]** ﵁, pleaded with all his might, reciting the Prophet's ﷺ absolute command:
> *«Have you forgotten what the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ said to you?! By Allāh, I shall not leave this post until the Messenger sends for me!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

Tragically, **forty archers** succumbed to the temptation of spoils and deserted their station, leaving Ibn Jubayr with fewer than **ten steadfast men** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

Observing the knoll virtually undefended, **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** seized the tactical moment with brilliant military precision. Wheel-charging his 100 cavalry around the base of the mountain and joined by ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl, Khālid charged up the reverse slope of Mount ʿAynayn, surrounded and martyred **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Jubayr]]** ﵁ and his tiny band, and crashed directly into the exposed, undefended rear of the scattered Muslim army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:195).

Raising their war-cry (*«Yā li-l-ʿUzzā! Yā li-Hubal!»*), the Meccan cavalry trampled the scattered Muslims from behind while the fleeing Qurayshite infantry rallied and struck from the front. Caught in an inescapable pincer encirclement, dust clouds obscured vision, lines disintegrated, and Muslims in their confusion began striking one another. In this terrifying melee, **Iblīs** ☠ shrieked with a piercing voice across the battlefield:
> **«أَلَا إِنَّ مُحَمَّدًا قَدْ قُتِلَ!»**
> *"Behold! Indeed, Muḥammad has been killed!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:196, 249).

## 10. Defense of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ

The rumor of the Prophet's death paralyzed the army: one-third broke and fled toward Medina, one-third fell wounded or martyred, and a small elite core coalesced around the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, forming an impenetrable human shield (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:196).

### The Heroic Stand of the Inner Guard
- **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]] ﵁**: Stood directly before the Prophet ﷺ, intercepting arrows with his bare hand and deflecting sword-blows with his body until his fingers were severed, leaving his hand permanently paralyzed (*kullat yaduh*). Sustaining over seventy wounds, he was hailed by the Prophet ﷺ as *"a living martyr walking upon the face of the earth"* (*al-shahīd al-ḥayy*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:199–200).
- **[[Abū Dujānah]] ﵁**: Bent his broad torso over the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ like a protective vault (*turs*), absorbing dozens of arrows into his back without shifting an inch until the storm passed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:200).
- **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]] ﵁**: An unparalleled archer, the Prophet ﷺ emptied his personal leather quiver before him, commanding:
  > «ارْمِ سَعْدُ! فِدَاكَ أَبِي وَأُمِّي!»
  > *"Shoot, Saʿd! May my father and mother be ransomed for you!"*
  Saʿd loosed nearly a thousand arrows that day, striking every target with lethal accuracy (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:200–201, 245).
- **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]] ﵁**: When two iron links of the Prophet's helmet circlets (*ḥalaqatā al-mighfar*) penetrated deeply into his cheek, Abū ʿUbaydah refused to use forceps lest he inflict agony; biting down on the rings with his front incisors, he pulled them free with immense force, losing his two front teeth (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:199–200).
- **[[Nusaybah bint Kaʿb]] (Umm ʿUmārah) ﵂**: Dropped her waterskin, took up a sword and bow, and fought fiercely against oncoming horsemen, sustaining twelve wounds, including a deep gash on her shoulder (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:202).
- **[[Mālik b. Sinān]] ﵁**: The father of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī bent down, sucked the holy arterial blood flowing from the Prophet's wounded cheek, and swallowed it. When asked why he did not spit it out, he replied: *"I shall never spit out the blood of the Messenger of Allāh!"* The Prophet ﷺ remarked: *«Whoever's blood has touched my blood, the Fire shall not touch him!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:200; *al-Iṣābah*, 5:538).

### The Wounds of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ
In the ferocious assault:
- ʿUtbah b. Abī Waqqāṣ hurled stones that chipped the Prophet's lower right tooth (*rubāʿiyyah*) and split his lower lip (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:198).
- ʿAbdullāh b. Shihāb al-Zuhrī struck his forehead.
- ʿAbdullāh b. Qamiʾah delivered a devastating sword blow to his cheek, shouting: *"Take this! I am Ibn Qamiʾah!"* driving the helmet links into his cheekbone and knocking the Prophet ﷺ into one of the pits covertly dug by Abū ʿĀmir al-Fāsiq (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:199).

Climbing out with the aid of Ṭalḥah, blood streaming down his face and beard, the Prophet ﷺ wiped the blood away, supplicating with divine mercy:
> «اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِقَوْمِي فَإِنَّهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ»
> *"O Allāh, forgive my people, for they do not know!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:198).

## 11. Divine Interventions: Angels & Miraculous Slumber

### The Archangels Fighting at Uḥud
Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ ﵁ narrated in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* and *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*:
> *"On the day of Uḥud, I saw upon the right of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and upon his left two men clad in white robes, fighting with the fiercest valor—men I had never seen before nor ever saw again!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205).
These were the Archangels **Jibrīl ﵊** and **Mīkāʾīl ﵊**, who descended to shield the Prophet ﷺ when the frontline collapsed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:205).

### The Miraculous Slumber (*al-Nuʿās*)
As terror threatened to overwhelm the isolated believers, Allāh cast upon the steadfast core in the ravine a miraculous, gentle slumber (*al-nuʿās*), dispelling panic and instilling supernatural tranquility. Abū Ṭalḥah ﵁ recounted:
> *"I was among those overtaken by slumber on the day of Uḥud, until my sword fell from my hand multiple times: it would drop, I would take it, it would drop again, and I would seize it!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:204).
This miraculous respite is memorialized in the Qurʾān:
$$\text{ثُمَّ أَنزَلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّن بَعْدِ الْغَمِّ أَمَنَةً نُّعَاسًا يَغْشَىٰ طَائِفَةً مِّنكُمْ}$$
> *"Then after grief, He sent down upon you security: a drowsiness overcoming a faction of you..."* (Qurʾān 3:154; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:204).

## 12. Slaying of the Arch-Enemy Ubayy b. Khalaf

As the Prophet ﷺ was assisted up toward the rocky ravine (*al-shiʿb*) of Mount Uḥud, the Qurayshite nobleman **[[Ubayy b. Khalaf]]** ☠ rode forward on his stallion *al-ʿAwd*, clad in heavy mail, bellowing:
> *"Where is Muḥammad?! May I not survive if he survives!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:208).

When Companions leaped forward to intercept him, the Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Leave him alone!»* Taking a short javelin (*ḥarbah*) from **[[al-Ḥārith b. al-Ṣimmah]]** ﵁, the Prophet ﷺ shook himself with such overwhelming vigor that the Companions scattered from around him like flies off a camel's back (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:208; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:673).

Advancing toward Ubayy, the Prophet ﷺ detected a tiny chink in his armor between his helmet and breastplate at the collarbone (*al-tarquwah*), and thrust the javelin into it. Ubayy tumbled repeatedly from his steed, bellowing like an ox. His fellow polytheists carried him away, assuring him: *"It is but a minor flesh wound!"* But Ubayy screamed in horror:
> *"By al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā, if this wound were distributed among all the inhabitants of Dhū al-Majāz, it would slaughter them all! He told me in Makkah: 'I shall kill you,' and by Allāh, had he but spat upon me he would have slain me!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:208).

Ubayy died in agonizing delirium on the return march at Sarif. He was the **only human being ever slain directly by the hand of the Prophet ﷺ** across his entire life (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:208).

## 13. Martyrdoms of the Elect: Profiles of Sacrifice

The battle witnessed the martyrdom of **seventy noble companions** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:256):

### 1. Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib ﵁ (*Sayyid al-Shuhadāʾ*)
Fighting heroically with two blades, the Prophet's uncle felled champions including Sibāʿ b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā. Lurking behind a rock, **[[Waḥshī b. Ḥarb]]** hurled his Abyssinian spear, striking Ḥamzah in the lower abdomen until it emerged between his legs (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:216–217). Hind bint ʿUtbah desecrated his corpse, tearing out his liver and attempting to chew it, and severing his ears and nose to fashion grotesque jewelry (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:218, 220).

### 2. Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr ﵁ (The Supreme Standard-Bearer)
Carrying the Great White Banner, **[[Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr]]** ﵁ defended his post against Ibn Qamiʾah. When his right hand was severed, he seized the banner with his left; when that was cut off, he clutched the staff to his chest with his bloody stumps, chanting: *«وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِن قَبْلِهِ الرُّسُلُ»* until run through with a lance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:219). Because Muṣʿab resembled the Prophet ﷺ in stature, Ibn Qamiʾah mistakenly believed he had killed the Messenger of Allāh.

### 3. Anas b. al-Naḍr ﵁ (The Aroma of Paradise)
Encountering Companions sitting in despair, **[[Anas b. al-Naḍr]]** ﵁ rebuked them: *«What will you do with life after him?! Arise and die upon that which the Messenger of Allāh died!»* Charging into the fray, he shouted to **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁: *«O Saʿd! The aroma of Paradise! By the Lord of the Kaʿbah, I scent the fragrance of Paradise from beyond Uḥud!»* He fought until martyred; over eighty wounds (sword cuts, spear thrusts, arrow wounds) were found on his corpse, identifiable only by his sister through his fingertips (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:215). Allāh revealed regarding him:
$$\text{مِّنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ رِجَالٌ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوا اللَّهَ عَلَيْهِ ۖ فَمِنْهُم مَّن قَضَىٰ نَحْبَهُ}$$
> *"Among the believers are men true to what they promised Allāh. Among them is he who has fulfilled his vow..."* (Qurʾān 33:23; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:215).

### 4. Ḥanẓalah b. Abī ʿĀmir ﵁ (*Ghasīl al-Malāʾikah*)
Married on the eve of battle to Jamīlah bint ʿAbdillāh b. Ubayy, **[[Ḥanẓalah b. Abī ʿĀmir]]** ﵁ rushed forth in a state of ritual impurity (*janābah*) upon hearing the mobilization cry (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:213). He was on the verge of slaying Abū Sufyān when Shaddād b. al-Aswad speared him from behind. The Prophet ﷺ announced: *«Indeed, I saw the angels washing Ḥanẓalah between heaven and earth with rainwater in silver basins!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:213).

### 5. ʿAmr b. al-Jamūḥ ﵁ & ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām ﵁
**[[ʿAmr b. al-Jamūḥ]]** ﵁, severely lame, insisted on entering combat, swearing: *«By Allāh, I hope to tread upon the soil of Paradise with this lame foot of mine!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:214). His brother-in-law **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām]]** ﵁ was the first Muslim to fall in the battle (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:215). The Prophet ﷺ ordered them buried in a single grave, saying: *«Bury these two together, for they were devoted companions in this world!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225).

### 6. Saʿd b. al-Rabīʿ ﵁ (The Final Testament)
Dispatched to search the battlefield, Zayd b. Thābit ﵁ discovered **[[Saʿd b. al-Rabīʿ]]** ﵁ breathing his last with seventy mortal wounds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:222). Saʿd whispered his final testament:
> *"Convey my greetings of peace to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and say to him: 'Saʿd says: May Allāh reward you on our behalf better than any Prophet has been rewarded on behalf of his nation.' And convey my greetings to your people among the Anṣār, and tell them: 'You will have no excuse before Allāh on the Day of Resurrection if any harm reaches your Prophet while a single eye among you yet blinks!'"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:222).

### 7. Al-Uṣayrim (ʿAmr b. Thābit) ﵁: Paradise without Prayer
**[[al-Uṣayrim (ʿAmr b. Thābit)]]** ﵁ of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal had long rejected Islam. On the morning of Uḥud, faith entered his heart; he took his sword, rushed into the battle without having performed a single formal prayer, and fought until mortally wounded (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:212; *al-Iṣābah*, 4:501). When Companions found him, he testified to his Islam. The Prophet ﷺ declared: *«He did little, yet was rewarded immensely; he has entered Paradise!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:212).

### 8. Ḥusīl b. Jābir (al-Yamān) ﵁: The Tragic Melee
**[[Ḥusīl b. Jābir (al-Yamān)]]** ﵁ (father of Ḥudhayfah b. al-Yamān) was an elderly companion who entered the battle late. In the swirling dust and chaos, Muslims mistook him for a polytheist and struck him down. Ḥudhayfah cried out: *"My father! My father!"* but he was martyred before they recognized him. Ḥudhayfah magnanimously forgave the believers: *«May Allāh forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of the merciful»*, refusing to accept the blood-money offered by the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:211; *al-Iṣābah*, 2:66).

### 9. Mukhayrīq ﵁: The Righteous Rabbi
**[[Mukhayrīq]]** ﵁, a learned Jewish rabbi of Medina, recognized the Prophet's ﷺ signs on Saturday morning and called his people to battle. Taking his weapons, he declared: *"If I am slain, my wealth belongs to Muḥammad to dispose of as Allāh guides him."* He fought until martyred. The Prophet ﷺ accepted his seven properties as the foundation of his charitable endowments (*awqāf*) and proclaimed: *«Mukhayrīq is the best of the Jews!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:211; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:47).

## 14. Regrouping in the Ravine & Dialogue of Defiance

The surviving Muslims consolidated their positions in the high ravine (*al-shiʿb*) of **[[Jabal Uḥud]]**. When the Prophet ﷺ attempted to mount a massive boulder to survey the enemy, the weight of his double coats of mail and severe wounds prevented him; **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁ knelt beneath him, hoisting the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ upon his back until he stood atop the rock, prompting the Prophet ﷺ to declare: *«Awjaba Ṭalḥah!»* (*«Ṭalḥah has made Paradise obligatory for himself!»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:210).

When a cavalry squadron led by Khālid b. al-Walīd attempted to scale the crest above the ravine, the Prophet ﷺ supplicated: *«O Allāh, it is not fitting that they should be above us!»* **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ and a squad of Muhājirūn drove them back down with volleys of stones and arrows (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:210–211).

### The Dialogue between Abū Sufyān and ʿUmar ﵁
Before sounding the retreat, **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** ascended a nearby outcrop and called out across the ravine (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:220):
- *«Is Muḥammad among the people?»* — The Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Do not answer him.»*
- *«Is Ibn Abī Quḥāfah [Abū Bakr] among the people?»* — The Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Do not answer him.»*
- *«Is Ibn al-Khaṭṭāb among the people?»* — The Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Do not answer him.»*

Abū Sufyān boasted: *«As for these, they have been slain; had they been alive they would have answered!»* Unable to contain his outrage, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ shouted back:
> *"You lie, O enemy of Allāh! Those you named are all alive, and Allāh has preserved what will bring you grief!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:220).

Abū Sufyān chanted: **«اعْلُ هُبَلْ!»** (*«Exalt Hubal!»*).  
The Prophet ﷺ instructed: *«Arise and answer him!»*  
ʿUmar cried out: **«اللَّهُ أَعْلَى وَأَجَلُّ!»** (*«Allāh is Higher and Greater!»*).  
Abū Sufyān shouted: *«We have al-ʿUzzā, and you have no ʿUzzā!»*  
The Prophet ﷺ commanded: *«Say: Allāh is our Protector, and you have no protector!»* (**«اللَّهُ مَوْلَانَا وَلَا مَوْلَى لَكُمْ»**) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:220).

Abū Sufyān concluded: *«A day for the day of Badr; warfare is alternate!»* and issued a challenge: *"Our appointment with you is Badr next year!"* The Prophet ﷺ bade a Companion answer: *«Yes, it is an appointment between us!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:220).

## 15. Burial of the Martyrs & Return to Medina

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ wept bitterly over the mutilated body of his uncle Ḥamzah ﵁, draping his striped mantle over his face while grass (*al-idhkhir*) was placed over his exposed feet (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:224–225). He decreed an absolute Islamic prohibition against wartime mutilation (*al-muthlah*) forever (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:224).

The Prophet ﷺ ordered that all seventy martyrs be interred directly upon the field of battle where they fell, prohibiting their transfer to the cemeteries of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225–226):
> «ادْفِنُوهُمْ بِدِمَائِهِمْ وَثِيَابِهِمْ، فَإِنَّهُ لَيْسَ جَرِيحٌ يُجْرَحُ فِي اللَّهِ إِلَّا جَاءَ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ يَدْمَى، لَوْنُهُ لَوْنُ الدَّمِ، وَرِيحُهُ رِيحُ الْمِسْكِ!»
> *"Bury them in their blood and garments! For there is no wound suffered in the cause of Allāh except that on the Day of Resurrection it bleeds afresh: its color is the color of blood, and its fragrance is the fragrance of musk!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225).

He paired two and three martyrs in a single grave, inquiring: *«Which of them held more of the Qurʾān?»* and placing the one with greater memorization first into the burial niche (*al-laḥd*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225). He offered no funeral prayers over them and did not wash them, as martyrs are living with their Lord (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:225, 248).

Returning to Medina on Saturday evening, the women of the Anṣār were weeping for their fallen men. Hearing lamentation from every house except Ḥamzah's, the Prophet ﷺ remarked sorrowfully: *«But Ḥamzah has no weeping women»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:228). The Anṣār immediately instructed their households to mourn Ḥamzah before weeping for their own kin (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:228).

At dawn the following morning, despite excruciating wounds and exhaustion, the Prophet ﷺ summoned the veterans of Uḥud to reassemble for **[[Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad]]** (8 Shawwāl 3 AH) to pursue Quraysh and demonstrate decisive military resilience (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:308).
