# al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah (المدينة المنورة)
*The Illuminated City — The Capital of Prophethood and Haven of the Faithful*

## 1. Topography & Sanctification as a Divine Sanctuary (al-Ḥaram)
Al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah is an oasis city situated upon a fertile volcanic plateau in the Ḥijāz, naturally fortified by its two extensive black basalt lava fields (*al-Lābatayn*): *Lābat Wāqim* to the east and *Lābat al-Wabarah* to the west (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:318).

Just as Prophet Ibrāhīm ﵊ declared Makkah a sacred sanctuary, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ established Medina as an inviolable sanctuary (*Ḥaram*):
> «إِنِّي حَرَّمْتُ الْمَدِينَةَ مَا بَيْنَ لَابَتَيْهَا، لَا يُقْطَعُ عِضَاهُهَا وَلَا يُقْتَلُ صَيْدُهَا»
> *"Indeed, I have made Medina a sacred sanctuary between its two lava fields: its thorny trees shall not be lopped, and its game shall not be hunted"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:318).

Its sacred boundaries extend longitudinally between Mount ʿAyr in the south to Mount Thawr in the north, within which weapons of war may not be borne, blood may not be shed, and stray lost property may only be picked up by one publicly announcing it (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:318–319).

## 2. Prophetic Renaming & Repudiation of Yathrib
Prior to the Hijrah, the settlement was called **Yathrib** (يثرب). Upon his arrival, the Prophet ﷺ repudiated this ancient name due to its linguistic associations with blame (*tathrīb*) or corruption, renaming the city **al-Madīnah** (The City), **Ṭaybah**, and **Ṭābah** (The Pure and Wholesome) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:286–296).

The Prophet ﷺ invoked divine blessing upon the city:
> «اللَّهُمَّ حَبِّبْ إِلَيْنَا الْمَدِينَةَ كَحُبِّنَا مَكَّةَ أَوْ أَشَدَّ... وَبَارِكْ لَنَا فِي صَاعِنَا وَفِي مُدِّنَا...»
> *"O Allāh! Endear Medina to us as our love for Makkah or even more... and bless for us our measuring vessel [Ṣāʿ] and our grain-measure [Mudd]..."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:297).

He affirmed its spiritual purity, stating: «هَذِهِ طَابَةُ... تَنْفِي خَبَثَ أَهْلِهَا كَمَا يَنْفِي الْكِيرُ خَبَثَ الْحَدِيدِ» (*"This is Ṭābah... it purges its filth just as the bellows purges the dross of iron"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:469).

## 3. Capital of the Islamic Commonwealth
Following **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]** in 1 AH, the city underwent a total social and constitutional transformation:
- **[[Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]**: The construction of the Prophet's Mosque and his dwellings upon the vacant land purchased from two orphaned youths of Banū al-Najjār (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:321–325).
- **[[al-Muʾākhāh]]**: The formal fraternal pact established in the home of **[[Anas b. Mālik]]** ﵁ between ninety companions — half Muhājirūn and half Anṣār — binding them in mutual financial solidarity and brotherhood (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:358–360).
- **[[Dustūr al-Madīnah]]**: The comprehensive civic covenant (*kitāb*) promulgated by the Prophet ﷺ uniting the Muhājirūn, **[[al-Aws]]**, **[[al-Khazraj]]**, and the Jewish tribes of Medina into a single commonwealth (*ummah wāḥidah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:384–390).
- **The Central Necropolis**: The sanctification of **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** as the resting place of thousands of the Ṣaḥābah, alongside the final tomb of the Prophet ﷺ and his two successors **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ inside his apartment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:279, 322; 12:44).
