# Khilāfat ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān (خلافة عثمان بن عفان)
*The Era of Dhū al-Nūrayn: Standardisation of the Qurʾānic Codex, Transcontinental Naval Expeditions, and the Trial of the Siege (24–35 AH)*

## 1. Accession & The Council of Six (Shūrā)
Following the mortal wounding of **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ in Dhū al-Ḥijjah 23 AH, ʿUmar restricted the consultation for the succession to six Companions whom the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had passed away well-pleased with: **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]**, **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]**, **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]**, **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]**, **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]**, and **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275). 

**[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁ voluntarily withdrew his own candidacy to preside over the deliberations. For three consecutive nights, he interviewed the senior Emigrants (*al-Muhājirūn*), Helpers (*al-Anṣār*), and commanders of the garrison towns. Discovering a firm public consensus favoring ʿUthmān, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān took ʿUthmān by the hand at the dawn of the New Year—the first day of Muḥarram 24 AH—in al-Masjid al-Nabawī and swore allegiance to him, followed immediately by ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib and the congregation of believers (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:277). His caliphate lasted twelve years less several nights (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:277).

---

## 2. Conquests & Naval Expeditions
The twelve-year reign of ʿUthmān witnessed massive territorial expansion, pushing Islamic sovereignty deep into North Africa, Central Asia, and the Mediterranean:
- **24 AH**: Conquest of Rayy (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **25 AH**: Conquest of Armenia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **26 AH**: Recapture and stabilization of Alexandria for a second time, and the opening of al-Qayrawān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **27 AH**: Opening of North Africa (*Ifrīqiyyah*) and the western regions (*al-Maghrib*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **28 AH**: Conquest of Iṣṭakhr (Persepolis) and its adjacent territories (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **29 AH**: Consolidation and reconquest of Fāris (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **30 AH**: Launch of the great maritime campaign (*Ghazwat al-Baḥr*) and extensive openings across the western seaboard (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **31 AH**: Opening of Sicily (*Ṣiqilliyyah*) and surrounding islands (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **32 AH**: Conquest of Cyprus (*Qubruṣ*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **33 AH**: Expeditions extending toward the Iberian peninsula (*al-Andalus*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **34 AH**: The battle of Dhū Ḥasab and the subjugation of Khurāsān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- **35 AH**: Expansive openings in Sind, the western lands, and the frontiers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).

ʿUthmān presided over the annual pilgrimage (*Ḥajj*) in person for ten consecutive years (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:284).

## 3. The Standardization of the Qurʾānic Codex (Jamʿ al-Qurʾān)
Among ʿUthmān's paramount religious achievements was the unification of the Muslim community upon a single orthographic codex:
- During the campaigns in Armenia and Azerbaijan, **[[Ḥudhayfah b. al-Yamān]]** ﵁ observed alarming disputes over Qurʾānic recitation among troops from Iraq and the Levant.
- Ḥudhayfah hastened to Medina and appealed: *“O Amīr al-Muʾminīn! Rescue this nation before they differ over the Scripture as the Jews and Christians differed!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- ʿUthmān requested the original parchment sheets gathered under Abū Bakr from **[[Ḥafṣah bint ʿUmar]]** ﵂. He commissioned an expert editorial committee comprising **[[Zayd b. Thābit]]**, ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr, Saʿīd b. al-ʿĀṣ, and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. al-Ḥārith b. Hishām (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:282).
- The committee transcribed standardized copies in the dialect of Quraysh (*al-Maṣāḥif al-ʿUthmāniyyah*). Authoritative copies were dispatched to Kūfah, Baṣrah, Damascus, and Makkah, while the master copy remained in Medina (*al-Muṣḥaf al-Imām*), and all variant non-standard manuscripts were ordered burned (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).
- Hearing criticism of this step, **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ declared:
  > «لَوْ لَمْ يَصْنَعْهُ هُوَ لَصَنَعْتُهُ!»
  > *"Had he not done it, I would have done it myself!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).

## 4. Expansion of al-Masjid al-Nabawī & Civic Works
- In 29 AH, ʿUthmān undertook the monumental expansion of **[[Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī|al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]**, widening its perimeter, constructing walls of carved stone and mortar, erecting pillars of decorated stone reinforced with iron and lead, and roofing the sanctum with Indian teak wood (*sāj*).
- He maintained generous stipends, instituted a special financial allowance for newly born infants (such as granting fifty dirhams to an infant and raising it to one hundred after one year) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285), and emancipated an enslaved person every Friday (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285).

## 5. The Sedition (Fitnah) & The Siege of the House (Yawm al-Dār)
During the latter years of his caliphate, dissident factions and agitators from the garrison cities of Kūfah, Baṣrah, and Egypt gathered under false grievances. In Shawwāl and Dhū al-Qaʿdah 35 AH, mutineers converged upon Medina under the pretext of pilgrimage and surrounded ʿUthmān's personal residence (*al-Dār*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:148; 11:283).

### ʿUthmān's Steadfast Refusal of Violence
- The senior Companions and their sons, including **[[al-Ḥasan b. ʿAlī]]**, **[[al-Ḥusayn b. ʿAlī]]**, ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr, and ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar, took up guard at the gates of his residence to defend him.
- ʿUthmān strictly adjured them by Allāh to sheath their swords, refusing to let a single drop of Muslim blood be spilled on his protection (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379).
- When the rebels demanded that he abdicate the Caliphate, he recalled the solemn counsel of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ:
  > «يَا عُثْمَانُ، إِنَّكَ سَتَبُوءُ بِالْخِلَافَةِ مِنْ بَعْدِي، وَسَيُرِيدُكَ الْمُنَافِقُونَ عَلَى خَلْعِهَا فَلَا تَخْلَعْهَا!»
  > *"O ʿUthmān, you will take charge of the Caliphate after me, and the hypocrites will seek that you strip off this garment; do not strip it off!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:281).
- The rebels cut off the supply of sweet water to the residence—despite ʿUthmān having purchased and endowed **[[Biʾr Rūmah]]** for the public welfare (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:148; 11:280).

### Martyrdom (18 Dhū al-Ḥijjah 35 AH)
- On Friday morning, 18 Dhū al-Ḥijjah 35 AH, ʿUthmān was fasting. Mutineers scaled the perimeter walls from an adjoining house and rushed into his quarters (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:284).
- As he sat reciting the Holy Qurʾān from the *Muṣḥaf*, the assassins struck him. His loyal wife **Nāʾilah bint al-Farāfiṣah** threw herself over him to shield his body, having her fingers severed in the assault (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:284).
- ʿUthmān was struck down, his blood spilling across the opened page of the Qurʾān upon the verse:
  > «فَسَيَكْفِيكَهُمُ اللَّهُ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ»
  > *«So Allāh will suffice you against them, and He is the Hearing, the Knowing»* (al-Baqarah 2:137) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:148).
- He was martyred at eighty-two years of age (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379). He was buried on Saturday night in **Ḥushsh Kawkab** in **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379).
