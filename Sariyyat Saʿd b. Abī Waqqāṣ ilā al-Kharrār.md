# Sariyyat Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ ilā al-Kharrār (سرية سعد بن أبي وقاص إلى الخرار)
*The Expedition of Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ to al-Kharrār — The Disciplined Foot Reconnaissance and Strict Obedience to Prophetic Orders*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 6, *al-Bāb al-Sādis fī Sariyyat Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ rḍy Allāh taʿālā ʿanhu... ilā al-Kharrār fī Dhī al-Qaʿdah ʿalā raʾs tisʿat ashhur min al-Hijrah* (p. 15), cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah*, 3:62.

---

## 1. Context & Prophetic Instructions

In the month of Dhū al-Qaʿdah in the year one AH, at the head of nine months following the Hijrah, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commissioned **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁ to lead a patrol of twenty warriors drawn exclusively from **[[al-Muhājirūn]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).

The Prophet ﷺ tied for him a white military standard (*liwāʾ abyaḍ*), which was entrusted to the newly arrived hero **[[al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ gave Saʿd a strict strategic covenant (*ʿahida ilayhi*):
> «أَلَّا يُجَاوِزَ الْخَرَّارَ»
> *"That he must never advance beyond al-Kharrār!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).

---

## 2. The March of the Foot Soldiers

The detachment set out from Medina completely on foot (*kharajū ʿalā aqdāmihim*), possessing no riding mounts (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15). 

To avoid detection by Bedouin scouts or enemy lookouts, Saʿd employed textbook counter-reconnaissance tactics (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15):
- They marched under cover of night (*yasīrūna al-layl*).
- They concealed themselves in ravines and desert depressions throughout the daytime heat (*yakmunūna al-nahār*).

They continued this rigorous march until dawn on the fifth morning (*ṣabbaḥū ṣubḥa khams*), reaching **al-Kharrār** (*al-Kharrār*), a wadi that drains into **[[al-Juḥfah]]** near Ghadīr Khumm (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).

---

## 3. Adherence to Orders & Safe Return

Upon arriving at al-Kharrār, Saʿd's scouts investigated the caravan tracks and discovered that the trade caravan of Quraysh had passed by the watering point on the previous day (*wajadū al-ʿīr qad marrat bil-ams*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).

Although some men suggested pursuing the caravan further south toward Makkah, Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ ﵁ strictly refused to violate the boundary set by the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15). 

Having fulfilled his intelligence mission without exceeding his mandate, Saʿd led his twenty companions on foot back to Medina, arriving safely without loss or injury (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).

---

## 4. Military Significance

1. **Strategic Discipline**: The expedition demonstrated the uncompromising obedience of the Ṣaḥābah to the commander's geographical boundaries, establishing a core military doctrine of the Islamic state (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).
2. **Physical Endurance**: The march of over one hundred miles across arid summer desert on foot, moving exclusively by night, proved the exceptional stamina and operational discipline of the early Muhājirūn (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).
3. **Intelligence Gathering**: It provided vital geographical data on the trails around al-Juḥfah, Ghadīr Khumm, and the Red Sea corridor, preparing the ground for the sovereign patrols of **[[Ghazwat al-Abwāʾ]]** and **[[Ghazwat Buwāṭ]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:15).
