The Expedition of the Barley Meal — Abū Sufyān's night raid on the outskirts of Madinah and the Prophet's ﷺ pursuit to Qarqarat al-Kudr

Ghazwat al-Suwayq was the Prophet's ﷺ pursuit of Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb and two hundred riders of Quraysh, who had come to the outskirts of Madinah to fulfil a vow of vengeance for Badr. The Prophet ﷺ followed them as far as Qarqarat al-Kudr without overtaking them, and the expedition took its name from the sacks of sawīq (roasted, ground grain) that the fleeing Quraysh threw down along the way.[1]

Abū Sufyān's vow

When the routed remnant of the polytheists returned to Makkah, bereaved and grieving, Abū Sufyān forbade himself oil and vowed that no water would touch his head for janābah until he had taken vengeance on the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and his Companions for those struck down at Badr. To fulfil the oath he set out with two hundred riders of Quraysh.[1]

At Madinah

He took the Najd road and halted at the head of Qanāh, one of the valleys of Madinah. That night he went to Banū al-Naḍīr under cover of darkness. He came to Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab and knocked at his door, but Ḥuyayy was afraid of him and refused to open. He went on to Sallām b. Mishkam, the chief of Banū al-Naḍīr at that time and the keeper of their treasure, who gave him food and drink and told him of the people's private affairs.[1][2]

The pursuit

The people learned of the raiders, and on Sunday the fifth of Dhū al-Ḥijjah the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ went out in pursuit with two hundred of the Muhājirūn and Anṣār. He went as far as Qarqarat al-Kudr. Abū Sufyān and his companions were lightening their loads to flee, throwing down sacks of sawīq, and the Muslims picked them up. The Muslims did not catch up with the Quraysh.[1]

«يا رسول الله أتطمع أن تكون لنا غزوة؟ قال: نعم»

“Messenger of Allāh, do you hope that this will count for us as an expedition?” He said: “Yes.”[1]

Disputes and variants

The size of the Muslim force. The text gives two hundred of the Muhājirūn and Anṣār, while al-Ishārah has eighty. The two figures have been reconciled by saying that the riders were eighty and the force as a whole two hundred.[1]
