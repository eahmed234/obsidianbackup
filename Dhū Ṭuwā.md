# Dhū Ṭuwā (ذو طوى)
*The Gateway Valley of Makkah and Sacred Encampment of Kings and Prophets*

## 1. Topography & Ancient Prophetic Lineage
**Dhū Ṭuwā** is a historic, open valley situated immediately at the northern gate of Makkah, identified classically with the wells of al-Zāhir (*Ābār al-Zāhir*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:211, 5:287, 8:461).

Centuries before the mission of Islam, the ancient messengers and righteous prophets of the Children of Israel would encamp at Dhū Ṭuwā upon approaching the sanctuary, removing their sandals out of reverence before crossing into the Sacred Territory (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:211).

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones

### The Tactical Staging Ground of Fatḥ Makkah (Ramaḍān 8 AH)
On the morning of the Conquest of Makkah (**[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**), the Muslim vanguard halted and assembled at Dhū Ṭuwā (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:226). 

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ entered the valley mounted upon his camel al-Qaṣwāʾ, flanked by his green battalion (*al-katībah al-khaḍrāʾ*), wearing a black turban. Overwhelmed by humility before Allāh's manifest grant of victory, the Prophet ﷺ lowered his head until his chin nearly touched the wooden saddle of his camel (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:226).

From Dhū Ṭuwā, the Prophet ﷺ divided the Muslim forces into strategic tactical detachments:
- Commissioning **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ to enter Makkah from the upper pass of Kadāʾ and plant his standard at al-Ḥajūn (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:227).
- Commissioning **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁ to command the right wing—encompassing the tribes of Aslam, Sulaym, Ghifār, Muzaynah, and Juhaynah—and enter through the lower pass of Kudā (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:227).

### The Overnight Encampment of the Farewell Pilgrimage (10 AH)
During the Farewell Pilgrimage (**[[Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ]]**), the Prophet ﷺ reached Dhū Ṭuwā on the evening of the fourth of Dhū al-Ḥijjah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:461). He encamped there overnight, performed the Fajr prayer in the valley, performed ritual washing (*ghusl*), and ascended into Makkah from the upper mountain path overlooking al-Ḥajūn (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:461, 8:489).
