# Banū ʿĀmir b. Luʾayy (بنو عامر بن لؤي)
*The Qurashī Clan of Diplomatic Eloquence, Sacred Lineage, and Key Early Believers*

## 1. Lineage & Quarters in Makkah
**Banū ʿĀmir b. Luʾayy** are a distinguished branch of the tribe of **[[Quraysh]]**, tracing their genealogical descent to ʿĀmir b. Luʾayy b. Ghālib b. Fihr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:420).

When Quṣayy b. Kilāb unified the administration of Makkah and partitioned its residential quarters into four sectors among Quraysh, the rapid demographic expansion of Banū Kaʿb b. Luʾayy and Banū ʿĀmir b. Luʾayy led them to settle the central valley bowl of the sanctuary (*Baṭḥāʾ Makkah*), numbering them among *Quraysh al-Biṭāḥ* alongside Banū ʿAbd Manāf, Banū Makhzūm, and Banū Sahm (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:420).

## 2. Role in the Sīrah

### Mother of the Believers Sawdah bint Zamʿah
Among the foremost early believers from Banū ʿĀmir b. Luʾayy was the Mother of the Believers **[[Sawdah bint Zamʿah]]** ﵂, who accepted Islam in the dawn of the Makkan mission, endured persecution alongside her first husband al-Sakrān b. ʿAmr al-ʿĀmirī, participated in the migration to Abyssinia, and was wed by the Prophet ﷺ following the death of Khadījah ﵂.

### The Diplomatic Envoy of Ḥudaybiyah
The clan held notable oratorical and diplomatic weight in Makkah through its chieftain **[[Suhayl b. ʿAmr]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:48–53). 

During **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]**, Quraysh dispatched Suhayl b. ʿAmr as their chief plenipotentiary to negotiate the treaty with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:48–53). Suhayl famously insisted upon the formal erasure of *«Bismillāh al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm»* in favor of *«Bismika Allāhumma»*, and the substitution of *«Muḥammad b. ʿAbdillāh»* for *«Rasūlullāh»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:50).

### The Trials and Devotion of the Sons of Suhayl
Two of Suhayl's sons embraced Islam early and suffered severe trials:
- **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Suhayl]]** ﵁ — Concealed his Islam until Badr, where he crossed over to the Muslim ranks and fought for the Prophet ﷺ.
- **[[Abū Jandal b. Suhayl]]** ﵁ — Bound in chains in Makkah, he escaped to Ḥudaybiyah just as the treaty was signed. In compliance with the pact, the Prophet ﷺ returned him to his father, comforting him with the promise of divine relief; he subsequently escaped to the coastal outpost of al-ʿĪṣ with Abū Baṣīr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:51–53, 5:61).

At **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**, Suhayl b. ʿAmr embraced Islam wholeheartedly, transforming into one of the most generous benefactors and stalwart defenders of the faith during the trials of the Caliphate.
