# Mawlid al-Nabī ﷺ (مولد النبي ﷺ)
*The Birth of the Noble Messenger of Allāh ﷺ*

## 1. The Pregnancy and Its Signs
Before ʿAbdullāh's marriage to Āminah, the book records two women who perceived the "light of
prophethood" in his face and sought him for themselves — a woman of Banū Asad, and a soothsayer of
Tabālah named Fāṭimah bint Murr al-Khathʿamiyyah, who offered him **a hundred camels**; ʿAbdullāh
refused both, replying to the second in verse: *"As for the forbidden, death lies before it…"*
After his marriage to Āminah, both women found the light had left him, Fāṭimah declaring in verse that
Allāh had placed it where He willed rather than in her (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:326–327). Fāṭimah's
brother, **[[Waraqah b. Nawfal]]**, who had adopted Christianity in the Jāhiliyyah and studied the
scriptures, used to say a prophet of Banū Ismāʿīl would arise in this nation.

Āminah herself reported that she scarcely felt the pregnancy's weight, only that her courses ceased.
A visitor came to her between sleep and waking and said: *"You have conceived the master of this
nation and its Prophet"* — telling her this occurred on a **Monday**, that a light would emerge with
him that would fill the palaces of **Buṣrā** in Syria, that she should name him **Muḥammad**, and
teaching her a protective invocation against envy, *«أُعِيذُهُ بِالْوَاحِدِ مِنْ شَرِّ كُلِّ حَاسِدٍ»*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:328–329). Elsewhere she was told to name him **Aḥmad**. Yaḥyā b. ʿĀʾidh records
that **she carried him nine full months, complaining of no pain, colic, or wind** — none of what
customarily afflicts pregnant women (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:329).

## 2. Death of His Father, Before His Birth
See **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** for the fuller account. The majority position — Ibn Isḥāq,
al-Wāqidī, Ibn Saʿd, al-Balādhurī, graded ṣaḥīḥ by al-Dhahabī and called *mashhūr* by Ibn Kathīr — is
that ʿAbdullāh died **while Āminah was still carrying him**, at two months of pregnancy. A minority
report instead places his death after the birth, when the Prophet ﷺ was already in the cradle — variously
given as two months, twenty-eight months, or nine months old (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:331). ʿAbdullāh had
fallen ill returning from a trading journey to Ghazzah and stayed behind among his maternal kin,
**Banū ʿAdī b. al-Najjār**, at Medina, where he died and was buried in Dār al-Nābighah. Āminah's own
elegy for him is preserved: *"ʿAfā jānib al-Baṭḥāʾ min-ibni Hāshim…"* He left as inheritance
**Umm Ayman**, five camels, and a flock of sheep (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:331–332).

## 3. The Date and Place
**Day:** The Prophet ﷺ himself, asked about the merit of Mondays, answered: *"That is the day I was
born, or he said, revelation was sent down to me in it"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:333). Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄
reported that he was **born on a Monday, made a prophet on a Monday, died on a Monday, and the Black
Stone was raised on a Monday**. He was born **during the day**, at *ibhār al-nahār* (full daylight),
per the position al-Ḥāfiẓ al-ʿIrāqī judged correct.

**Month:** The majority hold it was **Rabīʿ al-Awwal**; al-Suhaylī calls this well known, and some
claim consensus (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:333–334). Ibn Isḥāq specifies the **twelfth night** of it had
passed.

**Year:** Confirmed as the **Year of the Elephant** on the authority of Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄, graded ṣaḥīḥ by
al-Ḥākim and al-Dhahabī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:335). Reports on the interval between Abrahah's expedition
and the birth vary enormously — fifty-five nights (which al-Dimyāṭī judged correct), forty days, a
month and six days, or as much as ten, thirty, or forty years (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:336) — a spread the
book records without resolving.

**Why Rabīʿ al-Awwal and Monday:** Ibn al-Ḥājj gives four reasons this timing suited him ﷺ — among
them that Allāh created the trees on a Monday, and that spring (*rabīʿ*) is the most temperate of
seasons, mirroring the ease of his ﷺ own religious law (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:337).

## 4. Foretold the Night Before It Happened
**Ḥassān b. Thābit** ﵁, as a boy of seven or eight, recalled hearing a Jewish man cry out from atop
his fortress: *"O assembly of Jews! The star of Aḥmad, under which he is born, has risen this
night!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:339). **ʿĀʾishah** ﵂ relates that a Jewish trader resident in Makkah
asked a gathering of Quraysh whether a child had been born among them that night, then told them:
*"Tonight is born the Prophet of this last nation — between his shoulders is a mark with hairs like a
horse's mane, and he will not nurse for two nights."* Quraysh went home in wonder, only to learn a son
named Muḥammad had indeed been born to ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib that very night
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:339–340).

A hermit named **ʿAyṣ**, who kept a cell near Makkah, had for years told visitors: *"A child is soon
to be born among you, O people of Makkah, to whom the Arabs will submit and whom the non-Arabs will
serve."* On the morning after the birth, **[[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** came to him, and the hermit told him
directly:

> «كُنْ أَبَاهُ فَقَدْ وُلِدَ ذَلِكَ الْمَوْلُودُ الَّذِي كُنْتُ أُحَدِّثُكُمْ عَنْهُ … فَإِنْ طَالَ عُمْرُهُ لَمْ يَبْلُغِ السَّبْعِينَ، يَمُوتُ فِي وِتْرٍ دُونَهَا، فِي السِّتِّينَ، فِي إِحْدَىٰ وَسِتِّينَ أَوْ ثَلَاثٍ وَسِتِّينَ»
> *"Be his father, for that child I told you of has now been born … if his life is long he will not
> reach seventy; he will die at an odd number below it — in his sixties, at sixty-one or
> sixty-three."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:340)

## 5. The Light, the Stars, and the Midwife's Testimony
The Prophet ﷺ said of himself: *"My mother saw, when she delivered me, a light shine from her that
illuminated the palaces of Buṣrā"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:341). **ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ**'s mother, who
was present at the birth, said she saw nothing in the house but light, and the stars seemed to draw so
close she feared they would fall upon her; when he was born, a light lit the house until she could see
nothing else. Āminah herself, in multiple transmissions, describes the newborn falling not as infants
usually do, but **resting on his hands with his head raised to the sky**, then taking a handful of
earth and lifting his gaze upward — light spreading between east and west, illuminating the palaces
and markets of Syria as far as **Buṣrā**, where the necks of camels could be seen by it
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:342). Al-Ṣāliḥī notes the significance of Syria specifically being singled out:
it would become *"the abode of his ﷺ glory and dominion."* His midwife, **[[al-Shifāʾ bint ʿAwf]]**,
mother of **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]**, is named as a direct witness among these reports
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:341).

## 6. The Splitting of the Cooking Pot
Following the Jāhiliyyah custom of covering a newborn with a vessel overnight unseen, the family
placed him under a cooking pot (*burmah*). By morning it had **split in two**, and he was found with
his eyes already open, gazing at the sky (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:346).

## 7. Born Already Circumcised, With the Cord Already Cut
The Prophet ﷺ said: *"It is among my honours before my Lord that I was born circumcised, and no one
saw my nakedness"* — transmitted from Anas b. Mālik ﵁ and others, with al-Ḥākim claiming the reports
mass-transmitted and al-Dhahabī disputing that characterisation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:347). Āminah said:
*"I delivered him clean — he was not born soiled as a lamb is."* The book lists other prophets
tradition holds were likewise born circumcised, among them Ādam ﵊, Shīth ﵊, Nūḥ ﵊, Lūṭ ﵊, Yūsuf ﵊,
Shuʿayb ﵊, and Mūsā ﵊ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:347–348).

## 8. Speaking to the Moon in the Cradle
**[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ asked the Prophet ﷺ directly for a sign of his prophethood
from infancy, and was told: *"I used to converse with it, and it with me, and it would occupy me from
crying — and I could hear its prostration beneath the Throne"* — describing his cooing and pointing to
the moon in his cradle, toward which it would incline (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:349). He is also said to
have spoken in the cradle, his first words being *"Allāhu akbaru kabīran, wal-ḥamdu lillāhi
kathīran."*

## 9. Iblīs's Grief
Al-Suhaylī transmits that **Iblīs cried out four times**: when he was cursed, when he was cast down,
when the Prophet ﷺ was born, and when *al-Fātiḥah* was revealed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:350). ʿIkrimah
reports Iblīs saying, *"a child is born tonight who will ruin our affair,"* and moving to approach the
newborn — whereupon Jibrīl ﵊ struck him with his foot, casting him down as far as **ʿAdan**. Before this
he had been barred from three heavens yet could still reach a fourth; **at this birth he was barred
from all seven** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:350). The idols of the Kaʿbah are reported to have toppled, and
heralds cried out from the mountains of **Abū Qubays** and **al-Ḥajūn**, praising Banū Zuhrah for
having produced *"the best of creation, Aḥmad"* through Āminah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:352).

## 10. The Persian Omens
On the same night, in the Sāsānid capital, **the palace (*al-Īwān*) of Kisrā cracked apart though
bearing no unusual weight**, and **fourteen of its battlements fell**; the sacred fire the Zoroastrian
priesthood had kept burning without interruption for a thousand years **went out**; the waters of
**Lake Sāwah receded**; and the chief priest (*al-Mūbadhān*) dreamed of Arab camels leading Arabian
horses across the Tigris to disperse through Persian lands. Kisrā — **Anūshirwān b. Qubādh**, the
reigning king at the time of the Prophet's ﷺ birth (his successor Abrawīz would later tear up the
Prophet's ﷺ own letter; a still later Yazdajird would be defeated by the Muslims in ʿUthmān's ﵁
caliphate) — gathered his soothsayers and astrologers in alarm, among them an Arab named al-Sāʾib, who
alone had glimpsed lightning flash from the direction of the Ḥijāz and rightly divined its meaning
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:353–357).

## 11. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib's Joy, and the Naming
Āminah sent word to **[[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** that a son had been born to him; he came, and she told
him all she had seen and been commanded. He took the infant into the **Kaʿbah**, thanking Allāh, then
brought him back out reciting verses of praise and protection, closing with a line naming him **Aḥmad**
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:360). On the seventh day, ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib slaughtered an animal, invited Quraysh
to a feast, and named the child **Muḥammad** — a name unknown among his people at the time, so that
they asked him why he had chosen it over the names of his own forefathers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:360).

## 12. Later Commemoration of This Night
Al-Ṣāliḥī closes the Mawlid chapters with the scholars' discussion of formally commemorating this
night. Al-Sakhāwī notes the practice was **not transmitted from the earliest three generations** and
arose afterward, yet became widespread, with almsgiving, gatherings, and recitation of the birth
narrative. Al-Ṣāliḥī names **al-Malik al-Muẓaffar Abū Saʿīd Kawkabūrī of Irbil** as the first ruler to
institute it formally (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:362).

## 13. What Follows
He was nursed first by his own mother, then by **Thuwaybah**, before being given to
**[[Ḥalīmah al-Saʿdiyyah]]** — see her note for the nursing period, including the event of
**[[Shaqq al-Ṣadr]]**, the opening of his chest.
