# Saʿd b. Zayd al-Ashhalī (سعد بن زيد الأشهلي)
*Badri Veteran of al-Aws, Commander of the Cavalry at Dhī Qarad, and Smasher of the Idol Manāt*

## 1. Lineage and Early Islam
His full genealogy is recorded by Ibn Ḥajar as:
**Saʿd b. Zayd b. Mālik b. ʿUbayd b. Kaʿb b. ʿAbd al-Ashhal al-Anṣārī al-Awsī al-Ashhalī** (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:51, no. 3165).

He belonged to the noble chieftain house of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal of **[[al-Aws]]**. According to al-Wāqidī, he was present at the **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah|Second Pledge of al-ʿAqabah]]**, and he witnessed **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā|Badr]]** according to Mūsā b. ʿUqbah, Ibn Isḥāq, and the consensus of the maghāzī scholars (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:51–52; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:91). He subsequently fought at **[[Ghazwat Uḥud|Uḥud]]**, the Battle of the Trench, and all subsequent expeditions.

## 2. Mission to Najd with the Captives of Banū Qurayẓah (5 AH)
Following the surrender of **[[Banū Qurayẓah]]**, the Prophet ﷺ dispatched Saʿd b. Zayd al-Ashhalī to the plateau of Najd with a contingent of captives. He was instructed to barter them among the Bedouin tribes in exchange for thoroughbred cavalry horses (*khayl*) and weapons (*silāḥ*) to fortify the defense forces of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:26; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:52).

## 3. Cavalry Commander at Ghazwat Dhī Qarad (6 AH)
During **[[Ghazwat Dhī Qarad]]** (the Expedition of the Forest), when ʿUyaynah b. Ḥiṣn al-Fazārī raided the milch-camels of the Prophet ﷺ outside Madīnah, Saʿd b. Zayd al-Ashhalī was the foremost equestrian commander (*al-raʾīs yawmaʾidhin*) who led the Anṣārī horsemen in fierce pursuit alongside **[[al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr]]** ﵁ and **[[Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:52).

## 4. Demolition of the Idol Manāt at al-Mushallal (8 AH)
In Ramaḍān 8 AH, following the Conquest of Makkah, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commissioned Saʿd b. Zayd al-Ashhalī at the head of a mounted detachment of twenty horsemen on a special expedition (**[[Sariyyat Saʿd b. Zayd al-Ashhalī ilā Manāt]]**) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:394–395).

Their objective was to obliterate **Manāt**, the ancient pagan idol situated by the seashore at al-Mushallal near Qudayd, which had long been venerated by the tribes of Aws, Khazraj, and Ghassān. Upon reaching the sanctuary, Saʿd advanced upon the shrine; the pagan custodian warned him of the goddess's wrath, whereupon a disheveled, naked sorceress emerged cursing him. Saʿd struck her down with his sword, demolished the stone idol to rubble, broke open its treasury, and returned to Madīnah having eradicated one of the greatest idols of Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:394–395; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:52).
