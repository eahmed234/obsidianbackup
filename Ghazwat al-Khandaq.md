# Ghazwat al-Khandaq (غزوة الخندق)
*The siege of Medina by the Confederates, and the trench that held them off*

The **Battle of the Trench**, also called the **Battle of the Confederates** (*al-Aḥzāb*), was the siege of Medina by an alliance of [[Quraysh]], [[Ghaṭafān]] and other Arab tribes, brought together by leaders of the exiled Jewish tribe of [[Banū al-Naḍīr]].[^s4.363][^s4.364] On the advice of [[Salmān al-Fārisī]] ﵁ the Muslims dug a trench across the open northern side of the city, something the Arabs had never done, and the confederate army of ten thousand could not bring its strength across it.[^s4.364][^s4.371] During the siege [[Banū Qurayẓah]], inside Medina, broke their treaty with the Prophet ﷺ, and the Muslims were threatened from both sides.[^s4.373][^s4.374] After weeks of skirmishing, the single combat in which [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]] ﵁ killed ʿAmr b. ʿAbd Wudd, and a breakdown of trust between the allies brought about by [[Nuʿaym b. Masʿūd]] ﵁, a bitter wind struck the confederate camp and they withdrew.[^s4.378][^s4.386] The Prophet ﷺ said afterwards: *"Now we will march against them, and they will not march against us."*[^s4.389] The battle is the subject of a long passage of [[Sūrat al-Aḥzāb]].[^s4.391]

## Background

After the Prophet ﷺ expelled Banū al-Naḍīr from Medina, they settled at [[Khaybar]].[^s4.363] Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab, Kinānah b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq, Hawdhah b. Qays al-Wāʾilī and Abū ʿĀmir "the Sinner" went with a group of others to Makkah and called on Quraysh to make war on him, promising to stand with them until he was destroyed. Quraysh, remembering Badr, welcomed the offer. Abū Sufyān brought out fifty men from every clan of Quraysh, and they swore at the Kaʿbah, pressing themselves against it between the building and its covering, that none of them would abandon the others.[^s4.363]

Abū Sufyān asked the Jewish delegation, as people of the earlier scripture, whether the religion of Quraysh was better than Muḥammad's. They answered that Quraysh, who tended the House, gave water to the pilgrims and sacrificed camels, were closer to the truth than he was. The verses of [[Sūrat al-Nisāʾ]] beginning *"Have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Book, who believe in idols and false gods, and say of the disbelievers: 'These are better guided than the believers'?"* (al-Nisāʾ 4:51–55) were revealed about this.[^s4.363]

The delegation then went to Ghaṭafān, to whom, according to al-Wāqidī, they promised a year's date harvest of Khaybar, and to [[Banū Sulaym]].[^s4.364] Quraysh set out four thousand strong, with three hundred horses and fifteen hundred camels; their banner was tied in the Dār al-Nadwah and carried by ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥah, who later became Muslim. Banū Sulaym joined them at Marr al-Ẓahrān with seven hundred men under Sufyān b. ʿAbd Shams. Banū Asad came under Ṭalḥah b. Khuwaylid al-Asadī; Fazārah, a thousand strong, under ʿUyaynah b. Ḥiṣn; Ashjaʿ, four hundred, under Masʿūd b. Rukhaylah; and Murrah, four hundred, under al-Ḥārith b. ʿAwf al-Murrī. In all ten thousand men came to the trench, under the overall command of [[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]].[^s4.364]

## The trench

Riders of [[Banū Khuzāʿah|Khuzāʿah]] brought the Prophet ﷺ word of the Meccan preparations in four nights. He told the Muslims what was coming and asked their advice: should they go out to meet the enemy, or stay and fight from Medina and its streets? Salmān proposed a trench: *"O Messenger of Allāh, in the land of Persia, when we feared cavalry, we dug a trench around ourselves."* The Muslims liked the idea and chose to hold the city.[^s4.364] Two scouts sent out ahead, Salīṭ and Sufyān b. ʿAwf al-Aslamī, were surrounded by Abū Sufyān's horsemen at al-Baydāʾ and killed; they were buried together in one grave.[^s4.364][^s4.365]

The Prophet ﷺ rode out with some of the Muhājirūn and Anṣār to choose the line. He decided to keep Mount Salʿ at the army's back and to dig from al-Madhād to Dhubāb to Rātij.[^s4.365] The editors' gloss places the trench on the north (Syrian) side of Medina, running from the edge of the eastern lava field to the edge of the western one.[^s4.399] The Muslims borrowed spades, picks and baskets from Banū Qurayẓah for the work. The Muhājirūn dug from Rātij to Dhubāb and the Anṣār from Dhubāb to Jabal Abī ʿUbaydah, and in a report of al-Ṭabarānī the Prophet ﷺ allotted forty cubits to every ten men.[^s4.365]

Salmān was a strong man who did the work of ten, and the Muhājirūn and Anṣār each claimed him as one of their own, until the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Salmān is of the people of the house."*[^s4.365][^s4.397] The Prophet ﷺ dug and carried earth on his back until the dust covered his back and stomach; Umm Salamah ﵂ said she never forgot the day of the Trench, when he was handing things to the men, his hair grey with dust.[^s4.365] [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq|Abū Bakr]] and [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb|ʿUmar]] ﵄, finding no baskets, carried earth in their clothes. Those who finished their own share helped others, and no Muslim held back from the work.[^s4.365] Once the Prophet ﷺ, worn out, fell asleep leaning on a stone, and Abū Bakr and ʿUmar stood over him keeping people from waking him; when he woke he asked why they had not roused him and went back to work.[^s4.367] The trench was finished, according to al-Wāqidī and Ibn Saʿd, in six days.[^s4.367]

The Companions worked through cold mornings, hungry, with no servants to do the labour for them.[^s4.366] They sang as they dug. One of them, Juʿayl, was renamed ʿAmr by the Prophet ﷺ that day, and the diggers made a verse of it.[^s4.366] When the Prophet ﷺ saw their hunger and fatigue he said:

> «اللهم لا عيش إلا عيش الآخرة، فاغفر للأنصار والمهاجرة»
> *"O Allāh, there is no life but the life of the Hereafter, so forgive the Anṣār and the Muhājirūn."*

and they answered him:

> «نحن الذين بايعوا محمدا ... على الجهاد ما بقينا أبدا»
> *"We are those who pledged to Muḥammad to strive in jihād for as long as we live."*[^s4.366][^bukh4099]

[[al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib]] ﵁ saw the Prophet ﷺ carrying earth until it hid the whiteness of his stomach, reciting the verses of [[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]] ﵁: *"By Allāh, had it not been for Allāh, we would not have been guided, nor given charity, nor prayed…"*[^s4.366]

Some of the hypocrites hung back from the work and slipped away to their families without permission, while believers who had a real need asked leave and came back when it was done. The verses of [[Sūrat al-Nūr]] on those who do not leave the Prophet ﷺ without asking his permission (al-Nūr 24:62–64) were revealed about them.[^s4.370][^s4.371] The Prophet ﷺ also reviewed the boys who had come to work. He admitted those of fifteen, among them [[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar]], [[Zayd b. Thābit]], [[Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī]] and al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib, and sent the younger ones back to the forts with the women and children once the fighting drew near.[^s4.371]

## Signs during the digging

The diggers came upon a great white rock that broke their picks. The Prophet ﷺ, who had a stone tied against his stomach from hunger after three days without food, spat into a vessel of water, made a supplication and sprinkled the water on it, and those present said it became like loose sand. Then he took the pick from Salmān and struck three times.[^s4.367][^s4.368] With each blow a third of the rock broke away and a flash of light lit up the land between Medina's two lava fields. At the first, from the direction of Yemen, he said he had been given the keys of Yemen and could see the gates of Ṣanʿāʾ. At the second, from the direction of the Byzantines, he said he had been given the keys of Syria and could see its red palaces. At the third, from the direction of Persia, he said he had been given the keys of Persia and could see the palaces of al-Ḥīrah and the cities of Chosroes, and that Jibrīl ﵊ had told him his community would prevail over them.[^s4.368] The hypocrites mocked: Muḥammad promised them the palaces of Chosroes while they could not step out from behind their own trench. About this was revealed: *"And when the hypocrites and those in whose hearts is sickness said: Allāh and His Messenger promised us nothing but delusion"* (al-Aḥzāb 33:12).[^s4.368]

[[Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh]] ﵄, seeing the Prophet's hunger, had his wife prepare a ṣāʿ of barley and slaughter a young goat, and quietly invited him with one or two others. The Prophet ﷺ instead called out to everyone at the trench. He blessed the dough and the pot, and had the men come in ten at a time; Jābir swore that a thousand ate, and that the pot was still bubbling and the dough still being baked when they had finished.[^s4.369][^bukh4102] A daughter of Bashīr b. Saʿd, sent with a handful of dates for her father and her maternal uncle ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah, gave them to the Prophet ﷺ; he scattered them on a cloth and called the people of the trench to eat, and they ate until they were full while the dates spilled over its edges.[^s4.369][^s4.370] Other reports tell of a bowl of *ḥays* sent by Umm ʿĀmir al-Ashhaliyyah, from which the people of the trench ate their fill while it stayed as it was, and of a sheep brought by [[Abū Rāfiʿ]] ﵁.[^s4.370]

## The armies take position

When the trench was done the Prophet ﷺ left Ibn Umm Maktūm in charge of Medina, according to Ibn Hishām, and camped in front of Salʿ, with the mountain behind him and the trench in front. His leather tent was pitched by the upper mosque at the foot of the mountain. The Muslims numbered three thousand; al-Ṣāliḥī notes that the figure of seven hundred given by some is an error, and that Jābir's "thousand" counts only those who ate.[^s4.371][^s4.399] The banner of the Muhājirūn was carried by Zayd b. Ḥārithah and that of the Anṣār by Saʿd b. ʿUbādah. The women and children were placed in the forts, and the city's buildings were closed up on every side so that it was like a fortress.[^s4.371] Three of the Prophet's wives, ʿĀʾishah, Umm Salamah and Zaynab bint Jaḥsh, took turns staying with him at the trench; the rest stayed in a fort of Banū Ḥārithah.[^s4.367]

Quraysh camped at the meeting of the torrent-beds near Rūmah, with their Aḥābīsh and the men of Kinānah and Tihāmah; Ghaṭafān and the men of Najd camped at Dhanab Naqamā beside Uḥud. The crops had been harvested a month earlier, so the Meccans found nothing for their horses but the fodder they had brought, and the horses of Ghaṭafān nearly starved.[^s4.372] Seeing the trench, they said: *"This is a stratagem the Arabs have never used,"* and some guessed that a Persian with the Prophet ﷺ had suggested it.[^s4.376] For most of the siege there was no fighting beyond arrows and stones across the ditch. Abū Sufyān, [[Khālid b. al-Walīd]], [[ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl]] and Ḍirār b. al-Khaṭṭāb took turns leading their horsemen along it, looking for a weak point.[^s4.376]

When the believers saw the confederates, they said: *"This is what Allāh and His Messenger promised us; Allāh and His Messenger spoke the truth,"* and it only increased their faith (al-Aḥzāb 33:22).[^s4.373]

## Banū Qurayẓah break the treaty

Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab went to Kaʿb b. Asad, the chief of Banū Qurayẓah, who held the treaty between his tribe and the Prophet ﷺ. Kaʿb shut the door of his fort against him, saying he had found nothing in Muḥammad but truthfulness and loyalty. Ḥuyayy shamed him into opening it, and told him he had brought "the glory of the age": Quraysh and Ghaṭafān encamped outside Medina and sworn not to leave until Muḥammad was destroyed. Kaʿb answered that he had brought the humiliation of the age, a cloud that thunders and flashes but holds no rain. Ḥuyayy pressed him until he agreed, on Ḥuyayy's promise that if the confederates left without victory he would come into Kaʿb's fort and share his fate. Kaʿb broke the treaty.[^s4.373] ʿAmr b. Suʿdā warned his people against it and they refused him; three brothers of the family of Saʿnah, Asad, Usayd and Thaʿlabah, went over to the Prophet ﷺ and became Muslim.[^s4.373] Kaʿb asked Ḥuyayy to obtain hostages from Quraysh and Ghaṭafān as a guarantee.[^s4.374]

The news reached ʿUmar, who told the Prophet ﷺ. He sent the two chiefs Saʿd b. Muʿādh and Saʿd b. ʿUbādah with ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah and Khawwāt b. Jubayr, and in al-Wāqidī's account [[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]] ﵁. If the report was true they were to tell him in words only he would understand, so as not to break the people's spirit; if it was false they were to say so openly.[^s4.374] They found Qurayẓah had broken the treaty. Kaʿb told them he had cut it as he had cut the strap of his sandal, and asked who the Messenger of Allāh was, saying there was no covenant between them. Insults were exchanged, and Usayd warned Kaʿb that Quraysh would leave in defeat and the Muslims would bring him down from his fort on their own terms. Returning, Saʿd b. ʿUbādah said only: *"ʿAḍal and al-Qārah"* — treachery like that of the tribes at [[Sariyyat al-Rajīʿ|al-Rajīʿ]].[^s4.374]

The Prophet ﷺ told the Muslims to rejoice at Allāh's help, and said he hoped to circle the Kaʿbah and take its key, and that Chosroes and Caesar would perish and their wealth be spent in the path of Allāh. According to Ibn ʿUqbah he then covered himself with his garment and lay still for a long time.[^s4.374] The news spread and fear grew for the women and children; it was, in the words of the Qurʾān, the time *"when they came at you from above you and from below you, and when eyes shifted and hearts reached the throats"* (al-Aḥzāb 33:10).[^s4.374] Muʿattib b. Qushayr said: *"Muḥammad promised us the treasures of Chosroes and Caesar, and today none of us is safe going out to relieve himself."*[^s4.374]

Banū Qurayẓah planned a night raid on Medina. The Prophet ﷺ sent Salamah b. Aslam b. Ḥarīsh with two hundred men and Zayd b. Ḥārithah with three hundred to guard the city, calling out the *takbīr* through the night.[^s4.375] A group of Banū Ḥārithah asked leave to go home, saying their houses were exposed; Saʿd b. Muʿādh objected that they always did this in hardship, and the Prophet ﷺ sent them back to their posts.[^s4.375] The verse *"And a party of them asked the Prophet's leave, saying: Our houses are exposed — and they were not exposed; they wanted only to flee"* (al-Aḥzāb 33:13) describes them.[^s4.391]

### Ṣafiyyah and the fort of Fāriʿ

The Prophet's aunt [[Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]] ﵂ was in the fort of Fāriʿ with his wives and with [[Ḥassān b. Thābit]] ﵁. A party of Jews came up and one of them approached the gate. Ṣafiyyah told Ḥassān to go down and kill him; he excused himself, saying this was not something he was suited for. She took a post — or, in another wording, a sword — went down and killed the man, then threw his head down at the Jews, who concluded the fort was defended and went away. In Abū Yaʿlā's report the Prophet ﷺ gave her a share of the spoils as he did for the men.[^s4.371][^s4.372] The picture of Ḥassān's timidity in this story is disputed. Abū ʿUmar and others reject Ibn Isḥāq's chain as broken and note that the poets who satirised Ḥassān never accused him of cowardice. al-Ṣāliḥī answers that the story also comes through ʿUrwah from Ṣafiyyah, his grandmother, and that if ʿUrwah met her the chain is strong; the author of *al-Rawḍ* suggests Ḥassān may have been ill that day.[^s4.398][^s4.399]

## The offer to Ghaṭafān

When Qurayẓah's breach was confirmed, the Prophet ﷺ sent for the two leaders of Ghaṭafān, ʿUyaynah b. Ḥiṣn and al-Ḥārith b. ʿAwf, and asked whether they would go home with their men, and turn the Bedouin away, for a third of Medina's dates. They asked for half; he would not go above a third, and they accepted. He called for a sheet and ink and gave the sheet to [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]] to write the agreement.[^s4.376] Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr came up, saw ʿUyaynah sitting with his legs stretched out in front of the Prophet ﷺ, and rebuked him sharply. He then told the Prophet ﷺ that if this was a command from heaven he should carry it out, but otherwise the Muslims would give them nothing but the sword.[^s4.376]

The Prophet ﷺ consulted Saʿd b. Muʿādh and Saʿd b. ʿUbādah. They asked him the same question: if Allāh had commanded it, or if it was something he wished, they would obey; but if it was only a judgement, the enemy would have nothing from them but the sword. He told them he had seen the Arabs shoot at them *"from a single bow"* and wanted to break their strength. Saʿd b. Muʿādh replied that when they had been idolaters, these tribes had never hoped to eat a single date of Medina except as guests or by buying it. Now that Allāh had honoured them with Islam and with him, they would not hand over their wealth. *"You and that,"* said the Prophet ﷺ, and Saʿd wiped out what was written on the sheet.[^s4.376][^s4.377] al-Ṣāliḥī notes that the offer shows it is permissible to give wealth to an enemy where it serves and protects the Muslims.[^s4.399]

## ʿAlī and ʿAmr b. ʿAbd Wudd

After some twenty nights of the siege, as Ibn Isḥāq puts it, the leaders of the confederates rode out together looking for a narrow place in the trench, and found one the Muslims had left unguarded. ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl, Nawfal b. ʿAbdillāh, Ḍirār b. al-Khaṭṭāb, Hubayrah b. Abī Wahb and ʿAmr b. ʿAbd Wudd forced their horses across into the salt flat between the trench and Salʿ; the rest stayed on the other side.[^s4.377][^s4.378] A party of Muslims went out and closed the gap behind them.[^s4.378]

ʿAmr had fought at Badr until he was disabled by wounds, had missed Uḥud, and had sworn to use no oil on his hair until he had taken revenge. He was an old man by then — ninety, according to Ibn Saʿd — and one of the famous champions of the idolaters. He rode out marked so that his place could be seen, and called for single combat.[^s4.378] ʿAlī asked the Prophet ﷺ for permission. The Prophet ﷺ gave it, handed him his own sword, wound a turban on him and prayed: *"O Allāh, help him against him."* ʿAlī walked out to him reciting verses.[^s4.378]

ʿAlī reminded ʿAmr that he used to say he would grant any man one of three requests. He offered him Islam; ʿAmr put it off. He offered him the chance to go home, leaving Muḥammad to others; ʿAmr said the women of Quraysh would never speak of such a thing, and he had taken his vow. The third was to fight. ʿAmr laughed and asked who he was. When ʿAlī named himself, ʿAmr said he had uncles older than him, and he did not want to shed his blood; ʿAlī answered that he had no objection to shedding ʿAmr's.[^s4.378]

ʿAmr, enraged, dismounted, hamstrung his horse and came at ʿAlī with his sword. ʿAlī took the blow on his leather shield, which it split, and the sword wounded his head; al-Balādhurī adds that it is also said ʿAlī was never wounded. ʿAlī struck ʿAmr on the shoulder, or in another report speared him through the collarbone, and he fell in a cloud of dust. The Prophet ﷺ heard the *takbīr* and knew ʿAlī had killed him.[^s4.378] Verses on the killing are attributed to ʿAlī, but Ibn Hishām says most authorities on poetry doubt they are his. ʿAlī did not strip ʿAmr of his armour, the finest among the Arabs, out of modesty.[^s4.378][^s4.379]

The other horsemen fled back across the trench; ʿIkrimah threw away his spear as he ran, and Ḥassān mocked him for it. [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]] and ʿUmar went out after them. al-Zubayr struck Nawfal b. ʿAbdillāh with such force that the sword cut through him and his saddle to the horse's back; when people praised the sword, he said, *"It is not the sword, it is the forearm."*[^s4.379] Quraysh offered ten thousand for ʿAmr's body, according to Ibn Isḥāq; in a report of Ibn ʿAbbās the offer was twelve thousand for the body of a man killed that day. The Prophet ﷺ took nothing: *"Take him; we do not eat the price of the dead."*[^s4.379] Ibn ʿUqbah says the body requested was Nawfal's, and Ibn Jarīr has a report, which al-Ṣāliḥī calls odd, that ʿAlī killed Nawfal in the trench.[^s4.380]

## The day of the missed prayers

After ʿAmr's death the confederates agreed to attack together. They arrived before sunrise and surrounded the Muslims from every side, sending a strong force under Khālid b. al-Walīd against the Prophet's tent, and fought until well into the night. Neither the Prophet ﷺ nor any of the Muslims could leave their positions, and they were unable to pray Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr, Maghrib or ʿIshāʾ.[^s4.380] That night Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr held the trench with two hundred men against a further attack by Khālid's horsemen, in which [[Waḥshī b. Ḥarb|Waḥshī]] killed al-Ṭufayl b. al-Nuʿmān al-Anṣārī with his javelin, as he had killed Ḥamzah at Uḥud.[^s4.380]

ʿAlī reported that the Prophet ﷺ said that day: *"May Allāh fill their houses and their graves with fire, as they kept us from the middle prayer until the sun set."*[^s4.381][^bukh2931] In Jābir's report ʿUmar came after sunset, cursing Quraysh, and said he had barely prayed ʿAṣr before the sun set; the Prophet ﷺ said he had not prayed it at all. They went down to Buṭḥān, made ablution, and he prayed ʿAṣr after sunset and then Maghrib.[^s4.381] In other reports, from Abū Saʿīd, Ibn Masʿūd and Jābir, four prayers were missed. When part of the night had passed the Prophet ﷺ had [[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ|Bilāl]] give the call to prayer and then the *iqāmah* for each in turn, and prayed each as he would have prayed it in its time. Abū Saʿīd notes that this was before the revelation of the prayer of fear.[^s4.382]

## Wounds, alarms and supplies

During the fighting Ḥibbān b. Qays b. al-ʿAriqah shot an arrow that cut the medial vein of Saʿd b. Muʿādh's arm, crying, *"Take it — I am Ibn al-ʿAriqah!"* Saʿd, or by another account the Prophet ﷺ, answered: *"May Allāh make your face sweat in the Fire."*[^s4.380][^s4.381] Saʿd prayed: if any war with Quraysh remained, that Allāh keep him alive for it; if not, that He make the wound his martyrdom; and that He not let him die until he had seen justice done to Banū Qurayẓah. The shot is also attributed to Abū Usāmah al-Jushamī or Khafājah b. ʿĀṣim.[^s4.381] Earlier, ʿĀʾishah ﵂ had seen Saʿd pass the fort in a coat of mail too short to cover his arms, and wished it had been fuller.[^s4.372]

Two Muslim patrols met in the dark, each taking the other for the enemy, and fought until they called out the Muslim watchword, *"Ḥā Mīm, they will not be helped."* The Prophet ﷺ told them their wounds were in the path of Allāh, and that whoever had been killed was a martyr.[^s4.381] Men who asked leave to visit their families were told to take their weapons, for fear of Qurayẓah. One young man, newly married, found his wife standing at the door. He raised his spear at her, but she told him to look first in the house, where he found a snake on his bed. He speared it and both died, the man and the snake, so quickly that no one knew which died first.[^s4.381]

ʿAbbād b. Bishr ﵁, who rarely left the Prophet's tent, led his men out one night against horsemen under Abū Sufyān probing a narrow place in the trench, and drove them off with stones and arrows.[^s4.375][^s4.376] [[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]] ﵁ was set to guard a gap in the trench that the Prophet ﷺ himself used to watch through the cold nights.[^s4.375] When the confederates ran out of fodder, Ḥuyayy arranged with Kaʿb b. Asad for Qurayẓah to send twenty camels loaded with barley, dates and straw. A party of twenty men of Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf, among them [[Abū Lubābah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir|Abū Lubābah]], came upon the caravan on their way to bury one of their dead and captured it after a fight with its escort. The Muslims at the trench ate from it until it was gone, and Abū Sufyān complained that Ḥuyayy had left them with nothing to ride home on.[^s4.382]

Abū Sufyān also wrote to the Prophet ﷺ, swearing by al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā that he had come to destroy him, taunting him for sheltering behind the trench, and promising him a day like Uḥud. The Prophet ﷺ replied that Allāh would come between him and what he intended, and that a day would come when he would break al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, Isāf, Nāʾilah and Hubal.[^s4.390][^s4.391]

## Supplication, and Nuʿaym b. Masʿūd

The Muslims remained in the fear and hardship the Qurʾān describes.[^s4.383] Jābir reported that the Prophet ﷺ came to the mosque of al-Aḥzāb on the Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, between Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr, laid aside his cloak and raised his hands in supplication against the confederates, and that they saw gladness in his face.[^s4.383] He told the people not to wish to meet the enemy but to ask Allāh for well-being, and to be steadfast if they met them, knowing that Paradise lies beneath the shade of swords.[^s4.383] Then he prayed:

> «اللهم منزل الكتاب، سريع الحساب، اهزم الأحزاب. اللهم اهزمهم وانصرنا عليهم»
> *"O Allāh, Revealer of the Book, swift in reckoning, defeat the Confederates. O Allāh, defeat them and give us victory over them."*[^s4.383]

Asked by the Companions for something to say now that their hearts had reached their throats, he taught them: *"O Allāh, cover our weaknesses and calm our fears."*[^s4.383]

Nuʿaym b. Masʿūd of Ashjaʿ had come with his tribe and was a friend of Banū Qurayẓah. As the siege dragged on, Allāh put Islam into his heart. He came secretly to the Prophet ﷺ between Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ and became Muslim, telling him that neither his own people nor anyone else knew. The Prophet ﷺ told him: *"You are only one man among us, so turn the people away from us as much as you can, for war is deception,"* and gave him leave to say what he saw fit.[^s4.383][^s4.384]

Nuʿaym went first to Qurayẓah, reminded them of his friendship, and advised them that Quraysh and Ghaṭafān could go home if things went badly, while they would be left alone with Muḥammad. They should not fight, he said, until they had taken some of the confederates' nobles as hostages. He then told Abū Sufyān that Qurayẓah regretted breaking with Muḥammad and planned to take seventy nobles of Quraysh and Ghaṭafān and hand them over to be killed, and gave Ghaṭafān the same warning.[^s4.384][^s4.385]

Qurayẓah's envoy ʿAzzāl b. Samawʾal duly came asking for hostages, and Abū Sufyān said after he left, *"This is what Nuʿaym said."* On the eve of the Sabbath Abū Sufyān and the leaders of Ghaṭafān sent ʿIkrimah to tell Qurayẓah to prepare for a final assault. Qurayẓah answered that they would not fight on the Sabbath, nor at all without hostages. Quraysh and Ghaṭafān concluded that Nuʿaym had told the truth and refused to send a single man; Qurayẓah concluded the same in their turn. Only al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā urged his people to fight without hostages, and no one agreed with him.[^s4.385][^s4.386] So Allāh divided them at Nuʿaym's hands.[^s4.386]

## The wind

Allāh sent a wind on a cold winter night that overturned their pots and scattered their vessels.[^s4.386] In a report of Saʿīd b. Jubayr, Jibrīl ﵊ came with the wind, and the Prophet ﷺ, seeing him, said three times, *"Rejoice!"* It tore down their tents, overturned the pots, pulled up the tent-pegs and buried men, and they fled, no one waiting for anyone else. About this was revealed: *"When armies came against you, and We sent against them a wind and armies you did not see"* (al-Aḥzāb 33:9).[^s4.386] The Prophet ﷺ said: *"I was given victory by the east wind, and ʿĀd were destroyed by the west wind."*[^s4.386][^bukh4105] Mujāhid explained the unseen armies as the angels, adding that they did not fight that day.[^s4.387] According to Ibn Saʿd, ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ and Khālid b. al-Walīd stayed behind with two hundred horsemen to guard the army's rear.[^s4.389]

### Ḥudhayfah's night in the enemy camp

[[Ḥudhayfah b. al-Yamān]] ﵁ later described that night: the darkest and windiest they had ever known, the confederates above them and Qurayẓah below, the hypocrites slipping away one after another until about three hundred men were left. The Prophet ﷺ went along the line asking who would bring him news of the enemy and be with him on the Day of Resurrection; three times no one answered.[^s4.387] Abū Bakr suggested Ḥudhayfah, who had only his wife's wrap to protect him from the cold. The Prophet ﷺ told him no heat or cold would harm him until he returned, and prayed for his protection on every side. Ḥudhayfah said all his fear and cold left him, and he walked as if in a warm bath-house. As he went the Prophet ﷺ called him back: *"Ḥudhayfah, do nothing among the people until you come back to me."*[^s4.388]

In the enemy camp Ḥudhayfah saw by firelight a large dark man warming his hands and saying, *"Let us go, let us go."* He did not know Abū Sufyān by sight, and had an arrow fitted to his bow before he remembered the Prophet's command and held back. Abū Sufyān, sensing a stranger among them, told every man to take the hand of the one beside him. Ḥudhayfah grabbed his neighbours' hands first and asked who they were, and was told [[Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān]] and [[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]].[^s4.388] The wind, he said, went no more than a span beyond the confederates' camp, and he could hear the stones it threw striking their baggage. At dawn Abū Sufyān ordered them to load up, and leapt on a camel that was still hobbled and urged it on before it was untied.[^s4.388][^s4.389]

On his way back, Ḥudhayfah was met by about twenty turbaned horsemen — in another wording, two — who told him to tell his master that Allāh had sufficed him against the enemy with armies and wind. He found the Prophet ﷺ praying wrapped in a cloak; as the cold returned to him and he began to shiver, the Prophet ﷺ drew him in under the cloak and heard his news. Ḥudhayfah slept until morning, when the Prophet ﷺ woke him: *"Get up, sleeper."*[^s4.389] In al-Bukhārī there is also a report of al-Zubayr volunteering to bring news of the enemy at al-Aḥzāb; Ibn Ḥajar explains that al-Zubayr's mission was to confirm Qurayẓah's breach of the treaty, a different occasion from Ḥudhayfah's.[^s4.397][^s4.398]

## Aftermath

In the morning no part of the confederate army remained. When Allāh had driven the confederates away, the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Now we will march against them, and they will not march against us; we will go to them."* Ibn Isḥāq notes that Quraysh never came against him again, and that he marched against them from then on until the conquest of Makkah.[^s4.389] He used to say: *"There is no god but Allāh alone; He gave might to His army, helped His servant, and defeated the Confederates alone, and there is nothing after Him."*[^s4.389]

The Muslims were given leave to go home and hurried off gladly. The Prophet ﷺ, not wanting Qurayẓah to see how eager they were, sent men to call them back, but not one returned, for cold and hunger; Jābir, reporting this to him, made him laugh.[^s4.389][^s4.390] The hypocrites on the edge of Medina, not knowing the confederates had gone, were still saying the Muslims had not been destroyed yet.[^s4.390]

After the return from the trench, when the Prophet ﷺ had prayed Ẓuhr and was washing in ʿĀʾishah's house, Jibrīl ﵊ came in the form of Diḥyah al-Kalbī, dust on his face. He asked whether they had laid down their arms, when the angels had not, and told him Allāh commanded him to march against Banū Qurayẓah.[^s5.3][^s5.4] The campaign that followed is the subject of [[Ghazwat Banī Qurayẓah]].

The Muslims who died at the trench were eight, according to Ibn Isḥāq and al-Wāqidī: Saʿd b. Muʿādh, who died later of his wound; Anas b. Aws; ʿAbdullāh b. Sahl; al-Ṭufayl b. al-Nuʿmān; Thaʿlabah b. ʿAnamah, killed by Hubayrah b. Abī Wahb; and Kaʿb b. Zayd, who had survived [[Maʾsāt Biʾr Maʿūnah|Biʾr Maʿūnah]] badly wounded and was killed by Ḍirār b. al-Khaṭṭāb. al-Dimyāṭī adds Qays b. Zayd, ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Khālid, and Abū Sinān b. Ṣayfī, said to have fought at Badr.[^s4.390] Three of the idolaters were killed: ʿAmr b. ʿAbd Wudd by ʿAlī; Nawfal b. ʿAbdillāh b. al-Mughīrah by al-Zubayr, or by ʿAlī; and ʿUthmān b. Munabbih, who died in Makkah of an arrow wound received at the trench.[^s4.390]

## The Qurʾān and the poets

[[Sūrat al-Aḥzāb]] 33:9–25 is about the battle, which al-Ṣāliḥī sets out with a running commentary. The passage covers:

- the wind and the unseen armies;
- the enemy coming *"from above you and from below you"*;
- the hypocrites' taunts and their excuses;
- the Prophet ﷺ as the *"excellent example"* for those who hope in Allāh;
- the believers who *"were true to what they pledged to Allāh"*.

It ends: *"And Allāh turned back those who disbelieved in their rage; they gained nothing, and Allāh sufficed the believers in fighting."*[^s4.391][^s4.392]

[[Kaʿb b. Mālik]] ﵁ and Ḥassān b. Thābit answered the Meccan poets Ḍirār b. al-Khaṭṭāb and ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zibaʿrā in several poems on the siege. Ibn Hishām reports that when Kaʿb said, *"Sakhīnah came to overcome their Lord, and the one who tries to overcome the Overcomer will be overcome,"* the Prophet ﷺ told him: *"Allāh has thanked you, Kaʿb, for these words of yours."*[^s4.392][^s4.394]

## Date and duration

Ibn Isḥāq and those who follow him place the battle in Shawwāl; al-Wāqidī and Ibn Saʿd in Dhū al-Qaʿdah. The majority date it to the year 5, which al-Dhahabī calls certain and Ibn Ḥajar the reliable view. Ibn ʿUqbah, from al-Zuhrī, and Mālik date it to the year 4, and al-Bukhārī inclined to this. He supports it with Ibn ʿUmar's report that he was turned away at fourteen at Uḥud and accepted at fifteen at the trench.[^s4.396] Ibn Ḥajar and al-Bayhaqī answer that Ibn ʿUmar had just entered his fourteenth year at Uḥud and completed his fifteenth by the trench. al-Bayhaqī explains the disagreement by an older reckoning that counted the years from the first Muḥarram after the Hijrah; on that count Badr falls in the year 1, Uḥud in 2 and the trench in 4.[^s4.397]

How long the siege lasted is also disputed:[^s4.397]

- **About fifteen days:** the view al-Wāqidī considered best established, which Ibn Saʿd, al-Balādhurī and al-Nawawī adopt;
- **Twenty days:** from Jābir;
- **Twenty-four nights,** or **ten-odd nights:** from Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab, in different transmissions;
- **Twenty-odd nights, close to a month:** Ibn Isḥāq;
- **A month:** in *Zād al-Maʿād*.
