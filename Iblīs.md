# Iblīs (إبليس)
*The Adversary — His Three Appearances in the Sīrah*

## 1. At the Mawlid: Grief and Exclusion
Al-Ṣāliḥī opens with him at the very beginning: *al-Bāb al-ʿĀshir fī ḥuzn Iblīs wa-ḥajbihi min
al-samāwāt* — "the grief of Iblīs and his being barred from the heavens" at the birth of the
Prophet ﷺ, together with the voices heard at that hour (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:350). See
**[[Mawlid al-Nabī]]**.

## 2. At Badr: The Borrowed Likeness
At **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** he appeared among Quraysh **in the form of
[[Surāqah b. Mālik b. Juʿshum]]** ﵁ to harden their resolve for battle, and then broke and fled when
the descent of Jibrīl ﵊ and the angelic host became apparent (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:22, 4:42).

This is the incident the Qurʾān records:
> ﴿وَإِذْ زَيَّنَ لَهُمُ الشَّيْطَانُ أَعْمَالَهُمْ … فَلَمَّا تَرَاءَتِ الْفِئَتَانِ نَكَصَ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيْهِ﴾
> *"…and when the two hosts came in sight of one another, he turned upon his heels."* (8:48)

See **[[Sūrat al-Anfāl]]**.

## 3. At the Conquest: The Shriek
Al-Ṣāliḥī records his cry and grief again around the collapse of the Makkan order, alongside the
attempts of the jinn against the Prophet ﷺ and their repulsion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:232) — framing
**[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**.

## 4. Pattern
The three appearances bracket the Sīrah at its opening, its military turning point, and its
consummation — and in each he is shown *withdrawing*: barred from the heavens, turning on his heels,
crying out. The sources present him not as a rival power but as one whose designs are undone.
