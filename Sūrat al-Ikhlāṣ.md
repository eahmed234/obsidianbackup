# Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ (سورة الإخلاص)
*The Quintessence of Divine Unity and Transcendent Reality*

## 1. Overview and Core Meaning
**Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ** (The Chapter of Sincerity and Monotheism) constitutes the definitive theological definition of the Divine Essence in Islamic revelation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396). It negates multiplicity, composition, anthropomorphism, lineage, and physical descent from the Creator (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396).

## 2. Occasion of Revelation (*Sabab al-Nuzūl*)
Imām al-Ṣāliḥī cites transmissions from Anas b. Mālik, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and Qatādah that a group of inquiring antagonists—including prominent Jewish leaders such as Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf and Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab—confronted the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and asked:
> «يَا مُحَمَّدُ، هَذَا اللَّهُ خَلَقَ الْخَلْقَ فَمَنْ خَلَقَ اللَّهَ؟»
> *"O Muḥammad, this Allāh created creation; who then created Allāh?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396).

The Prophet ﷺ was filled with profound indignation for the majesty of his Lord until his complexion changed. Thereupon Jibrīl ﵊ descended to comfort him, saying: *«Calm yourself, O Muḥammad,»* and brought from Allāh the decisive answer (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396):

> «قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ»
> *"Say: He is Allāh, the One. Allāh, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396).

## 3. Theological Exposition & Divine Attributes
Imām al-Ṣāliḥī details the comprehensive semantic depth of the divine names and attributes in this sūrah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396):

- **al-Aḥad (The One)**: Derived linguistically from *al-Wāḥid*, pointing to all attributes of Divine Majesty (*ṣifāt al-jalāl*). The true One is inherently exalted above composite structure, multiplicity, corporeality (*jismiyyah*), spatial confinement (*taḥayyuz*), or physical boundaries (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396).
- **al-Ṣamad (The Eternal, Self-Sufficient Refuge)**: The Sovereign to whom all creatures turn continuously for their needs while He remains absolutely free of need from all creation. Classical authorities note that *al-Ṣamad* denotes:
  - The One consummate in absolute nobility and supremacy (*al-ladhī qad intahā fī suʾdudih*).
  - The One upon whose mercy and sustenance all creation depends.
  - The One who neither eats nor produces sustenance from Himself.
  - The Everlasting who endures after the extinction of all creation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396).
- **Negation of Descent and Equality**: «لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ» totally extinguishes every form of polytheistic parentage, childship, ancestral lineage, or peer, asserting absolute transcendence across existence (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:396).
