# al-Masjid al-Nabawī (المسجد النبوي الشريف)
*The Grand Sanctuary of Medina, the Abode of Revelation, the Noble Rawḍah, and the Center of the Caliphate*

> [!NOTE]
> **Canonical Sourcing**: This topographical profile is established upon *[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād]]* by Imām Muḥammad b. Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī (vol. 3, pp. 335–355; vol. 12, pp. 349–353).

---

## 1. Establishment & Historical Genesis (1 AH)
- **The Divine Halting of al-Qaṣwāʾ**: Upon arriving in Medina during **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**, the Prophet's she-camel was divinely directed to halt at an open date-drying yard (*mirbad*) belonging to two orphan boys, Sahl and Suhayl, under the guardianship of Asʿad b. Zurārah of **[[Banū al-Najjār]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335).
- The Prophet ﷺ dismounted, reciting: *«رَّبِّ أَنزِلْنِي مُنزَلًا مُّبَارَكًا وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ الْمُنزِلِينَ»* (*"My Lord, cause me to land at a blessed landing place, and You are the best to accommodate"*), and proclaimed: *«هَذَا الْمَنْزِلُ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ»* (*"This is the dwelling place, if Allāh wills"*).
- The land was acquired for ten golden dinars provided by **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:344).
- **Physical Foundations**: The Prophet ﷺ personally hauled mud-bricks (*al-labin*) and stones alongside the Muhājirūn and Anṣār, laying the bedrock foundation roughly three cubits deep, erecting walls of sun-dried adobe, columns of palm trunks, and a thatch roof of palm fronds (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).
- **Orientation of Prayer**: Initially faced northward toward Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem) for sixteen to seventeen months until the divine command of **[[Taḥwīl al-Qiblah]]** oriented the sanctuary southward toward the Kaʿbah in Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).

---

## 2. Supreme Spiritual Virtues & Preeminence
In canonical prophetic tradition, al-Masjid al-Nabawī holds unmatched spiritual rank among the sanctuaries of the earth (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:351–353):
- **The Mosque Founded upon Piety (*Masjid Ussisa ʿalā al-Taqwā*)**:
  Regarding the divine verse:
  > ﴿لَمَسْجِدٌ أُسِّسَ عَلَى التَّقْوَى مِنْ أَوَّلِ يَوْمٍ أَحَقُّ أَنْ تَقُومَ فِيهِ﴾
  > *"A mosque founded on righteousness from the first day is more worthy for you to stand in..."* (Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:108).
  The Prophet ﷺ explicitly confirmed to his Companions: *«It is this mosque of mine!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:351; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*).
- **Multiplication of Prayer**: The Prophet ﷺ stated:
  > «صَلَاةٌ فِي مَسْجِدِي هَذَا خَيْرٌ مِنْ أَلْفِ صَلَاةٍ فِيمَا سِوَاهُ، إِلَّا الْمَسْجِدَ الْحَرَامَ»
  > *"A single prayer in this mosque of mine is better than a thousand prayers anywhere else, except for al-Masjid al-Ḥarām."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:352; *al-Ṣaḥīḥayn*).
- **One of the Three Destinations of Sacred Journey**:
  > «لَا تُشَدُّ الرِّحَالُ إِلَّا إِلَى ثَلَاثَةِ مَسَاجِدَ: مَسْجِدِي هَذَا، وَالْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ، وَالْمَسْجِدِ الْأَقْصَى»
  > *"Mounts shall not be saddled [for sacred journey] except to three mosques: this mosque of mine, al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, and al-Masjid al-Aqṣā."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:352).

## 3. The Noble Rawḍah & The Sacred Chamber
- **al-Rawḍah al-Sharīfah**: The sacred area between the domestic apartment of **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ and the Prophetic Pulpit (*al-Minbar*), regarding which the Prophet ﷺ revealed:
  > «مَا بَيْنَ بَيْتِي وَمِنْبَرِي رَوْضَةٌ مِنْ رِيَاضِ الْجَنَّةِ»
  > *"Whatever is between my house and my pulpit is a garden from the gardens of Paradise."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:349).
  Scholars including Imām Mālik b. Anas understood this on its literal reality: that this earthly blessed piece of ground shall be translated directly into the Eternal Abode (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:349).
- **The Sacred Chambers (*al-Ḥujarāt*)**: Simple dwellings of mud-brick and palm thatch built adjoining the eastern and northern walls for the Mothers of the Believers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).
- **The Prophet's Tomb**: In the chamber of ʿĀʾishah, the Prophet ﷺ breathed his last on 12 Rabīʿ al-Awwal 11 AH and was buried directly beneath his bed according to the apostolic rule narrated by Abū Bakr: *«No prophet dies except that he is buried where he passes»*. Beside him lie his two companions, **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:321).
- **Ahl al-Ṣuffah**: The shaded rear bench where destitute and unmarried Companions—including **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁—resided dedicated exclusively to scripture, memorization, and worship (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:340).
