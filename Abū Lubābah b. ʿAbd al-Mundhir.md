# Abū Lubābah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir (أبو لبابة بن عبد المنذر)
*The Nuqayb of al-Aws — Deputy Over Medina and Companion of the Pillar of Repentance*

## 1. Lineage & Early Islam
Abū Lubābah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir b. Zanbar b. Zayd b. Umayyah b. Zayd b. Mālik b. ʿAwf b. ʿAmr b. ʿAwf b. Mālik b. al-Aws al-Anṣārī al-Awsī was a senior chieftain of Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf of **[[al-Aws]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290). Authorities differ regarding his given name: Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn Numayr state that his name was Rifāʿah, while others give it as Bashīr or Marwān (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290). His brothers included Rifāʿah and Mubashshir b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir (a martyr of Badr) (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:215).

He married Khansāʾ bint Khidām b. Khālid al-Anṣāriyyah from Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf; after her father had previously forced her into a marriage she disliked, the Prophet ﷺ annulled that forced union, whereupon Abū Lubābah proposed to her and married her, fathering his son al-Sāʾib (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290; 8:108). His other son was ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290).

Abū Lubābah embraced Islam early, attended **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]]**, and was chosen as one of the twelve *nuqabāʾ* representing the Anṣār (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290).

## 2. Deputy Over Medina During Badr (2 AH)
When the Muslim forces mobilized toward **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, Abū Lubābah initially marched out with the Prophet ﷺ. At al-Rawḥāʾ, the Prophet ﷺ turned him back to Medina and appointed him civil governor and deputy over the capital for the duration of the campaign (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:23; *al-Iṣābah*, 7:290). The Prophet ﷺ assigned him a combatant's share of the spoils and spiritual reward, counting him among the veterans of Ahl Badr (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290).

At the conquest of Makkah, Abū Lubābah carried the tribal standard of Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290).

## 3. Banū Qurayẓah and the Pillar of Repentance (5 AH)
During the siege of **[[Ghazwat Banī Qurayẓah]]**, the besieged Jewish clan—long-standing pre-Islamic confederates of al-Aws—pleaded with the Prophet ﷺ to send Abū Lubābah to consult them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:8; *al-Iṣābah*, 7:290). When he entered their fortresses, their women and children wept before him. They asked: *"O Abū Lubābah, shall we submit to the judgment of Muḥammad?"* He replied: *"Yes,"* but pointed to his throat, indicating that unconditional surrender would mean execution (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:8).

Immediately realizing that he had betrayed the trust of Allāh and His Messenger, Abū Lubābah did not return to the Prophet ﷺ. Instead, he went straight to the **[[Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī|Prophet's Mosque]]** in Medina, tied himself securely to one of the mosque's pillars (*Isṭiwānat al-Tawbah*), and vowed:
> *"By Allāh, I shall not depart from this spot, nor taste food or drink, until Allāh forgives me, and none shall untie me save the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ with his own hand!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:8–24).

He remained bound there for several days until his repentance was revealed to the Prophet ﷺ in the early morning at the dwelling of **[[Umm Salamah]]** ﵂ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:8). The Prophet ﷺ came personally to the mosque and untied him with his own blessed hands (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:8).

## 4. Demise & Legacy
Abū Lubābah transmitted several hadiths from the Prophet ﷺ. Among those who narrated from him were his two sons al-Sāʾib and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar]]** ﵁, Sālim b. ʿAbdillāh, Nāfiʿ, and ʿAbdullāh b. Kaʿb b. Mālik (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290). He lived through the caliphates of the Rightly Guided Caliphs and passed away during the caliphate of ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib or in the early years of Muʿāwiyah (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:290).
