# Muḍar b. Nizār (مضر بن نزار)
*Muḍar al-Ḥamrāʾ — Praised by the Prophet ﷺ as Having Died Upon the Religion of Ibrāhīm ﵊*

## 1. The Testament of Nizār and the Four Brothers
Nizār left an inheritance to be divided among his four sons — **Muḍar**, Rabīʿah, Iyād, and Anmār —
and instructed them, if they disputed the division, to bring their case to **al-Afʿā al-Jurhumī** at
Najrān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:290).

## 2. The Tracking of the Lost Camel
On their way to Najrān, the four brothers passed grazed pasture and each deduced a detail about a
camel none of them had seen: Muḍar said it was **one-eyed**; Rabīʿah, that it was **lame in one leg**;
Iyād, that it was **tailless**; Anmār, that it was a **stray**. A traveller soon met them searching for
his lost camel, and each detail proved true. Suspecting them of theft, he pursued them to Najrān,
where al-Afʿā al-Jurhumī heard the case. Muḍar explained: *"I saw that it grazed on one side and left
the other, so I knew it was one-eyed"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:290–291). The judge accepted their
deductions and directed the traveller to search elsewhere.

## 3. The Judgment on the Inheritance
Al-Afʿā then settled the brothers' own dispute over Nizār's estate:

> *"Whatever resembles the **red tent** of wealth is for **Muḍar**"* — so the gold coins and camels
> went to him, and **Muḍar was named "the Red" (al-Ḥamrāʾ)** for it.
> *"Whatever resembles the black tent, of mount and wealth, is for Rabīʿah"* — the dark horses went to
> him, and Rabīʿah was named for the horse.
> *"Whatever resembles the grizzled maidservant, of piebald livestock, is for Iyād."*
> Anmār was awarded the silver coin and the land. (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:291)

Each brother, asked afterward how he had known his portion, explained his reasoning: Muḍar knew wine
by his own great thirst; Rabīʿah knew meat because a dog's fat rises to the top unlike a sheep's; Iyād
knew of a lineage not his true father's because the man had laid out food and not sat to share it —
implying a base origin (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:291).

## 4. First to Call the Camels
Al-Balādhurī, from Muḥammad b. al-Sāʾib, reports that **Muḍar was the first to chant *ḥidāʾ* (the
camel-driving song)**: as a young man he fell from his camel and broke his hand, crying *"yā yadāh,
yā yadāh!"* — and the camels, hearing his cry, came to him from their grazing. When he had recovered
and rode again, he sang, and he had one of the finest voices among men (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:291).

## 5. Prophetic Praise
Al-Suhaylī transmits the ḥadīth:

> «لَا تَسُبُّوا رَبِيعَةَ وَمُضَرَ فَإِنَّهُمَا كَانَا مُؤْمِنَيْنِ»
> *"Do not curse Rabīʿah and Muḍar, for the two of them were believers."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:291)

And Ibn Ḥibbān transmits, from **Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab**, that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> «لَا تَسُبُّوا مُضَرَ فَإِنَّهُ كَانَ عَلَى مِلَّةِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ»
> *"Do not curse Muḍar, for he was upon the religion (millah) of Ibrāhīm."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:291)

## 6. His Son
The book names among his children **[[Ilyās b. Muḍar|Ilyās]]** and **Qays ʿAylān**, the latter said
either to be Muḍar's own son or the fosterling of a man named ʿAylān who raised Muḍar's son Qays
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:292).
