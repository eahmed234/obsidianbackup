# Lubaybah (لبيبة) ﵂
*The Steadfast Believer of Banū al-Muʾammal — Severe Trials in Makkah and Emancipation by al-Ṣiddīq*

## 1. Nomenclature & Social Status
Lubaybah is commemorated among the heroic, defenseless early female believers (*al-mustaḍʿafāt*) who endured severe physical torture for their devotion to Allāh:
- **Tribal Attachment**: She was an enslaved maidservant (*jāriyah*) owned by the Banū al-Muʾammal clan of [[Banū ʿAdī]] b. Kaʿb—specifically Banū al-Muʾammal b. Ḥabīb b. Tamīm b. ʿAbdillāh b. Qurṭ b. Razāḥ b. ʿAdī b. Kaʿb, the very clan of [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]] (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:301, entry 11705).
- **Naming in Classical Sīrah**: In the majority of early Maghāzī and Ḥadīth transmissions, she is referred to anonymously by her patronymic designation as *Jāriyat Banī al-Muʾammal* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:301). Al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar and Imām al-Ṣāliḥī record that the renowned historian al-Balādhurī explicitly identified her by her personal name **Lubaybah** upon the authority of Abū al-Bakhtarī (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:301; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361).
- **Early Islam**: She embraced Islam at the dawn of the Makkan call, accepting the message of tawḥīd before the conversion of ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361).

## 2. Persecution by ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb (Prior to his Islam)
Before his dramatic entry into Islam in the sixth year of the Prophetic mission, ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb was among the fiercest opponents of the believers and took charge of punishing the enslaved converts belonging to his clan (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361).

Because Lubaybah belonged to Banū al-Muʾammal (a branch of Banū ʿAdī), ʿUmar subjected her to relentless physical beatings and strangulation to force her to renounce Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361):
- **The Dialogue of Endurance**: ʿUmar would beat her continuously throughout the day until his arms grew completely exhausted (*ḥattā yaftur*). Pausing from fatigue, he would say to her in frustration: *"Verily, I only apologize to you that I leave you out of weariness, not out of mercy!"* Lubaybah would look up at him with unyielding faith and reply (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361):
  > «كَذَلِكَ يُعَذِّبُكَ رَبُّكَ إِنْ لَمْ تُسْلِمْ!»
  > *"Even so shall your Lord torment you if you do not submit to Islam!"*

### Eyewitness Account of Ḥassān b. Thābit
The poet of the Prophet ﷺ, **[[Ḥassān b. Thābit]]** ﵁, recalled witnessing her torture firsthand while visiting Makkah as a pilgrim before his own conversion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361):
> *"I arrived in Makkah performing ʿUmrah while the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions were being harmed and tormented. I stood watching ʿUmar, clad in a waist-wrapper (*mutawazzir*), violently throttling the slave-girl of Banū al-Muʾammal until she became completely limp in his hands, so that I said: 'She has died!'"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361).

## 3. Emancipation by Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq
Witnessing her dire plight and the brutality inflicted upon her, **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ intervened (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361; *al-Iṣābah*, 8:301). He purchased Lubaybah from her pagan masters for a substantial price and immediately emancipated her for the sake of Allāh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:361).

She was counted among the seven vulnerable enslaved believers whom Abū Bakr ransomed and freed from torture in Makkah—alongside [[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]], [[ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah]], [[Zunnīrah al-Rūmiyyah]], [[Umm ʿUnays]], [[al-Nahdiyyah]], and [[Abū Fukayhah]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360–361; *al-Iṣābah*, 8:301).
