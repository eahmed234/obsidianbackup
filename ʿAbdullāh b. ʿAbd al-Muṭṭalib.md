# ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib (عبد الله بن عبد المطلب)
*The Father of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ — the Youngest and Most Beloved Son, Twice Redeemed*

## 1. Identity
The most beloved son of **[[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]**, of **[[Banū Hāshim]]**, and — al-Ṣāliḥī states —
his **youngest** son (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:245). His father arranged his marriage to
**[[Āminah bint Wahb]]** of **[[Banū Zuhrah]]** — the subject of a dedicated chapter on why that
particular match was sought (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:325).

His mother was **Fāṭimah bint ʿAmr b. ʿĀʾidh b. ʿImrān b. Makhzūm**. Ibn Ḥazm and Ibn Saʿd are both
cited stating that **ʿAbdullāh had no offspring at all besides the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ** — no son and
no daughter beyond him — and that Āminah likewise bore no child but him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:246).

## 2. Ibn al-Dhabīḥayn — "Son of the Two Redeemed"
When [[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]] was commanded in a dream to re-dig **[[Zamzam]]** and had at that time only
one son, **al-Ḥārith**, he vowed that if he were given ten sons who lived to manhood, he would sacrifice
one of them at the Kaʿbah. When the ten had grown, he gathered them, told them of his vow, and asked
each to write his name upon a divining arrow. Taken before **Hubal** inside the Kaʿbah, the arrows were
cast — **ʿAbdullāh's name was drawn**, being (as al-Ṣāliḥī notes, on the authority of Ibn Isḥāq) *"the
youngest of his father's sons and the most beloved to him."* ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib took him by the hand and
the blade to slaughter him at the idols Isāf and Nāʾilah; **[[al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁
pulled him from beneath his father's leg (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:245).

Quraysh intervened, refusing to let the sacrifice proceed, and — at the counsel of al-Mughīrah b.
ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Makhzūm, ʿAbdullāh's maternal kinsman — the matter was referred to a soothsayer at
Khaybar with a jinn-familiar. She instructed them to cast lots between ʿAbdullāh and **ten camels**,
raising the stake by ten each time the lot fell on ʿAbdullāh, until at **one hundred camels** the lot
finally fell on the camels. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib insisted on casting three times more to be certain, and
each time the lot fell on the camels; the hundred were slaughtered and left for anyone — man or beast
— to eat freely (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:245–246).

Al-Zuhrī notes: *"ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib was the first to establish the blood-price of a life at one hundred
camels, and it continued among Quraysh and the Arabs, and the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ confirmed it"*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:246).

The book records that a Bedouin once addressed the Prophet ﷺ as *"Ibn al-Dhabīḥayn"* — "son of the two
redeemed" — and the Prophet ﷺ smiled and did not object. Asked who the two redeemed were, Muʿāwiyah ﵁
answered: **Ismāʿīl ﵊ and ʿAbdullāh** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:246) — joining the near-sacrifice of
ʿAbdullāh to that of Ismāʿīl ﵊ himself.

## 3. Death
He died young, on a trading journey, while [[Āminah bint Wahb]] was carrying the Prophet ﷺ — so his
son was born already fatherless. Al-Ṣāliḥī devotes a chapter to it: *al-Bāb al-Thālith fī wafāt
ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:331).

His death is the first of the three bereavements that framed the Prophet's ﷺ childhood — father
before birth, mother at a young age (see [[Āminah bint Wahb]]), grandfather at eight (see
[[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]) — the condition the Qurʾān addresses in *«أَلَمْ يَجِدْكَ يَتِيمًا فَآوَىٰ»*
(93:6). Guardianship then passed to **[[Abū Ṭālib]]**.
