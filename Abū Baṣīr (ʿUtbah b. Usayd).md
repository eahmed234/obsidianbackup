# Abū Baṣīr (ʿUtbah b. Usayd) (أبو بصير عتبة بن أسيد الثقفي)
*The Hero of al-ʿĪṣ — Striker of the Coastal Redoubt and Breaker of the Extradition Clause*

## 1. Lineage & Background
His full name and lineage is **ʿUtbah b. Usayd b. Jāriyah b. Usayd b. ʿAbdillāh b. Ghīrah b. ʿAwf b. Thaqīf al-Thaqafī** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359). He was widely known by his *kunyah* **Abū Baṣīr** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359). Although belonging by blood to the prominent tribe of Thaqīf, he resided in Makkah as a confederate ally (*ḥalīf*) of **[[Banū Zuhrah]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:61). 

Ibn Ḥajar clarifies that his name is agreed upon by scholars as *ʿUtbah*, and that those who recorded his name as *ʿUbayd* committed a clerical corruption (*ṣaḥḥaf*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359).

## 2. Early Islam & Imprisonment in Makkah
Abū Baṣīr embraced Islam in Makkah during the era of persecution, but was numbered among the vulnerable and oppressed believers (*al-mustaḍʿafīn*) prevented by Quraysh from migrating to Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:61). When the Prophet ﷺ concluded the historic treaty of **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]** with Quraysh in Dhū al-Qaʿdah 6 AH—stipulating that any man who came from Quraysh to Medina without the permission of his guardian must be extradited back to Makkah—Abū Baṣīr seized an opportunity, broke free from confinement, and escaped on foot across the desert to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:61).

## 3. Extradition Demand & The Slaying at Dhū al-Ḥulayfah
Soon after his arrival in Medina, two leaders of Banū Zuhrah—[[al-Akhnas b. Sharīq]] and Azhar b. ʿAbd ʿAwf—sent a formal letter carried by two emissaries (a man from Banū ʿĀmir b. Luʾayy and his *mawlā*) to the Prophet ﷺ demanding the enforcement of the extradition article (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:61).

Fulfilling the solemn oath of the covenant, the Prophet ﷺ handed Abū Baṣīr over to the two guards, counselling him to place his trust in Divine deliverance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:61). The two captors bound him and set out toward Makkah. When they reached **Dhū al-Ḥulayfah**, approximately six or seven miles outside Medina, they dismounted beneath the shade of a wall to eat dates (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:61). 

Abū Baṣīr engaged the ʿĀmirī guardsman in friendly conversation, admiring his fine sword:
> «يا أخا بني عامر، أصارم سيفك هذا؟ قال: نعم، قال: أنظر إليه؟ قال: إن شئت»
> *"O brother of Banū ʿĀmir, is this sword of yours truly sharp? He replied: 'Yes.' Abū Baṣīr said: 'May I inspect it?' The man answered: 'If you wish.'"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:61).

The moment Abū Baṣīr gripped the blade, he drew it and struck the ʿĀmirī down (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:61). The *mawlā* panicked and fled on foot back to Medina, running so violently that dust and gravel flew from beneath his sandals (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:62). As the terrified guard burst into the Prophet's Mosque during the ʿAṣr assembly, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ remarked:
> «لَقَدْ رَأَى هَذَا ذُعْرًا»
> *"This man has indeed seen a terrifying sight!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:62).

The guard cried out that his companion had been slain and begged for sanctuary. Moments later, Abū Baṣīr rode up on the slain captor's camel, entered the mosque girt with the sword, and said:
> «يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، قَدْ وَفَتْ ذِمَّتُكَ وَأَدَّى اللهُ عَنْكَ، قَدْ أَسْلَمْتَنِي بِيَدِ الْعَدُوِّ فَامْتَنَعْتُ بِدِينِي أَنْ أُفْتَنَ فِيهِ أَوْ يُعْبَثَ بِي»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh! Your pledge has been fully honored and Allāh has absolved your covenant: you delivered me into the hands of the enemy, and I defended my religion lest I be seduced from it or mocked!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:62).

Observing Abū Baṣīr's ferocious independence and martial resolve, the Prophet ﷺ uttered his legendary pronouncement:
> «وَيْلُ أُمِّهِ مِسْعَرَ حَرْبٍ لَوْ كَانَ مَعَهُ رِجَالٌ!»
> *"Woe to his mother! An igniter of war (*misʿara ḥarb*), if only he had men with him!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:62).

## 4. Establishing the Coastal Bastion at al-ʿĪṣ
Recognizing that remaining in Medina would subject him to renewed extradition under treaty terms, Abū Baṣīr departed immediately and established an independent camp on the Red Sea coastal route at **al-ʿĪṣ** in the territory of Dhū al-Marwah (near Baṭn Idam) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:62–63).

When the news of the Prophet's words reached Makkah, **[[Abū Jandal b. Suhayl]]** ﵁ broke his chains and escaped to join Abū Baṣīr (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:58; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:63). Other oppressed Muslims fleeing Makkah followed, accompanied by warriors from the surrounding tribes of Banū Ghifār, Aslam, and Juhaynah, until a formidable autonomous guerrilla brigade of three hundred fighters had assembled under their command (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:63). Abū Baṣīr deferentially yielded command of the prayers to Abū Jandal due to the latter's noble Qurashī lineage (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:63).

Operating outside the legal jurisdiction of the Madinan state, Abū Baṣīr's force blockaded the primary maritime trade corridor between Makkah and the Levant:
> «لَا تَمُرُّ بِهِمْ عِيرٌ لِقُرَيْشٍ إِلَّا أَخَذُوهَا وَقَتَلُوا مَنْ فِيهَا، وَضَيَّقُوا عَلَى قُرَيْشٍ»
> *"Not a single trade caravan of Quraysh passed them except that they seized it and executed those escorting it, placing Quraysh in an intolerable strangulation."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:63).

Desperate and economically crippled, the leadership of Quraysh dispatched urgent envoys to Medina begging the Prophet ﷺ by the sacred bonds of kinship to revoke the extradition clause entirely, pleading that any fugitive who reached Medina be granted full asylum (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:63).

## 5. Death & Burial at al-ʿĪṣ
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ dispatched a written epistle to Abū Baṣīr and Abū Jandal summoning them and their men to Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:63). 

However, when the courier reached al-ʿĪṣ, Abū Baṣīr was lying on his deathbed stricken with severe illness. He passed away holding the blessed letter of the Prophet ﷺ pressed against his chest (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:63). 

**[[Abū Jandal b. Suhayl]]** ﵁ ritually washed and shrouded him, led the funeral prayer over him, buried him at his encampment at al-ʿĪṣ, and erected a mosque over his gravesite (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:359; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:63). Abū Jandal then led the remaining three hundred fighters to join the Prophet ﷺ in Medina.
