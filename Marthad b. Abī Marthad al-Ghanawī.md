# Marthad b. Abī Marthad al-Ghanawī (مرثد بن أبي مرثد الغنوي) ﵁
*Hero of the Makkah Prisoner Rescues, Badri Cavalier, and Commander-Martyr of al-Rajīʿ*

## 1. Lineage & Confederacy
**Marthad b. Abī Marthad** was an early Muhājir Companion whose father was the venerable Sahābī **Abū Marthad Kannāz b. al-Ḥuṣayn** (بنون ثقيلة وزاي) (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56; 7:305).

His complete genealogical chain ascends: **Marthad b. Abī Marthad Kannāz b. Ḥiṣn b. Yarbūʿ b. ʿAmr b. Kharashah b. Saʿd b. Ṭarīf b. Jallān b. Ghanm b. Ghanī b. Yaʿṣur b. Saʿd b. Qays b. Ghaylān b. Muḍar al-Ghanawī** (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:305; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:39). 

Both Marthad and his father Abū Marthad ﵄ entered into a sworn confederacy (*ḥilf*) in Makkah with the Prophet's ﷺ paternal uncle **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ and **[[Banū Hāshim]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56; 7:305). Following the Hijrah to Medina, they were among the foremost Muhājirūn who dedicated their lives to the defense of Islam.

## 2. Military Service & Sīrah Milestones

### The Battle of Badr al-Kubrā
Both Marthad and his father Abū Marthad participated in **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56; 7:305). 

During the arduous march from Medina to Badr, seventy camels were shared in rotation among more than three hundred men (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:24). According to authoritative transmissions cited by Ibn Isḥāq, Mūsā b. ʿUqbah, al-Dhahabī, and Ibn al-Qayyim, Marthad b. Abī Marthad and **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ were the two companions assigned to share a single riding camel in rotation with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:24).

Marthad was also distinguished as one of the very few mounted cavalrymen at Badr. In the narration of Yazīd b. Rūmān recorded by Ibn Saʿd, the Muslims had three horses at Badr: a horse belonging to al-Miqdād b. al-Aswad, a horse belonging to al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām, and a third horse ridden by Marthad b. Abī Marthad al-Ghanawī, known by the name **al-Sayl** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:25).

He subsequently participated in **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, defending the Prophet ﷺ alongside the steadfast believers.

### Prophetic Security Guard
Owing to his prowess and loyalty, Marthad was selected as one of the dedicated swordsmen and security guards who guarded the person of the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:399).

### Liberation of Muslim Prisoners in Makkah & Sūrat al-Nūr
Marthad was a man of immense physical stature, swiftness, and bravery (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56). Following the Hijrah, he undertook daring clandestine missions into polytheist Makkah to physically carry weakened and captive Muslim prisoners out of the city across the mountains to safety in Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56). 

During these operations, he was recognized in Makkah by a woman named ʿAnāq with whom he had acquaintance during the Jāhiliyyah. When she invited him, he firmly refused, stating that Islam forbade unlawful relations. She threatened to expose him to Quraysh, but Allāh shielded him from their pursuit (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56). Marthad later consulted the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ about whether he might marry her, whereupon Allāh revealed the verse:
> «الزَّانِي لَا يَنْكِحُ إِلَّا زَانِيَةً أَوْ مُشْرِكَةً وَالزَّانِيَةُ لَا يَنْكِحُهَا إِلَّا زَانٍ أَوْ مُشْرِكٌ وَحُرِّمَ ذَلِكَ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ»
> *"The fornicator does not marry except a [female] fornicator or polytheist, and none marries her except a fornicator or a polytheist, and that has been made unlawful to the believers."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56).

The Prophet ﷺ recited the revelation to him and commanded: *«Do not marry her»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56).

## 3. Martyrdom at al-Rajīʿ
In Ṣafar of the third year of the Hijrah, a delegation of tribesmen from ʿAḍal and al-Qārah came to Medina professing Islam and requesting instructors to teach them the Qurʾān and religious duties (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:40). 

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ dispatched a detachment of his companions and appointed over them **Marthad b. Abī Marthad al-Ghanawī** (and in other sound reports, **[[ʿĀṣim b. Thābit]]** ﵁) (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:39–40).

When the party reached al-Hadʾah between ʿUsfān and Makkah near the well of al-Rajīʿ, the guides betrayed them and summoned approximately two hundred armed fighters of Banū Liḥyān from the tribe of Hudhayl (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:40). The Muslims took refuge upon an elevated knoll (*qardad* or *fadfad*) in Wādī Ghurrān. When the enemy offered false promises of safety, the believers refused to surrender into the custody of polytheists. Marthad b. Abī Marthad fought resolutely with bow and sword until he fell as a martyr under the enemy's arrows alongside ʿĀṣim b. Thābit and their comrades (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:40).

## 4. Transmission & Legacy
Ibn Ḥajar records that hadith transmissions from Marthad b. Abī Marthad al-Ghanawī were related by al-Qāsim b. Abī ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Shāmī (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56). Among these is the prophetic exhortation:
> «إِنْ سَرَّكُمْ أَنْ تُقْبَلَ مِنْكُمْ صَلَاتُكُمْ فَلْيَؤُمَّكُمْ خِيَارُكُمْ»
> *"If it pleases you that your prayer be accepted from you, then let the best of you lead you in prayer."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56).

And in the narration of al-Ṭabarānī:
> «فَلْيَؤُمَّكُمْ عُلَمَاؤُكُمْ، فَإِنَّهُمْ وَفْدُكُمْ فِيمَا بَيْنَكُمْ وَبَيْنَ رَبِّكُمْ»
> *"Let your scholars lead you, for indeed they are your delegation between you and your Lord."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:56).
