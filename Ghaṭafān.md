# Ghaṭafān (غطفان)
*The Formidable Northern Bedouin Confederation of Najd*

## 1. Lineage & Territory
Ghaṭafān is a major branch of the northern Qays ʿAylān branch of ʿAdnānī Arabs, descended from **Ghaṭafān b. Saʿd b. Qays ʿAylān** b. Muḍar (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:280). Their sprawling confederacy encompassed numerous powerful nomadic tribes inhabiting the vast arid plateau of Najd stretching northeast toward Khaybar, primarily:
- **Banū Fazārah**, led by the haughty chieftain ʿUyaynah b. Ḥiṣn b. Ḥudhayfah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:364).
- **Banū Murrah**, commanded by al-Ḥārith b. ʿAwf (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:364).
- **Banū Ashjaʿ**, led by Masʿūd b. Rukhaylah and **[[Nuʿaym b. Masʿūd]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:364, 382).
- **Banū Thaʿlabah** and Banū Anmār (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:280).

Known for their rugged martial endurance, predatory raids, and swift cavalry, they constituted one of the most formidable military threats to Medina throughout the early Madinan era.

## 2. Key Sīrah Milestones
- **Coalition at the Trench (5 AH)**: Instigated by Jewish emissaries from Banū al-Naḍīr who promised them the entire annual date harvest of Khaybar, Ghaṭafān mobilized thousands of warriors (estimated between 2,000 and 4,000 fighters across Fazārah, Murrah, and Ashjaʿ) to form the eastern wing of the confederate army at **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:364).
- **Diplomatic Manoeuvres**: To alleviate the desperate siege, the Prophet ﷺ entered tentative negotiations with ʿUyaynah b. Ḥiṣn and al-Ḥārith b. ʿAwf, offering them one-third of the date harvest of Medina on the condition they withdraw their forces (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:372–373). When the Prophet ﷺ consulted the Anṣārī chieftains **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁ and **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁, they asked whether this was divine revelation or human strategy; learning it was human strategy to lift the pressure, they boldly tore up the draft treaty, declaring: *“By Allāh, we will give them nothing except the sword until Allāh judges between us!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:373).
- **The Dissolution by Nuʿaym b. Masʿūd**: One of their prominent noblemen, **[[Nuʿaym b. Masʿūd]]** al-Ashjaʿī ﵁, arrived secretly before the Prophet ﷺ and accepted Islam. Acting on the Prophetic maxim *“War is deceit”*, Nuʿaym sowed mutual distrust between Ghaṭafān, Quraysh, and **[[Banū Qurayẓah]]**, completely paralyzing the coalition until a freezing hurricane tore up their camp, forcing Ghaṭafān to retreat into Najd (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:382–386).
- **Severing the Alliance at Khaybar (7 AH)**: During **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]**, Ghaṭafān mobilized to aid their Jewish allies in exchange for half their agricultural wealth. The Prophet ﷺ marched along a northern route to cut them off, while divine panic fell upon Ghaṭafān as they heard mysterious voices shouting behind them that their own families and herds were under attack, causing them to abandon Khaybar and rush back to protect their territory (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:117).

## 3. Conversion to Islam
Following the Conquest of Mecca and the Battle of Ḥunayn, delegations from Ghaṭafān—including Banū Ashjaʿ and eventually Banū Fazārah—arrived in Medina to pledge allegiance to Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:137; 6:300).
