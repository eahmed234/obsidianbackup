# Badr (بدر)
*The Valley of the Criterion — The Watershed Battlefield of Islam*

## 1. Topography & Historical Significance
Badr is a desert oasis valley nestled between rugged hills along the ancient trade artery connecting the Ḥijāz with Syria and the Red Sea coast. Prior to Islam, it served as an established annual commercial gathering ground and seasonal fair for the Arab tribes (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29: «وكان بدر موسما من مواسم العرب، يجتمع لهم به سوق كل عام»).

The valley became the theater of the decisive military encounter in early Islamic history: **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** (*Yawm al-Furqān*), fought on Friday, 17 Ramaḍān 2 AH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:18, 30). In the Qurʾānic description, the two armies converged upon the valley without prior coordination:
> ﴿إِذْ أَنتُم بِالْعُدْوَةِ الدُّنْيَا وَهُم بِالْعُدْوَةِ الْقُصْوَىٰ وَالرَّكْبُ أَسْفَلَ مِنكُمْ وَلَوْ تَوَاعَدتُّمْ لَاخْتَلَفْتُمْ فِي الْمِيعَادِ﴾
> *"When you were on the nearer slope [al-ʿUdwah al-Dunyā], and they were on the farther slope [al-ʿUdwah al-Quṣwā], and the caravan was below you. Even if you had agreed upon a meeting, you would have failed in it…"* (Sūrat al-Anfāl 8:42; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:31).

## 2. The Battlefield Topography & The Water Wells
The valley possesses key natural contours that dictated the tactics of the battle:
- **Al-ʿUdwah al-Dunyā (العدوة الدنيا)**: The northern, nearer incline situated closer to Medina, occupied by the Prophet ﷺ and the three hundred and thirteen Muslim warriors (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29, 31).
- **Al-ʿUdwah al-Quṣwā (العدوة القصوى)**: The southern, farther incline behind the great sand-ridge (*al-ʿAqanqal*), where the one-thousand-strong host of Quraysh descended into the valley (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29, 31).
- **The Wells of Badr (قُلُب بدر)**: When the Prophet ﷺ initially encamped by the first water of Badr, **[[al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir]]** ﵁ respectfully asked whether this position was divine revelation or human military tactical maneuvering (*al-raʾy wal-ḥarb wal-makīdah*). Upon learning it was tactical, al-Ḥubāb advised advancing directly to the well closest to the enemy, filling in the remaining wells behind them, and constructing an embankment basin (*ḥawḍ*) so the believers could drink and deny water to Quraysh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:30). The Prophet ﷺ praised his counsel and immediately executed the maneuver.
- **The Divine Rain**: On the eve of the battle, Allāh sent down rainfall upon the valley: upon the polytheists it fell as a blinding, torrential deluge (*wābil shadīd*) obstructing their movement, while upon the Muslims it fell as a gentle shower (*ṭall*) that purified them, solidified the shifting sands underfoot, and dispelled the whispers of Shayṭān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29–30; Qurʾān 8:11).

## 3. Notable Landmarks within the Valley
- **[[al-ʿArīsh]] (العريش)**: The royal command palm-branch booth constructed on the elevated slope of the valley under the initiative of **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁. Here the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ stood in fervent supplication throughout the night and morning of the battle (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:30, 36–37).
- **Al-Qalīb (القليب)**: The dry pit-well into which the corpses of twenty-four chieftains of Quraysh — including **[[Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)]]**, **[[ʿUtbah b. Rabīʿah]]**, Umayyah b. Khalaf, and Shaybah b. Rabīʿah — were cast following the Muslim victory (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:101, 107).
- **Jabal al-Malāʾikah (جبل الملائكة)**: The hill associated with the descent of the angelic legions dispatched by Allāh under Jibrīl ﵊ to support the believers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:38–41).
