# Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī (أبو أيوب الأنصاري) ﵁
*Khālid b. Zayd al-Najjārī al-Khazrajī — Host of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and The Steadfast Warrior Buried at Constantinople*

## 1. Lineage, Clan Associations & Brotherhood
He is Khālid b. Zayd b. Kulayb b. Thaʿlabah b. ʿAbd ʿAwf b. Ghanam b. Mālik b. al-Najjār al-Anṣārī al-Khazrajī al-Najjārī (famously known by his kunya: Abū Ayyūb) (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:199–200). His mother was **Hind bint Saʿīd b. ʿAmr**, from the clan of Banū al-Ḥārith b. al-Khazraj (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200). Through Banū al-Najjār, Abū Ayyūb belonged to the maternal clan of the Prophet's grandfather **[[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]**.

Abū Ayyūb was among the early converts of Yathrib, participating in [[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]], fighting at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, and witnessing all subsequent expeditions (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200). Following the Hijrah, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ established fraternal brotherhood (**[[al-Muʾākhāh]]**) between Abū Ayyūb and **[[Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200).

## 2. Hosting the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ in Medina
When the Prophet ﷺ entered Medina at the conclusion of **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**, every clan of the Anṣār stepped forward grasping the halter of his camel al-Qaṣwāʾ, begging him to lodge with them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:275). The Prophet ﷺ bade them to let her walk freely, for she was guided by divine command. She knelt at the site of the future Prophet's Mosque before the door of Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:275; *al-Iṣābah*, 2:200). Abū Ayyūb eagerly carried the Prophet's saddle-bags into his house, securing the highest honor in Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200).

The Prophet ﷺ resided in Abū Ayyūb’s two-story residence while **[[Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]** and the adjoining apartments for his wives were constructed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:275). Several notable events marked this stay:
- **The Broken Water Vessel**: The Prophet ﷺ initially chose to stay on the ground floor for the convenience of his visitors. One night, a water vessel shattered on the upper floor where Abū Ayyūb and his wife Umm Ayyūb slept. Having only a single velvet cloak (*qaṭīfah*) for warmth, they spent the night wiping the spilled water with their cloak, terrified lest a single drop leak through the ceiling onto the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200). Overcome with emotion, Abū Ayyūb begged the Prophet ﷺ until he agreed to move to the upper level (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200).
- **Seeking Barakah in Food**: Abū Ayyūb and Umm Ayyūb prepared meals for the Prophet ﷺ daily. When the dish returned, Abū Ayyūb would inspect it and place his fingers specifically where he saw the impressions of the Prophet's ﷺ fingers to partake of his blessings (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200). When a dish containing onions was once returned untouched, Abū Ayyūb asked in alarm; the Prophet ﷺ reassured him: *“I converse with that which you do not converse with [the Angel Jibrīl], but as for you, eat!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200).
- **Miraculous Increase of Food**: Al-Ṣāliḥī records the miraculous multiplication of food in Abū Ayyūb's house, where a modest meal prepared for two sufficed scores of Companions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 9:482).

## 3. Lifelong Jihād & Political Role
- **Unbroken Campaigning**: Muḥammad b. Sīrīn recorded that Abū Ayyūb fought at Badr and never once stayed behind from any military campaign undertaken by the Muslims, being engaged in one expedition after another (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200). He refrained only during a single year when a youth was placed in command, a decision he later regretted deeply, saying: *“What does it matter to me who is appointed over me?”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200).
- **Governorship of Medina**: When the Caliph **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ departed Medina for Iraq, he appointed Abū Ayyūb as his deputy governor over Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200). Abū Ayyūb later joined ʿAlī in Iraq and stood by his side against the Khawārij at Nahrawān (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200).

## 4. Final Campaign & Burial at Constantinople
In his extreme old age, Abū Ayyūb joined the naval and land expedition dispatched against Constantinople (in year 50 AH, with reports also citing fifty-one or fifty-two AH) (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200–201). When he fell fatally ill during the siege, the army commander Yazīd b. Muʿāwiyah visited him and inquired after his final wish (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200). Abū Ayyūb replied:
> «حَاجَتِي إِذَا أَنَا مِتُّ فَارْكَبْ بِي مَا وَجَدْتَ مَسَاغًا فِي أَرْضِ الْعَدُوِّ، فَإِذَا لَمْ تَجِدْ فَادْفِنِّي ثُمَّ ارْجِعْ!»
> *"My request is that when I die, carry me upon a mount as far as you can advance into enemy territory; and when you can go no further, bury me there, then return!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:200–201).

The Muslim army rode forward bearing his body under the cover of night until they reached the base of the fortified walls of Constantinople, where they buried him (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:201).
