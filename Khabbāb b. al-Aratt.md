# Khabbāb b. al-Aratt (خباب بن الأرت)
*Abū ʿAbdullāh — Among the First to Openly Profess Islam, Tortured on Burning Coals*

## 1. Lineage and Enslavement
Al-Iṣābah gives his full nasab as **Khabbāb b. al-Aratt b. Jundalah b. Saʿd b. Khuzaymah b. Kaʿb b.
Saʿd b. Zayd Manāt b. Tamīm al-Tamīmī** — also said to be al-Khuzāʿī. He was **taken captive in the
Jāhiliyyah and sold at Makkah**, becoming the mawlā of **Umm Anmār al-Khuzāʿiyyah** (though other
accounts are given), before allying himself with **Banū Zuhrah**. Al-Ṭabarī explains the Zuhrah
affiliation: Āl Sibāʿ, the allies of ʿAmr b. ʿAbd ʿAwf b. al-Ḥārith b. Zuhrah, included Sibāʿ, the son
of Umm Anmār herself (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:221, no. 2215).

## 2. Among the Earliest Muslims
*"He accepted Islam early, and was among the **mustaḍʿafīn** [the oppressed weak]."* Ibn Saʿd
transmits that he was sold at Makkah and then allied with Zuhrah; al-Bāwardī reports he was the
**sixth person to accept Islam**, and that **he was the first to openly profess his Islam and was
severely tortured for it** (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:221, no. 2215).

## 3. Torture
Al-Ṣāliḥī places him among those tormented for their faith: he was laid upon burning coals and
scorching stones until, in his own words, *"the fat/water of my spine was gone."* The Prophet ﷺ later
told **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]**, when ʿUmar sat with him, that Khabbāb (with Bilāl) was among those
most deserving of honour for what they had endured (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:359). Khabbāb also brought his
complaint of the persecution's severity directly to the Prophet ﷺ, who was found reclining in the
shade of the Kaʿbah when he did so (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:359).

His torment came at the hands of his mistress **Umm Anmār**, who heated an iron and pressed it to his
head; the Prophet ﷺ prayed against her, after which she was struck with a howling affliction and told
that only cauterization would relieve it — **Khabbāb himself performed the cauterization on her head**
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:359).

## 4. Badr and Beyond
Al-Iṣābah records that **he witnessed Badr and the battles after it**, then settled in **Kūfah**. The
Prophet ﷺ paired him in brotherhood with **Jubayr b. ʿAtīk** (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:221, no. 2215).

## 5. Sword-Making, Wealth, and Illness
*"He worked as a sword-maker in the Jāhiliyyah — established in both Ṣaḥīḥs — and it is likewise
established in both that he grew wealthy."* He later suffered a severe illness. Muslim narrates via
Qays b. Abī Ḥāzim: *"We entered upon Khabbāb after he had been cauterized, and he said: 'Were it not
that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ forbade us to call for death, I would have called for it.'"* He is said
to have been **the first person buried on the outskirts of Kūfah**, and **he lived sixty-three years**
(*al-Iṣābah*, 2:222, no. 2215).

## 6. Death and ʿAlī's Eulogy
Al-Ṭabarānī narrates, via Zayd b. Wahb, that when **ʿAlī** returned from **Ṣiffīn** he passed by
Khabbāb's grave and said:

> «رَحِمَ اللَّهُ خَبَّابًا، أَسْلَمَ رَاغِبًا، وَهَاجَرَ طَائِعًا، وَعَاشَ مُجَاهِدًا، وَابْتُلِيَ فِي جِسْمِهِ أَحْوَالًا، وَلَنْ يُضِيعَ اللَّهُ أَجْرَهُ»
> *"May Allah have mercy on Khabbāb — he accepted Islam willingly, emigrated obediently, lived as a
> fighter, was afflicted in his body through various trials, and Allah will not let his reward be
> lost."*

Ibn Ḥibbān adds that Khabbāb died **in the year 37 AH**, at the very time of ʿAlī's return from
Ṣiffīn, and that **ʿAlī led his funeral prayer**; a second report gives 19 AH, but al-Iṣābah judges the
former *"more correct"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:222, no. 2215).
