# Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib (صفية بنت عبد المطلب) ﵂
*Paternal Aunt of the Prophet ﷺ, Mother of the Disciple al-Zubayr, Heroic Defender of Fāriʿ*

## 1. Lineage & Prophetic Kinship
She is **Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib b. Hāshim b. ʿAbd Manāf al-Qurashiyyah al-Hāshimiyyah**, the noble paternal aunt of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and the full sister of the Lion of Allāh **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:213, no. 11411). 

Her mother was **Hālah bint Wahb b. ʿAbd Manāf b. Zuhrah**, maternal aunt of the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:213). 

She was first married in Jāhiliyyah to al-Ḥārith b. Ḥarb b. Umayyah; after his demise, she married **al-ʿAwwām b. Khuwaylid b. Asad** (brother of the Mother of the Believers **[[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid]]** ﵂), bearing him **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁, the disciple of the Messenger of Allāh (*Ḥawārī Rasūlillāh ﷺ*), and al-Sāʾib b. al-ʿAwwām (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:213–214).

## 2. Early Conversion & Migration to Medina
Ṣafiyyah embraced Islam early in Makkah when the Prophet ﷺ summoned his closest kin upon Mount al-Ṣafā (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:323; *al-Iṣābah*, 8:213). She bore the persecutions of Quraysh with steadfast dignity and performed the Hijrah to Medina alongside her son al-Zubayr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:239, 312; *al-Iṣābah*, 8:214).

## 3. Heroic Fortitude at Uḥud (3 AH)
At **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, when confusion struck the Muslim rearguard and men began falling back, Ṣafiyyah advanced boldly with a spear in her hand, striking at those retreating and crying out: *«Are you fleeing from the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ?!»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:214). 

When the Prophet ﷺ observed her approaching the battlefield, fearful that she would see the severe mutilation inflicted upon her full brother Ḥamzah, he called out to her son: *«O Zubayr! The woman! The woman!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:224; *al-Iṣābah*, 8:214). Al-Zubayr rushed to intercept her, saying: *«Mother, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commands you to turn back!»* She resolutely replied:
> «بَلَغَنِي أَنَّهُ قَدْ مُثِّلَ بِأَخِي، وَذَلِكَ فِي اللَّهِ، فَمَا أَرْضَانَا بِمَا كَانَ مِنْ ذَلِكَ، لَأَحْتَسِبَنَّ وَلَأَصْبِرَنَّ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ!»
> *"It has reached me that my brother was mutilated; and that was for the sake of Allāh! How well-pleased we are with what occurred for His sake; I shall surely anticipate its divine reward and remain patient, if Allāh wills!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:224).

When al-Zubayr reported her words to the Prophet ﷺ, he permitted her to see Ḥamzah; she stood over his body, supplicated for him, and sought forgiveness on his behalf (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:224). She composed a sorrowful elegy lamenting his martyrdom (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:239).

## 4. Defender of the Fortress of Fāriʿ (Khandaq, 5 AH)
During **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**, the Prophet ﷺ placed the Muslim women and children inside the formidable fortress of Fāriʿ (*uṭum Fāriʿ*), the stronghold of **[[Ḥassān b. Thābit]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:214). 

A hostile spy from Banū Qurayẓah began scaling the ramparts to ascertain if the fortress was undefended (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:214). Observing the intruder, Ṣafiyyah urged Ḥassān to confront him, but Ḥassān was overwhelmed with apprehension. Demonstrating lionhearted resolve, Ṣafiyyah wrapped her head tightly with her scarf (*iʿtajarat*), seized a heavy wooden tent pole (*ʿamūd*), quietly descended to the gate, stepped outside, and struck the intruder decisively with the pole until he fell slain (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:214). 

She severed his head and cast it down over the battlements into the enemy detachment below. Seeing the head roll down, the hostile scouts exclaimed: *«We knew well that this man would never leave his womenfolk undefended without armed men!»* and broke off their assault in panic (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:214). Ibn Ḥajar noted that Ṣafiyyah was the very first woman in Islam to kill an enemy fighter (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:214).

## 5. Participation at Khaybar & Demise
At **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]** (7 AH), Ṣafiyyah was among the noble women who accompanied the army to attend to the wounded and provide logistics, and the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ rewarded her with a generous honorary share (*raḍkh/iḥdhāʾ*) from the spoils (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:16; *al-Iṣābah*, 8:213).

She lived through the early caliphate and passed away in Medina around twenty AH during the caliphate of **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, being buried in **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:214).
