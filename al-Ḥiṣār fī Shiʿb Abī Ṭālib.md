# al-Ḥiṣār fī Shiʿb Abī Ṭālib (الحصار في شعب أبي طالب)
*Quraysh's Boycott Document and the Three-Year Confinement of Banū Hāshim and Banū al-Muṭṭalib*

## 1. The Assassination Plot
Seeing the Muslims' safety in Abyssinia, al-Najāshī's refusal to surrender them, **ʿUmar**'s conversion,
and Islam's continuing spread through the tribes, Quraysh resolved to kill the Prophet ﷺ outright.
Recognizing that **Banū Hāshim** would demand blood vengeance, they offered the clan a doubled
blood-price (*diyah muḍāʿafah*) and proposed that a man from outside Quraysh entirely strike the fatal
blow, so that no single clan would bear Hāshim's retaliation. **Banū Hāshim refused**, and
**Banū al-Muṭṭalib b. ʿAbd Manāf** stood with them in that refusal (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:377).

## 2. The Boycott Document
Unable to reach the Prophet ﷺ through his own protecting clan, Quraysh's polytheists resolved
collectively to **cut off and expel** Banū Hāshim and Banū al-Muṭṭalib into the ravine (*al-Shiʿb*),
and drew up a document to that effect (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:377). **[[Abū Ṭālib]]** answered the
gathering storm with verse, addressing Luʾayy's sons directly:

> «أَلَمْ يَعْلَمُوا أَنَّا وَجَدْنَا مُحَمَّدًا … نَبِيًّا كَمُوسَى خُطَّ فِي أَوَّلِ الْكُتُبِ»
> *"Do they not know that we have found Muḥammad to be a prophet like Mūsā, inscribed in the earliest
> scriptures?"*

> «فَلَا وَرَبِّ الْبَيْتِ نُسْلِمُ أَحْمَدَا … لِعَزَّاءَ مِنْ عَضِّ الزَّمَانِ وَلَا كَرْبِ»
> *"No — by the Lord of the House, we will not hand over Aḥmad, for any calamity time may bite us
> with, nor for any distress."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:378)

## 3. Three Years in the Ravine
The two clans remained confined together **"until three full years had passed"** (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:379).

## 4. The Termite and the Parchment
Allāh sent a termite (*al-araḍah*) against the document, which *"ate,"* or in another wording
*"licked away,"* what was written of covenant and pact in it — one report specifying that it left
nothing of Allāh's name untouched while sparing what recorded their shirk, wrongdoing, and severance of
kinship. Al-Ṣāliḥī notes both versions are transmitted and defers on which is correct: *"Allāh knows
best the truth of that"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:379, 382).

Allāh informed His Messenger ﷺ of this, and the Prophet ﷺ told his uncle. Abū Ṭālib asked, *"Did your
Lord tell you this?"* The Prophet ﷺ said yes, and Abū Ṭālib answered — in one wording — *"By the
[celestial] piercing stars, you have not lied to me"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:379).

## 5. Abū Ṭālib's Confrontation
Abū Ṭālib went out with a band of Banū Hāshim and Banū al-Muṭṭalib to the mosque, fearful of Quraysh.
Seeing them gathered, Quraysh assumed they had come, worn down by hardship, to hand the Prophet ﷺ
over. Abū Ṭālib instead said: *"Matters have passed between us that we have not raised with you —
bring your document with the pacts in it, that there may yet be peace between us."* He said this,
al-Ṣāliḥī notes, precisely so they would not think to inspect the parchment before producing it.
Quraysh brought it confidently, certain the Prophet ﷺ would now be surrendered, and told Abū Ṭālib it
was time he abandoned what he and his had begun. Abū Ṭālib answered:

> *"I have come to you over a matter that will judge fairly between us: my nephew has told me — and he
> has never lied to me — that Allāh has sent a creature against this document in your hands, which has
> spared Allāh's name and devoured your treachery and your collective wrongdoing against us. If it is
> as he says, then by Allāh we will not hand him over until the last of us is dead; and if what he says
> is false, then we will deliver our companion to you, to kill or to spare as you wish."*

Quraysh agreed to this test. They opened the parchment and found *"the truthful, the trusted one"* ﷺ
had described its contents exactly, before it was even opened (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:379).

## 6. Vindication Refused
Rather than relent, Quraysh's response was to say, *"This is your nephew's sorcery,"* which only
increased their transgression and hostility. The men of Banū Hāshim and Banū al-Muṭṭalib retorted that
those most deserving of the charge of falsehood and sorcery were not themselves — *"for we know that
what you have agreed upon in cutting us off is nearer to idolatry (*al-jibt*) and sorcery."* Abū Ṭālib
pressed them directly: *"O assembly of Quraysh, why should we remain confined and imprisoned now that
the matter has become clear, and it is plain that you are the ones foremost in wrongdoing, severance of
kinship, and ill-treatment?"* He then entered with his companions between the curtains of the Kaʿbah
and prayed: *"O Allāh, grant us victory over whoever has wronged us, severed our ties of kinship, and
made lawful against us what is unlawful."* **They then returned to the ravine** (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:379).

The chapter entirely devoted to the boycott's imposition (2:377–388) ends here, without narrating the
siege's formal lifting — that material belongs to a separate, later chapter (below).

## 7. The Annulment of the Document
A further chapter, *al-Bāb al-Ḥādī wal-ʿIshrūn fī Naqḍ al-Ṣaḥīfah al-Ẓālimah* ("On the Annulment of
the Unjust Document"), narrates how the siege was actually ended. Ibn Isḥāq names **Hishām b. ʿAmr b.
al-Ḥārith** — nephew, through his mother, of Naḍlah b. Hāshim b. ʿAbd Manāf — as foremost among those
who worked to undo it. Being close to Banū Hāshim, Hishām had already been smuggling food into the
Shiʿb by night on camel-back, loosing the animal's head-rope and striking its flank so it would wander
in to them loaded with grain. Ibn Saʿd adds that on one night he brought in three camel-loads at once;
confronted by Quraysh the next morning, he promised not to repeat it, then did so again regardless
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:413).

Hishām then approached **Zuhayr b. Abī Umayyah** — whose mother, ʿĀtikah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, made
Banū Hāshim his own maternal uncles — and shamed him: *"Are you content to eat food and wear clothes
and marry women while your own uncles are as you know — neither bought from nor sold to, neither
married nor giving in marriage? By Allāh, if they were Abū al-Ḥakam b. Hishām's [Abū Jahl's] uncles and
you called him to what you are calling me to, he would not answer you."* Zuhayr agreed but said he was
only one man; Hishām offered himself as a second, and the two recruited **al-Muṭʿim b. ʿAdī** as a
third, then **Abū al-Bakhtarī b. Hishām** as a fourth, and **Zamʿah b. al-Aswad** as a fifth (a report
via al-Zubayr b. Abī Bakr names Suhayl b. Bayḍāʾ instead; Ibn Saʿd adds ʿAdī b. Qays to the group — of
these conspirators, Hishām, Zuhayr, Suhayl, and ʿAdī later accepted Islam) (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:413–414).

The five met by night at **Khaṭm al-Ḥajūn**, above Makkah, and pledged themselves to undoing the
document, with Zuhayr agreeing to speak first. The next morning, Zuhayr circled the Kaʿbah in fine
dress, then addressed the people: *"O people of Makkah, shall we eat food and wear clothes while Banū
Hāshim perish, neither bought from nor sold to? By Allāh, I will not sit until this cutting, unjust
document is torn up."* **Abū Jahl**, from a corner of the mosque, shouted him down — *"You lie, by
Allāh, it will not be torn up"* — but **Zamʿah b. al-Aswad**, **Abū al-Bakhtarī**, **al-Muṭʿim b.
ʿAdī**, and **Hishām b. ʿAmr** each spoke in turn to disown the document and declare Quraysh had never
truly agreed to it. Abū Jahl protested that the matter had been decided by night, elsewhere — while
**Abū Ṭālib** himself sat silently in a corner of the mosque, watching (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:414).

**Al-Muṭʿim b. ʿAdī** then went to the document to tear it — and found, exactly as foretold, that the
termite had eaten it away save for the words *"Bismika Allāhumma"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:414; see §4
above).

## 8. The Duration, and Abū Ṭālib's Praise of the Five
Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄ (via Abū Nuʿaym) gives the duration confined in the Shiʿb as **three years**. Muḥammad b.
ʿUmar al-Aslamī reports that Muḥammad b. Ṣāliḥ and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz dated the exit from
the Shiʿb to **the tenth year of the mission, three years before the Hijrah**; Ṣāʿid's *al-Fuṣūṣ*
states the Prophet ﷺ was **forty-nine years old** when he left it (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:414).

Once the document was torn up and voided, Abū Ṭālib composed verse in praise of the five who had acted
against it:

> «جَزَى اللَّهُ رَهْطًا بِالْحَجُونِ تَتَابَعُوا … عَلَى مَلَإٍ يَهْدِي لِحَزْمٍ وَيُرْشِدُ»
> *"May Allāh reward a band [who gathered] at al-Ḥajūn, who acted together upon a counsel that guides
> to resolve and to right conduct."*

> «هُمْ رَجَعُوا سَهْلَ بْنَ بَيْضَاءَ رَاضِيًا … وَسُرَّ أَبُو بَكْرٍ بِهَا وَمُحَمَّدُ»
> *"They won back Sahl b. Bayḍāʾ content, and Abū Bakr and Muḥammad rejoiced at it."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:415)

## 9. Abū Ṭālib's Earlier Ode
Composed in this period out of fear that the wider Arabs would be turned against his clan, Abū
Ṭālib's *lāmiyyah* invokes the sanctuary of Makkah and his own standing within it, while making plain
to Quraysh's nobles that he would never surrender the Prophet ﷺ:

> «ثِمَالُ الْيَتَامَىٰ عِصْمَةٌ لِلْأَرَامِلِ … يَلُوذُ بِهِ الْهُلَّاكُ مِنْ آلِ هَاشِمِ»
> *"[Muḥammad,] sustainer of orphans, refuge of widows, to whom the ruined of the House of Hāshim take
> refuge."*

> «وَلَوْلَا أَنْ أَجِيءَ بِسُبَّةٍ … تُجَرُّ عَلَىٰ أَشْيَاخِنَا فِي الْقَبَائِلِ … لَكُنَّا اتَّبَعْنَاهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ حَالَةٍ»
> *"Were it not that I should bring upon our elders, before the tribes, a reproach that would be
> dragged after them — we would have followed him in every circumstance."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:381, 388)
