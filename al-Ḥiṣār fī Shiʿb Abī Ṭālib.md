# al-Ḥiṣār fī Shiʿb Abī Ṭālib (الحصار في شعب أبي طالب)
*The Three-Year Economic and Social Boycott in the Ravine of Abū Ṭālib*

## 1. Overview
Furious that the migration to Abyssinia had secured safety for the believers and that chieftains like **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** had embraced Islam, the leaders of Quraysh drafted a punitive parchment (*al-ṣaḥīfah*). They pledged never to intermarry, trade, socialize, or speak with [[Banū Hāshim]] and [[Banū al-Muṭṭalib]] until they surrendered the Prophet ﷺ to be slain. The parchment was suspended inside the Kaʿbah.

For three agonizing years, the clan was confined to the barren ravine (*Shiʿb*). They endured starvation so acute that they subsisted on tree bark and dried camel leather, with the cries of starving infants heard across Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:377).

## 2. Annulment of the Parchment
Sympathetic Meccan noblemen—led by **Hishām b. ʿAmr**, **Zuhayr b. Abī Umayyah**, **Muṭʿim b. ʿAdī**, and **Abū al-Bakhtarī**—demanded the annulment of the tyrannical charter. Concurrently, the Prophet ﷺ informed his uncle Abū Ṭālib that Allāh had sent woodworms (*al-araqah*) to consume the parchment in the Kaʿbah, destroying every word except the name of Allāh (*Bismika Allāhumma*). When the elders inspected the scroll, they found it precisely as prophesied, fracturing the boycott and freeing the besieged (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:388).
