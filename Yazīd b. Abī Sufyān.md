# Yazīd b. Abī Sufyān (يزيد بن أبي سفيان)
*Yazīd al-Khayr: Supreme Commander of the Armies of Shām, Governor of Damascus, and Noble Umayyad Companion ﵁*

## 1. Lineage, Kinship & Maternal Background
**Yazīd b. Abī Sufyān** ﵁ belonged to the prominent aristocratic lineage of Banū Umayyah of Quraysh (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:516):

> «يزيد بن أبي سفيان صخر بن حرب بن أمية بن عبد شمس القرشي الأموي، أمير الشام، وأخو الخليفة معاوية، كان من فضلاء الصحابة، من مسلمة الفتح... وكان يقال له يزيد الخير، وكان أفضل أولاد أبي سفيان»
> *"Yazīd b. Abī Sufyān Ṣakhr b. Ḥarb b. Umayyah b. ʿAbd Shams al-Qurashī al-Umawī: Commander of the Levant, brother of Caliph Muʿāwiyah. He was among the most virtuous of the Companions, of those who embraced Islam at the Conquest... He was called 'Yazīd al-Khayr' (Yazīd the Good), and he was the most excellent of the children of Abū Sufyān."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:516–517)

His mother was **Umm al-Ḥakam Zaynab bint Nawfal b. Khalaf** of [[Banū Kinānah]]. Through his maternal lineage, his uncles were the chivalrous **Banū Firās b. Ghanm** of Kinānah (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:516). His kunyah was **Abū Khālid** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:517).

He was the full brother of Caliph **[[Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān]]** ﵁ and a maternal half-brother to the Mother of the Believers **[[Umm Ḥabībah]]** (Ramlah bint Abī Sufyān) ﵂ (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:516). He died leaving no surviving offspring (*lam yaʿqib*, *al-Iṣābah*, 6:517).

---

## 2. Conversion, Scribeship & Prophetic Milestones
Yazīd entered Islam alongside his father **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** ﵁ and his brother Muʿāwiyah ﵁ upon the [[Fatḥ Makkah|Conquest of Makkah]] in 8 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:516). Recognising his noble character and kinship authority, the Prophet ﷺ honored him with high administrative trust:
- **Collector of Maternal Zakat**: The Prophet ﷺ commissioned Yazīd to oversee and collect the obligatory alms (*ṣadaqāt*) of his maternal kin, the Banū Firās (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:516).
- **Gifts at al-Jiʿrānah**: At the distribution of the spoils of [[Ḥunayn]] at al-Jiʿrānah in 8 AH, the Prophet ﷺ bestowed upon him one hundred camels and forty ounces (*awqiyah*) of silver, matching the generous gifts granted to his father and brother (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:400).
- **Scribe to the Prophet ﷺ**: Yazīd is enumerated by Imām al-Ṣāliḥī and al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar among those who recorded correspondence and official documents for the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:57; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:532).

## 3. Leadership in the Conquest of ash-Shām & Governance
Following the passing of the Prophet ﷺ and the pacification of the peninsula during [[Ḥurūb al-Riddah]], Caliph **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ organized the military expeditions to liberate the Levant (*al-Shām*) in 12 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:517).

Abū Bakr appointed Yazīd as one of the four supreme commanders of the regional armies (*Umarāʾ al-Ajnād*), alongside [[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]] ﵁, [[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]] ﵁, and [[Shuraḥbīl b. Ḥasanah]] ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:517). When dispatching his column toward southern Syria, the Caliph famously honored Yazīd by walking barefoot alongside his horse, issuing the foundational ethical charter of Islamic rules of engagement (forbidding the killing of non-combatants, hermits, destroying orchards, or slaughtering livestock except for food).

Yazīd served with distinction across the Syrian theater:
1. He fought with conspicuous gallantry in the major engagements, including the [[Ghazwat al-Yarmūk|Battle of al-Yarmūk]] (15 AH), where he commanded a vital wing of the Muslim army.
2. He participated centrally in the siege and surrender of Damascus, commanding an encircling camp at the eastern gate (*Bāb Sharqī*).
3. Caliph **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ assigned him governorship over Palestine and southern Syria, and subsequently elevated him over Damascus (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:517).
4. When **[[Muʿādh b. Jabal]]** ﵁ fell critically ill, he nominated Yazīd to succeed him over the broad administrative command of Syria, an appointment immediately endorsed by ʿUmar (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:517).
5. Yazīd spearheaded coastal campaigns, successfully subduing Sidon, Beirut, and laying the groundwork for the fall of Caesarea (*Qayṣāriyyah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:517).

## 4. Ascetic Accountability, Martyrdom in ʿAmwās & Succession
Despite his high station and aristocratic background, Yazīd operated under the unsparing oversight of Caliph ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁. When reports reached Medina that Yazīd had partaken in elaborate Syrian dishes (*alwān al-ṭaʿām*), ʿUmar rebuked him firmly, warning him against departing from the austere, disciplined path of the Prophet ﷺ and early emigrants (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:517).

In 18 AH, the devastating epidemic known as **Ṭāʿūn ʿAmwās** (the Plague of Emmaus) struck the Muslim forces across Syria, claiming an estimated twenty-five thousand souls (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:323). Yazīd contracted the plague and succumbed to it alongside fellow supreme commanders Abū ʿUbaydah and Muʿādh b. Jabal ﵃ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:323; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:517):

> «توفي بالطاعون عام عمواس هو ومعاذ بن جبل ويزيد بن أبي سفيان وغيرهم من أشراف الصحابة رضي الله تعالى عنهم»
> *"He died of the plague in the Year of ʿAmwās, alongside Muʿādh b. Jabal, Yazīd b. Abī Sufyān, and others from the nobility of the Companions ﵃."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:323)

Some historians record his demise in 19 AH following the final reduction of Caesarea (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:517). 

On his deathbed, Yazīd designated his younger brother **[[Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān]]** ﵁ to assume command of his troops and the governorship of Damascus. Caliph ʿUmar formally confirmed this appointment, writing to Muʿāwiyah to take charge of his brother's jurisdiction—an act that laid the groundwork for Muʿāwiyah's two-decade governorship of Syria and subsequent founding of the Umayyad Caliphate (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:120, 517).
