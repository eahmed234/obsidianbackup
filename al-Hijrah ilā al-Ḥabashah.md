# al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah (الهجرة إلى أرض الحبشة)
*The historic dual migrations across the Red Sea to the court of the righteous Negus: the preservation of the early Muslim community from Makkan persecution*

## 1. Context & The Prophetic Directive

By the fifth year of Prophethood (5 BH / 615 CE), persecution by the oligarchy of Quraysh against the early believers reached unbearable levels (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363). Seeing his Companions subjected to torment while he himself was shielded by divine protection and the tribal patronage of his uncle Abū Ṭālib, the Prophet [[Muḥammad b. ʻAbdullah|Muḥammad]] ﷺ directed them:
> «لَوْ خَرَجْتُمْ إِلَى أَرْضِ الْحَبَشَةِ، فَإِنَّ بِهَا مَلِكًا لَا يُظْلَمُ عِنْدَهُ أَحَدٌ، وَهِيَ أَرْضُ صِدْقٍ، حَتَّى يَجْعَلَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ فَرَجًا مِمَّا أَنْتُمْ فِيهِ»
> *"If only you were to depart to the land of Abyssinia! For verily, therein is a king beneath whose rule no one is wronged; it is a land of truthfulness, until Allāh grants you relief from your present afflictions!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363).

## 2. The First Migration to Abyssinia (Rajab 5 BH)

In Rajab of the 5th year of the Mission, a pioneer group of **twelve men and four or five women** slipped out of Makkah covertly under cover of darkness (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363). 

### Foremost Pioneers
The very first household to depart was **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ accompanied by his wife, the Prophet's daughter **[[Ruqayyah bint Rasūlillāh]]** ﵂ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363). Regarding them, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ remarked: *«Inna ʿUthmāna la-awwalu man hājara bi-ahlihi baʿda Lūṭ»* (*"Verily, ʿUthmān is the first to emigrate with his family after Lot ﵊"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363).

The pioneer company included distinguished companions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363):
- **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁
- **[[Abū Ḥudhayfah b. ʿUtbah]]** ﵁ and his wife Sahlah bint Suhayl
- **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁
- **[[Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr]]** ﵁
- **Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Asad** ﵁ and his wife **[[Umm Salamah]]** ﵂
- **[[ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn]]** ﵁ (who served as their designated head or amīr, *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:364)
- **[[ʿĀmir b. Rabīʿah]]** ﵁ and his wife Laylā bint Abī Ḥathmah

### The Embarkation at al-Shuʿaybah
The emigrants made their way southwest on foot and camel to the ancient Red Sea port of **al-Shuʿaybah** (the historic harbor of Makkah before the development of Jeddah) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:364). By divine providence, they found two merchant vessels ready to set sail, which carried them across the sea to the African coast for the modest fare of half a dinar per person (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:364). Quraysh dispatched trackers in hot pursuit, but by the time they reached the shore, the sails were already over the horizon (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:364).

In Abyssinia, they found complete sanctuary under King **[[al-Najāshī (Aṣḥamah)]]** ﵁:
> «وقدمنا أرض الحبشة فجاورنا بها خير جار، أمنّا على ديننا وعبدنا اللَّه تعالى لا نؤذى ولا نسمع شيئا نكرهه»
> *"We arrived in the land of Abyssinia and resided with the finest of hosts; we were secure in our religion and worshipped Allāh without suffering injury or hearing anything hurtful!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:364).

## 3. The False Rumor of Reconciliation & Temporary Return

In Ramaḍān of that year, during the recitation of Sūrat al-Najm at the Kaʿbah, the polytheists of Quraysh—overwhelmed by the majesty of the divine revelation—fell prostrate in unison with the Muslims (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:364). 

Distorted rumors reached Abyssinia that all of Makkah had accepted Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:365). Yearning for their homeland, the migrants sailed back in Shawwāl 5 BH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:366). Outside Makkah, travelers from Kinānah informed them that Quraysh remained as fiercely hostile as ever (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:366). None could re-enter Makkah except under the armed clan protection (*jiwār*) of a prominent polytheist or in secrecy; ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn entered under the protection of al-Walīd b. al-Mughīrah, which he later publicly renounced in order to share the suffering of his brothers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:366–367).

## 4. The Grand Second Migration (*al-Hijrah al-Thāniyah*)

With persecutions intensifying and the imposition of the tribal boycott in the Defile of Abū Ṭālib (**[[Shiʿb Abī Ṭālib]]**), the Prophet ﷺ ordered a second, far larger migration to Abyssinia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:389). 

This second exodus was commanded by **[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:389). The company numbered **eighty-three men** (and eighty-two according to another reckoning) and **eighteen or nineteen women** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:389, 407–408). 

Among the noble emigrants of the Second Migration were:
- **[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ and his wife **Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays** ﵂
- **[[Khālid b. Saʿīd b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁ and his wife Umaynah bint Khalaf
- **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd]]** ﵁
- **Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī** ﵁ (who joined from Yemen with the Ship)
- Children born in the realm of Aksum, including **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Jaʿfar]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:409).

## 5. Quraysh's Extradition Embassy & Jaʿfar's Historic Address

Alarmed by the establishment of an autonomous Muslim community abroad, Quraysh dispatched an aristocratic diplomatic mission consisting of **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** and **ʿAbdullāh b. Abī Rabīʿah**, laden with luxurious leather gifts to bribe the royal bishops and the Negus (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:390).

Demanding extradition, ʿAmr argued that these youths had abandoned the faith of their ancestors and introduced an invented religion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:390). The Negus summoned the Muslims to his court. Standing before the Christian king and his bishops, **Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib** ﵁ delivered the immortal manifesto of Islam:
> «أَيُّهَا الْمَلِكُ! كُنَّا قَوْمًا أَهْلَ جَاهِلِيَّةٍ: نَعْبُدُ الأَصْنَامَ، وَنَأْكُلُ الْمَيْتَةَ، وَنَأْتِي الْفَوَاحِشَ، وَنَقْطَعُ الأَرْحَامَ، وَنُسِيءُ الْجِوَارَ، وَيَأْكُلُ الْقَوِيُّ مِنَّا الضَّعِيفَ... حَتَّى بَعَثَ اللَّهُ إِلَيْنَا رَسُولًا مِنَّا نَعْرِفُ نَسَبَهُ وَصِدْقَهُ وَأَمَانَتَهُ وَعَفَافَهُ...»
> *"O King! We were an uncivilized people of ignorance: worshipping idols, eating carrion, committing abominations, severing ties of kinship, ill-treating our neighbors, and the strong among us devoured the weak... Until Allāh raised up among us a Messenger whose lineage, truthfulness, trustworthiness, and chastity we knew..."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:391).

When Jaʿfar recited the opening passages of **Sūrat Maryam** depicting the miraculous birth of Jesus ﵊ and the virginity of Mary, the Negus wept until his beard was drenched, and his bishops wept until their scrolls were soaked (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:391). The Negus proclaimed:
> «إِنَّ هَذَا وَالَّذِي جَاءَ بِهِ عِيسَى لَيَخْرُجُ مِنْ مِشْكَاةٍ وَاحِدَةٍ! انْطَلِقَا فَلَا وَاللَّهِ لَا أُسَلِّمُهُمْ إِلَيْكُمَا أَبَدًا!»
> *"Verily, this and that which Jesus brought emanate from the very same niche of light! Depart, for by Allāh, I shall never surrender them to you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:391).

The Negus embraced Islam in secret, protected the Muslim community through all years of trial, and the community remained in peace until they returned triumphantly to the Prophet ﷺ at Khaybar in 7 AH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:394–395; 5:135).

---

## 6. Strategic Significance

1. **The First International Asylum in Islam**: The migration established that the Islamic mission was not bound to Arabian geography, creating an overseas haven protected by international safe-conduct (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363).
2. **The Diplomatic Eloquence of Jaʿfar**: Jaʿfar's address to the Negus remains the classical archetype of Islamic daʿwah, highlighting monotheism, moral reform, social justice, and common scriptural roots with Christianity (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:391).
3. **Preservation of the Core Leadership**: The presence of eighty-three elite pioneers in Abyssinia guaranteed that even if Quraysh had succeeded in crushing the embryonic movement in Makkah, the authentic transmission of the message was permanently preserved (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:389).
