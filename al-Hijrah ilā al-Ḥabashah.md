# al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah (الهجرة إلى الحبشة)
*The First and Second Migrations of the Believers to the Kingdom of Aksum*

## 1. The Prophet's ﷺ Counsel
Seeing what his companions suffered, and unable — despite his own safety under Abū Ṭālib's
protection — to shield them from it, the Prophet ﷺ told them:

> «لَوْ خَرَجْتُمْ إِلَى أَرْضِ الْحَبَشَةِ، فَإِنَّ بِهَا مَلِكًا لَا يُظْلَمُ عِنْدَهُ أَحَدٌ، وَهِيَ أَرْضُ صِدْقٍ، حَتَّى يَجْعَلَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ فَرَجًا مِمَّا أَنْتُمْ فِيهِ»
> *"If only you were to go forth to the land of Abyssinia — for therein is a king in whose realm no
> one is wronged, and it is a land of truthfulness — until Allāh grants you relief from what you are
> in."*

This was **the first hijrah in Islam**, undertaken in **Rajab of the fifth year of the mission**
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363).

## 2. The First Hijrah
Reports vary on the numbers: twelve men and two women; or ten men (Ibn Isḥāq's and Ibn Hishām's
figure); or twelve men and three, four, or five women. **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** and his wife
**Ruqayyah bint Rasūlillāh** ﵂ were the first to emigrate — the Prophet ﷺ said of him, *"ʿUthmān is
the first to emigrate with his household since [[Lūṭ]] ﵊."* Also among them: **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b.
ʿAwf**, **Abū Ḥudhayfah b. ʿUtbah b. Rabīʿah** with his wife Sahlah bint Suhayl, **al-Zubayr b.
al-ʿAwwām**, **Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr**, **Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Asad** with his wife **Umm Salamah bint Abī
Umayyah**, **ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn**, and ʿĀmir b. Rabīʿah with his wife Laylā bint Abī Ḥathmah, among
others (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:363).

They slipped away in secret to al-Shuʿaybiyyah and crossed by two merchant ships for half a dinar
each; Quraysh pursued them to the coast but arrived too late. *"We came to the land of Abyssinia and
found in it the best of neighbours; we were secure in our religion and worshipped Allāh, unmolested
and hearing nothing we disliked"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:364).

### The Return and the Report of the Gharānīq
Word reached them that Makkah's opposition had broken — even **al-Walīd b. al-Mughīrah** and Abū
Uḥayḥah were said to have prostrated behind the Prophet ﷺ — and the emigrants, saying *"our own kin
are dearer to us,"* turned back. Al-Ṣāliḥī gives the report behind this rumour: reciting **Sūrat
al-Najm**, and reaching the verse on al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā and Manāt, the Prophet ﷺ is reported by some
transmitters to have had words inserted into his recitation by Satan implying intercession through
the idols, provoking Quraysh's premature celebration that *"Muḥammad has returned to our religion."*
Al-Ṣāliḥī records this by three chains through Ibn Jarīr (from Ibn ʿAbbās, from ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b.
al-Ḥārith b. Hishām, and from Abū al-ʿĀliyah), transmits al-Ḥāfiẓ [Ibn Ḥajar]'s defence of the report's
substance against critics such as Ibn al-ʿArabī and al-Qāḍī [ʿIyāḍ] who rejected it outright — arguing
that numerous, independently-sourced chains point to some basis for the incident even where individual
chains are weak — and cites Allāh's own resolution of the matter:

> «فَيَنسَخُ اللَّهُ مَا يُلْقِي الشَّيْطَانُ ثُمَّ يُحْكِمُ اللَّهُ آيَاتِهِ»
> *"Then Allāh abrogates what Satan casts in, then Allāh confirms His verses"* (Qurʾān 22:52).

Al-Ṣāliḥī notes the matter is treated at greater length elsewhere in the book, in the chapters on the
Prophet's ﷺ *ʿiṣmah* (infallibility) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:364–365).

On learning that the rumour of Makkah's mass conversion was false — a caravan of Kinānah told them
Quraysh had only briefly relented before returning to their hostility — the emigrants deliberated
whether to press on to Makkah regardless, entering under the protection of a clansman or in secret;
none did so openly save **ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd**, who stayed only briefly before returning to Abyssinia.
They had left in Rajab of year 5, remained through Shaʿbān and Ramaḍān — the Satanic interjection
having occurred in Ramaḍān — and arrived back in Makkah in Shawwāl of that year (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:365–366).

## 3. Renewed Persecution and ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn's Renunciation of Protection
On their return the returnees faced their clans' hostility undiminished. **ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn**, seeing
his own companions suffer while he himself moved freely under the protection (*jiwār*) of **al-Walīd
b. al-Mughīrah**, judged this a diminishment of his own standing and publicly renounced al-Walīd's
protection in the very assembly where it had been granted, declaring he wished only Allāh's
protection. He was then struck in the eye in a dispute with the poet Labīd, and when al-Walīd
lamented that his eye had gone without proper protection, ʿUthmān answered that his other, sound eye
was equally in need of what had befallen its fellow, *"for the sake of Allāh"* (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:367–368).

## 4. The Second Hijrah
Persecution in Makkah persisting, the Prophet ﷺ permitted a **second, larger migration** to Abyssinia
— *"more grievous than the first,"* meeting sharper reproach from Quraysh, who had also learned of the
Negus's good treatment of the first group. **ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān** asked, *"O Messenger of Allāh, this is
our first hijrah and now this second one, and you are not with us?"* The Prophet ﷺ answered: *"You are
emigrants to Allāh and to me; you have the reward of both hijrahs together."* ʿUthmān replied, *"Then
that suffices us, O Messenger of Allāh"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:389).

Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn Saʿd give the number of this second migration as **eighty-three men**; Ibn Saʿd adds
**eleven Qurashī women and seven foreign-born women**, with other reports giving still higher figures
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:389).

## 5. Quraysh's Embassy to al-Najāshī
Quraysh sent **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** and **ʿAmmārah b. al-Walīd**, laden with gifts for the Negus and
his bishops, to demand the emigrants' extradition, briefing the court beforehand to side against them.
The two prostrated before the Negus, presented their gifts, and argued that the emigrants were mere
foolish youths who had abandoned their ancestral religion for an invented one their own kinsmen
understood better than the king could. His courtiers concurred — but the Negus refused to surrender
guests who had chosen his protection over any other's without hearing them himself (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:389–390).

## 6. Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib Before the Negus
Summoned, the Muslims agreed to answer with nothing but the truth of what their Prophet ﷺ had brought.
**[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** spoke for them: *"We used to worship Allāh alone, ascribing no partner to
Him, and forbade what He forbade and permitted what He permitted — so our people set upon us,
tormenting us and trying to turn us back to the worship of idols and to the abominations we once
practised. When they overpowered us, wronged us, and constrained us and stood between us and our
religion, we came out to your land, choosing you over all others, and hoping we would not be wronged
in your presence, O King"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:390–391).

The Negus asked if he carried anything of what had been revealed, and Jaʿfar recited the opening of
**Sūrat Maryam** (*"Kāf-Hā-Yā-ʿAyn-Ṣād"*). *"By Allāh, the Negus wept until his beard was drenched, and
his bishops wept until their scriptures were drenched,"* hearing what was recited. The Negus declared:
*"This and what Jesus brought come from the same lamp-niche."* He asked ʿAmr whether the emigrants
were slaves owed to Quraysh or debtors — and on hearing neither, dismissed the envoys: *"Go, for by
Allāh I will never hand them over to you, ever"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:391).

## 7. ʿAmr's Second Attempt, and Its Failure
The next day ʿAmr returned alone, proposing to tell the Negus that the Muslims held a *"monstrous"*
belief about ʿĪsā (Jesus) — despite ʿAmmārah's warning against provoking a king toward people who,
whatever their disagreement, remained his own kin by faith. Summoned again and asked what they said of
ʿĪsā b. Maryam, the Muslims answered as their Prophet ﷺ had taught them, and the Negus affirmed that
this matched his own scripture *"to the very breadth of a twig,"* refusing his own bishops' murmured
displeasure. He then ordered a crier proclaimed that anyone who harmed the Muslims would be fined —
doubling the fine when asked if it sufficed — and returned the envoys' gifts, saying: *"Allāh did not
take a bribe from me when He restored my kingdom to me, that I should now take a bribe over it; nor
did the people obey me in Him, that I should now obey the people concerning Him"* (*Subul al-Hudā*,
2:392).

## 8. A Rebellion Against the Negus, and His Confession of Faith
Abyssinian nobles, angered at what they took for the Negus's abandonment of their religion, rose
against him. He met them on boats, prepared to flee if defeated, and — before going out to address
them — wrote out a private confession, *"that ʿĪsā is His servant and His Messenger, His word cast
unto Maryam, and His spirit,"* concealing the parchment inside his garment near his shoulder. Before
the assembled Abyssinians he placed his hand on it and swore he believed nothing more of ʿĪsā than
what he had written — outwardly satisfying them while privately affirming the Muslims' own testimony
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:392). Umm Salamah ﵂, who lived through it, recalled: *"We remained with him in the
best of homes with the best of neighbours"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:392).

A further account, transmitted via ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Abī Laylā, adds the sequel of ʿAmr and
ʿAmmārah's own falling-out on the voyage home — a rivalry over ʿAmr's wife that led ʿAmmārah to try to
drown ʿAmr at sea, and ʿAmr's eventual revenge: engineering ʿAmmārah's ruin with the Negus's own wife,
after which the Negus had him bewitched into a feral madness, roaming with wild beasts until, it is
said, he was eventually sought out (unsuccessfully) in the time of ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb (*Subul
al-Hudā*, 2:393–394).

## 9. Departure and the Negus's Later Letter
The emigrants remained in Abyssinia in the Negus's protection; **ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd** returned early
to Makkah. When word reached them of the Prophet's ﷺ own hijrah to Madīnah, thirty-three men and eight
women returned; two of the returnees died in Makkah, and twenty-four of them lived to witness Badr
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:394). Al-Ṣāliḥī later transmits the Negus's own letter to the Prophet ﷺ, professing
his faith explicitly: *"I bear witness that you are the truthful, confirmed Messenger of Allāh… I have
followed you and pledged allegiance at the hand of your cousin, and submitted to Allāh, Lord of the
worlds"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:395).

## 10. The Register of the Second Emigrants
Al-Ṣāliḥī closes the chapter with a dedicated register, *"On the names of those who emigrated in the
second hijrah,"* explaining its purpose: *"that they be known, and that they are among the great and
righteous"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:396). Among those it and the surrounding chapters name, beyond the
figures already given individual notes above, are **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd]]**, and — per the book's own
list of the earlier returnees — Abū Ḥudhayfah b. ʿUtbah, **ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh**, ʿUtbah b. Ghazwān,
al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām, Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr, Suwaybiṭ b. Saʿd, Ṭulayb b. ʿAmr, and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:368).
