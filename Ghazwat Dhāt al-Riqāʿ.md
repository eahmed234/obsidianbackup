# Ghazwat Dhāt al-Riqāʿ (غزوة ذات الرقاع)
*The Expedition of the Rags & The Expedition of Wonders (Ghazwat al-Aʿājīb) — The Institution of Ṣalāt al-Khawf and the Luminous Sentry of the Defile*

## 1. Strategic Context & Provocation
In the opening month of al-Muḥarram, intelligence reached Medina via a desert merchant who had arrived with trade goods (*jalab*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175). He informed the Muslims that the warlike bedouin clans of Najd—specifically Banū Anmār b. Baghyīḍ and Banū Saʿd b. Thaʿlabah of **[[Ghaṭafān]]**—were massing troops and gathering a formidable coalition to launch an assault on Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175). 

To preempt this gathering storm and secure the eastern desert approach, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ mobilized an army. He appointed **[[Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī]]** ﵁ (according to Ibn Isḥāq) or **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ (according to Ibn Saʿd, Ibn Hishām, and al-Wāqidī) as his deputy over Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175).

The Prophet ﷺ marched out on Saturday night, the tenth of al-Muḥarram, commanding a force of 400 or 800 (also reported: seven hundred) Muslim warriors (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175). The army traversed through the mountain pass of al-Maḍīq and debouched into **Wādī al-Shaqrah**, where they halted for one day while sending reconnaissance patrols into the surrounding wilderness (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175). Finding fresh tracks but no standing force, the army pushed deep into the palm oasis of **Nakhl**, where they reached the enemy encampments (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175).

## 2. Etymology of *Dhāt al-Riqāʿ* & *Ghazwat al-Aʿājīb*
Classical authorities record multiple origins for the title *Dhāt al-Riqāʿ* (The Expedition of Patches/Rags):
1. **The Rags of the Foot-Soldiers**: In *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, **[[Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī]]** ﵁ reported that mounts were scarce; six men shared a single camel by rotation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180). As they marched across the scorching, jagged basalt volcanic rocks (*ḥarrah*), the men's boots disintegrated and their toenails fell off. They tore their extra garments into strips and bandaged their blistered, bleeding feet with rags (*riqāʿ*), leading the campaign to be remembered by that name (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180). This derivation was favored by al-Suhaylī and al-Nawawī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180).
2. **The Patched Mountain**: Muḥammad b. ʿUmar al-Wāqidī recorded that the expedition was named after a distinctive mountain in the territory whose variegated strata featured alternating red, black, and white patches resembling patched cloth (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180).
3. **The Tree of Dhāt al-Riqāʿ**: A renowned desert tree named *Dhāt al-Riqāʿ* marked the location of their encampment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180).
4. **The Expedition of Wonders (*Ghazwat al-Aʿājīb*)**: al-Bazzār and al-Ṭabarānī recorded from Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh ﵄ that the Companions termed it *Ghazwat al-Aʿājīb* due to the overwhelming succession of prophetic miracles and celestial signs manifested during the campaign (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175).

## 3. The Chronological Debate in Maghāzī Historiography
A major scholarly debate exists regarding the precise year of this expedition (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180–182):
- **The Early Dating (4 AH or 5 AH)**: Ibn Isḥāq and early biographers placed it in 4 AH (or early 5 AH), shortly following the expulsion of Banū al-Naḍīr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175).
- **The Late Dating (7 AH / Post-Khaybar)**: Imām al-Bukhārī, Ibn al-Qayyim, and al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī proved that the expedition took place after the conquest of Khaybar in 7 AH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180–182). Their decisive proofs include:
  1. **[[Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī]]** ﵁ explicitly witnessed the expedition, yet he only arrived from Yemen and Abyssinia at Khaybar in 7 AH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180).
  2. **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁ prayed *Ṣalāt al-Khawf* with the Prophet ﷺ in Najd, and he embraced Islam at Khaybar (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180).
  3. **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar]]** ﵄ confirmed praying *Ṣalāt al-Khawf* in Najd; his first military participation was at al-Khandaq (5 AH) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:181).
  4. In *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, **[[Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh]]** ﵄ explicitly stated that the Prophet ﷺ prayed *Ṣalāt al-Khawf* in his seventh campaign: *Dhāt al-Riqāʿ* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:181).

## 4. Standoff & Strategic Deterrence
Upon arriving at Nakhl, the Muslim vanguard discovered that the bedouin clans had fled their dwellings and taken up positions upon the summits of the surrounding mountains, looking down upon the Muslim army (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175).

A massive contingent of Ghaṭafān assembled opposite the Muslims. Both hosts drew close and observed each other with intense apprehension:
> «فَلَقِيَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ جَمْعًا مِنْ غَطَفَانَ، فَتَقَارَبَ النَّاسُ، وَلَمْ يَكُنْ بَيْنَهُمْ قِتَالٌ، فَخَافَ الْفَرِيقَانِ بَعْضُهُمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ»
> *"The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ met a large host of Ghaṭafān. The two armies drew near to one another, but no open battle took place; each group stood in dread of the other."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175).

Seeing the unwavering discipline, cohesion, and readiness of the Muslim ranks, Ghaṭafān hesitated to charge down from the heights. The expedition achieved total strategic deterrence without a pitched clash, dispersing the gathering threat to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175).

## 5. The Institution of Ṣalāt al-Khawf (The Fear Prayer)
While facing the assembled host of Ghaṭafān, the time for prayer entered. Because the enemy was positioned close at hand and could launch a sudden ambush while the Muslims were prostrating, Allāh revealed the legislation of **Ṣalāt al-Khawf** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:175):
> ﴿وَإِذَا كُنتَ فِيهِمْ فَأَقَمْتَ لَهُمُ الصَّلَاةَ فَلْتَقُمْ طَائِفَةٌ مِّنْهُم مَّعَكَ وَلْيَأْخُذُوا أَسْلِحَتَهُمْ فَإِذَا سَجَدُوا فَلْيَكُونُوا مِن وَرَائِكُمْ وَلْتَأْتِ طَائِفَةٌ أُخْرَىٰ لَمْ يُصَلُّوا فَلْيُصَلُّوا مَعَكَ وَلْيَأْخُذُوا حِذْرَهُمْ وَأَسْلِحَتَهُمْ﴾
> *"When you are among them and lead them in prayer, let a group of them stand with you and keep their weapons. When they prostrate, let them be behind you, and let the other group who have not prayed come forward and pray with you, taking their precautions and their weapons."* (Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:102).

The Prophet ﷺ divided the army into two companies: one contingent stood armed facing the enemy while the other prayed one unit (*rakʿah*) behind him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:181). The first group completed their prayer and shifted to guard the lines, while the second group stepped forward to pray their *rakʿah* behind the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ.

## 6. The Sentry of the Defile & The Three Arrows
On the return journey, the Muslim army encamped in a steep, wind-swept ravine (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179). The Prophet ﷺ called out: *“Who will stand guard over us tonight?”* 

**[[ʿAbbād b. Bishr]]** ﵁ (of the Aws) and **[[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]]** ﵁ (of the Muhājirūn) stepped forward together, saying: *“We shall guard you, O Messenger of Allāh!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179). They stationed themselves at the mouth of the defile. ʿAbbād asked ʿAmmār: *“Which part of the night do you prefer I cover for you: the first or the second?”* ʿAmmār chose to sleep during the first watch, while ʿAbbād stood erect in the darkness to perform voluntary night prayer (*tahajjud*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179).

A bedouin scout tracked the army to seek vengeance. In the pale darkness, he spotted the silhouette of ʿAbbād standing in prayer at the mouth of the pass (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179). Concluding he was the sentinel, the scout fitted an arrow to his bow and shot it straight into ʿAbbād's body. ʿAbbād calmly grasped the shaft, pulled it out of his flesh, and continued reciting without interrupting his prayer (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179).

The scout released a second arrow, burying it into ʿAbbād; ʿAbbād withdrew it and kept reciting. The archer shot a third arrow, wounding him deeply. Bleeding profusely, ʿAbbād pulled out the third arrow, concluded his bowing (*rukūʿ*) and prostrations (*sujūd*), and then crawled to wake ʿAmmār, saying: *“Sit up, for I have been wounded!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179).

Seeing ʿAmmār rise, the enemy realized he had been discovered and fled into the dark. ʿAmmār gasped in shock at ʿAbbād's blood-soaked garments: *“Subḥānallāh! Why did you not awaken me when he shot the very first arrow?”* ʿAbbād replied with words immortalized in Islamic history:
> «كُنْتُ فِي سُورَةٍ أَقْرَؤُهَا، وَهِيَ سُورَةُ الْكَهْفِ، فَكَرِهْتُ أَنْ أَقْطَعَهَا حَتَّى أَفْرُغَ مِنْهَا، وَلَوْلَا أَنِّي خَشِيتُ أَنْ أُضَيِّعَ ثَغْرًا أَمَرَنِي بِهِ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ مَا انْصَرَفْتُ، وَلَوْ أُتِيَ عَلَى نَفْسِي!»
> *“I was in the midst of reciting a sūrah—Sūrat al-Kahf—and I hated to cut it off until I completed it! By Allāh, were it not for the fear that I would compromise an outpost that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanded me to guard, I would not have broken my prayer even if it cost me my life!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:180).

## 7. Miracles of the Campaign (*Dalāʾil al-Nubūwah*)
The expedition witnessed numerous extraordinary miracles that led the Ṣaḥābah to designate it *Ghazwat al-Aʿājīb*:

### The Drawn Sword of Ghawrath b. al-Ḥārith
During a scorching midday halt (*qāʾilah*) in a valley thick with thorny acacia trees (*ʿuḍāh*), the Companions dispersed to seek shade beneath the trees (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:176). The Prophet ﷺ took shelter alone under a dense acacia tree and hung his sword from its bough (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:176).

While the army slept, a bedouin warrior named **Ghawrath b. al-Ḥārith** (غورث بن الحارث) snuck into the camp, crept up to the resting Prophet ﷺ, and unsheathed the hung sword (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:176). The Prophet ﷺ awoke to find Ghawrath standing directly over his head with the bare blade gleaming in his hand. Ghawrath sneered:
> «مَنْ يَمْنَعُكَ مِنِّي؟»
> *"Who will protect you from me now?"*
> The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ replied with absolute calm:
> «اللَّهُ!»
> *"Allāh!"*

Ghawrath demanded again: *“Who will protect you from me?”* The Prophet ﷺ repeated: *“Allāh!”* Ghawrath demanded a third time, and the Prophet ﷺ responded a third time: *“Allāh!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:176). A sudden terror overcame Ghawrath; his hand trembled, and he sheathed the sword and sat down meekly (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:176). The Prophet ﷺ summoned his Companions, recounted what had transpired while Ghawrath sat beside him, and pardoned him without inflicting any punishment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:176).

### The Convergence of the Two Trees
During the march, the Prophet ﷺ needed to relieve himself in a wide open valley (*wādin afyaḥ*) devoid of cover (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:178). Noticing two trees at the edge of the valley, the Prophet ﷺ grasped a branch of the first tree and said: *“Follow me by the permission of Allāh!”* The tree moved alongside him like a docile camel (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:178). He then approached the second tree, grasped its branch, and commanded it likewise. When he stood between them, he commanded: *“Join together over me by the permission of Allāh!”* The two trees leaned inward and interlocked their branches to form a screen (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:178). After he finished, each tree returned to its trunk and stood upon its own roots (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:178).

### The Miracle of Jābir's Mount (*Laylat al-Baʿīr*)
On the return march, the camel of **[[Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh]]** ﵄ was so fatigued and emaciated that it fell to the rear of the column (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:177). The Prophet ﷺ caught up to him, asked about his mount, and gently struck the beast with his riding rod while supplicating for blessing (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:177). Instantly, the camel surged forward with such vigor that Jābir had to pull back on its reins to keep it from outpacing the Prophet's own she-camel (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:187). 

During the journey, the Prophet ﷺ entered into an extended affectionate conversation with Jābir, offered to purchase the camel from him to relieve his debts, permitted him to ride it all the way home to Medina, and upon reaching the city, paid him the full purchase price in gold and gifted the camel back to him as well (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:177; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:546).

### The Compassion of the Parent Bird
A Companion captured a fledgling bird from its nest (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179). Seeing its chick captured, the parent birds flew desperately toward the camp, and the mother bird cast herself down into the very hands of the man holding her offspring (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179). Seeing the Companions marvel at the mother's sacrifice, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ declared:
> «أَتَعْجَبُونَ مِنْ هَذَا الطَّائِرِ؟ أَخَذْتُمْ فَرْخَهُ، فَطَرَحَ نَفْسَهُ رَحْمَةً بِفَرْخِهِ، وَاللَّهِ لَرَبُّكُمْ أَرْحَمُ بِكُمْ مِنْ هَذَا الطَّائِرِ بِفَرْخِهِ!»
> *"Do you marvel at this bird? You seized its chick, and it cast itself down out of tender mercy for its young! By Allāh, your Lord is more merciful to you than this bird to its fledgling!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:179).

## 8. Aftermath & Strategic Significance
- **Securing the Bedouin Frontier**: The proactive march into the heart of Najd shattered the aggressive posture of Ghaṭafān, showing the desert clans that the Muslims could deploy into remote desert terrains at will.
- **Establishment of Military Jurisprudence**: *Dhāt al-Riqāʿ* became the foundational precedent in Islamic law for *Ṣalāt al-Khawf*, demonstrating that the preservation of ritual prayer remains obligatory even when facing imminent enemy attack.
- **Spiritual Exemplars**: The unwavering devotion of **[[ʿAbbād b. Bishr]]** ﵁ established an eternal standard of *khushūʿ* (reverence) in prayer and sentry duty for all generations of Muslims.
