# ʿĀm al-Wufūd (عام الوفود — سنة الوفود)
*The Year of Delegations — The Universal Influx of the Arabian Tribes into Islam and the Fulfillment of Sūrat al-Naṣr*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 6, *Jimāʿ Abwāb Baʿḍ al-Wufūd ilayhi ﷺ wa-bāraka ʿalayhi* (pp. 254–260), incorporating the transmissions of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[Ibn Hishām]], [[al-Wāqidī]], and [[Ibn Saʿd]], cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah*, 1:605.

---

## 1. Definition & Strategic Context

Following the Conquest of Makkah (**[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**), the decisive defeat of Hawāzin and Thaqīf at Ḥunayn (**[[Ghazwat Ḥunayn]]**), and the deterrence of the Byzantine Empire at Tabūk (**[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]**), tribal Arabia witnessed a seismic political and spiritual realignment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254). 

The pre-Islamic Arabs had historically hesitated to embrace Islam, watching intently to see how the conflict between the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and his own people, **[[Quraysh]]**, would culminate (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254). Quraysh were regarded as the religious leaders and custodians of the Sanctuary (*imām al-nās wa-hādīhim wa-ahl Bayt Allāh al-Ḥarām*); thus, when Makkah surrendered and Quraysh submitted to Islam, all of Arabia recognized that resistance was futile and that divine truth had prevailed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254).

As Abū ʿUbaydah and Ibn Hishām relate, the year nine AH was universally designated as **ʿĀm al-Wufūd** (*The Year of Delegations*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254, 260). Tribal deputations arrived in Medina from every quarter of the Arabian Peninsula—from Najd, the Yemen, Hadramawt, Bahrain, Oman, and the Syrian frontiers—to pledge allegiance to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and accept Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254).

---

## 2. Qurʾānic Manifestation: Sūrat al-Naṣr

The phenomenon of ʿĀm al-Wufūd is the direct historical fulfillment of the divine prophecy revealed in **Sūrat al-Naṣr** (110:1–3):

> «إِذَا جَاءَ نَصْرُ اللَّهِ وَالْفَتْحُ * وَرَأَيْتَ النَّاسَ يَدْخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا * فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَاسْتَغْفِرْهُ إِنَّهُ كَانَ تَوَّابًا»
> *"When the victory of Allāh has come and the Conquest, and you see the people entering into the religion of Allāh in multitudes, then exalt with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him; indeed, He is ever Accepting of repentance."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254).

As recorded in *Subul al-Hudā*, the early scholars understood this sūrah as both a celebration of the universal victory of Tawḥīd and a subtle divine premonition that the Prophet's earthly mission was nearing its sublime completion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254–256).

## 3. Protocol of Welcoming Delegations in Medina

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ instituted an elevated protocol of diplomatic hospitality for receiving tribal embassies (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:259):
1. **Dressing with Dignity**: Jundub b. Makīth ﵁ reported:
   > «كَانَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَآلِهِ وَسَلَّمَ إِذَا قَدِمَ عَلَيْهِ الْوَفْدُ لَبِسَ أَحْسَنَ ثِيَابِهِ وَأَمَرَ أَصْحَابَهُ بِذَٰلِكَ، فَرَأَيْتُهُ وَقَدْ قَدِمَ عَلَيْهِ وَفْدُ كِنْدَةَ وَعَلَيْهِ حُلَّةٌ يَمَانِيَّةٌ، وَعَلَى أَبِي بَكْرٍ وَعُمَرَ مِثْلُهُ»
   > *"Whenever a delegation arrived before the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, he would don his finest garments and instruct his Companions to do likewise. I saw him when the delegation of Kindah arrived: he was wearing a Yemeni mantle (ḥullah Yamāniyyah), and Abū Bakr and ʿUmar were attired similarly!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:259).
2. **Hospitality & Lodging**: The delegations were lodged either at designated guest estates in Medina (such as the residence of Ramlah bint al-Ḥārith al-Najjāriyyah) or in tents pitched directly within the courtyard of **[[al-Masjid al-Nabawī]]** so they could observe the congregation praying behind the Prophet ﷺ and witness the nobility of the Islamic fraternity (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:259).
3. **Instruction and Diplomatic Gifts (*al-Ijāzah*)**: The Prophet ﷺ appointed learned Companions—such as **[[Ubayy b. Kaʿb]]** ﵁, **[[Zayd b. Thābit]]** ﵁, and **[[Muʿādh b. Jabal]]** ﵁—to teach each delegation the recitation of the Qurʾān, the pillars of the faith, and ritual purification (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:259). Upon departure, the Prophet ﷺ personally bestowed generous monetary stipends and fine robes (*jawāʾiz*) upon every envoy according to their rank (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:259).

## 4. The Major Delegations Recorded in *Subul al-Hudā*

Imām al-Ṣāliḥī enumerates and analyzes more than seventy distinct tribal delegations in Volume 6 (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254–440). Among the paramount delegations were:

1. **[[Wafd Thaqīf]] (Delegation of Thaqīf)** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296–302):
   Following the martyrdom of ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd al-Thaqafī ﵁, the lords of al-Ṭāʾif sent a delegation led by ʿAbd Yālīl b. ʿAmr. They requested exemptions from prayer, zakāt, and jihad, and asked to keep their idol **[[al-Lāt]]** for three years. The Prophet ﷺ categorically rejected every compromise of creed, declaring: «لَا خَيْرَ فِي دِينٍ لَا رُكُوعَ فِيهِ» (*"There is no good in a religion that has no bowing in prayer!"*). They surrendered unconditionally to Islam, and the Prophet dispatched **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** ﵁ and **[[al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah]]** ﵁ to demolish al-Lāt.

2. **[[Wafd Banī Tamīm]] (Delegation of Banū Tamīm)** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:287–294):
   A proud bedouin delegation comprising seventy to eighty chieftains, including al-Aqraʿ b. Ḥābis and ʿUṭārid b. Ḥājib. They called out brazenly from behind the Prophet's private chambers: «يَا مُحَمَّدُ اخْرُجْ إِلَيْنَا!» (*"O Muḥammad, come out to us!"*), occasioning the revelation of **Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt** (49:4–5). They challenged the Muslims to an oratorical and poetic contest; **[[Thābit b. Qays]]** ﵁ delivered the response on behalf of the Prophet's orators, and **[[Ḥassān b. Thābit]]** ﵁ recited his immortal poetry, completely eclipsing the bedouin bards and prompting the entire delegation to embrace Islam.

3. **[[Wafd Najrān]] (The Christian Delegation of Najrān)** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:415–422):
   Sixty prominent Christian scholars and dignitaries from southern Arabia, led by the *Sayyid* (al-Ayham), the *ʿĀqib* (ʿAbd al-Masīḥ), and the Bishop *Abū Ḥārithah b. ʿAlqamah*. They engaged the Prophet ﷺ in deep theological disputations regarding ʿĪsā b. Maryam ﵊, occasioning the revelation of the opening sections of **Sūrat Āl ʿImrān** and the verse of mutual imprecation (**Āyat al-Mubāhalah**, 3:61). Recognizing the Prophet's truthfulness, they declined the imprecation, concluded a peace treaty (*Ṣulḥ Najrān*), and requested an honest trustee; the Prophet dispatched **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁, naming him «أَمِينُ هَٰذِهِ الْأُمَّةِ» (*"The Trustworthy of this Ummah"*).

4. **[[Wafd Banī Ḥanīfah]] (Delegation of Banū Ḥanīfah & Musaylimah)** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:326–328):
   Arrived from al-Yamāmah; among them was the pretender **Musaylimah al-Kadhdhāb** ☠, who insolently demanded a share in the Prophet's authority. The Prophet ﷺ confronted him holding a dry palm-stalk (*jarīdah*) and declared that he would not grant him even that dry twig, foretelling his ruin.

5. **[[Wafd Ṭayyiʾ]] (Delegation of Ṭayyiʾ & Zayd al-Khayl)** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:334–337):
   Led by the famed knight Zayd al-Khayl ﵁. The Prophet ﷺ admired his noble character and remarked: «مَا ذُكِرَ لِي رَجُلٌ مِنَ الْعَرَبِ فَرَأَيْتُهُ إِلَّا رَأَيْتُهُ دُونَ مَا ذُكِرَ لِي، إِلَّا زَيْدَ الْخَيْلِ!» (*"No Arab was ever described to me and then I saw him, except that I found him less than described—save for Zayd al-Khayl!"*), renaming him **Zayd al-Khayr** (*Zayd of Goodness*).

6. **[[Wafd al-Ashʿariyyīn wa-Ahl al-Yaman]] (Delegation of the Ashʿarīs & Folk of Yemen)** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:273–274):
   The arrivals from the southern lands whose hearts were soft and faith deep, about whom the Prophet ﷺ proclaimed: «أَتَاكُمْ أَهْلُ الْيَمَنِ، هُمْ أَرَقُّ أَفْئِدَةً وَأَلْيَنُ قُلُوبًا، الْإِيمَانُ يَمَانٍ وَالْحِكْمَةُ يَمَانِيَّةٌ!» (*"The people of Yemen have come to you; they have the gentlest hearts and softest souls; faith is Yemeni, and wisdom is Yemeni!"*).

---

## 5. Enduring Strategic Legacy

The Year of Delegations completed the internal consolidation of Islam:
- It dismantled the fractured tribalism of the Jāhiliyyah, forging a single united *Ummah* under one divine Law (*Sharīʿah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254).
- It established judicial, administrative, and taxation networks (*ʿummāl al-zakāh*) across the entirety of Arabia, dispatching teachers, judges, and governors (such as **[[Muʿādh b. Jabal]]** and **[[Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī]]** to Yemen) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:259).
- It prepared the spiritual and geopolitical launchpad for the Farewell Pilgrimage (**[[Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ]]**) and the international expansion of Islam beyond the borders of Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:254).
