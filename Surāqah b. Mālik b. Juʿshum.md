# Surāqah b. Mālik b. Juʿshum (سراقة بن مالك بن جعشم)
*The Pursuer Who Became a Companion — Bearer of the Bracelets of Kisrā*

## 1. Lineage & Background
Surāqah b. Mālik b. Juʿshum b. Mālik b. ʿAmr b. Taym b. Mudlij b. Murrah b. ʿAbd Manāh b. Kinānah al-Kinānī al-Mudlijī, agnomen Abū Sufyān, was an expert desert scout and tracker residing at Qudayd (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35). He belonged to Banū Mudlij, a celebrated branch of Kinānah renowned throughout Arabia for intuitive physiognomy and tracking (*qiyāfah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:248).

## 2. The Pursuit During the Hijrah (1 AH)
When the Prophet ﷺ and **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ departed Mecca during **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**, Quraysh posted a proclamation offering a bounty of one hundred camels for whoever captured or killed either of them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:248; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:35). Armed and mounted upon his mare, Surāqah galloped in relentless pursuit.

As he drew within striking range, his mare stumbled and pitched him to the earth; three times he drew lots with arrows and cast them, each time the arrows forbidding the endeavor (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:248). Persisting, he charged again until he was close enough to hear the recitation of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:249). The Prophet ﷺ did not look back while Abū Bakr looked around anxiously (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:249). The Prophet ﷺ supplicated, and suddenly the forelegs of Surāqah's mare sank into the firm desert rock up to its knees (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:249). When he urged it forward, a pillar of smoke like a dust-devil rose from the hoof-prints (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:249).

Realizing that divine protection surrounded the Prophet ﷺ, Surāqah cried out for quarter (*amān*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35). The Prophet ﷺ granted him protection and had a parchment of security inscribed for him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:250). In that moment, the Prophet ﷺ prophetically foretold:
> «كَيْفَ بِكَ إِذَا لَبِسْتَ سِوَارَيْ كِسْرَى؟»
> *"How will it be with you when you wear the bracelets of Kisrā (Chosroes)?"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35).

Surāqah departed, turning back every searching Meccan scout he encountered by telling them: *"I have already searched this path thoroughly for you"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:250).

## 3. Impersonation by Iblīs at Badr & Later Islam
At **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** (2 AH), when Quraysh hesitated before marching, **[[Iblīs]]** took the physical form of Surāqah b. Mālik (as he was a respected chieftain of Banū Kinānah) to assure them of alliance and victory, but fled when the archangel Jibrīl ﵊ descended (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:22; Qurʾān 8:48).

Surāqah embraced Islam upon the conquest of Makkah (**[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**, 8 AH) when he presented the written guarantee given to him at the Hijrah to the Prophet ﷺ at Jiʿrānah (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35; *Subul al-Hudā*, 3:250).

## 4. Fulfillment of the Prophecy: The Bracelets of Kisrā
Years later, during the caliphate of **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, following the conquest of the Persian Empire and the capture of Ctesiphon (al-Madāʾin), the regalia of the Persian emperor—including his gold crown, bejeweled belt, and golden armbands (*siwāray Kisrā*)—were brought before ʿUmar (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35).

ʿUmar summoned Surāqah b. Mālik, whose arms were notably hairy, and placed the golden bracelets, belt, and crown of Chosroes upon him (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35). ʿUmar commanded: *"Raise your hands and say: Praise be to Allāh who stripped them from Kisrā b. Hurmuz and bestowed them upon Surāqah, an Arab Bedouin of Mudlij!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:35–36).

Surāqah passed away in 24 AH during the caliphate of ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:36).
