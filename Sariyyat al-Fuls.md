# Sariyyat al-Fuls (سرية علي بن أبي طالب إلى الفلس)
*The expedition of ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib to dismantle the central pagan shrine of Ṭayyiʾ, the seizure of the temple treasury swords, and the honorable treatment of the house of Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī*

## 1. Strategic Context & Mandate

Following the liberation of Makkah and the pacification of northern coalitions, the Prophet ﷺ ordered the comprehensive obliteration of major tribal pagan sanctuaries across the Arabian peninsula (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). In Rabīʿ al-Ākhir 9 AH, the Prophet ﷺ targeted **al-Fuls**, the colossal stone idol and national sanctuary of the great confederation of **Ṭayyiʾ**, situated at Rakk near Mount Salmā in northern Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218, 219).

The Prophet ﷺ commissioned **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ at the head of a fast-moving detachment of **one hundred and fifty (150) men** (recorded by Ibn Saʿd as 200) chosen entirely from the **[[al-Anṣār|Anṣār]]**, mounted upon 100 camels and 50 horses (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). The Prophet ﷺ gave ʿAlī a black battle banner (*rāyah sawdāʾ*) and a white standard (*liwāʾ abyaḍ*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).

## 2. The Dawn Strike & Demolition of the Sanctuary

ʿAlī advanced rapidly into the territory of Ṭayyiʾ, launching a synchronized dawn assault (*shannū al-ghārata maʿa al-fajr*) upon the settlement of the clan of Ḥātim (*maḥallat Āl Ḥātim*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). 

The Muslim warriors completely dismantled and demolished the idol of **al-Fuls** (*hadamū al-Fulsa wa-kharrahūhu*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). The paramount chieftain of Ṭayyiʾ, **[[ʿAdī b. Ḥātim]]**, who was a Christian nobleman at the time, fled north to the Christian territories of Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).

The detachment secured the area, capturing numerous prisoners—foremost among them **Saffānah bint Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī**, sister of ʿAdī b. Ḥātim—and rounding up extensive herds of camels and sheep (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). Within the underground secret vault and treasury of the idol (*khizānat al-Fuls*), ʿAlī discovered three legendary swords:
1. **Rasūb**
2. **al-Makhdham** (both previously presented to the idol by the Ghassanid monarch al-Ḥārith b. Abī Shamir)
3. **al-Yamānī**, along with three coats of mail (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).

## 3. Distribution at Rakk & Protection of Āl Ḥātim

ʿAlī appointed **[[Abū Qatādah al-Anṣārī]]** ﵁ as officer over the captives and placed **ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAtīk** ﵁ in charge of the livestock and silver currency (*al-māshiyah wal-riqqah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). 

Upon halting at **Rakk** (a settlement near Mount Salmā), the spoils were divided (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218, 219). ʿAlī set aside the swords *Rasūb* and *al-Makhdham* as the personal special portion (**Ṣafiyy**) for the Prophet ﷺ, extracted the fifth (*khumus*), and distributed the remainder to the troops (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). 

Crucially, in deference to the proverbial nobility and generosity of Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī, ʿAlī strictly isolated the captives belonging to the family of Ḥātim (*wa-ʿazala Āla Ḥātim*), refusing to distribute them among the warriors as ordinary war booty, and escorted them under honorable guard directly to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).

## 4. Magnanimity toward Saffānah & The Catalyst for ʿAdī's Conversion

In Medina, when the Prophet ﷺ walked past the enclosure of the captives, Saffānah bint Ḥātim stood before him with eloquent dignity and pleaded:
> «يا رسول الله، غاب الوافد وانقطع الوالد، وأنا عجوز كبيرة ما بي من خدمة، فمُنّ عليّ منّ الله عليك!»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh, the provider has fled, the father has died, and I am a helpless woman incapable of servitude; bestow your favor upon me, may Allāh bestow His favor upon you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).

Recognizing her lineage and the noble character of her father, the Prophet ﷺ emancipated her unconditionally, clothed her honorably, and arranged safe caravan transport for her to Syria to rejoin her brother (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218). 

Upon reaching ʿAdī in Syria, Saffānah said to him: *"I have witnessed conduct such as no earthly king displays; go to him at once! If he is a Prophet, you will have precedence in following him; and if he is a worldly ruler, you will not be disgraced in his realm."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218; *al-Iṣābah*, 4:388–389). Convinced by her testimony, ʿAdī traveled immediately to Medina and embraced Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).

---

## 5. Strategic Significance

1. **Elimination of Northern Polytheism**: The destruction of al-Fuls ended organized idolatry within the formidable tribal confederation of Ṭayyiʾ, consolidating Islam's spiritual dominion across northern Arabia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).
2. **Prophetic Reverence for Noble Character**: The Prophet's gracious treatment of Saffānah in honor of Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī exemplified the Islamic principle that moral virtue in Jahiliyyah is acknowledged and perfected in Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218).
3. **Peaceful Integration of Ṭayyiʾ**: The magnanimity shown to the household of Ḥātim transformed ʿAdī b. Ḥātim from a fleeing adversary into one of the most stalwart and loyal champions of the Caliphate (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:218; *al-Iṣābah*, 4:388).
