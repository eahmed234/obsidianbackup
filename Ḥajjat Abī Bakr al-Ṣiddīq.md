# Ḥajjat Abī Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (حجة أبي بكر الصديق بالناس وإبلاغ سورة براءة)
*The Pilgrimage Led by Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq and the Proclamation of Sūrat Barāʾah — The Purification of the Sacred Sanctuary from Idolatry*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 12, *al-Nawʿ al-Thānī ʿAshar fī Ḥawādith al-Sanah al-Tāsiʿah* (pp. 73–75), compiling the canonical transmissions from *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, and *Sunan al-Nasāʾī* from Abū Hurayrah ﵁, ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib ﵁, and ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar ﵄, cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah*, 4:446 (Abū Bakr) and 4:269 (ʿAlī).

---

## 1. Appointment of Abū Bakr as Amīr al-Ḥajj

In the year nine AH, following the return from Tabūk, the season of pilgrimage approached (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:73). The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ appointed his closest companion and supreme counselor, **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, as the supreme leader of the pilgrimage (**Amīr al-Ḥajj**) over the Muslims (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:73).

Abū Bakr set out from Medina at the head of three hundred Muslim pilgrims (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:73). The Prophet ﷺ sent with him twenty sacrificial camels (*badanāt*), which he garlanded and marked with his own blessed hand (*qalladahā wa-ashʿarahā bi-yadihi*), putting them under the charge of Nājiyah b. Jundub al-Aslamī ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:73). Abū Bakr personally drove five sacrificial camels, and **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁ also drove sacrificial livestock (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:73).

---

## 2. Revelation of Sūrat Barāʾah & Dispatch of ʿAlī ﵁

Shortly after Abū Bakr's caravan had departed Medina, the opening verses of **Sūrat al-Tawbah** (**Barāʾah**) were revealed, severing all covenants with idolaters and establishing the total purification of the Sacred Sanctuary (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:73–75).

The Prophet ﷺ was advised: "O Messenger of Allāh, why do you not send this revelation to Abū Bakr?" 

The Prophet ﷺ replied:
> «لَا يُؤَدِّي عَنِّي إِلَّا رَجُلٌ مِنْ أَهْلِ بَيْتِي!»
> *"No one may discharge this on my behalf except a man from the folk of my household!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74–75).

As Imām al-Ṣāliḥī clarifies, according to the inviolable customary law of the Arabs, when a paramount leader entered into a covenant or dissolved a treaty, nobody possessed the legal authority to bind or dissolve that covenant except the leader himself or a male member of his immediate household (*min ahl baytihi*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:75). 

Therefore, the Prophet ﷺ summoned **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁, mounted him upon his own noble she-camel, **al-ʿAḍbāʾ**, and dispatched him swiftly to overtake the pilgrimage caravan (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:73–74).

---

## 3. The Meeting at al-ʿArj

ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib ﵁ rode at high speed and intercepted Abū Bakr's caravan at **al-ʿArj** (or at Ḍajnān) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:73–74).

Hearing the gait of the Prophet's camel, Abū Bakr turned with concern and asked:
> «أَمِيرٌ أَوْ مَأْمُورٌ؟»
> *"Are you a commander or under command?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74).

ʿAlī ﵁ replied with immense respect:
> «لَا، بَلْ مَأْمُورٌ! أَمَرَنِي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ أَنْ أَلْحَقَ بِكَ فَأُؤَذِّنَ فِي النَّاسِ بِبَرَاءَةَ!»
> *"No, rather I am under command! The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanded me to join you and proclaim Barāʾah among the people!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74).

Abū Bakr ﵁ continued as the supreme commander of the pilgrimage, leading the prayers and directing the sacred rites, while ʿAlī ﵁ accompanied him to execute the diplomatic and legal proclamation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74).

---

## 4. The Proclamation at Minā on the Day of Sacrifice

On the Day of Sacrifice (**Yawm al-Naḥr**, the tenth of Dhū al-Ḥijjah) at Minā, between the Jamarāt, ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib ﵁ stood before the vast multitude of gathered tribes and recited the opening forty verses of Sūrat Barāʾah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74; *al-Iṣābah*, 4:269).

ʿAlī and his designated heralds—including **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁—called out at the top of their voices four foundational decrees (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74):

> 1. «أَلَا لَا يَدْخُلُ الْجَنَّةَ إِلَّا نَفْسٌ مُؤْمِنَةٌ!»
>    *"Behold! No soul shall enter Paradise except a believing soul!"*
> 2. «وَلَا يَطُوفُ بِالْبَيْتِ عُرْيَانٌ!»
>    *"And no naked person shall ever circumambulate the House!"* (abolishing the pre-Islamic practice where pilgrims outside the *Ḥums* stripped off their clothes).
> 3. «وَلَا يَحُجُّ بَعْدَ الْعَامِ مُشْرِكٌ!»
>    *"And no polytheist shall ever perform pilgrimage after this year!"*
> 4. «وَمَنْ كَانَ لَهُ عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ عَهْدٌ فَعَهْدُهُ إِلَى مُدَّتِهِ!»
>    *"And whoever holds a covenant with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, his covenant is honored until its stated expiration!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74).

For those polytheist tribes without a specific treaty, a four-month period of grace was granted during which they could travel in safety, after which no polytheist armed entity would be tolerated in the sacred Ḥijāz (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74).

---

## 5. Enduring Strategic & Legal Significance

1. **Paving the Way for Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ**: This historic expedition cleansed the Kaʿbah, Minā, and ʿArafāt of every vestige of pagan nudity, idolatry, and polytheistic ritual. When the Prophet ﷺ performed his Farewell Pilgrimage (**[[Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ]]**) the following year in 10 AH, not a single polytheist stood among the believers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74–75).
2. **Harmonious Partnership of the Companions**: The pilgrimage demonstrated the profound mutual veneration between Abū Bakr and the Ahl al-Bayt: Abū Bakr was confirmed by the Prophet ﷺ as the religious and administrative leader (*Amīr*) of the community, while ʿAlī was honored as the noble representative of the Prophet's household entrusted with legal dissolution (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:74–75).
