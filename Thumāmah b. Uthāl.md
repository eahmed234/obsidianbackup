# Thumāmah b. Uthāl (ثمامة بن أثال)
*Abū Umāmah al-Ḥanafī — Chieftain of al-Yamāmah, the first pilgrim to chant the Talbiyah in Makkah, imposer of the wheat embargo, and stalwart against the apostasy of Musaylimah*

## 1. Lineage & Chieftaincy in al-Yamāmah

**Thumāmah b. Uthāl b. al-Nuʿmān b. Maslamah b. ʿUtbah b. Thaʿlabah b. Yarbūʿ b. Thaʿlabah b. al-Duʾil b. Ḥanīfah al-Ḥanafī al-Yamāmī**, known by his kunya **Abū Umāmah**, was the paramount hereditary chieftain of the powerful Banū Ḥanīfah in al-Yamāmah (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:525). 

Before his conversion, Thumāmah was a formidable adversary of the emerging Muslim state (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). Musaylimah al-Kadhdhāb had previously dispatched him toward the Prophet ﷺ, and Thumāmah sought an opportunity to assassinate the Prophet ﷺ, prompting the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ to supplicate that Allāh grant him power over him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:526).

## 2. Capture & Miraculous Transformation in Medina

In al-Muḥarram 6 AH, a Muslim cavalry detachment operating in Najd under [[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]] ﵁ apprehended Thumāmah while he was traveling toward the Ḥijāz intending ʿUmrah, unaware of his status (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). They brought him to Medina and secured him to one of the central pillars of [[Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī|the Prophetic Mosque]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:525).

When the Prophet ﷺ observed him bound, he ordered his Companions to treat his captivity with nobility and commanded that food and milk from his own milch-camel (*liqḥah*) be brought to him daily (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). Over three days, the Prophet ﷺ approached him, offering him Islam or ransom, to which Thumāmah famously replied:
> «عندي خير يا محمد، إن تقتل تقتل ذا دم، وإن تنعم تنعم على شاكر، وإن ترد الفداء فسل منه ما شئت»
> *"I have good expectation, O Muḥammad. If you kill, you kill one possessing blood revenge; if you show bounty, you bestow it on one who is grateful; and if you seek ransom, demand whatever you wish!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:525).

On the third day, the Prophet ﷺ commanded his immediate, unconditional release (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72). Deeply moved by this prophetic magnanimity, Thumāmah retired to a grove of date palms near the mosque, washed himself, returned, and openly proclaimed:
> «أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله، وأشهد أن محمداً عبده ورسوله... واللَّه ما كان على الأرض وجه أبغض إليّ من وجهك فقد أصبح وجهك أحبّ الوجوه كلها إليّ»
> *"I bear witness that there is no god but Allāh, and that Muḥammad is His servant and Messenger... By Allāh, there was no face on earth more hated to me than your face, but this day your face has become the most beloved of all faces to me!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:525).

## 3. The Public Talbiyah in Makkah & The Economic Embargo

After his conversion, Thumāmah proceeded to Makkah to perform ʿUmrah with the blessing of the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72). Entering the sanctuary, he raised his voice with the Talbiyah—becoming the first person to openly recite the Islamic pilgrim chant in polytheist Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

When Quraysh surrounded him in fury, threatening his life, an elder restrained them, reminding them that Makkah was entirely dependent upon the fertile plains of al-Yamāmah for its grain (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72). Thumāmah swore an oath:
> «والّذي نفسي بيده لا تأتيكم حبّة من اليمامة حتّى يأذن فيها رسول اللَّه صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم»
> *"By Him in Whose Hand is my soul, not a single grain of wheat from al-Yamāmah shall reach you until the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ grants permission!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:526; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

Thumāmah returned to al-Yamāmah and enforced an uncompromising total embargo. The blockade brought Quraysh to severe starvation, forcing them to consume *al-ʿilhiz* (blood clotted with hair), until Abū Sufyān beseeched the Prophet ﷺ by the ties of womb and mercy to intervene (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:526). The Prophet ﷺ dispatched a letter to Thumāmah instructing him to permit the caravans of provision to pass, which he promptly obeyed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:526), fulfilling the context of Qurʾān 23:76:
> ﴿وَلَقَدْ أَخَذْنَاهُم بِالْعَذَابِ فَمَا اسْتَكَانُوا لِرَبِّهِمْ وَمَا يَتَضَرَّعُونَ﴾
> *«And We had already seized them with agony, but they did not submit to their Lord, nor did they humble themselves»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:526; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

## 4. Steadfastness during the Riddah Wars & Martyrdom

When the false prophet Musaylimah al-Kadhdhāb declared apostasy and swept al-Yamāmah into rebellion following the Prophet's ﷺ passing, Thumāmah b. Uthāl stood immovably upon orthodoxy (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:526). He openly preached against Musaylimah, admonishing Banū Ḥanīfah and reciting poetry in defense of the truth:
> أهمّ بترك القول ثمّ يردّني ... إلى القول إنعام النّبيّ محمّد
> شكرت له فكّي من الغلّ بعد ما ... رأيت خيالا من حسام مهنّد
> *"I resolve to hold my speech, but I am brought back to speak by the bounty of the Prophet Muḥammad;*
> *I rendered thanks to him for releasing my shackles, after I had glimpsed the shadow of an Indian blade."* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:526).

Rallying those of his tribe who remained true to Islam, Thumāmah evacuated al-Yamāmah and joined the campaign of **al-ʿAlāʾ b. al-Ḥaḍramī** against the apostates of Bahrain (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:526). He fought with extraordinary courage until the victory was won (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:526).

Following the triumph in Bahrain (12 AH), Thumāmah purchased a decorative cloak (*ḥullah*) that had belonged to the slain apostate chieftain al-Ḥuṭam (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:526). While wearing it, he was encountered by men of Banū Qays b. Thaʿlabah who mistakenly assumed that he was the killer and despoiler of their clansman; they attacked and martyred him (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:526).
