# Minā (منى)
*The Sacred Valley of Encampment, Pelting, Sacrifice, and the Prophetic Covenants*

## 1. Topography & Sanctity
Minā is a sacred valley nestled in the mountains between Mecca and Muzdalifah, situated entirely within the boundaries of the Ḥaram sanctuary (*al-Ḥaram al-Makkī*). In Islamic law and Prophetic biography, it is the primary encampment for pilgrims during the days of Hajj:
- **Yawm al-Tarwiyah** (8th Dhū al-Ḥijjah): Pilgrims depart from Mecca in the forenoon and enter Minā, performing Zuhr, ʿAṣr, Maghrib, ʿIshāʾ, and Fajr of the 9th Dhū al-Ḥijjah there (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:468).
- **Yawm al-Naḥr & Ayyām al-Tashrīq** (10th–13th Dhū al-Ḥijjah): Pilgrims return from Muzdalifah to pelt the Jamarāt, slaughter sacrificial offerings (*hady*), shave or trim their hair, and spend the nights of Tashrīq in devotion (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:475–478).

Regarding the physical nature of Minā as open sacred ground accessible to all pilgrims without private enclosure, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was asked whether a permanent dwelling or pavilion might be erected for him at Minā to shade him from the sun, to which he ﷺ replied:
> «لَا، مِنًى مَنَاخُ مَنْ سَبَقَ إِلَيْهِ»
> *"No, Minā is an open encampment ground for whoever arrives first."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:477).

## 2. Pre-Hijrah Milestones: The Daʿwah and the Pledges of al-ʿAqabah
Long before the legislation of the Farewell Pilgrimage, Minā was the annual theater where the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ preached Islam to visiting Arab tribes during the pilgrimage seasons:
- **Tribal Outreach**: Sayyidunā Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh ﵄ reported that for ten years in Mecca, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ would follow the tribes to their campsites in Majannah, ʿUkāẓ, and during the pilgrimage seasons at Minā, saying:
  > «مَنْ يُؤْوِينِي؟ وَمَنْ يَنْصُرُنِي؟ حَتَّى أُبَلِّغَ رِسَالَاتِ رَبِّي وَلَهُ الْجَنَّةُ»
  > *"Who will shelter me? Who will aid me so that I may convey the messages of my Lord, and for him is Paradise?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:201).
- **The Pledges of al-ʿAqabah**: At Thaniyyat al-ʿAqabah, on the northern edge of Minā near Jamrat al-ʿAqabah, the early converts of Yathrib from the Aws and Khazraj secretly met the Prophet ﷺ:
  - **The First Pledge of al-ʿAqabah** (**[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā]]**, 12 BH): Twelve Anṣārī delegates pledged allegiance upon moral rectitude, purity, and monotheism (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:195–196).
  - **The Second Pledge of al-ʿAqabah** (**[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]]**, 13 BH): Seventy-three men and two women of the Anṣār concluded the historic covenant of mutual military protection and allegiance that laid the geopolitical foundation for the Hijrah to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:201–211).

## 3. Rites of the Farewell Pilgrimage (10 AH)
During the Farewell Pilgrimage (**[[Hajjat al-Wadāʿ]]**), Minā witnessed the definitive establishment of the rites (*manāsik*) of Hajj:

### The Pelting of Jamrat al-ʿAqabah & Proclamation of Rites
On the morning of the 10th of Dhū al-Ḥijjah (*Yawm al-Naḥr*), the Prophet ﷺ rode his she-camel al-Qaṣwāʾ to Jamrat al-ʿAqabah and pelted it with seven pebbles (*ḥaṣayāt al-khadhf*), stating to the multitudes:
> «خُذُوا عَنِّي مَنَاسِكَكُمْ، فَإِنِّي لَا أَدْرِي لَعَلِّي لَا أَحُجُّ بَعْدَ حَجَّتِي هَذِهِ»
> *"Take from me your rites of pilgrimage, for indeed I do not know, perhaps I shall not perform Hajj after this pilgrimage of mine."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:475).

### The Great Sacrifice at the Manḥar
The Prophet ﷺ then advanced to the slaughtering area (*al-Manḥar*) in Minā, declaring:
> «نَحَرْتُ هَاهُنَا، وَمِنًى كُلُّهَا مَنْحَرٌ»
> *"I have sacrificed here, and all of Minā is a place of sacrifice."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:477).

He ﷺ personally slaughtered sixty-three sacrificial camels (*budn*) corresponding to the sixty-three years of his blessed life, after which he handed the spear (*ḥarbah*) to **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁, who completed the slaughter of the remaining thirty-seven camels to fulfill the total offering of one hundred camels (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:477).

### The Shaving of the Head & Distribution of the Prophetic Hair
After the sacrifice, the Prophet ﷺ called the barber, Maʿmar b. ʿAbdillāh ﵁, and turned the right side of his blessed head to be shaved, distributing those hair strands to the Companions surrounding him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:477). He ﷺ then turned the left side to be shaved and presented that hair to **[[Abū Ṭalḥah al-Anṣārī]]** ﵁ and his wife **[[Umm Sulaym bint Milḥān]]** ﵂ to distribute among the believers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:478). When **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁ requested a portion, the Prophet ﷺ granted him his blessed forelock (*nāṣiyah*), which Khālid stitched into the front of his battle-helmet (*qalanaswah*), remarking that he never faced a hostile gathering thereafter without putting them to flight (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:478).

### The Invocation for the Shaven
The Prophet ﷺ invoked divine mercy and forgiveness for the pilgrims:
> «اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِلْمُحَلِّقِينَ»
> *"O Allāh, forgive those who shave their heads!"*
The Companions asked: *"And those who trim their hair (*al-muqaṣṣirīn*), O Messenger of Allāh?"* He ﷺ repeated the prayer for the shaven three times, and on the fourth time added: *«وَالْمُقَصِّرِينَ»* (*"And those who trim"*), granting three-to-one merit to those who shaved completely (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:478).

### The Proclamation of Ease and Joy
To ensure pilgrims experienced no spiritual distress from inadvertent transposition of rites on the busy Day of Sacrifice, whenever anyone came inquiring: *"I shaved before sacrificing"* or *"I performed the ṭawāf before sacrificing"*, the Prophet ﷺ consistently answered:
> «افْعَلْ وَلَا حَرَجَ»
> *"Do so, and there is no harm."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:478).

He ﷺ dispatched ʿAbdullāh b. Ḥudhāfah al-Sahmī ﵁ (and in one transmission Kaʿb b. Mālik ﵁) to proclaim throughout the encampments of Minā:
> «إِنَّهَا أَيَّامُ أَكْلٍ وَشُرْبٍ وَذِكْرِ اللَّهِ»
> *"These are days of eating, drinking, and remembrance of Allāh."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:478).

## 4. Key Topographical Landmarks
- **[[Masjid al-Khayf]] (مسجد الخيف)**: Situated at the base of the northern slope of Mount Ṣabīḥ in Minā, where seventy prophets were reported to have prayed, and where the Prophet ﷺ camped and prayed during the days of Tashrīq (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:467, 475).
- **The Three Jamarāt (الجمرات الثلاث)**:
  - *al-Jamrah al-Ṣughrā* (The Smallest/First Pillar).
  - *al-Jamrah al-Wusṭā* (The Middle Pillar).
  - *Jamrat al-ʿAqabah* (The Largest Pillar, closest to Mecca).
- **Wādī Muḥassir (وادي محسر)**: The small intervening valley separating Minā from Muzdalifah, where the army of Abraha and his elephant were destroyed, through which the Prophet ﷺ hastened his pace (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:475).
