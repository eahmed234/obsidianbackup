# Minā (منى)
*The Sacred Valley of the Rites, the Pledges of al-ʿAqabah, and the Great Sacrifice*

> [!NOTE]
> **Canonical Sourcing**: This topographical profile is established upon *[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād]]* by Imām Muḥammad b. Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī (vol. 3, pp. 179, 199–200; vol. 8, pp. 468, 473–482).

---

## 1. Topography, Nomenclature & Sacred Sanctity
- **Location**: An elongated valley situated east of **[[Makkah al-Mukarramah]]**, lying between the sacred station of **[[Muzdalifah]]** and the pass of al-ʿAqabah, approximately six to seven kilometers east of the Kaʿbah within the bounds of the Holy Sanctuary (*al-Ḥaram*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:468).
- **Etymology**: Classical Arabic philologists state that it was named *Minā* either from the outpouring of blood during the animal sacrifices (*yumnā fīhi al-dam*), or from the *Umniyyah* (divine hope and yearning) granted to Ibrāhīm ﵊ when the ram was ransomed for his son Ismāʿīl ﵊ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:468).
- **Masjid al-Khayf**: The famous central congregational sanctuary of Minā where the Prophet ﷺ encamped and led prayers, regarding which prophetic tradition affirms that seventy prophets prayed before him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:468).

---

## 2. The Cradle of the Madinan Dawn: The Pledges of al-ʿAqabah
Before the valley became the eternal stage of the farewell pilgrimage, its furthest boundary toward Makkah—the defile of **al-ʿAqabah**—served as the secretive nocturnal birthplace of the Islamic polity (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:179, 199):
- **First Contacts (11th & 12th Years of Prophethood)**: At the seasonal trade fairs of Minā, the Prophet ﷺ met the six early seekers from Khazraj and later ratified **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā]]** with twelve Anṣārī leaders beneath the pass (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:179).
- **The Historic Second Pledge (13th Year of Prophethood)**: In the deep midnight of the final day of Tashrīq, seventy-three men and two women of the Aws and Khazraj slipped away from their caravans to **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]]**, pledging their lives and protection to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and unlocking the door to **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:199–201).

## 3. Rites of the Farewell Pilgrimage (*Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ*) (10 AH)
During the culminating Farewell Pilgrimage, Minā was the principal encampment and focal theater of the pilgrimage rituals (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:468, 473–482):
- **Yawm al-Tarwiyah (8th Dhū al-Ḥijjah)**: The Prophet ﷺ marched to Minā with over 100,000 pilgrims, praying Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr, Maghrib, ʿIshāʾ, and Fajr shortened as two-rakʿah prayers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:468).
- **Yawm al-Naḥr (10th Dhū al-Ḥijjah)**: Returning from Muzdalifah, the Prophet ﷺ performed four monumental actions:
  1. *Ramy Jamrat al-ʿAqabah*: Cast seven pebbles at the large pillar, reciting the Takbīr with each throw (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:473–475).
  2. *The Sacrificial Offering*: Personally slaughtered sixty-three sacrificial camels (*budn*) with his own blessed hand—one for each year of his life—before handing the blade to **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ to complete the hundred camels (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:476).
  3. *Shaving the Head*: Shaved his head, distributing his blessed hairs to **[[Abū Ṭalḥah al-Anṣārī]]** ﵁ for the believers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:477–478).
- **The Days of Tashrīq (11th–13th Dhū al-Ḥijjah)**:
  - Encamped at Minā during the three festive days, stoning the three pillars (*al-Ṣughrā*, *al-Wusṭā*, and *al-Kubrā*) daily after the sun passed its meridian (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:481–482).
  - Delivered the Sermon of Minā, reiterating the sanctity of Muslim life, honor, and property, and declaring:
    > «إِنَّهَا أَيَّامُ أَكْلٍ وَشُرْبٍ وَذِكْرٍ لِلَّهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ»
    > *"Verily, they are days of eating, drinking, and the remembrance of Allāh, Mighty and Majestic!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:479).
