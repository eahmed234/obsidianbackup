# Saʿd b. ʿUbādah (سعد بن عبادة الساعدي) ﵁
*Abū Thābit al-Khazrajī al-Sāʿidī — Sayyid al-Khazraj (Paramount Chieftain of the Khazraj), Standard-Bearer of the Anṣār, and The Paragon of Generosity*

## 1. Lineage, Clan Associations & Pre-Islamic Eminence
He is Saʿd b. ʿUbādah b. Dulaym b. Ḥārithah b. Ḥarām b. Ḥuzaymah b. Thaʿlabah b. Ṭarīf b. al-Khazraj b. Sāʿidah b. Kaʿb b. al-Khazraj al-Anṣārī al-Sāʿidī (kunyas: Abū Thābit and Abū Qays; title: *Sayyid al-Khazraj*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55). His mother was **ʿAmrah bint Masʿūd** ﵂, who embraced Islam and was counted among the female Companions; she passed away during the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime in 5 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55).

In pre-Islamic Yathrib, Saʿd held uncontested hereditary nobility as the supreme chief of Banū Sāʿidah and the paramount leader of the entire Khazraj confederation (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55). He was titled *al-Kāmil* ("The Complete") by his peers because he had mastered three aristocratic skills: Arabic literacy and fine penmanship, swimming, and archery (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55).

Saʿd belonged to a household celebrated across the Arabian Peninsula for four unbroken generations of boundless generosity: Dulaym, Ḥārithah, Saʿd, and his son Qays (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55). At their ancestral fortress (*uṭum*), a herald stood every single day calling aloud to travelers and the needy: *“Whoever desires meat and fat, let him come to the fortress of Dulaym b. Ḥārithah!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55). Saʿd was accustomed to supplicate:
> «اللَّهُمَّ هَبْ لِي مَجْدًا، لَا مَجْدَ إِلَّا بِفِعَالٍ، وَلَا فِعَالَ إِلَّا بِمَالٍ، اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّهُ لَا يُصْلِحُنِي الْقَلِيلُ وَلَا أَصْلُحُ عَلَيْهِ»
> *"O Allāh, bestow upon me glory! There is no glory without noble deeds, and there are no noble deeds without wealth. O Allāh, little wealth does not suffice me, nor am I suited for it!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:56).

## 2. Conversion, ʿAqabah & The Hijrah
Saʿd embraced Islam in the early dawn of the Madinan mission. When the Yathribī delegation gathered at [[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]], Saʿd was chosen as one of the twelve Nuqabāʾ (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55). 

Quraysh recognized the existential threat posed by the two Saʿds — **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁ for the Aws and Saʿd b. ʿUbādah for the Khazraj — who together commanded the total allegiance of Yathrib (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:198).

Following the Prophet's ﷺ arrival in Medina, Saʿd's legendary hospitality became a cornerstone of the nascent community. Every single day, a large bowl of nourishing broth and meat (*jafnah*) sent from Saʿd's home circulated to whichever apartment of the Prophet's ﷺ wives the Messenger of Allāh was residing in (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55). When evening fell over the Prophet's Mosque, individual Sahābah would host one, two, or a small group of the impoverished Companions of the Bench (**[[Ahl al-Ṣuffah]]**); Saʿd b. ʿUbādah alone would habitually escort eighty men home to feast each night (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:56).

The Prophet ﷺ visited Saʿd in his home, made peace upon him, and raised his hands in supplication:
> «اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ صَلَوَاتِكَ وَرَحْمَتَكَ عَلَى آلِ سَعْدِ بْنِ عُبَادَةَ»
> *"O Allāh, bestow Your blessings and Your mercy upon the family of Saʿd b. ʿUbādah!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55–56).
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ also affirmed: *“May Allāh reward the Anṣār with good on our behalf, especially ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām and Saʿd b. ʿUbādah!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:56).

## 3. Military Role & The Standard of the Anṣār
- **The Battle of Badr (2 AH)**: Al-Bukhārī affirmed his presence at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**; Ibn Saʿd noted that while he was eagerly preparing to depart, he suffered a venomous bite (*nahasa*) that immobilized him in Medina, prompting the Prophet ﷺ to console him: *“He was keen upon participating”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55).
- **The Standard-Bearer of the Anṣār**: In the military campaigns of the Prophet ﷺ, the supreme flags of command were strictly designated. Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄ related: *“The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had two principal standards in all battles: the standard of the Muhājirūn was with ʿAlī, and the standard of the Anṣār was with Saʿd b. ʿUbādah”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:55).
- **At the Trench (5 AH)**: At **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**, he held the standard of the Anṣār (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:371). When the Prophet ﷺ consulted the leaders regarding buying off Banū Ghaṭafān with one-third of Medina's fruit harvest, Saʿd joined Saʿd b. Muʿādh in firmly refusing compromise (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:198).

## 4. Succession at Saqīfah & Demise in Syria
Following the demise of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ in 11 AH, the Anṣār initially convened at **[[Saqīfat Banī Sāʿidah]]** — the ancestral portico belonging to Saʿd's own clan — where Saʿd was proposed as the candidate of the Anṣār for leadership (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:56). When the discussion culminated in consensus upon **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, Saʿd withdrew quietly.

Saʿd subsequently traveled north to Syria and settled in the region of Ḥawrān (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:56). He passed away in Ḥawrān around 15 AH (or 16 AH) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:56). Authorities mention his grave in al-Munayḥah, a village in the Ghūṭah of Damascus, or at Buṣrā (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:56). 

His legacy endured through his illustrious sons **Qays b. Saʿd**, Saʿīd, and Isḥāq, and his grandson Shuraḥbīl b. Saʿīd (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:56). His son Qays was renowned as one of the most formidable strategists, commanders, and generous leaders of the next generation, appointed commander to Ṣudāʾ by the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:211).
