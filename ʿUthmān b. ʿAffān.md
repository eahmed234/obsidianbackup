# ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān (عثمان بن عفان) ﵁
*Abū ʿAbdillāh al-Umawī al-Qurashī — Dhū al-Nūrayn (Possessor of the Two Lights), Third Caliph*

## 1. Lineage, Ancestry & Physical Description
### Patrilineal Descent & Clan
He is **ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ b. Umayyah b. ʿAbd Shams b. ʿAbd Manāf b. Quṣayy b. Kilāb al-Qurashī al-Umawī** (kunyas: **Abū ʿAbdillāh** and **Abū ʿAmr**; honorific: **Dhū al-Nūrayn**) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:237, 277). 

His lineage merges directly with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at their fourth-generation ancestor **ʿAbd Manāf b. Quṣayy** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377). As the foremost scion of **Banū Umayyah b. ʿAbd Shams**, he belonged to one of the two most illustrious and noble branches of **[[Quraysh]]**. Even in the pre-Islamic era (*Jāhiliyyah*), ʿUthmān was celebrated across Arabia for his consummate dignity, gentleness, immense commercial wealth, generosity, and unimpeachable morality, having never prostrated to an idol or consumed intoxicants (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:277).

### Maternal Heritage & Sacred Kinship
- **Mother**: **Arwā bint Kurayz b. Rabīʿah b. Ḥabīb b. ʿAbd Shams**, who embraced Islam and lived to witness her son's prominence (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:277).
- **Maternal Grandmother**: **Umm Ḥakīm al-Bayḍāʾ bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib**, the paternal aunt of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and twin sister of the Prophet's father, ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377).
- Through this maternal descent, ʿUthmān's mother Arwā was the direct first cousin of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, making ʿUthmān his first cousin once removed, and weaving an exceptionally tight bond of shared Hāshimite and Umayyad blood.

### Birth & Physical Appearance
ʿUthmān was born six years after the Year of the Elephant on the soundest transmission (576 CE) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377). 

He was distinguished by an exceptionally noble and striking appearance:
- **Build**: Medium stature (*rabʿah*), neither overly tall nor short, possessing broad shoulders (*baʿīd mā bayn al-mankibayn*) and well-formed limbs (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377).
- **Facial Features**: Radiant and exceedingly handsome face (*ḥasan al-wajh*), delicate and soft skin (*raqīq al-basharah*), with a full, handsome beard that extended down his chest (*ʿaẓīm al-liḥyah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377). 
- When the Prophet ﷺ dispatched a companion to the house of ʿUthmān and Ruqayyah, the companion lingered in wonder, prompting the Prophet ﷺ to ask: *"Did you linger marveling at the beauty of ʿUthmān and Ruqayyah?"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377).
- **Peerless Modesty**: He possessed supreme bashfulness and spiritual refinement. The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ stated of him:
  > «أَلَا أَسْتَحِي مِنْ رَجُلٍ تَسْتَحِي مِنْهُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ؟»
  > *"Shall I not feel bashful before a man from whom the angels feel bashful?!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:278).

## 2. Marriages, Kinship Networks & Progeny
ʿUthmān’s domestic alliances established sacred ties with the Prophetic household as well as prominent families across Quraysh and the wider tribes:

1. **[[Ruqayyah bint Rasūlillāh]]** ﵂:
   - Daughter of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and **[[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid]]** ﵂. ʿUthmān wed her in Makkah, and together they undertook both the first and second migrations to Abyssinia (**[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]**) and subsequently the migration to Medina (**[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:382; 11:33).
   - **Children**: Bore him **ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUthmān**, after whom ʿUthmān took his principal kunya Abū ʿAbdillāh; the child passed away in infancy in 4 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:34). Ruqayyah fell gravely ill and passed away in Medina in Ramaḍān 2 AH while the Muslim army was victorious at Badr (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:24; 11:34).
2. **Umm Kulthūm bint Rasūlillāh** ﵂:
   - Second daughter of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ. In Rabīʿ al-Awwal 3 AH, the Prophet ﷺ wed her to ʿUthmān to succeed her deceased sister Ruqayyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:36, 278). She remained with him until her passing in Shaʿbān 9 AH without bearing children (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:36).
   - Upon her death, the Prophet ﷺ expressed his love and honor for ʿUthmān: *"Were I to have forty daughters, I would marry them to ʿUthmān one after another until none remained!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:36, 278). Because no other man in human history wed two daughters of a Prophet, ʿUthmān was proclaimed **Dhū al-Nūrayn** ("Possessor of the Two Lights") by the heavenly assembly (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377–378; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:278).
3. **Fāṭimah bint al-Walīd b. ʿAbd Shams**:
   - Qurashī noblewoman from his own clan of ʿAbd Shams; bore him **al-Walīd** and **Saʿīd**.
4. **Fākhitah bint Ghazwān**:
   - Sister of the prominent early Companion **ʿUtbah b. Ghazwān** ﵁; bore him **ʿAbdullāh the younger**.
5. **Umm ʿAmr bint Jundub al-Azdiyyah**:
   - Bore him **ʿAmr**, **Khālid**, **Abān** (the prominent scholar and governor of Medina), **ʿUmar**, and **Maryam**.
6. **Asmāʾ bint Abī Jahl b. Hishām**:
   - Bore him **al-Mughīrah**.
7. **Nāʾilah bint al-Farāfiṣah al-Kalbiyyah**:
   - Noblewoman from the Banū Kalb confederacy. She was renowned for her intellect, eloquent speech, and heroic loyalty. During the insurrection and siege of ʿUthmān's home, she threw herself over ʿUthmān, intercepting sword strokes with her bare hands and suffering severed fingers in his defense; she bore him **ʿAnbasah** and **Maryam**.

## 3. Key Sīrah Milestones
### Earliest Conversion & Abyssinian Migration
ʿUthmān was among the foremost vanguard to embrace Islam, accepting the faith at the invitation of his close friend **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:377; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:277). Facing severe familial persecution from his uncle al-Ḥakam b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ, he stood unwavering. In the fifth year of Prophethood, he led the first group of emigrants with his wife Ruqayyah across the Red Sea in **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]**, prompting the Prophet ﷺ to proclaim:
> *"May Allāh accompany them! ʿUthmān is the first person to migrate with his family for Allāh's sake after the Prophet Lūṭ!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:382; 11:33).

### Status at Badr (2 AH)
As the Muslim army marched toward **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**, Ruqayyah lay incapacitated with smallpox. The Prophet ﷺ explicitly commanded ʿUthmān to remain at her bedside in Medina to care for her, assuring him:
> *"You shall have the reward and spoils of a man who participated at Badr!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:378; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:24; 11:278).
The Prophet ﷺ formally allocated ʿUthmān his full military share of the spoils and war booty, enrolling him canonically among the veterans of **[[Ahl Badr]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:378; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:24).

### The Endowment of Biʾr Rūmah
Upon arriving in Medina, the Muhājirūn found fresh potable water scarce and controlled by an exploitative well owned by a man of Banū Ghifār (or a Jewish resident), who sold a waterskin for a measure of grain (*mudd*). The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ asked who would purchase it in exchange for a spring in Paradise. ʿUthmān purchased **[[Biʾr Rūmah]]** for thirty-five thousand dirhams and endowed it as a universal public waqf for all Muslims (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:378; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:280).

### Plenipotentiary of Ḥudaybiyah & Bayʿat al-Riḍwān (6 AH)
At **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]**, the Prophet ﷺ selected ʿUthmān as his supreme envoy to enter Makkah and negotiate terms with Quraysh under the clan protection of Banū Umayyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:44). When Quraysh detained ʿUthmān and rumors spread that he had been assassinated, the Prophet ﷺ summoned the 1,400 companions beneath the acacia tree to swear **[[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]]** unto death. Placing his right hand into his left, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ declared:
> «اللَّهُمَّ هَذِهِ عَنْ عُثْمَانَ!»
> *"O Allāh, this hand is on behalf of ʿUthmān!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:378; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:46; 11:279).

### Outfitting Jaysh al-ʿUsrah at Tabūk (9 AH)
During extreme summer heat and drought before **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]**, the Prophet ﷺ appealed for the outfitting of the distressed army (*Jaysh al-ʿUsrah*). ʿUthmān responded with unprecedented magnanimity:
- Outfitted three hundred fully-equipped camels with saddles and gear (*thalāthamāʾah baʿīr bi-aḥlāsihā wa-aqtābihā*), and according to other accounts up to nine hundred camels and one hundred horses (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:435–436; 11:280).
- Brought one thousand gold dinars in his sleeve and poured them into the lap of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:378; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:435; 11:280).
The Prophet ﷺ turned the gold pieces in his hands with boundless joy, proclaiming repeatedly:
> «مَا ضَرَّ عُثْمَانَ مَا عَمِلَ بَعْدَ الْيَوْمِ!»
> *"Nothing ʿUthmān does after today can ever harm him!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:378; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:435; 11:280).

## 4. Caliphate, Martyrdom & Legacy
Chosen as the Third Caliph by the consultative Shūrā led by **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]** ﵁ in Muḥarram 24 AH (or late Dhū al-Ḥijjah 23 AH) (see master entry **[[Khilāfat ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]**) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379), ʿUthmān governed for nearly twelve years:
- **Territorial Expansion**: Expanded the Islamic state across North Africa, Cyprus, Armenia, and Khurasan.
- **The Canonical Qurʾānic Codex**: Recognizing regional dialectical divergences among reciters in border garrisons, ʿUthmān commissioned **[[Zayd b. Thābit]]** ﵁, ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr, Saʿīd b. al-ʿĀṣ, and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. al-Ḥārith b. Hishām to transcribe the authoritative master codices (*al-Maṣāḥif al-ʿUthmāniyyah*) in the dialect of Quraysh, uniting the Ummah on a single orthographic standard (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:27 n. 1; 11:282).
- **Expansion of al-Masjid al-Nabawī**: Financed the substantial physical expansion and architectural embellishment of the Prophet's Mosque in Medina using carved stone and teak wood.

### Siege & Martyrdom
When political agitators from provincial garrisons converged upon Medina and besieged his residence (*al-Dār*), demanding his abdication, ʿUthmān recalled the Prophet's prophecy: *"O ʿUthmān! Allāh may clothe you in a garment; if the hypocrites seek to strip it from you, do not take it off for them"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:280). He strictly forbade the Companions and their sons from drawing weapons in his defense to ensure no blood was shed on his account (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379).

On Friday, 18 Dhū al-Ḥijjah 35 AH, after the afternoon prayer (*ʿAṣr*), while fasting and reciting the Qurʾān (upon the verse: *«Allāh will suffice you against them, and He is the Hearing, the Knowing»*, 2:137), mutineers scaled the walls and struck him down (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:285–286). He was martyred at the age of eighty-two years and several months (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379). He was laid to rest on Saturday night between Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ in **Ḥushsh Kawkab**, an orchard plot he had purchased during his life to expand **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:379).
