# Hāshim b. ʿAbd Manāf (هاشم بن عبد مناف)
*The One Who Crumbled Bread for a Starving Makkah, and Established the Two Journeys*

## 1. Name
His given name was **ʿAmr al-ʿAlā** ("ʿAmr the Exalted"). He was called **Hāshim** — "one who
crumbles" — because he was **the first to crumble bread into broth (*tharīd*) for his people at
Makkah and feed them**: when the people of Makkah suffered a severe famine, Hāshim journeyed to
Palestine, bought a great quantity of flour and bread-cake, brought it back to Makkah, and had it
baked; he then slaughtered a camel and crumbled the bread into the broth, feeding all of Makkah — and
he kept doing this until the famine passed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:268).

## 2. The Two Journeys
Hāshim was **the first to establish the two seasonal journeys** — the winter journey to Abyssinia (or
Yemen) and the summer journey to Syria — the trade pattern later commemorated in the Qurʾān's *Sūrat
Quraysh*, *«لِإِيلَافِ قُرَيْشٍ … رِحْلَةَ الشِّتَاءِ وَالصَّيْفِ»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:268).

## 3. His Standing, Praised in Verse
A poet paired him with his father in a single line, using his given name:

> «عَمْرُو الْعَلَا هَشَمَ الثَّرِيدَ لِقَوْمِهِ … سَفَرَ الشِّتَاءِ وَرِحْلَةَ الْإِيلَافِ»
> *"ʿAmr the Exalted crumbled the bread for his people — [author of] the winter journey and the
> journey of trade-covenant."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:269)

Another poem, cited by al-Ṣāliḥī honouring the whole line of ancestors down to the Prophet ﷺ, names
him directly among them: *"and Hāshim, builder of the edifice of his pride, through excellent deeds
and the giving of gifts"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:242).

## 4. Ending the Practice of *al-Iḥtifād*
ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz is cited, via al-Zubayr b. Bakkār, describing a pre-Islamic Qurashī custom
called *al-iḥtifād*: when a household's wealth failed, they would go out to open ground, pitch tents,
and wait there to die of want rather than let their poverty be known. **Hāshim ended this practice**
once he grew prominent among his people, telling Quraysh that might lay in numbers, that they had
become the wealthiest and mightiest of the Arabs, and that this custom of concealed ruin had consumed
too many of them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:269).

## 5. Succeeding His Father, and the Rivalry With Umayyah
Hāshim inherited **al-siqāyah and al-rifādah** from **[[ʿAbd Manāf b. Quṣayy|ʿAbd Manāf]]**. This
provoked the envy of **Umayyah b. ʿAbd Shams**, who — lacking Hāshim's wealth — could not match his
feeding of Quraysh, and was mocked for the shortfall by his own people. Enraged, Umayyah challenged
Hāshim to a contest of honour (*munāfarah*), staking fifty black-eyed camels to be slaughtered at
Makkah, with the loser exiled for ten years. They submitted the contest to a soothsayer of Khuzāʿah,
grandfather of ʿAmr b. al-Ḥumayq, who ruled — swearing by the moon, the shining star, and the rain
cloud — that *"Hāshim preceded Umayyah to noble deeds, first and last."* Hāshim took the camels and
fed their meat to all present; Umayyah went into exile in Syria for ten years. Al-Ṣāliḥī records:

> «فَتِلْكَ أَوَّلُ عَدَاوَةٍ وَقَعَتْ بَيْنَ بَنِي … »
> *"And that was the first enmity that arose between the sons of …"* [the two houses of ʿAbd Manāf]
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:271)
