# al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah (الإصابة في تمييز الصحابة)
*The Definitive Biographical Encyclopedia of the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ*

> [!NOTE] Canonical Reference of this Wiki
> Alongside *[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād]]*, *al-Iṣābah* serves as the co-equal canonical foundation for every Companion biography and prosopographical node across this knowledge graph. All biographical details—lineage, kinships, conversions, campaign participations, and deaths—are verified directly against its eight volumes and biographical entries.

## 1. Author & Scholarly Stature
Authored by the preeminent *Amīr al-Muʾminīn fī al-Ḥadīth*, **[[Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī]]** (d. 852 AH in Cairo). He spent over four decades researching, refining, and cross-referencing early registers—including the works of Ibn Saʿd, al-Bukhārī, Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr (*al-Istīʿāb*), Ibn al-Athīr (*Usd al-Ghābah*), and Ibn Qāniʿ—to produce the most comprehensive and critical biographical encyclopedia of the Ṣaḥābah in Islamic history.

## 2. Structure & Classification System
Ibn Ḥajar organized the encyclopedia alphabetically across eight volumes, dividing each letter into four rigorous analytical categories (*aqṣām*):
- **Qism I**: Those whose companionship is established through sound prophetic reports, eyewitness accounts, or reliable historical consensus.
- **Qism II**: Those born during the lifetime of the Prophet ﷺ who were children or infants when he passed away, including those brought to him for blessing (*taḥnīk*).
- **Qism III**: The *Mukhaḍramūn*—those who lived during the Jāhiliyyah and embraced Islam during the Prophet's lifetime, but did not have the honor of meeting him ﷺ in person.
- **Qism IV**: Those mistakenly included among the Companions by earlier authors due to scribal corruptions, lineage confusions, or weak transmissions, which Ibn Ḥajar systematically rectifies.

## 3. Integral Role in the Knowledge Graph
In *[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād]]*, Imām al-Ṣāliḥī constantly relies upon *al-Iṣābah* to adjudicate differences in lists of veterans (such as the roster of **[[Ahl Badr]]**), to identify combatants in single duels, and to corroborate transmission chains.
