# Ghazwat al-Yarmūk (معركة اليرموك)
*The Decisive Clash of the Levant — The Shattering of the Roman Legions, The Martyrdom of the Suicidal Corps, and the Permanent Departure of Heraclius*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Sources
> Anchored in *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād* by Imām al-Ṣāliḥī (Volume 11, p. 275) and *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 1, p. 170; Vol. 3, p. 476; Vol. 4, p. 539).

---

## 1. Strategic Context & Imperial Mobilization

In the year fifteen AH, during the Caliphate of **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, Emperor Heraclius gathered the entire military might of the Byzantine Empire—drawing legions from Armenia, the Balkans, European Rome, and Christian Arab confederations—in a massive, desperate campaign to reconquer the Levant and permanently expel the Muslims from Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:170).

Recognizing the concentration of imperial forces, the Muslim commanders executed a brilliant strategic consolidation: they evacuated Damascus and Homs, refunded the jizyah tax to the Christian citizens due to temporary inability to defend them, and concentrated their combined armies along the **Yarmouk River** plain south of Lake Tiberias (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

---

## 2. Unification of Command & Army Organization

The Muslim army numbered between twenty-four thousand and forty thousand men, containing over one thousand Companions of the Prophet ﷺ and approximately one hundred veterans of **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476).

To counter the massive Roman front, **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁ reorganized the Muslim forces into thirty-six to forty unified tactical brigades (*karādīs*):
- **Center**: Commanded by **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁.
- **Right Flank**: Commanded by **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁.
- **Left Flank**: Commanded by **[[Yazīd b. Abī Sufyān]]** ﵁.
- **Mobile Cavalry Reserve**: Commanded personally by **Khālid b. al-Walīd**, flanked by the fearless cavalier **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:170; 4:539).

During the height of battle preparations, the dispatch from Caliph ʿUmar arrived transferring supreme administrative authority to **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]**; with sublime brotherhood and military wisdom, Abū ʿUbaydah concealed the letter until the battle was won, leaving tactical command completely in Khālid's hands (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:476).

---

## 3. The Six Days of Epic Combat

The confrontation lasted six agonizing, intense days:
1. **The Heroic Charge of al-Zubayr**: Mounted on his horse, **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁ cleaved through the dense Roman lines twice single-handedly, emerging unbroken through the rearguard of the enemy army (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:170).
2. **The Women's Defense of the Camp**: When the right and left wings were driven back toward the camp by the weight of Roman numbers, Muslim women—led by Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr, Umm Ḥakīm, and Hind bint ʿUtbah—picked up tent poles and rocks, driving the men back to the frontlines with fierce battle cries.
3. **The Suicide Pact of ʿIkrimah**: When the Muslim lines faced total breach, **[[ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl]]** ﵁ cried out: *"I fought the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at every crossroad; shall I flee from you today?!"* He called for a pledge unto death (*bayʿat al-mawt*); four hundred heroes—including his uncle al-Ḥārith b. Hishām and Ḍirār b. al-Azwar—charged the Roman center, turning the tide at the cost of their lives (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:539).

---

## 4. The Final Encirclement & The Gorge of al-Wāqūṣah

On the sixth day, Khālid launched a sweeping cavalry maneuver that drove a wedge between the Roman cavalry and infantry, driving the enemy cavalry into headlong flight into the desert.

Khālid then encircled the Roman infantry, pressing them relentlessly toward the jagged cliffs of the **Wāqūṣah** ravine (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:170). Trapped in blinding dust storms, thousands of Roman soldiers, chained together in ranks to prevent retreat, plummeted over the sheer cliffs to their deaths (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

The Roman army was utterly extinguished as a fighting force.

---

## 5. Strategic Aftermath & The Departure of Heraclius

The victory at al-Yarmūk permanently decided the fate of Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275):
- Hearing of the catastrophe at Antioch, Emperor Heraclius turned toward Constantinople and uttered his tragic parting lament:
  > «عَلَيْكِ يَا سُورِيَا السَّلَامُ، سَلَامًا لَا اجْتِمَاعَ بَعْدَهُ، وَنِعْمَ الْبَلَدُ هَذَا لِلْعَدُوِّ!»
  > *"Peace be upon you, O Syria—a farewell after which we shall never meet again! And what an excellent realm this is for the enemy!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
- The battle led directly to the peaceful capitulation of all Syrian strongholds and the historic entry of Caliph ʿUmar into **[[Fatḥ Bayt al-Maqdis]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
