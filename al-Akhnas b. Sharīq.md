# al-Akhnas b. Sharīq (الأخنس بن شريق)
*The Shrewd Ally of Banū Zuhrah — The Chieftain Who Withdrew from Badr*

## 1. Lineage & Status in Makkah
Ubayy b. Sharīq al-Thaqafī, known as **al-Akhnas** (The Retracter) because he retracted with his clan from Badr, was an ally (*ḥalīf*) and recognized spokesman of the clan of **[[Banū Zuhrah]]** (the maternal clan of the Prophet ﷺ) in **[[Quraysh]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:352; 4:29). Despite being from Thaqīf, his persuasive eloquence and political cunning elevated him to undisputed authority among Banū Zuhrah.

In the early Makkan period, he was among the Quraysh leaders who covertly crept by night to the Prophet's house to listen to the Qurʾān, only to reject it out of tribal pride (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:352).

## 2. The Withdrawal at Badr (2 AH)
When Quraysh mobilized their one-thousand-man host to protect the Syrian caravan of **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]**, al-Akhnas marched at the head of the Banū Zuhrah contingent (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29).

Upon reaching **[[al-Juḥfah]]**, a dispatch arrived from Abū Sufyān announcing that the caravan had safely navigated past the Muslims and instructing the army to return to Makkah. While **[[Abū Jahl (ʿAmr b. Hishām)]]** ☠ passionately insisted on continuing to Badr to hold an arrogant three-day carnival of feasting and wine, al-Akhnas addressed Banū Zuhrah:
> *"O Banū Zuhrah! Allāh has delivered your property and spared your companions; you marched only to protect your wealth, so return!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29).
Banū Zuhrah obeyed him completely and marched back to Makkah in their entirety — approximately three hundred men — without a single Zuhzī attending the battlefield of Badr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29). Consequently, not one man of Banū Zuhrah was killed or captured at Badr, and the clan held al-Akhnas in perpetual veneration (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:29).
