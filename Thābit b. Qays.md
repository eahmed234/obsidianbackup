# Thābit b. Qays (ثابت بن قيس) ﵁
*Thābit b. Qays b. Shammās — Khaṭīb Rasūlillāh ﷺ (The Orator of the Messenger of Allāh), Promised Paradise, and Standard-Bearer Martyr of al-Yamāmah*

> [!NOTE]
> **Canonical Sourcing**: This biographical profile is established upon *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (vol. 1, pp. 511–512, entry 906) and *[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād]]* by Imām Muḥammad b. Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī (vol. 5, pp. 14–15; vol. 6, pp. 287–288; vol. 10, p. 95; vol. 11, p. 395).

---

## 1. Lineage, Family & Physical Traits
- **Full Lineage**: Thābit b. Qays b. Shammās b. Zuhayr b. Mālik b. Imriʾ al-Qays b. Mālik b. Thaʿlabah b. Kaʿb b. al-Khazraj al-Anṣārī al-Khazrajī (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511, no. 906; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:395).
- **Maternal Lineage**: His mother was Hind bint Asqaʿ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:395). Kunyah: **Abū Muḥammad** (and it was said: Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān) (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511).
- **Traits & Voice**: Known throughout Medina for his towering, thunderous voice (*jahīr al-ṣawt*), his soaring Arabic eloquence, and his passionate devotion to the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:95; 11:395).

---

## 2. Early Milestones: Pledge of the Hijrah & Campaign History
- **The Welcoming Address of the Hijrah**: When the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ arrived in Medina upon the Hijrah, Thābit b. Qays stood as the orator of the Anṣār and proclaimed (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511):
  > «نَمْنَعُكَ مِمَّا نَمْنَعُ مِنْهُ أَنْفُسَنَا وَأَوْلَادَنَا، فَمَا لَنَا؟»
  > *"We shall defend you from all that from which we defend our own selves and our children; what then shall be our reward?"*
  The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ replied: *«الْجَنَّةُ»* (*"Paradise!"*), and the Anṣār roared: *«رَضِينَا!»* (*"We are well pleased!"*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511).
- **Campaigns**: Though he did not participate at Badr, his first battle was **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, after which he witnessed all subsequent expeditions with the Prophet ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511).
- **Chivalrous Intercession at Banū Qurayẓah (5 AH)**:
  - During the pre-Islamic battle of Buʿāth, the Jewish chieftain al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā had spared Thābit's life and clipped his forelock (*jクラウド naṣiyatihi*).
  - When the judgment of Banū Qurayẓah was executed, Thābit approached the Prophet ﷺ and requested that al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā, his family, and his wealth be gifted to him to repay that ancient favor (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:14).
  - The Prophet ﷺ generously granted all of them to Thābit (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:14).

## 3. The Orator of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Khaṭīb Rasūlillāh*)
Thābit held the official appointment as *Khaṭīb Rasūlillāh ﷺ*, answering foreign embassies and rival tribal orators whenever challenges of prestige (*mufākharah*) were presented (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:395).
- **The Contest Against Banū Tamīm (9 AH)**:
  - When seventy to eighty aristocratic chieftains of **[[Banū Tamīm]]** came to Medina demanding a public contest of verbal prowess, their orator ʿUṭārid b. Ḥājib delivered an arrogant speech boasting of their wealth and power (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:287).
  - The Prophet ﷺ turned to Thābit and commanded: *«Arise, O Thābit, and answer him!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:287).
  - Thābit delivered a magnificent, thunderous oration praising Allāh, honoring the Messenger ﷺ, and proclaiming the mission of Islam, utterly extinguishing the pagan pride of Banū Tamīm (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:287–288).
  - Combined with the verses of **[[Ḥassān b. Thābit]]** ﵁, this rhetorical victory led the entire delegation of Banū Tamīm to embrace Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:289).

## 4. The Glad Tidings of Paradise (*al-Bushrā bil-Jannah*)
- When the opening verses of Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt were revealed:
  > ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَرْفَعُوا أَصْوَاتَكُمْ فَوْقَ صَوْتِ النَّبِيِّ﴾
  > *"O you who have believed, do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet..."* (Qurʾān 49:2).
  Thābit was struck with profound terror. Knowing that he was naturally loud-voiced, he locked himself in his house and wept continuously, believing that all his righteous deeds had been rendered null and that he was doomed to the Fire (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:95; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*).
- When the Prophet ﷺ noticed his absence, ʿĀṣim b. ʿAdī sought him out and found him weeping. Upon learning the reason, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ sent for him and comforted him with the supreme glad tidings:
  > «يَا ثَابِتُ، أَمَا تَرْضَى أَنْ تَعِيشَ حَمِيدًا، وَتُقْتَلَ شَهِيدًا، وَتَدْخُلَ الْجَنَّةَ؟»
  > *"O Thābit! Are you not pleased to live praiseworthy, to be slain as a martyr, and to enter Paradise?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:95; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:511).
  Thābit rejoiced, saying: *«I am well pleased with the glad tidings of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ!»*
- In *Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī*, the Prophet ﷺ declared:
  > «نِعْمَ الرَّجُلُ ثَابِتُ بْنُ قَيْسِ بْنِ شَمَّاسٍ»
  > *"What an excellent man is Thābit b. Qays b. Shammās!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:395).

## 5. Martyrdom at al-Yamāmah & The Miraculous Posthumous Bequest
- **Battle of al-Yamāmah (11/12 AH)**: During the Ridda Wars under **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁, when the Muslim ranks wavered before the legions of Musaylimah, Thābit and **[[Sālim Mawlā Abī Ḥudhayfah]]** dug trenches for themselves up to their shins, put on embalming perfume (*taḥannuṭ*), and held the standard (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511).
- Thābit cried out to the army: *«How wretched is that which you have accustomed your adversaries to! O Allāh, I declare my innocence to You of what these [the followers of Musaylimah] have brought, and I apologize to You for what these [the retreating Muslims] have done!»* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511).
- He fought fiercely until he attained the promised martyrdom (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:395).
- **The Visionary Testament Executed in Waking Life**:
  - Thābit had been wearing a valuable coat of mail (*dirʿ nafīsah*), which was taken by a soldier and concealed under an overturned pot under a horse-tether at the periphery of the army camp (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:395).
  - A Muslim soldier saw Thābit in a dream describing the exact location of the armor, instructing him to tell Khālid b. al-Walīd to retrieve it, and commanding him to inform Caliph **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ to pay off his debts and emancipate his slave (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:511–512).
  - Khālid recovered the armor exactly as described, and Abū Bakr executed his posthumous bequest in full. Scholars noted that no person in the history of Islam ever had a testament delivered in a dream after death executed in waking reality except Thābit b. Qays (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:395; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:511–512).
