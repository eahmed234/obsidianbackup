# Banū Qaynuqāʿ (بنو قينقاع)
*The Artisan-Goldsmith Tribe of Medina and the First Treaty Breach*

## 1. Lineage & Settlement in Medina
Banū Qaynuqāʿ were one of the three principal Jewish tribes inhabiting pre-Islamic Yathrib alongside Banū al-Naḍīr and **[[Banū Qurayẓah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:170). Unlike the agricultural clans who held extensive date groves outside the city center, Banū Qaynuqāʿ resided in compact, heavily fortified stone tower-dwellings (*āṭām*) situated within the southwestern precincts of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:170).

They were master artisans, famed throughout Arabia for goldsmithing, ironmongery, manufacture of armor and weapons, and commercial finance centered in their renowned bazaar, **Sūq Banī Qaynuqāʿ** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:170). Before the Hijrah, they were entrenched in a defensive alliance with the Khazraj tribe, particularly with the chieftain **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]**.

## 2. Treaty & Post-Badr Belligerence
Upon the Prophet's arrival in Medina, Banū Qaynuqāʿ were integrated as signatories into **[[Dustūr al-Madīnah]]** (the Constitution of Medina), guaranteeing their religious freedom and mutual defense of the oasis.

Following the decisive Muslim victory at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** in Ramaḍān 2 AH, Banū Qaynuqāʿ grew openly resentful and bellicose. The Prophet ﷺ gathered them in their marketplace and urged them to accept Islam or heed the fate of Quraysh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:170). They responded insolently:
> *"O Muḥammad, do not be deceived because you met a people with no knowledge of warfare and took advantage of them! By God, if we fight you, you shall know that we are real men and you have never met the likes of us!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:170).

In response, Allāh revealed in Sūrat Āl ʿImrān: *«Say to those who disbelieve: You will be overcome and gathered together to Hell, and wretched is the resting place»* (3:12) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:170).

## 3. The Provocation & Expulsion (Shawwāl 2 AH)
Matters escalated when an Arab Muslim woman arrived in Sūq Banī Qaynuqāʿ to sell goods and sat before a goldsmith. When she refused to unveil her face, the goldsmith tied the lower hem of her garment to her back without her realizing. When she stood up, her modesty was exposed, provoking mocking laughter from the onlookers. A Muslim bystander sprang upon the goldsmith and slew him; the Jews fell upon the Muslim and killed him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:171).

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ marched with the Muslim army and laid strict siege to their citadels for fifteen consecutive nights from mid-Shawwāl 2 AH until the crescent of Dhū al-Qaʿdah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:171). Cast into terror, they capitulated unconditionally, submitting to the judgment of the Prophet ﷺ.

**[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** aggressively intervened on their behalf, grasping the Prophet's armor and demanding clemency for his four hundred unarmored and three hundred armored allies (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:171). The Prophet ﷺ spared their lives, commanding their expulsion from Medina. Entrusting the oversight of their eviction to ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit ﵁ (who renounced his pre-Islamic alliance with them), they were granted three days to collect their movable property and debts, and departed toward Adhriʿāt in the Levant (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:171–172).
