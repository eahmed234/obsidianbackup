# al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah (المغيرة بن شعبة)
*Mughīrat al-Raʾy — Master Diplomat, Knight of al-Ḥudaybiyah, Conqueror of Maysān, and Governor of Basra and Kufa*

> «كَانَ مِنْ دُهَاةِ الْعَرَبِ … لَوْ أَنَّ مَدِينَةً لَهَا ثَمَانِيَةُ أَبْوَابٍ لَا يُخْرَجُ مِنْ بَابٍ مِنْهَا إِلَّا بِمَكْرٍ لَخَرَجَ الْمُغِيرَةُ مِنْ أَبْوَابِهَا كُلِّهَا!»
> *"He was among the foremost shrewd statesmen (duhāt) of the Arabs… Were a city to possess eight gates, and not a single gate could be exited except through supreme astuteness, al-Mughīrah would have found his way out of all eight gates!"*
> — Qabīṣah b. Jābir al-Asadī (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156–157)

---

## 1. Lineage, Kunya, and Physical Traits

### Lineage
He is **al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah b. Abī ʿĀmir b. Masʿūd b. Muʿattib b. Mālik b. Kaʿb b. ʿAmr b. Saʿd b. ʿAwf b. Qays al-Thaqafī** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156, no. 8197). He was the paternal nephew of the noble Thaqafī chief **ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd al-Thaqafī** ﵁.

### Kunya
- **Abū ʿĪsā** (أبو عيسى): He was originally given this kunya by the Prophet ﷺ himself. When al-Mughīrah later sought permission to enter upon **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ using this kunya, ʿUmar questioned whether ʿĪsā had a father; Companions testified that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had personally bestowed this kunya upon al-Mughīrah, after which ʿUmar also called him **Abū ʿAbdillāh** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).
- Also called **Abū Muḥammad** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156).

### Physical Features
Al-Mughīrah was distinguished by an imposing physical presence: he was tall in stature (*ṭuwāl*), possessing stout forearms (*ʿabl al-dhirāʿayn*), broad between the shoulders (*baʿīd mā bayn al-mankibayn*), with reddish-brown curly hair (*aṣhab al-shaʿr jaʿdah*) that he did not part (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156, 158). He was one-eyed, having lost his eye fighting valiantly at the **Battle of Yarmūk** (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157, 158).

---

## 2. Conversion and Armed Sentry at al-Ḥudaybiyah (6 AH)

Al-Mughīrah accepted Islam prior to ʿUmrat al-Ḥudaybiyah, traveled to Medina, and took part in the momentous **[[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]]** beneath the tree (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156).

During the tense diplomatic negotiations at **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]**, his uncle ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd came as the envoy of Quraysh to negotiate with the Prophet ﷺ. As ʿUrwah spoke to the Prophet ﷺ, he habitually extended his hand toward the Prophet's ﷺ noble beard in the familiar manner of Arab tribal debate (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:375). 

Al-Mughīrah was standing directly behind the Prophet ﷺ, completely clad in chainmail and helmet with his sword drawn (*shāk fī al-silāḥ*). Each time ʿUrwah reached forward, al-Mughīrah struck ʿUrwah's knuckles with the sheath of his sword and barked:
> «كُفَّ يَدَكَ عَنْ لِحْيَةِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَبْلَ أَنْ لَا تَصِلَ إِلَيْكَ!»
> *"Restrain your hand from the beard of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ before your hand fails to return to you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:375; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

When ʿUrwah looked up and recognized his nephew beneath the iron visor, he exclaimed: *"Who is this? … O treacherous one, am I not still striving to clean up your past treachery?"* (referring to a pre-Islamic feud that ʿUrwah had paid blood-money to settle; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:156; *Subul al-Hudā*, 10:375). Upon returning to Quraysh, ʿUrwah declared in awe: *"O my people! By Allāh, I have visited emperors, Khosrow, and Caesar, but by Allāh, never have I seen a king whose companions revere him as the companions of Muḥammad revere Muḥammad!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:375).

## 3. Demolition of the Idol of al-Lāt (9 AH)

Following the conversion of Thaqīf in 9 AH, the Prophet ﷺ dispatched **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** ﵁ and **al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah** ﵁ on an official expedition to al-Ṭāʾif to demolish **al-Lāt** (الطاغية, the principal idol of Thaqīf) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:226; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:157). 

Al-Mughīrah struck the idol with an axe while the women of Thaqīf wept and lamented their ancient deity. He eradicated its shrine, unearthed its hidden treasures and jewels, and brought them back to the Prophet ﷺ to pay off the debts of ʿUrwah and his brother al-Aswad b. Masʿūd (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:226).

---

## 4. Administrative & Scribal Office Under the Prophet ﷺ

Al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah was among the literate Companions whom the Prophet ﷺ employed as an official scribe (*kātib*). He personally penned treaties and covenant documents for tribes on behalf of the Prophet ﷺ, including peace pacts granted to Banū al-Jirmiz of Juhaynah and land covenants assigned to Ḥuṣayn b. Naḍlah al-Asadī (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:394).

---

## 5. Statesmanship, Conquests, and Governance

Al-Mughīrah was famed across Arabia as **Mughīrat al-Raʾy** (المغيرة الرأي, "Mughīrah of Sound Counsel") and was ranked by al-Shaʿbī and al-Zuhrī alongside Muʿāwiyah, ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ, and Ziyād b. Abīhi as the supreme political minds (*duhāt*) of the era (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156). Imām al-Ṭabarī recorded of him: *"He never fell into any predicament except that he discovered an exit from it, nor did two complex affairs ever present themselves to him without the correct judgment manifesting in one of them"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).

### The Battlefields of Iraq and Syria
- **Battle of the Yamāmah** (11 AH): Fought in the suppression of Musaylimah during the Riddah Wars (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156).
- **Expedition to al-Nujayr**: Dispatched by **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ against apostate forces in Hadramawt (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).
- **Battle of Yarmūk** (15 AH): Fought on the front lines against the Byzantines, where an arrow struck and took out his eye (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157, 158).
- **Envoy to Rostam at al-Qādisiyyah**: Acted as the fearless emissary of **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]** ﵁ to the Persian commander-in-chief Rostam, boldly crossing his opulent carpets and delivering the ultimatum of Islam, tribute, or the sword (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).
- **Campaign of Nihāwand**: Served as the envoy of al-Nuʿmān b. Muqarrin and participated in the final opening of Persia (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).

### Governance over Basra and Kufa
- **Governor of Basra**: Appointed by **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ over Basra. He spearheaded the conquest of **Maysān**, **Hamadhān**, and surrounding provinces (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157). He was the first in administrative history to establish the financial register (*dīwān*) of Basra, and Ibn Ḥibbān notes he was the first leader greeted with the administrative title of *Imārah* (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).
- **Governor of Kufa**: Appointed by ʿUmar over Kufa, confirmed by **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁, and later reappointed by Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān, administering the city with consummate statesmanship until his passing (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).

---

## 6. Death and Legacy

Al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah passed away in Kufa in the year **50 AH** (or a year before or after per variant reports; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:157), an event upon which al-Khaṭīb al-Baghdādī transmitted scholarly consensus (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157). 

He narrated over a hundred hadiths directly from the Prophet ﷺ, transmitted by his sons ʿUrwah, ʿAqqār, and Ḥamzah, as well as renowned junior Companions and Tābiʿīn such as al-Miswar b. Makhramah, Masrūq, Qays b. Abī Ḥāzim, Qabīṣah b. Dhuʾayb, and Nāfiʿ b. Jubayr (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156).
