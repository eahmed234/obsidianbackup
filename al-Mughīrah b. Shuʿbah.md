# al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah (المغيرة بن شعبة) ﵁
*Abū ʿAbdillāh al-Thaqafī — Dahyat al-ʿArab (The Shrewd Diplomat of the Arabs), Veteran of Ḥudaybiyah, Conqueror and Governor of Baṣrah and Kūfah*

## 1. Lineage, Ancestry & Conversion
### Patrilineal Descent & Clan
He is **al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah b. Abī ʿĀmir b. Masʿūd b. Muʿattib b. Mālik b. Kaʿb b. ʿAmr b. Saʿd b. ʿAwf b. Qays al-Thaqafī** (kunyas: **Abū ʿAbdillāh**, **Abū ʿĪsā**, and **Abū Muḥammad**) (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156). 

- He belonged to the noble aristocracy of **[[Thaqīf]]** in **[[al-Ṭāʾif]]** and was the nephew of the illustrious chieftain **ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd al-Thaqafī** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:39).
- **Physical Appearance**: Tall, powerfully built, broad-shouldered, with thick forearms and reddish-brown curly hair that he wore parted (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156). He was renowned for immense intelligence and diplomatic cunning, celebrated as one of the four legendary political geniuses of the Arabs (*Duhāt al-ʿArab*).

### Conversion (5 AH)
Embracing Islam before the expedition of Ḥudaybiyah, he arrived in Medina declaring his faith before the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and was accepted into the companionship (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156).

---

## 2. Diplomatic Stand at Ḥudaybiyah (6 AH)
At **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]**, al-Mughīrah took part in **[[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]]** beneath the tree (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:156).

When his paternal uncle **ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd** came as the supreme plenipotentiary of Quraysh to negotiate with the Prophet ﷺ, al-Mughīrah stood behind the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ wearing a steel helmet and chainmail, sword drawn. Whenever ʿUrwah reached out his hand toward the Prophet's beard while speaking—as was customary in Arab discourse—al-Mughīrah struck ʿUrwah's hand with the scabbard of his sword, warning:
> «أَمْسِكْ يَدَكَ عَنْ لِحْيَةِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَبْلَ أَنْ لَا تَصِلَ إِلَيْكَ!»
> *"Withhold your hand from the beard of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ before it does not return to you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:40; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).
Peering under the helmet, ʿUrwah recognized his nephew and was deeply shaken by the Companions' fierce, utter reverence for the Prophet ﷺ.

---

## 3. Demolition of al-Lāt at al-Ṭāʾif (9 AH)
Following the submission of his tribe during **[[Wafd Thaqīf]]** (**[[ʿĀm al-Wufūd]]**), the Prophet ﷺ dispatched al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah alongside **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** to dismantle and demolish the paramount idol sanctuary of **al-Lāt** in al-Ṭāʾif (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:308–310).

Taking up an axe before the weeping women of Thaqīf, al-Mughīrah struck the idol while feigning a sudden fall, causing the pagan onlookers to exclaim that the goddess had slain him. Rising laughing, al-Mughīrah proclaimed:
> *“May Allāh deform your faces! She is nothing but a deaf, dumb stone!”*
He then systematically destroyed the idol and its foundation down to the earth, distributing its gold and silver ornaments to settle tribal debts on the Prophet's instruction (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:310).

---

## 4. Commander, Governor & Civic Innovator
During the Caliphate of **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁:
- **Battle of al-Qādisiyyah (15 AH)**: al-Mughīrah served as chief Muslim envoy to the Sasanian commander Rostam, astonishing the Persian court with his dignified fearlessness, eloquence, and unshakeable conviction (**[[Ghazwat al-Qādisiyyah]]**).
- **Governor of Baṣrah**: Appointed governor of Baṣrah by ʿUmar, he conquered Maysān, Ahwāz, and Hamadhān, and was the first administrator to establish the government payroll register (*dīwān*) in Baṣrah (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).
- **Governor of Kūfah**: Later appointed governor of Kūfah by ʿUmar and confirmed by ʿUthmān, governing with consummate political acumen (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).

During the civil discord (*al-fitnah*), al-Mughīrah withdrew from armed strife, later participating in the diplomatic delegations and serving as governor of Kūfah under Muʿāwiyah until his death (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157).

---

## 5. Death
al-Mughīrah passed away in Kūfah in the year 50 AH (670 CE) at approximately seventy years of age (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:157). More than one hundred and thirty authentic Prophetic traditions are transmitted from him across the canonical compilations.
