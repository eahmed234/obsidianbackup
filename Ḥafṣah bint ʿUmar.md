# Ḥafṣah bint ʿUmar (حفصة بنت عمر) ﵂
*Umm al-Muʾminīn (Mother of the Believers) — Custodian of the Master Qurʾānic Codex, The Devout Fasting and Praying Matron of Paradise*

## 1. Lineage, Ancestry & Early Life
### Patrilineal Descent & Clan
She is **Ḥafṣah bint ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb b. Nufayl b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. Riyāḥ b. ʿAbdillāh b. Qurṭ b. Razāḥ b. ʿAdī b. Kaʿb al-Qurashiyyah al-ʿAdawiyyah** (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:85; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:184). 

- **Father**: Amīr al-Muʾminīn **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ (al-Fārūq), Second Rightly-Guided Caliph.
- **Mother**: **Zaynab bint Maẓʿūn al-Jumaḥiyyah** ﵂, sister of the ascetic companion ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:85; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:184).
- **Full Brother**: The eminent jurist and narrator **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar]]** ﵄.

### Birth & Early Precedence
Ḥafṣah was born in Makkah approximately five years before the Prophetic mission, during the historic year when Quraysh reconstructed the sacred Kaʿbah (~605 CE) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:184). Embracing Islam early in Makkah alongside her parents and extended family of Banū ʿAdī and Banū Jumaḥ, she was among the elite vanguard who endured early trials.

### First Marriage & Migration to Medina
Ḥafṣah was first wed in Makkah to the early hero **Khunays b. Ḥudhāfah al-Sahmī** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:184). Together, they undertook the migration to Medina (**[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**). Khunays fought valiantly at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** (2 AH), but sustained severe combat wounds from which he passed away in Medina shortly thereafter, leaving Ḥafṣah a young widow (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:85; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:184).

---

## 2. Marriage to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ
Following the death of Khunays, ʿUmar sought an honorable husband for his widowed daughter:
- He approached **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ after the death of the Prophet's daughter Ruqayyah; ʿUthmān demurred as he sought Umm Kulthūm bint Rasūlillāh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:185).
- ʿUmar then approached **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, who remained silent, causing ʿUmar distress.
- When ʿUmar complained to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, the Prophet ﷺ smiled and consoled him with divine glad tidings:
  > «يَتَزَوَّجُ حَفْصَةَ مَنْ هُوَ خَيْرٌ مِنْ عُثْمَانَ، وَيَتَزَوَّجُ عُثْمَانُ مَنْ هِيَ خَيْرٌ مِنْ حَفْصَةَ!»
  > *"Ḥafṣah will marry one who is better than ʿUthmān, and ʿUthmān will marry one who is better than Ḥafṣah!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:85; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:185).

The Prophet ﷺ married Ḥafṣah in Medina in Shaʿbān 3 AH (or 2 AH) for a dowry of four hundred dirhams, while wedding his daughter Umm Kulthūm to ʿUthmān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:185). Abū Bakr subsequently apologized to ʿUmar, explaining that he had heard the Prophet ﷺ mention his intention to marry Ḥafṣah and could not disclose the Prophet's secret (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:85).

## 3. Merits & Celestial Testimonial
Ḥafṣah was characterized by formidable intellect, deep literacy (being one of the few women of Quraysh who could write and read fluently), outspoken courage, and constant worship:
- In a celebrated incident of domestic friction, the Prophet ﷺ pronounced a revocable divorce upon Ḥafṣah. Immediately, the Archangel Jibrīl ﵊ descended from the heavens with a divine command:
  > «رَاجِعْ حَفْصَةَ؛ فَإِنَّهَا صَوَّامَةٌ قَوَّامَةٌ، وَإِنَّهَا زَوْجَتُكَ فِي الْجَنَّةِ!»
  > *"Take Ḥafṣah back! For indeed she is constantly fasting and standing in night prayer (ṣawwāmah qawwāmah), and she is verily your wife in Paradise!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:85; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:185).
- The Prophet ﷺ immediately took her back, honoring the angelic command (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:185).
- **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ praised her exceptional character and intellect, referring to her as *“the true daughter of her father”* (*ibnata abīhā*) in resolution, dignity, and eloquence (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:186).

---

## 4. The Custodian of the Master Qurʾānic Codex
Among Ḥafṣah's most monumental historical contributions to the preservation of Islam was her role as the sole trusted guardian of the master written scripture:
- Following **[[Ḥurūb al-Riddah]]** (12 AH), when Caliph **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ commissioned **[[Zayd b. Thābit]]** to gather the scattered leaves into the first unified codex (**[[Jamʿ al-Qurʾān]]**), the master sheets (*al-Ṣuḥuf*) were preserved with Abū Bakr until his death (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:282).
- They were subsequently entrusted to Caliph **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ throughout his decade-long caliphate.
- Upon ʿUmar's deathbed in 23 AH, he designated Ḥafṣah as the supreme executor of his estate and entrusted the original master sheets of the Holy Qurʾān into her custody (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:86; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:186).
- When Caliph **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ resolved to transcribe the authoritative imperial copies (*al-Maṣāḥif al-ʿUthmāniyyah*) in 25 AH, he sent an emissary to Ḥafṣah requesting the master sheets. She provided them to the editorial committee under solemn covenant that they be returned to her safely. Once the copies were distributed, ʿUthmān faithfully returned the original codex into her care, where it remained until her demise (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:186, 282, 285).

---

## 5. Death & Burial
Ḥafṣah passed away in Medina during the month of Shaʿbān in the year 45 AH (or 41 AH) at approximately sixty years of age (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:86; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:186).

Before her death, she entrusted her affairs to her brother ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar and endowed an extensive agricultural property in al-Ghābah as a charitable waqf for Allāh's sake (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:186). Her funeral prayer was led by Marwān b. al-Ḥakam (governor of Medina), her bier was carried by the governor and **[[Abū Hurayrah]]** ﵁, and she was lowered into her grave by her brothers ʿAbdullāh and ʿĀṣim and nephews Sālim, ʿAbdullāh, and Ḥamzah in **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:86; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:186). She transmitted sixty traditions directly from the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:186).
