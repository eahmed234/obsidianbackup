# Sariyyat Dhāt al-Salāsil (سرية ذات السلاسل)
*The Expedition of Dhāt al-Salāsil — The Northern Deterrence, The Strategic Precedents of ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ, and The Command of the Companions*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Sources
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād*, Volume 6, *al-Bāb al-Sābiʿ wal-Arbaʿūn fī Sariyyat ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ rḍy Allāh taʿālā ʿanhu ilā Dhāt al-Salāsil fī Jumādā al-Ākhirah Sanah Thamān* (pp. 167–175), cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 4, pp. 540–542). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the transmissions of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], Mūsā b. ʿUqbah, Imām Aḥmad, and the Ṣaḥīḥ collections.

---

## 1. Strategic Context & Prophetic Appointment

In Jumādā al-Ākhirah of the year eight AH (only months following the Battle of **[[Ghazwat Muʾtah]]** and shortly after ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ accepted Islam), intelligence reached the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ in Medina that a massive confederacy from the tribes of **Quḍāʿah** (specifically **Balī**, **ʿUdhrah**, and **Balqayn**) were massing near the northern frontiers with intent to raid the outskirts of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167).

The Prophet ﷺ summoned **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁, tied a white military banner for him, and gave him a black flag, placing him in supreme command over **three hundred** of the foremost Muhājirūn and Anṣār, accompanied by thirty horses (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167).

The appointment of ʿAmr was a masterstroke of tribal diplomacy and psychology (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167):
- ʿAmr's paternal grandmother was from the tribe of **Balī** (Rayṭah bint Munabbih b. Ghayānah of Balī); the Prophet ﷺ sought to utilize this maternal kinship (*istiffālan lahum*) to soften their hostility and invite them peacefully to Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167).
- The Prophet ﷺ said to ʿAmr upon commissioning him: *«إِنِّي أُرِيدُ أَنْ أَبْعَثَكَ عَلَى جَيْشٍ فَيُسَلِّمَكَ اللَّهُ وَيُغْنِمَكَ، وَأَرْغَبُ لَكَ مِنَ الْمَالِ رَغْبَةً صَالِحَةً»* (*"I intend to send you at the head of an army, that Allāh may keep you safe and grant you spoils, and I desire for you a righteous portion of wealth."*) ʿAmr replied: *"O Messenger of Allāh! I did not embrace Islam for the sake of wealth, but out of genuine desire for Islam!"* The Prophet ﷺ responded with the immortal maxim: *«يَا عَمْرُو، نِعْمَ الْمَالُ الصَّالِحُ لِلْمَرْءِ الصَّالِحِ!»* (*"O ʿAmr, how excellent is righteous wealth for a righteous man!"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167).

## 2. Night Marches & The Reinforcements

ʿAmr marched his force swiftly by night and hid by day (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167). Upon reaching a watering place behind Wādī al-Qurā in the territory of Balī named **al-Salāsil** (named for its interconnected springs resembling links of a chain), ʿAmr discovered that the enemy mobilization was far larger than initial intelligence reported (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167).

ʿAmr immediately dispatched **Rāfiʿ b. Makīth al-Juhanī** ﵁ as a swift courier to the Prophet ﷺ requesting reinforcements (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167).

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ promptly dispatched a relief detachment of **two hundred** elite warriors under the command of **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167). Among the ranks of Abū ʿUbaydah's force were the two paramount companions, **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167). 

The Prophet ﷺ gave Abū ʿUbaydah strict parting counsel:
> «لَا تَخْتَلِفَا، وَتَطَاوَعَا!»
> *"Do not disagree with one another, and cooperate with mutual compliance!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167).

### The Question of Unified Command
When Abū ʿUbaydah's detachment joined the camp, Abū ʿUbaydah stepped forward to lead the communal prayer. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ stated: *"You were sent only as a reinforcement to me; I am the supreme commander."* The companions of Abū ʿUbaydah asserted that Abū ʿUbaydah commanded his detachment while ʿAmr commanded his (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167–168).

Displaying sublime altruism, gentleness, and strict adherence to the Prophet's instruction, **[[Abū ʿUbaydah b. al-Jarrāḥ]]** ﵁ said:
> «إِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَآلِهِ وَسَلَّمَ قَالَ لِي: لَا تَخْتَلِفَا، وَإِنَّكَ إِنْ عَصَيْتَنِي أَطَعْتُكَ!»
> *"Indeed, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ said to me: 'Do not disagree.' Even if you disobey me, I shall obey you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168).

Abū ʿUbaydah ceded leadership of the prayer and the army to ʿAmr, and the entire combined army of five hundred fighters prayed behind ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168).

## 3. Strict Operational Decisions of ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ

During the campaign, the weather became bitterly, dangerously cold (*laylah bāridah shadīdat al-bard*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168). ʿAmr instituted three strict tactical commands that initially sparked intense bewilderment among the veteran Muhājirūn:

### 1. Prohibition of Campfires
When the soldiers gathered dry wood to kindle fires to warm themselves against the freezing night, ʿAmr issued a military order strictly forbidding anyone from lighting a fire (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168). The soldiers were deeply distressed, and **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ moved to confront ʿAmr angrily, but **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ restrained ʿUmar, saying: *"Leave him, for the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ only appointed him over us because of his superior knowledge of warfare"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168).

### 2. Prohibition of Pursuit
When the Muslim army advanced upon the enemy's encampment, the polytheist confederates were overwhelmed by the sudden strike, broke ranks, and fled into the treacherous mountain ravines (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168). Some Muslims sought to pursue the fleeing enemy, but ʿAmr strictly forbade anyone from pursuing them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168).

### 3. Tayammum in Severe Cold
During the campaign, ʿAmr experienced a nocturnal emission (*iḥtilām*) on a freezing night (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:169). Fearing that performing ritual washing (*ghusl*) with ice-cold water would cause his death, he performed dry ablution (*tayammum*) and led the army in the dawn prayer (*ṣalāt al-fajr*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:169).

---

## 4. The Prophetic Review in Medina

When the expedition returned victoriously to Medina having pacified the entire northern borderlands without casualties, certain companions approached the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ to report ʿAmr's decisions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168–169).

The Prophet ﷺ summoned ʿAmr and questioned him on each point (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168–169):
1. **Concerning the fires**: ʿAmr explained: *"O Messenger of Allāh, I disliked that they kindle fires lest the enemy discern their small numbers."* The Prophet ﷺ was pleased by his tactical acumen (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168).
2. **Concerning the pursuit**: ʿAmr explained: *"I disliked that they pursue the enemy into unknown terrain lest they fall into an ambush."* The Prophet ﷺ approved his judgment (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:168).
3. **Concerning the prayer with tayammum**: The Prophet ﷺ smiled and asked: *«يَا عَمْرُو، صَلَّيْتَ بِأَصْحَابِكَ وَأَنْتَ جُنُبٌ؟»* (*"O ʿAmr, did you pray with your companions while in a state of major ritual impurity?"*) ʿAmr answered: *"O Messenger of Allāh, I remembered the word of Allāh:*
   > ﴿وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا أَنْفُسَكُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ بِكُمْ رَحِيمًا﴾
   > *(And do not kill yourselves; indeed, Allāh is to you ever Merciful — al-Nisāʾ, 4:29).*
   *So I performed tayammum and prayed."*
   Upon hearing this profound legal deduction, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ laughed until his molars showed and endorsed ʿAmr's reasoning (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:169).
