# Tabūk (تبوك)
*The Northern Limit of Prophetic Expeditions — The Frontier of the Roman Border*

## 1. Topography & Strategic Horizon
Tabūk is an oasis situated in northwestern Arabia, lying near the border marches of the Byzantine Levant. It was the northernmost point ever reached by the Prophet ﷺ in person during his military campaigns (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:430).

In Rajab 9 AH, during the blistering heat of midsummer and intense material hardship, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ led thirty thousand Muslim fighters in the expedition of hardship (**Jaysh al-ʿUsrah**) during **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]** to confront Byzantine imperial mobilizations and their Arab client confederates (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:430–435).

## 2. The Miracle of the Spring of Tabūk (ʿAyn Tabūk)
Before reaching the oasis, the Prophet ﷺ announced to the army:
> «إِنَّكُمْ سَتَأْتُونَ غَدًا إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى عَيْنَ تَبُوكَ، وَإِنَّكُمْ لَنْ تَأْتُوهَا حَتَّى يُضْحِيَ النَّهَارُ، فَمَنْ جَاءَهَا فَلَا يَمَسَّ مِنْ مَائِهَا شَيْئًا حَتَّى آتِيَ»
> *"Tomorrow, if Allāh wills, you shall arrive at the spring of Tabūk; yet you will not reach it until the heat of mid-morning. Whoever arrives first must not touch any of its water until I come"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:450).

When the Prophet ﷺ reached the spring, it was trickling with only a thread of water like a shoe strap (*mithla al-shirāk tabuḍḍu*). The companions scooped small droplets into a leather bowl, wherein the Prophet ﷺ washed his face and hands and rinsed his mouth, pouring it back into the spring (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:451). 

Instantly, the spring burst forth with a roar resembling thunderbolts (*ka-ḥiss al-ṣawāʿiq*), creating the roaring fountain of Tabūk (*fawwārat Tabūk*) from which the thirty-thousand-strong host and their beasts drank to overflowing. Turning to **[[Muʿādh b. Jabal]]** ﵁, the Prophet ﷺ prophesied:
> «يَا مُعَاذُ، يُوشِكُ إِنْ طَالَتْ بِكَ حَيَاةٌ أَنْ تَرَى مَا هَاهُنَا مُلِئَ جِنَانًا»
> *"O Muʿādh! If your life is prolonged, you will soon see what is here filled with lush gardens"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:451).

## 3. The Twenty-Night Encampment and Imperial Diplomacy
The Prophet ﷺ encamped at Tabūk for **twenty nights**, during which the Roman legions chose not to advance across the desert frontier (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:455, 465).

From his headquarters at Tabūk:
- Delegations of northern frontier rulers arrived to submit to Islamic suzerainty, including **Yuḥannah b. Ruʾbah**, the Christian prince of Aylah (Aqaba), and the peoples of Jarbāʾ and Adhruḥ, with whom the Prophet ﷺ concluded peace covenants based upon the annual payment of the jizyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:458–459).
- **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁ was detached with 420 cavalrymen to seize the castle of Ukaydir at **[[Dūmat al-Jandal]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:220–223).

The demonstration of military power at Tabūk permanently dismantled Byzantine prestige in northern Arabia and secured the northern frontier of the Islamic state (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:465).
