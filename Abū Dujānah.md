# Abū Dujānah (أبو دجانة) ﵁
*The Anṣārī who took the Prophet's sword "with its right" at Uḥud*

**Abū Dujānah Simāk b. Kharashah al-Anṣārī al-Sāʿidī** was a Companion of the Prophet ﷺ from [[Banū Sāʿidah]], a clan of al-Khazraj in Medina.[^i7.99][^s4.104][^s8.450] A brave fighter known for the red headband he wore into battle, he is remembered above all for taking the Prophet's sword at [[Ghazwat Uḥud|Uḥud]] and shielding him with his own body.[^s4.192][^s4.200] He fought at Badr, Uḥud, Khaybar and Ḥunayn, and was killed at al-Yamāmah during the [[Ḥurūb al-Riddah|Riddah wars]].[^i7.100][^s5.123][^s5.330][^yam]

## Name

His name was Simāk b. Kharashah. Some give it as Simāk b. Aws b. Kharashah, but most authorities leave out Aws.[^i7.99][^i3.146] He is not to be confused with another Simāk b. Kharashah al-Anṣārī, who held one of the garrisons in the region of Hamadhān and was present at al-Qādisiyyah; the claim that Abū Dujānah fought at [[Waqʿat Ṣiffīn|Ṣiffīn]] probably comes from confusing the two men.[^i3.146b]

## The Prophet's arrival in Medina

When the Prophet ﷺ entered Medina at the end of the [[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah|Hijrah]] and rode through the quarter of Banū Sāʿidah, Abū Dujānah was among the men of the clan — with [[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]] and [[al-Mundhir b. ʿAmr]] — who came out to ask him to stay with them, offering him "strength, wealth, power and endurance". The Prophet ﷺ answered that his camel should be let go her way, for she was under command.[^s3.272]

## Badr

Abū Dujānah's presence at [[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā|Badr]] is agreed upon.[^i7.100][^s4.104] According to one report he killed Zamʿah b. al-Aswad there, though others credit Thābit b. al-Jidhʿ.[^s2.461]

## Uḥud

On the day of Uḥud the Prophet ﷺ held up a sword and asked who would take it "with its right". The Companions held back; [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām|al-Zubayr]] asked for it three times and was turned away, and [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib|ʿAlī]] was told to sit down. Then Abū Dujānah stood and asked what its right was. "That you strike the enemy with it until it bends," the Prophet ﷺ replied, and gave it to him.[^s4.192] In another wording, its right was that he should kill no Muslim with it and never flee from a disbeliever while holding it.[^i7.100] The episode is also recorded in the *Ṣaḥīḥ* of [[Muslim]].[^muslim]

Abū Dujānah was a brave man who would strut in battle, and he had a red headband that he tied on when he meant to fight; when the Anṣār saw it they said that Abū Dujānah had brought out the headband of death. He tied it on and walked proudly between the lines,[^s4.192] and the Prophet ﷺ, watching him, said:

> «إنها لمشية يبغضها الله إلا في مثل هذا الموطن»
> *"That is a gait Allāh detests — except in a place such as this."*[^s4.192]

Stung at having been refused the sword, al-Zubayr followed Abū Dujānah to see what he would do with it. He went into the fighting reciting:[^s4.192]

> «أنا الّذي عاهدني خليلي ... ونحن بالسّفح لدى النّخيل
> ألّا أقوم الدّهر في الكيّول ... أضرب بسيف الله والرّسول»
> *"I am the one my beloved bound by covenant, as we stood on the slope by the palms: never to stand in the rear ranks, while I strike with the sword of Allāh and the Messenger."*[^s4.192][^khalil]

He cut down everyone in his path and split the skulls of the polytheists, sharpening the blade on stones whenever it dulled. One of the enemy had been going from one wounded Muslim to the next, finishing them off; Abū Dujānah met him, caught his blow on his leather shield, and killed him.[^s4.192] Later al-Zubayr saw him raise the sword over the head of [[Hind bint ʿUtbah]] and then turn it away. He had taken her at first for a man urging the enemy on; when she cried out and no one came to her aid, he would not strike a woman with the sword of the Messenger of Allāh.[^s4.193] [[Kaʿb b. Mālik]] recalled seeing a heavily armed polytheist challenge the Muslims and an armoured Muslim cut him down with a single blow from shoulder to hip; the victor then uncovered his face and said, "What do you think, Kaʿb? I am Abū Dujānah."[^s4.193]

Abū Dujānah was one of the seven Anṣār who stood firm with the Prophet ﷺ at Uḥud, and one of eight men who pledged that day to fight to the death; none of the eight was killed.[^s4.197][^s4.198] He made himself a shield for the Prophet ﷺ, bending over him while arrows struck his back, and did not move however many struck him. Beside [[Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr]], who fell defending the Prophet ﷺ, he fought on until he was covered in wounds.[^s4.200][^i7.100] He also killed ʿUbayd b. Ḥājiz al-ʿĀmirī, who had wounded [[al-Ḥārith b. al-Ṣimmah]].[^s4.209] After the battle, when ʿAlī handed his bent sword to Fāṭimah, the Prophet ﷺ said that if ʿAlī had fought well, so had [[Sahl b. Ḥunayf]], Abū Dujānah, [[ʿĀṣim b. Thābit]] and al-Ḥārith b. al-Ṣimmah.[^s4.229]

## Later campaigns

During the siege of [[Ghazwat Banī al-Naḍīr|Banū al-Naḍīr]], Abū Dujānah and Sahl b. Ḥunayf were sent with ʿAlī and ten men to pursue a party of Jews; they caught and killed them.[^s4.322] When the property of Banū al-Naḍīr was divided, the Prophet ﷺ gave none of the Anṣār a share except two men who were in need — Sahl b. Ḥunayf and Abū Dujānah.[^s4.325]

At [[Ghazwat Khaybar|Khaybar]], a Jewish warrior who had killed one of the Muslims stood before the fortress of Ubayy calling for single combat. Abū Dujānah went out to him with his red headband tied over his helmet, struck first, cut off the man's leg and killed him; the Prophet ﷺ gave him the man's armour and sword. No one else came out to fight, and the Muslims stormed the fortress with Abū Dujānah at their head.[^s5.123] On the way back, at Wādī al-Qurā, he killed two of the defenders in single combat.[^s5.149]

He was among the Anṣār who stood firm at [[Ghazwat Ḥunayn|Ḥunayn]],[^s5.330] and on the march to [[Ghazwat Tabūk|Tabūk]] he carried the banner of al-Khazraj (some say it was [[al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir]]).[^s5.443] When the Prophet ﷺ left for the [[Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ|Farewell Pilgrimage]], he put Abū Dujānah in charge of Medina, though another report names Sibāʿ b. ʿUrfuṭah.[^s8.450]

## Death

Abū Dujānah was killed at al-Yamāmah, in the campaign against [[Musaylimah al-Kadhdhāb|Musaylimah]].[^yam] It is said that he was among those who took part in killing Musaylimah himself,[^i7.100] and he is one of the men proposed as the Anṣārī whose sword struck Musaylimah as [[Waḥshī b. Ḥarb|Waḥshī]]'s javelin hit him — though the better-known view is that this was ʿAbdullāh b. Zayd b. ʿĀṣim.[^s4.217] The sources do not record the year of his death, his age, or where he was buried.
