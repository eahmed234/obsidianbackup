# Quṣayy b. Kilāb (قصي بن كلاب)
*Mujammiʿ — "The Gatherer" Who United Quraysh and Seized the Kaʿbah for Them*

## 1. Name
His given name was **Zayd**. He was called *Quṣayy* — the diminutive of *qaṣiyy*, "one who is
distant" (*qaṣā, yaqṣū*, "he grew far") — for the circumstances of his upbringing described below
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:272).

## 2. Orphaned Young, Raised Far From His People
His father **[[Kilāb b. Murrah]]** married **Fāṭimah bint Saʿd b. Sayl**, who bore him **Zuhrah and
Quṣayy**. Kilāb died while Quṣayy was still small. Fāṭimah then married **Rabīʿah b. Ḥarām b.
Ḍabbah**, who took her — with the infant Quṣayy in her care (al-Suhaylī specifies he was still
nursing) — away to his own people. There Fāṭimah bore Rabīʿah a son, **Rizāḥ**, Quṣayy's maternal
half-brother, and Quṣayy was raised in Rabīʿah's household, far from his own people — hence *Quṣayy*,
"the distant one." Al-Khaṭṭābī gives a second sense: he was named for having **removed his own people
far away**, to Syria, and later brought them back to Makkah. When a dispute arose between Quṣayy and
Rabīʿah's household and he was taunted over his origins, not knowing any father but Rabīʿah, he
returned to his mother in distress; she told him: *"My son, you are of nobler self and father — you
are the son of Kilāb b. Murrah"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:272–273).

## 3. Marriage Into Khuzāʿah, and the Custody of the Kaʿbah
Quṣayy left for Makkah, where **Khuzāʿah** held authority over the plain (*al-Abṭaḥ*) while Quraysh
dwelt scattered among the ravines and hills. He sought the hand of **Ḥubbā bint Ḥulayl b. Ḥubshiyyah
al-Khuzāʿī** in marriage; her father, recognising Quṣayy's lineage, gave her to him. Ḥulayl at that
time held authority over the Kaʿbah and the governance of Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:274).

When Ḥulayl died, he bequeathed guardianship of the House to Ḥubbā — who said she could not manage
opening and closing its door, so it passed instead to **Abū Ghubshān**, whose true name was
**al-Muḥtarish**, a man of impaired judgement. **Quṣayy bought the custodianship of the House from him
for a wineskin of wine and a riding camel** — the origin of the Arab proverb: *"a worse bargain than
Abū Ghubshān's!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:274).

## 4. The Battle for Makkah
When Quṣayy took the key of the House, **Khuzāʿah** objected and resolved on war against him and
Quraysh, intending to expel them from Makkah entirely. Quṣayy summoned his half-brother
**Rizāḥ b. Rabīʿah**, who came with his own brothers. Gathering **Quraysh, [[Kinānah b. Khuzaymah|
Kinānah]], and [[Maʿadd b. ʿAdnān|Quḍāʿah]]** at al-ʿAqabah, he fought and defeated
**Banū Ṣūfah**, who had controlled the pilgrims' departure from ʿArafah. Khuzāʿah and Banū Bakr then
withdrew, seeing that Quṣayy would bar them from the Kaʿbah just as he had barred Banū Ṣūfah — but war
followed regardless, until both sides agreed to arbitration by **Yaʿmar b. ʿAwf b. Kaʿb**
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:274–275).

## 5. Mujammiʿ — Sole Ruler of Makkah
Quṣayy was **the first of Banū Kaʿb b. Luʾayy to hold a rule his people obeyed**. All five sacred
offices came to rest in him alone: **al-ḥijābah** (custody of the Kaʿbah), **al-siqāyah** (watering
the pilgrims), **al-rifādah** (feeding them), **al-nadwah** (the council), and **al-liwāʾ** (the
war-standard). He gathered the whole honour of Makkah in his own house, and was named ***Mujammiʿ***
— "the Gatherer" — for uniting his people. A poet said of him:

> «أَبُوكُمْ قُصَيٌّ كَانَ يُدْعَىٰ مُجَمِّعًا … بِهِ جَمَعَ اللَّهُ الْقَبَائِلَ مِنْ فِهْرِ»
> *"Your father Quṣayy was called Mujammiʿ — through him Allāh gathered the tribes of Fihr together."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:275)

He built **Dār al-Nadwah**, where Quraysh met to marry, to counsel, and to raise the war-standard — no
important matter was settled except within its walls.

## 6. "You Are the Neighbours of Allāh"
Abū ʿUbaydah transmits Quṣayy's address on assuming authority over Makkah:

> «يَا مَعْشَرَ قُرَيْشٍ، إِنَّكُمْ جِيرَانُ اللَّهِ، وَجِيرَانُ بَيْتِهِ، وَأَهْلُ حَرَمِهِ، وَإِنَّ الْحَاجَّ زُوَّارُ بَيْتِ اللَّهِ، فَهُمْ أَضْيَافُ اللَّهِ، وَأَحَقُّ الْأَضْيَافِ بِالْكَرَامَةِ أَضْيَافُ اللَّهِ، فَتَرَافَدُوا …»
> *"O Quraysh, you are the neighbours of Allāh, the neighbours of His House, and the people of His
> sanctuary; the pilgrims are the visitors of Allāh's House, and they are Allāh's guests — and the
> guests most deserving of honour are the guests of Allāh, so contribute to their provision …"*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:275)

He levied a tax upon Quraysh's wealth to feed and water the pilgrims — a practice that continued,
al-Ṣāliḥī notes, **until Islam arose**. He also dug **Biʾr al-ʿAjūl**, the first well Quraysh dug in
Makkah, and kindled the fire at **Muzdalifah** so it could be seen by those departing ʿArafah
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:275–276).

## 7. Wise Sayings
Among the maxims al-Ṣāliḥī records of him:

> «مَنْ عَظَّمَ لَئِيمًا شَرِكَهُ فِي لُؤْمِهِ، وَمَنِ اسْتَحْسَنَ مُسْتَقْبَحًا شَرِكَهُ فِيهِ … وَمَنْ سَأَلَ فَوْقَ قَدْرِهِ اسْتَحَقَّ الْحِرْمَانَ»
> *"Whoever magnifies a base man shares in his baseness, and whoever deems fair what is foul shares in
> it … and whoever asks beyond his due deserves to be denied."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:276)

## 8. Division of the Offices Among His Sons
Quṣayy divided his honours among his sons:

- **[[ʿAbd Manāf b. Quṣayy|ʿAbd Manāf]]** received **al-siqāyah and al-nadwah** — and it is of him that
  the book says, in words that reach forward to the Prophet ﷺ himself: *«فَكَانَتْ فِيهِ النُّبُوَّةُ
  وَالثَّرْوَةُ»*, *"for in him were prophethood and wealth."*
- **ʿAbd al-Dār** received **al-ḥijābah and al-liwāʾ**.
- **ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā** received **al-rifādah** and hospitality during the days at Minā.
- **ʿAbd Quṣayy** received the two sides of the valley.

Three houses descending from Quṣayy — those of ʿAbd Manāf, ʿAbd al-Dār, and the third combined line —
came to hold the rule of Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:276).

## 9. Death
Quṣayy died in Makkah; his sons upheld the governance of Makkah among their people after him, and he
was buried at **al-Ḥajūn** — after which, the book notes, the people of Makkah took to burying their
own dead there as well (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:276).
