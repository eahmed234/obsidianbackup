# Sariyyat Khālid b. al-Walīd ilā al-ʿUzzā (سرية خالد بن الوليد إلى العزى)
*The Expedition of Khālid b. al-Walīd to Demolish al-ʿUzzā — The Obliteration of the Chief Idol of Quraysh and Kinānah*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 6, *al-Bāb al-Thānī wal-Khamsūn fī Sariyyat Khālid b. al-Walīd ilā al-ʿUzzā* (pp. 196–197), drawing upon the transmissions of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], and al-Bayhaqī from Abū al-Ṭufayl ʿĀmir b. Wāthilah ﵁, cross-referenced with *al-Iṣābah*, 2:216.

---

## 1. Background & Status of al-ʿUzzā

Following the Conquest of Makkah (**[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**) on the twentieth of Ramaḍān in the year eight AH, the Prophet ﷺ set out to purify the Ḥijāz of pagan idolatry (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196). 

Among all the idols of pre-Islamic Arabia, **al-ʿUzzā** (*al-ʿUzzā*) was the greatest and most venerated idol of [[Quraysh]] and all of [[Banū Kinānah|Kinānah]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196). It was situated in **Nakhlah** (*Nakhlah*), erected as a shrine upon three sacred acacia trees (*samurāt*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196). Its hereditary custodians and temple gatekeepers (*sadanah* and *ḥujjāb*) were Banū Shaybān from [[Banū Sulaym]], who were traditional confederates (*ḥulafāʾ*) of [[Banū Hāshim]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

The origin of its worship traced back to ʿAmr b. Luḥayy, who had deceived the Arabs by asserting that the Lord spent the winter at al-Ṭāʾif with [[al-Lāt]] and spent the summer at Nakhlah with al-ʿUzzā (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196). Consequently, the polytheists built a sanctuary house over it and dispatched sacrificial offerings and gifts to it exactly as they did to the Kaʿbah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

---

## 2. Dispatch of Khālid b. al-Walīd ﵁

With five nights remaining in Ramaḍān in the year eight AH, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commissioned **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁ and dispatched him at the head of thirty cavalrymen from his Companions to demolish al-ʿUzzā (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

When the chief priest and custodian of al-ʿUzzā—Dubayyah b. Ḥaramī al-Sulamī—learned of Khālid's approach, he hung his sword upon the idol and fled up the adjacent mountain, crying out in desperate verse to the idol:

> «يَا عُزَّ شُدِّي شَدَّةً لَا شَوَى لَهَا ... عَلَى خَالِدٍ أَلْقِي الْقِنَاعَ وَشَمِّرِي»
> «يَا عُزَّ إِنْ لَمْ تَقْتُلِي الْمَرْءَ خَالِدًا ... فَبُوئِي بِإِثْمٍ عَاجِلٍ أَوْ تَنَصَّرِي»
> *"O ʿUzzā! Launch a ferocious assault leaving nothing spared! Cast off your veil against Khālid and tuck up your garments! O ʿUzzā, if you do not slay this man Khālid, then incur immediate ignominy or turn Christian!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

---

## 3. The First Demolition & The Return

Khālid ﵁ reached the sanctuary at Nakhlah, cut down the three sacred acacia trees, demolished the shrine building, and returned to Medina (or to the Prophet's camp in Makkah) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

Upon presenting himself, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ asked him:
> «هَلْ رَأَيْتَ شَيْئًا؟»
> *"Did you see anything?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

Khālid replied: "No." 

The Prophet ﷺ commanded him:
> «فَإِنَّكَ لَمْ تَهْدِمْهَا، فَارْجِعْ إِلَيْهَا فَاهْدِمْهَا!»
> *"Then you have not truly demolished her! Return to her and demolish her!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

---

## 4. The Encounter with the Demonic Manifestation

Khālid b. al-Walīd ﵁ returned to Nakhlah in a state of intense wrath (*mutaghayyiẓan*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196). When the custodians saw Khālid returning with drawn blade, they dispersed up the mountain shouting: "O ʿUzzā, confound him! O ʿUzzā, strike him blind! Do not perish in humiliation!" (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

As Khālid approached the ruined sanctuary, there emerged before him the inner reality of the pagan cult: a naked, pitch-black female entity (*imraʾah ʿajūz sawdāʾ ʿuryānah*) with disheveled, wild hair (*thāʾirat al-raʾs*), desperately scooping up dust and heaping it upon her head and face (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

Khālid b. al-Walīd ﵁ advanced upon her without hesitation, unsheathed his sword, and recited:

> «يَا عُزَّ كُفْرَانَكِ لَا سُبْحَانَكِ ... إِنِّي رَأَيْتُ اللَّهَ قَدْ أَهَانَكِ!»
> *"O ʿUzzā, denial is yours, not glorification! Verily, I have seen that Allāh has utterly humiliated you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

Khālid struck her down with his sword, severing her in two (*fa-jazza lahā thnatayn*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196). He then returned to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and narrated the encounter. 

The Prophet ﷺ affirmed:
> «نَعَمْ، تِلْكَ الْعُزَّى، قَدْ يَئِسَتْ أَنْ تُعْبَدَ ببلَادِكُمْ أَبَدًا!»
> *"Yes, that was al-ʿUzzā! She has despaired of ever being worshipped in your land again!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).

---

## 5. Historiographical & Strategic Notes

1. **Chronological Placement**: Imām al-Ṣāliḥī notes that [[Ibn Isḥāq]] placed this expedition after Khālid's deployment to [[Sariyyat Khālid b. al-Walīd ilā Banī Jadhimah|Banū Jadhimah]]. However, [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], and al-Balādhurī explicitly affirmed that the expedition to al-ʿUzzā took place five nights before the close of Ramaḍān 8 AH, whereas Banū Jadhimah occurred in Shawwāl 8 AH (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196–197). Al-Ṣāliḥī upholds the latter sequence, observing that after the tragic error at Banū Jadhimah, the Prophet ﷺ expressed distress over Khālid's action, making it chronologically harmonious that the mission to al-ʿUzzā preceded it (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196–197).
2. **The Nature of Pagan Idols**: The narrative in *Subul al-Hudā* illustrates the orthodox Sunni understanding that major idols often had demonic spirits (*shayāṭīn*) dwelling within or around them to deceive pagan worshippers with supernatural apparitions and auditory cues, and that the physical strike of Khālid severed this spiritual deception permanently (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:196).
