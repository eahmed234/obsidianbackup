# Ghazwat Banī al-Naḍīr (غزوة بني النضير)
*The Siege and Exile of Banū al-Naḍīr — Sūrat al-Ḥashr and the Allocation of the First Pure Fayʾ*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from *Subul al-Hudā*, Volume 4, *al-Bāb al-Khāmis ʿAshar fī Ghazwat Banī al-Naḍīr* (pp. 317–336). Al-Ṣāliḥī synthesizes the accounts of [[Ibn Isḥāq]], [[Mūsā b. ʿUqbah]], [[al-Wāqidī]], [[Ibn Saʿd]], [[al-Balādhurī]], [[al-Bukhārī]], and [[Muslim]].

---

## 1. Provocation: The Treacherous Plot at the Castle Wall
Following the calamity of Biʾr Maʿūnah in Ṣafar 4 AH, the survivor **ʿAmr b. Umayyah al-Ḍamrī** encountered two men of Banū ʿĀmir at Qanāt whom the Prophet ﷺ had previously granted safe-conduct (*amān*). Unaware of their covenant, ʿAmr slew them in retaliation for his martyred brethren (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:318).

Because Banū al-Naḍīr held a mutual defense treaty (*ḥilf*) with Banū ʿĀmir, and under the collective civic provisions of the Constitution of Medina (**[[Dustūr al-Madīnah]]**), the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ rode out on Saturday after praying at Masjid Qubāʾ to Banū al-Naḍīr to request their contractual contribution toward the blood-money (*diyah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:318). He was accompanied by a small retinue of fewer than ten Companions, including **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁, **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁, **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁, **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]** ﵁, **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]** ﵁, **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁, **[[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]]** ﵁, and **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:318).

The Jewish chieftains outwardly feigned hospitality, inviting the Prophet ﷺ to rest against the wall of one of their fortresses while they gathered the funds. Privately, Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab assembled their conspirators and urged:
> *"You will never find Muḥammad as vulnerable as he is at this very hour! Cast a boulder from atop the roof and crush him; once he is slain, his companions will scatter!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:318).

Sallām b. Mishkam cautioned vehemently against the plot, warning that divine revelation would disclose their betrayal and shatter their pact. Nevertheless, **ʿAmr b. Jaḥḥāsh** ascended the roof to roll a heavy millstone (*raḥā*) onto the Prophet's head (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:318). Jibrīl ﵊ descended instantly with revelation; the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ stood up abruptly without a word and walked swiftly back toward Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:318).

---

## 2. The Ultimatum Delivered by Muḥammad b. Maslamah
Upon returning to Medina, the Prophet ﷺ summoned the veteran Ausite knight **[[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]]** ﵁ and dispatched him as his official envoy to Banū al-Naḍīr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:319–320).

Entering their assembly, Muḥammad b. Maslamah reminded them of their own past declarations regarding the arrival of the final Prophet described in the Torah (*al-Ḍaḥūk al-Qattāl*). He delivered the Prophetic decree:
> «إِنَّكُمْ قَدْ نَقَضْتُمُ الْعَهْدَ الَّذِي جَعَلْتُ لَكُمْ، بِمَا هَمَمْتُمْ بِهِ مِنَ الْغَدْرِ بِي! اخْرُجُوا مِنْ بَلَدِي وَقَدْ أَجَّلْتُكُمْ عَشْرًا، فَمَنْ رُئِيَ بَعْدَ ذَلِكَ ضُرِبَتْ عُنُقُهُ!»
> *"Indeed, you have violated the covenant I established with you by what you plotted of treachery against me! Depart from my land; I grant you a respite of ten days. Whoever is seen here after that shall be beheaded!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:320).

They were stunned that an Ausite—their former pagan confederate—would deliver such an ultimatum. Muḥammad b. Maslamah replied: *"Hearts have changed through Islam"* (*taghayyarat al-qulūb*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:320). Resigned, they began hiring camels from Ashjaʿ and preparing for departure.

---

## 3. The Sedition of the Hypocrites & Rejection of Departure
As Banū al-Naḍīr packed their goods, the chief of hypocrites **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]]** dispatched messengers (Suwayd and Dāʿis) urging them to resist:
> *"Do not leave your lands and properties! Stand firm within your fortresses; for I have two thousand men of my tribe and Arab confederates who will enter your fortress and die to the last man before anyone reaches you! Banū Qurayẓah and Ghaṭafān will likewise reinforce you!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:320).

Ibn Ubayy attempted to pressure Kaʿb b. Asad, chieftain of Banū Qurayẓah, to join the rebellion, but Kaʿb refused: *"Not a single man among us shall break the covenant while I live"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:320). Despite Sallām b. Mishkam's warnings that Ibn Ubayy would abandon them just as he abandoned Banū Qaynuqāʿ, Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab fell victim to the deception. He sent his brother Judayy b. Akhṭab to the Prophet ﷺ with the message: *"We shall not depart our lands and properties; do whatever you will!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:321).

When Judayy delivered the defiance, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ proclaimed: **«Allāhu Akbar!»**, and the Muslims chanted the takbīr. The Prophet ﷺ declared: *«The Jews have declared war!»* (*ḥārabat Yahūd*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:321).

---

## 4. The March and the Encampment
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ appointed **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Umm Maktūm]]** ﵁ over Medina and marched to the fortresses of Banū al-Naḍīr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:322).
- **The Camp Tent**: **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁ dispatched a wooden dome-tent (*qubbah min khashab al-gharb*) roofed with coarse woolen cloth for the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:322).
- The Prophet ﷺ prayed ʿAṣr in the open plain of Banū al-Naḍīr. The enemy retreated atop their battlement walls, hurling arrows and stones (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:322).
- At nightfall, the Prophet ﷺ appointed **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ (or Abū Bakr ﵁) over the military camp, while he returned with ten guards to Medina.
- **The Sharpshooter ʿAzzawk**: An enemy marksman named ʿAzzawk fired arrows that reached the Prophet's tent. The Prophet ﷺ ordered the tent relocated to the site that later became known as **Masjid al-Faḍīkh** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:322).
- **The Slaying of ʿAzzawk**: One evening, ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib was missing from camp. He had lain in ambush for ʿAzzawk as he stalked the Muslims; ʿAlī slew him and returned with his severed head (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:322). The Prophet ﷺ then dispatched ʿAlī, **[[Abū Dujānah]]** ﵁, and **[[Sahl b. Ḥunayf]]** ﵁ with ten men, who pursued the fleeing accomplices and eradicated them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:322–323).

---

## 5. The Cutting of the Date-Palms at al-Buwayrah
To compel surrender without excessive bloodshed, the Prophet ﷺ ordered the felling of their unfortified date-palm groves at **al-Buwayrah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323, 331).
- **[[Abū Laylā al-Māzinī]]** and **[[ʿAbdullāh b. Salām]]** ﵁ were commissioned to cut the trees. Abū Laylā cut the premium *ʿajwah* palms to inflict strategic grief, while ʿAbdullāh b. Salām cut the inferior palms knowing Allāh would grant the best orchards to the Muslims (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).
- The Jewish women tore their garments and wept as their ancient orchards were severed. Ḥuyayy cried out: *"O Muḥammad, you used to forbid corruption on earth—why then do you cut down the palm trees?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).
- Some Muslims harbored inner unease, fearing it might constitute wanton destruction. Allāh settled the controversy by revealing **[[Sūrat al-Ḥashr]]** (59:5):
  > «مَا قَطَعْتُمْ مِنْ لِينَةٍ أَوْ تَرَكْتُمُوهَا قَائِمَةً عَلَىٰ أُصُولِهَا فَبِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ وَلِيُخْزِيَ الْفَاسِقِينَ»
  > *"Whatever you cut down of palm-trees or left standing on their trunks—it was by permission of Allāh, and that He might disgrace the defiantly disobedient"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323, 328).

---

## 6. The Conversions of Yāmīn and Abū Saʿd
Realizing the hopelessness of their cause as Ibn Ubayy abandoned them completely, two notable men of Banū al-Naḍīr—**[[Yāmīn b. ʿUmayr]]** and **[[Abū Saʿd b. Wahb]]**—conferred together (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).
- They slipped away from the fortress under darkness, embraced Islam before the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and thus fully secured their lives and landed properties (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:502, 7:146; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).
- Yāmīn subsequently financed the execution of his treasonous cousin ʿAmr b. Jaḥḥāsh, who had attempted the murder of the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323).

---

## 7. Capitulation & Terms of Exile
Seeing no relief from the hypocrites or Ghaṭafān, Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab surrendered unconditionally to the Prophet's terms (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:323–324):
1. Complete departure from Medina.
2. Each household was permitted to load only what **one camel** could carry (*mā ḥamalat al-ibil*).
3. Absolute prohibition against taking weapons or armor (*illā al-ḥalaqah*).
4. Forfeiture of all fortresses, landed estates, and stockpiles of armor.

The Prophet ﷺ entrusted the execution of the evacuation to **[[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324). When the Jews claimed they had outstanding loans owed to them by Medinans maturing over future months, the Prophet ﷺ established the famous mercantile rule:
> «تَعَجَّلُوا وَضَعُوا!»
> *"Expedite the collection and discount the principal!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).
For instance, Sallām b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq was owed 120 dinars by Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr due in one year; he settled for 80 dinars immediately and waived the remainder (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).

---

## 8. The Exodus Caravan
To conceal their humiliation, Banū al-Naḍīr made an extravagant spectacle of their departure (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).
- They loaded **six hundred camels** with elaborate silk litters (*hawādij*), carpets, gold, and silver.
- They dismantled their own homes, pulling down door frames, lintels, and roof timbers to deny them to the Muslims and take the carved woodwork with them (fulfilling Qurʾān 59:2: *«They demolished their houses with their own hands and the hands of the believers»*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).
- Their women wore fine brocades, and female musicians played tambourines (*dufūf*) and pipes (*mazāmīr*) as the caravan paraded through the market of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).
- The majority of their aristocracy, including Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab, Sallām b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq, and Kinānah b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq, resettled in **[[Khaybar]]**, where the local Jewish populace yielded leadership to them; others traveled onward to Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).

---

## 9. Spoils & The Jurisprudence of Fayʾ
Because Banū al-Naḍīr surrendered without cavalry engagements or pitched combat (*mā awjaftum ʿalayhi min khaylin walā rikāb*), their confiscated estates did not constitute standard wartime spoils of war (*ghanīmah*) subject to four-fifths division among the troops. Instead, they constituted the **first pure Fayʾ** in Islamic legal history, belonging solely to Allāh and His Messenger ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).

- **Arsenal Captured**: The Muslims seized 50 coats of mail (*dirʿ*), 50 helmets (*bayḍah*), and 340 swords (*sayf*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).
- **Prophetic Endowment**: The Prophet ﷺ set aside parcels of land to provide one year of basic sustenance (dates and barley) for his wives and Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib; the remaining surplus was consecrated as a perpetual military endowment (*ḥabs*) for mounts and weaponry (*al-kurāʿ wal-silāḥ*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:324).
- **The Altruism of the Anṣār**: Since the Hijrah, the Muhājirūn had lived as guests in the homes and orchards of the Anṣār. The Prophet ﷺ gathered the Anṣār and offered:
  > *"If you wish, I will divide this wealth and your homes among both you and the Muhājirūn; or if you wish, I will distribute this wealth exclusively to the Muhājirūn so they may vacate your houses."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:325).
  **[[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]]** ﵁ and **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** ﵁ proclaimed on behalf of the Anṣār:
  > *"Rather, divide it entirely among our brothers the Muhājirūn, and let them remain dwelling in our homes as long as they wish!"*
  The Prophet ﷺ supplicated: *«O Allāh, have mercy upon the Anṣār and the children of the Anṣār!»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:325).
- None of the Anṣār received a portion of the Fayʾ except two exceptionally impoverished men: **[[Sahl b. Ḥunayf]]** ﵁ and **[[Abū Dujānah]]** ﵁; Saʿd b. Muʿādh was granted the ceremonial sword of Ibn Abī al-Ḥuqayq (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:325).

## 10. Scriptural Exegesis: Sūrat al-Ḥashr (The Surah of Banū al-Naḍīr)
The entire 24 verses of **[[Sūrat al-Ḥashr]]** were revealed regarding this campaign, to the extent that **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbbās]]** ﵄ insisted on designating the chapter *Sūrat al-Naḍīr* (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:327).
