# Riḥlat al-Shām al-Thāniyah (سفره ﷺ الثاني إلى الشام)
*The Journey That Led to Marriage — Maysarah's Testimony, and Abū Ṭālib's Sermon*

## 1. Abū Ṭālib's Difficulty, and Why He Suggested It
By age twenty-five, **Nafīsah bint Munyah** reports, *"he had no name in Makkah but al-Amīn, for the
completeness of good qualities gathered in him."* **[[Abū Ṭālib]]**, admitting he had no wealth of his
own and that the years had turned hard, told him: *"this caravan of your people is about to leave for
Syria, and [[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid|Khadījah]] bint Khuwaylid sends men of your people out with her
goods to trade for her and profit by it — were you to go to her and offer yourself, she would move
quickly for you and favour you over others, for what has reached her of your purity."* He added,
candidly, that he disliked sending him to Syria and feared the Jews there for him, *"but I find no way
around it"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:158).

## 2. Khadījah Sends For Him Herself
Word of Abū Ṭālib's approach — and what she already knew of his truthful speech, great trustworthiness,
and noble character — reached Khadījah before he could come to her. She said, *"I did not know he
wanted this,"* and **sent to him herself**, offering **double what she paid other men**, and sent her
servant **Maysarah** with him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:158).

## 3. The Monk Nasṭūrā, and the Shading Cloud
On the road, a monk named **Nasṭūrā** saw him resting beneath a tree and asked Maysarah: *"who is the
man beneath this tree?"* — *"A man of Quraysh,"* Maysarah answered. *"None has ever stopped beneath
this tree except a prophet,"* the monk said. *"Is there redness in his eyes?"* — *"Yes, it never
leaves him,"* said Maysarah. *"That is him — he is the last of the prophets. Would that I might live
to see him summoned to go forth!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:159)

In a further account, Maysarah himself, alarmed at seeing a cloud shade him without visible cause,
approached secretly, kissed his head and feet, and declared:

> «آمَنْتُ بِكَ وَأَنَا أَشْهَدُ أَنَّكَ الَّذِي ذَكَرَهُ اللَّهُ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ … أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّكَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ»
> *"I believe in you, and I bear witness that you are the one Allāh mentioned in the Torah … I bear
> witness there is no god but Allāh, and I bear witness that you are the Messenger of Allāh."*

He asked to see the seal of prophethood, and on being shown it, continued to kiss it, reporting that
ʿĪsā ﵊ himself had said no one would ever again stop beneath that tree except *"the unlettered
Hāshimite Arab Makkan prophet, master of the Pool and of intercession, bearer of the banner of
praise"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:159).

## 4. At the Market of Buṣrā
He sold his goods and bought others. A dispute arose with a buyer, who told him: *"swear by al-Lāt and
al-ʿUzzā."* He answered simply: *"I have never sworn by them."* The man accepted this at once —
*"your word is your word"* — and settled on his terms alone. He then told Maysarah privately: *"this
is the prophet of this nation; by the One in whose hand is my soul, our scholars find him described in
their books"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:159).

## 5. Two Angels Shading Him
For the rest of the journey, Maysarah reports that whenever the midday heat grew fierce, **he would
see two angels shading the Prophet ﷺ from the sun** as he rode (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:159).

## 6. The Return, and Khadījah's Own Witness
As they approached Makkah, Khadījah climbed to watch for the caravan and **saw the same shading**
herself, confirming to her own eyes what Maysarah then narrated in full — the words of the monk
Nasṭūrā and of the merchant at Buṣrā. Ibn Isḥāq notes that when Khadījah saw her trade goods had
returned at double the usual profit, she paid him **more than she had promised**
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:159–160).

## 7. Waraqah's Confirmation
Khadījah had already spoken of all this to her cousin **Waraqah b. Nawfal**, a Christian well versed
in the scriptures. He told her:

> «يَا خَدِيجَةُ، إِنَّ مُحَمَّدًا لَنَبِيُّ هَٰذِهِ الْأُمَّةِ، وَقَدْ عَرَفْتُ أَنَّهُ كَائِنٌ لِهَٰذِهِ الْأُمَّةِ نَبِيٌّ يُنْتَظَرُ، هَٰذَا زَمَانُهُ»
> *"O Khadījah, Muḥammad is indeed the prophet of this nation. I have long known this nation awaits a
> prophet, and this is his time."*

He then grew impatient for the mission to begin, composing verses — preserved via [[Ibn Isḥāq]] —
naming him *"Ibn ʿAbdillāh Aḥmad,"* declaring his hope to live to see him *"raised as truthful, as
Hūd and Ṣāliḥ, Mūsā and Ibrāhīm were sent,"* and vowing that if he lived to witness it he would be
overjoyed, and if not, he would already have departed this world content
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:160–161).

## 8. The Marriage
The book records several accounts of exactly how the proposal came about:

- **Abū Saʿīd al-Naysābūrī**: Khadījah instructed him directly, *"go to your uncle and tell him to come
  to us early,"* then told Abū Ṭālib herself: *"go in to my uncle ʿAmr and speak to him, that he marry
  me to your nephew Muḥammad b. ʿAbdullāh."* Abū Ṭālib, taken aback, said, *"Khadījah, do not mock,"*
  and she answered, *"this is Allāh's own doing."*
- **Al-Zuhrī**: a woman challenged him — *"why do you not marry?"* — pointing out that no woman in
  Quraysh, Khadījah included, would refuse him as an equal; out of shyness before Khadījah he then
  proposed.
- **Nafīsah bint Munyah**, in her own words: she went to him directly and asked why he did not marry;
  when he answered he had no means, she said, *"if that were provided, and you were invited to wealth,
  beauty, nobility, and equal standing, would you not answer?"* — *"who is she?"* he asked. *"Khadījah,"*
  she said. *"And how would that be arranged?"* — *"leave that to me,"* she answered, and so relayed
  his consent to Khadījah, who sent for her uncle **ʿAmr b. Asad** to conduct the marriage.
- **Ibn Isḥāq**: he went in accompanied by his uncle **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib|Ḥamzah]]** ﵁.

(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:164–165)

### Abū Ṭālib and Ten Kinsmen Propose
Abū Ṭālib went with ten men of his people to her uncle and proposed on his behalf; **ʿAmr b. Asad**
consented at once, saying of him: *"this stallion's nose will not be pierced [refused]."*

### The Dowry
Ibn Hishām gives **twenty young she-camels**; al-Balādhurī and al-Dimyāṭī give **twelve and a half
ounces**; al-Muḥibb al-Ṭabarī specifies this was in **gold** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:165).

### Abū Ṭālib's Marriage Sermon
Abū al-Ḥusayn b. Fāris preserves the sermon in full:

> «الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي جَعَلَنَا مِنْ ذُرِّيَّةِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَزَرْعِ إِسْمَاعِيلَ وَضِئْضِئِ مَعَدٍّ … ثُمَّ إِنَّ ابْنَ أَخِي هَٰذَا مُحَمَّدَ بْنَ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ لَا يُوزَنُ بِهِ رَجُلٌ إِلَّا رَجَحَ بِهِ شَرَفًا وَنُبْلًا وَفَضْلًا وَعَقْلًا، وَإِنْ كَانَ فِي الْمَالِ قُلًّا، فَإِنَّ الْمَالَ ظِلٌّ زَائِلٌ وَأَمْرٌ حَائِلٌ وَعَارِيَةٌ مُسْتَرْجَعَةٌ، وَهُوَ وَاللَّهِ بَعْدَ هَٰذَا لَهُ نَبَأٌ عَظِيمٌ وَخَطَرٌ جَلِيلٌ»
> *"Praise be to Allāh who made us of the progeny of Ibrāhīm and the seed of Ismāʿīl and the stock of
> Maʿadd … as for this nephew of mine, Muḥammad b. ʿAbdullāh, no man can be weighed against him without
> being outweighed in nobility, excellence, virtue, and intellect — though he is modest in wealth, for
> wealth is a fading shadow, a passing thing, a borrowed possession recalled. He, by Allāh, has after
> this a great tiding and a weighty standing."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:165)
