# Banū Thaqīf (بنو ثقيف)
*The Mountain Chieftains of al-Ṭāʾif and Custodians of the Idol al-Lāt*

## 1. Lineage, Divisions & Seat of Power

**Banū Thaqīf** are the celebrated mountain tribe of the Hijaz inhabiting the fortified city and agricultural oasis of **[[al-Ṭāʾif]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:295; 2:433). Their eponymous ancestor was **Thaqīf** (named *Qasiyy*) b. Munabbih b. Bakr b. Hawāzin, linking them closely to the greater Qays ʿAylān confederation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:292; 5:310).

The tribe was organized into two great political factions within al-Ṭāʾif:
1. **Banū Mālik**: Representing the aristocratic warrior clans of the lower city.
2. **al-Aḥlāf (The Confederates)**: Descended from ʿAwf b. Thaqīf, including the influential clan of Banū Masʿūd, whose leaders held custody over the regional idol **[[al-Lāt]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296–298).

Endowed with formidable stone battlements, abundant water, and vineyards, Thaqīf maintained proud commercial and strategic rivalry with [[Quraysh]], often intermarrying with the Meccan patricians.

---

## 2. Early Encounters: The Journey to al-Ṭāʾif (Riḥlat al-Ṭāʾif)

In the tenth year of the Prophethood (the Year of Sorrow, following the deaths of Khadījah ﵂ and Abū Ṭālib), the Prophet ﷺ set out on foot with [[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]] ﵁ to al-Ṭāʾif seeking protection and support for the Daʿwah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:433).

He approached the three brothers who were the lords of Thaqīf: ʿAbd Yālīl, Masʿūd, and Ḥabīb, the sons of ʿAmr b. ʿUmayr (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:433). They met him with derision, insolence, and refusal:
- One retorted: *“He tears down the drapery of the Kaʿbah if Allāh sent you!”*
- Another sneered: *“Could Allāh find no one else to send but you?”*
- The third declared: *“By Allāh, I shall never speak to you! If you are a Messenger as you say, you are too formidable for me to address; and if you lie against Allāh, I ought not converse with you!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:433).

They incited their street rabble, slaves, and youths to line his path, hurling stones until the Prophet's ﷺ sandals were clotted with blood and Zayd's head was wounded protecting him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:433–434). Sheltering in the orchard of the sons of Rabīʿah, the Prophet ﷺ offered his legendary supplication of vulnerability: *«Allāhumma ilayka ashkū quwwata ḍaʿfī...»* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:434). When the Angel of the Mountains appeared asking permission to crush the town between the two mountains (*al-Akhshabān*), the Messenger of Mercy replied:
> «بَلْ أَرْجُو أَنْ يُخْرِجَ اللَّهُ مِنْ أَصْلَابِهِمْ مَنْ يَعْبُدُ اللَّهَ وَحْدَهُ لَا يُشْرِكُ بِهِ شَيْئًا!»
> *"Rather, I hope that Allāh will bring forth from their loins those who will worship Allāh alone, associating nothing with Him!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:436).

## 3. Confrontation at Ḥunayn and the Siege of al-Ṭāʾif (8 AH)

Following **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]**, Thaqīf joined **[[Banū Hawāzin]]** under Mālik b. ʿAwf to destroy the Islamic state at **[[Ghazwat Ḥunayn]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:310). Banū Mālik fought fiercely under their banners, but upon suffering defeat, the remnants of Thaqīf fled back to their high citadel in al-Ṭāʾif, barred their heavy gates, and gathered provisions for a year (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:357).

The Prophet ﷺ marched immediately and laid siege to the fortress during **[[Ghazwat al-Ṭāʾif]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:357–365):
- The Muslims deployed advanced siege engines, including a mangonel (*manjanīq*) and protective mantlets (*dabbābah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:358).
- Thaqīf defended their ramparts with deadly arrow volleys and molten scrap iron, martyring twelve Muslims (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:358).
- Recognizing the fortress was provisioned for prolonged siege and that Mecca was secure, the Prophet ﷺ lifted the siege. When asked to curse Thaqīf, he raised his hands and supplicated:
> «اللَّهُمَّ اهْدِ ثَقِيفًا وَأْتِ بِهِمْ!»
> *"O Allāh! Guide Thaqīf and bring them [unto Islam]!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:365).

## 4. The Delegation of Thaqīf (*Wafd Thaqīf*) & Eradication of al-Lāt (9 AH)

The Prophetic prayer bore fruit in Ramaḍān 9 AH. After the assassination of their beloved chief **ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd al-Thaqafī** ﵁ (who embraced Islam after the siege and called his tribe, dying a martyr; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296), Thaqīf found themselves surrounded and spiritually isolated.

They dispatched **[[Wafd Thaqīf]]**, led by ʿAbd Yālīl b. ʿAmr, to Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296). They attempted to negotiate conditional concessions:
- That their idol al-Lāt (*al-Ṭāghiyah*) be spared from demolition for three years (or even one month).
- That they be exempted from the ritual prayers (*al-Ṣalāh*).
The Prophet ﷺ rejected all compromise regarding faith and worship, declaring:
> «أَمَّا الصَّلَاةُ فَإِنَّهُ لَا خَيْرَ فِي دِينٍ لَا صَلَاةَ فِيهِ!»
> *"As for the prayer, there is no good in a religion that has no prayer in it!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297).

They submitted unconditionally. The Prophet ﷺ excused them only from demolishing the idol with their own hands, dispatching **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** and **[[al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah]]** (himself of Thaqīf), who leveled the sanctuary of al-Lāt to the ground (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:297–298). The Prophet ﷺ appointed the devoted youth **[[ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁ as their governor and Imām (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298).

---

## 5. Notable Figures from Banū Thaqīf

- **ʿUrwah b. Masʿūd al-Thaqafī** ﵁ — Chieftain of Thaqīf, negotiator of Quraysh at Ḥudaybiyah, and martyr of al-Ṭāʾif likened by the Prophet ﷺ to the Believer of Yā Sīn (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:296, 300).
- **[[al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah]]** ﵁ — Astute statesman, veteran of Ḥudaybiyah, and demolisher of al-Lāt.
- **[[ʿUthmān b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁ — The youngest delegate, scholar of the Qurʾān, who kept Thaqīf firm upon Islam during the Wars of Apostasy (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:298).
- **[[Abū Bakrah al-Thaqafī]]** ﵁ — Nufayʿ b. al-Ḥārith, who escaped down the ramparts of al-Ṭāʾif to freedom.
