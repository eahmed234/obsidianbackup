# Abū Fukayhah (أبو فكيهة) ﵁
*The Early Believer of the Burning Stones — Emancipated by al-Ṣiddīq and Emigrant to Abyssinia*

## 1. Nomenclature, Origin & Early Acceptance of Islam
Abū Fukayhah is one of the celebrated early converts whose unyielding steadfastness under torture became an archetype of faith in early Islam:
- **Name and Cognomen**: His personal name was **Yasār** (or Aflaḥ b. Yasār), though he is predominantly renowned across biographical literature by his kunya **Abū Fukayhah** (*bi-ḍamm al-fāʾ wa-fatḥ al-kāf*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:407; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:536, entry 9364; 7:268, entry 10397).
- **Lineage and Affiliation**: Attributed as *al-Jahmī*, he was an enslaved client (*mawlā*) of Ṣafwān b. Umayyah b. Khalaf al-Jumaḥī (and in other accounts, Banū ʿAbd al-Dār) (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:268). Classical authorities state that his ancestral roots traced to [[al-Azd]], while ʿUmar b. Shabbah noted an affiliation with the Ashʿarīs (*al-Iṣābah*, 7:268).
- **Early Islam**: He embraced the faith at the dawn of the Prophetic mission at the same time as **[[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]]** ﵁, forming part of the small nucleus of defenseless early believers (*al-mustaḍʿafūn*) who proclaimed tawḥīd in Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360).

## 2. Qurʾānic Occasion of Revelation (*Asbāb al-Nuzūl*)
Because of his humble socioeconomic status, the proud aristocrats of Quraysh objected to sitting in the company of the Prophet [[Muḥammad b. ʻAbdullah|Muḥammad]] ﷺ while slaves and destitute converts were present, demanding that they be expelled before any dialogue (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:536).

Ibn Isḥāq explicitly documents that Abū Fukayhah was among the core believers concerning whom Allāh revealed the divine protection (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:536):
> «وَلَا تَطْرُدِ الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُمْ بِالْغَدَاةِ وَالْعَشِيِّ يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُ»
> *"And do not send away those who call upon their Lord morning and evening, seeking His Face."* ([[Sūrat al-Anʿām]], 6:52).

## 3. The Ordeal of Torture in Makkah
When his conversion was discovered, the chieftains of Banū Jumaḥ subjected Abū Fukayhah to barbaric torment to compel him to renounce his faith (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360):
- **Dragging in Ropes**: [[Umayyah b. Khalaf]] tied a coarse rope around his ankle and ordered him dragged through the gravelly streets of Makkah, finally casting him out onto the sun-scorched black stones of the desert (*al-ramḍāʾ*) at midday (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360; *al-Iṣābah*, 7:268).
- **The Dialogue of Tawḥīd**: As he lay bound upon the blazing ground, a dung beetle (*juʿal*) crawled past. Umayyah mocked him derisively: *"Is this beetle not your lord as well?"* Abū Fukayhah responded with majestic spiritual defiance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360):
  > «اللَّهُ رَبِّي، خَلَقَنِي وَخَلَقَكَ وَخَلَقَ هَذَا الْجُعَلَ!»
  > *"Allāh is my Lord! He created me, He created you, and He created this beetle!"*
- **The Crushing Stone**: Enraged by his unshakeable conviction, Umayyah seized him by the neck and began choking him violently (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360). His brother **[[Ubayy b. Khalaf]]** urged him on: *"Increase his torment, until Muḥammad comes and delivers him through his sorcery!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360). They hauled him out at high noon, bound in iron fetters, and placed a massive burning boulder upon his chest until his tongue lolled from his mouth (*dalaʿa lisānuh*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360).
- Muḥammad b. Kaʿb al-Quraẓī reported: *"Abū Fukayhah was tortured so relentlessly that he lost awareness of what he was saying"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360). The polytheists abandoned him upon the scorching gravel, presuming he was dead (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360; *al-Iṣābah*, 7:268).

## 4. Emancipation by Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq
When Abū Fukayhah regained consciousness, **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ passed by the gruesome scene (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360). Moved by deep compassion and seeking only the countenance of Allāh, Abū Bakr purchased Abū Fukayhah from Umayyah b. Khalaf and immediately set him free (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360; *al-Iṣābah*, 7:268).

Abū Fukayhah thus became one of the celebrated seven enslaved believers whom Abū Bakr emancipated in Makkah—alongside Bilāl b. Rabāḥ, ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah, Umm ʿUbays, Zunnīrah, al-Nahdiyyah, and the maidservant of Banū Muʾammil (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:360–361).

## 5. Migration to Abyssinia (*al-Hijrah al-Thāniyah*)
Following his emancipation and the intensification of pagan persecutions in Makkah, Abū Fukayhah (Yasār) was granted permission by the Prophet ﷺ to emigrate (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:407). He joined the Second Migration to the Kingdom of Aksum in Abyssinia, where he found sanctuary under the righteous Negus **[[al-Najāshī (Aṣḥamah)|al-Najāshī]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:407).
