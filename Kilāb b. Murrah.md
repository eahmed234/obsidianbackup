# Kilāb b. Murrah (كلاب بن مرة)
*Ḥakīm b. Murrah — Grandfather of the Prophet ﷺ Through Both His Sons*

## 1. Name
His true given name was **Ḥakīm**, also read **al-Ḥakīm**, or **al-Muhadhdhab**, or **ʿUrwah** — the
book records these on the authority of al-Jawwānī. *Kilāb* itself is a title: al-Suhaylī derives it
either from *al-mukālabah*, ferocity in contest (as in *kālabtu al-ʿaduww*, "I fought the enemy
fiercely"), or from *kilāb*, "dogs," as an emphatic name for abundance, the way men were also named
*Sibāʿ* ("beasts of prey") or *Anmār* ("panthers"). A second account holds he was devoted to hunting
with hounds and kept a great many, so that when he passed by with them people said, *"this is the
pack of the son of Murrah"* — and the title stayed. Ibn Diḥyah records the pre-Islamic custom of
naming sons with harsh names (*Kilāb*, *Dhiʾb* — "wolf") and slaves with pleasant ones (*Marzūq*,
*Rabāḥ*) — Abū al-Duqaysh explained it as choosing fearsome names for sons because a son is a weapon
prepared against one's enemies (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:276).

## 2. Kunyah, Mother, and Marriage
His kunyah was **Abū Zuhrah**. His mother was **Hind**, also given as **Naʿm**, **bint Surayr b.
Thaʿlabah**. He married **Fāṭimah bint Saʿd b. Sayl**, whose own father was **the first to ornament
swords with silver and gold** — Saʿd b. Sayl gave two such ornamented swords as part of his daughter's
dowry, and Kilāb **placed them in the treasury of the Kaʿbah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:277).

## 3. Two Sons, One the Prophet's ﷺ Direct Ancestor, the Other His Maternal Great-Ancestor
Kilāb had **two sons: [[Quṣayy b. Kilāb|Quṣayy]] and Zuhrah**. Al-Ṣāliḥī states plainly:

> «وَهُوَ جَدُّ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ كَمَا تَقَدَّمَ مِنْ قِبَلِ أُمِّهِ»
> *"And he [Kilāb] is the grandfather of the Prophet ﷺ, as stated previously, through his mother."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:277)

**[[Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī]]** is cited on Zuhrah's own given name: *"the well-known position among
the genealogists is that Zuhrah is a man's name; Ibn Qutaybah stood alone in claiming it was a
woman's name, and this is refuted by the leading imām of genealogy, Hishām b. al-Kalbī, [who says]
Zuhrah's own name was **al-Mughīrah**."* Al-Suhaylī adds that what Ibn Qutaybah said is *"denounced and
unknown"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:277). Zuhrah is the ancestor of **[[Banū Zuhrah]]** and, through them,
of the Prophet's ﷺ mother **[[Āminah bint Wahb]]** — so Kilāb stands as the Prophet's ﷺ ancestor
through his father twice over, in a sense: once directly through Quṣayy, and once as the
great-ancestor of his own mother through Zuhrah.
