# Ghazwat Banī Qurayẓah (غزوة بني قريظة)
*The siege of the Jewish tribe that broke its treaty during the Battle of the Trench*

The **campaign against Banū Qurayẓah** followed directly on the [[Ghazwat al-Khandaq|Battle of the Trench]] in the year 5. During the siege of Medina the Jewish tribe of [[Banū Qurayẓah]] had broken its treaty with the Prophet ﷺ and sided with the Confederates.[^s5.3] On the day he returned from the trench, Jibrīl ﵊ came to him and told him to march against them.[^bukh4122] The Muslims besieged their forts until they surrendered to the Prophet's judgement, and he left the decision to [[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]] ﵁, chief of their former allies al-Aws. Saʿd judged that their fighting men should be killed, their women and children taken captive and their property divided, and the Prophet ﷺ said he had judged by the judgement of Allāh.[^s5.10][^musl1768][^bukh4122] The campaign is also remembered for the repentance of [[Abū Lubābah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir|Abū Lubābah]] ﵁ and for the Companions' two readings of the Prophet's order about the ʿAṣr prayer.[^s5.4][^s5.8] The Qurʾān refers to it in [[Sūrat al-Aḥzāb]] 33:26–27.[^s5.3]

## Jibrīl's command

The Muslims came back from the trench exhausted by the siege and laid down their arms.[^s5.3] The Prophet ﷺ was washing his head in ʿĀʾishah's house, having prayed Ẓuhr, when a man greeted them from outside. ʿĀʾishah ﵂, looking through the door, took him for Diḥyah al-Kalbī. He was turbaned, riding a grey mule with a brocade saddle-cloth, with dust on his face and his armour on.[^s5.3] He asked whether they had laid down their arms already, when the angels had not put theirs down since the enemy arrived. Allāh, he said, commanded the Prophet ﷺ to fight Banū Qurayẓah, and he himself was going ahead with the angels to shake their forts.[^s5.4] In a report of Ḥumayd b. Hilāl the Prophet ﷺ said his Companions were worn out, and asked whether he might give them a few days; Jibrīl told him to set out at once.[^s5.4] When ʿĀʾishah asked who the man had been, the Prophet ﷺ told her it was Jibrīl.[^s5.4] [[Anas b. Mālik]] ﵁ remembered the dust rising in the lane of Banū Ghanm from Jibrīl's procession as the Prophet ﷺ rode to Banū Qurayẓah.[^bukh4118]

ʿĀʾishah's report in al-Bukhārī is shorter: Jibrīl came to the Prophet ﷺ shaking the dust from his head, said that he himself had not laid down his arms, and pointed to Banū Qurayẓah.[^bukh4122]

A crier called out, *"O horsemen of Allāh, mount!"*, and [[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ|Bilāl]] announced that whoever heard and obeyed should not pray ʿAṣr except at Banū Qurayẓah.[^s5.4]

## The ʿAṣr prayer on the road

The time of the prayer came while some of the Companions were still on the way. Some said they would not pray until they reached Banū Qurayẓah, since they were under the Prophet's firm command, and prayed ʿAṣr there after sunset. Others prayed, saying he had not meant them to miss the prayer. When this was told to the Prophet ﷺ, he blamed neither group.[^s5.4]

In the report of Ibn ʿUmar in al-Bukhārī the prayer named is ʿAṣr; in the same report in Muslim, through the same chain, it is Ẓuhr. Kaʿb b. Mālik, ʿĀʾishah and the writers on the campaigns give ʿAṣr.[^s5.18][^s5.19] Ibn Ḥajar suggests ways of reconciling the two:

- some had already prayed Ẓuhr and some had not;
- the Companions set out in groups, the first told Ẓuhr and the later ones ʿAṣr;
- the stronger men, or those who lived nearer, were told Ẓuhr.

He notes that the single chain of Ibn ʿUmar's report counts against these.[^s5.19] Ibn al-Tīn's claim that those who prayed did so on their mounts, so as not to slow down, is rejected by Ibn Ḥajar as having no support in any version of the story.[^s5.19][^s5.20]

al-Ṣāliḥī draws from the episode that one who does his utmost to reach a judgement is not blamed for it. One group took the order literally even if the time passed; the other read it as a figure of speech urging them to hurry. In *Zād al-Maʿād*, both groups were rewarded for their intention, but those who prayed in time gained both merits, of hurrying and of guarding the prayer's time.[^s5.20]

## The march

Ibn Umm Maktūm was left in charge of Medina.[^s5.5] According to al-Wāqidī the Prophet ﷺ set out with seven nights of Dhū al-Qaʿdah remaining, in mail, helmet and shield, spear in hand, riding his horse al-Laḥīf. There were thirty-six horses, and Ibn Saʿd gives the army as three thousand.[^s5.5] Other reports, of al-Ṭabarānī from Abū Rāfiʿ and Ibn ʿAbbās, have him riding a bare-backed donkey called Yaʿfūr when he reached Banū Qurayẓah.[^s5.5]

On the way he passed a group of [[Banū al-Najjār]] at al-Ṣawrayn, among them Ḥārithah b. al-Nuʿmān, drawn up in arms. He asked whether anyone had passed them, and they said Diḥyah al-Kalbī had ridden by on a mule and told them to take up their weapons, for the Prophet ﷺ would soon be on them. *"That was Jibrīl,"* he said, *"sent to Banū Qurayẓah to shake their forts and cast terror into their hearts."*[^s5.5]

ʿAlī, who carried the banner, went ahead with a party of Muhājirūn and Anṣār, among them [[Abū Qatādah al-Anṣārī|Abū Qatādah]] ﵁, and planted it at the foot of the fort. From their towers the men of Qurayẓah cursed the Prophet ﷺ and his wives; the Muslims kept silent, saying only that the sword lay between them.[^s5.4][^s5.5] When the Prophet ﷺ arrived and camped at the well of Anā below the lava field of Banū Qurayẓah, ʿAlī came back to him. He urged him not to go near those people, for Allāh would suffice him against them. When the Prophet ﷺ guessed that he had heard abuse, he said: *"If they saw me, they would say nothing of the kind."*[^s5.6]

[[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]] ﵁ went ahead and told them the Muslims would not leave until they starved, like a fox in its hole. They pleaded that they were his clients (*mawālī*), not al-Khazraj's, and he answered that there was no pact between them. The Prophet ﷺ came close, his Companions shielding him, and called out to their leading men: *"Answer, O brothers of apes and swine and servants of false gods — has Allāh disgraced you and brought His punishment down on you? Do you curse me?"* They swore they had not, and said: *"O Abū al-Qāsim, you were never ignorant"* — or, in another wording, *"never coarse."*[^s5.6] That evening [[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]] ﵁ sent loads of dates, which were the army's food, and the Prophet ﷺ said: *"What a good food dates are."*[^s5.6]

## The siege

At dawn the Prophet ﷺ put his archers in front and drew up his men around the forts, and the two sides shot arrows and stones at each other until evening; the Muslims took turns keeping watch through the night.[^s5.6] When Qurayẓah were sure they would be destroyed, they stopped shooting and sent down Nabbāsh b. Qays to talk. He asked for the terms given to [[Banū al-Naḍīr]]: that they keep their lives and leave with their women and children and what their camels could carry, except weapons. When the Prophet ﷺ refused, he asked only for their lives and their families, leaving everything else; the Prophet ﷺ refused again, and would accept nothing but their surrender to his judgement.[^s5.6]

### Kaʿb b. Asad's three proposals

When Nabbāsh returned, Kaʿb b. Asad put three choices to his people.[^s5.6] The first was to follow the Prophet ﷺ and believe in him. It was clear, Kaʿb said, that he was the prophet they found described in their scripture, and that nothing had kept them from him except envy that the prophet had come from the Arabs and not from the Children of Israel. Kaʿb added that he himself had disliked breaking the treaty, and that the misfortune had come from Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab. Ḥuyayy had come into their fort when Quraysh and Ghaṭafān withdrew, keeping his promise to Kaʿb.[^s5.6][^s5.7] He reminded them of Ibn Jawwās, who had come to live among them because a prophet would appear in that town, and who had told them to follow him and give him his greeting. They answered that they would never leave the law of the Torah.[^s5.7]

The second was to kill their own women and children and go out against the Muslims with drawn swords, with nothing left behind to fear for. They answered: *"Shall we kill these poor ones? What good is life after them?"* The third was to attack that night, the eve of the Sabbath, when the Muslims might feel safe. They refused to break the Sabbath and bring on themselves what had befallen those who did so before them.[^s5.7]

### Those who left before the surrender

Thaʿlabah and Usayd, sons of Saʿyah, and their cousin Asad b. ʿUbayd were a family who were not of Qurayẓah or al-Naḍīr; the book names their tribe as Hudhayl. They told Qurayẓah that they knew he was the Messenger of Allāh, described to them by their own scholars. When their people refused, the three came down that night, the night before the surrender, and became Muslim, and their lives, families and property were secured.[^s5.7]

ʿAmr b. Suʿdā had refused to take part in breaking the treaty. He now offered his people one last course: if they would not join the Prophet ﷺ, they should keep their religion and pay the *jizyah*. They answered that they would sooner be killed than pay tribute to the Arabs, and he declared himself quit of them. He walked out that night past the Muslim guard. [[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]] ﵁, who commanded it, let him go, praying not to be denied the chance to pardon the slips of noble men. ʿAmr spent the night in the Prophet's mosque and was never seen again, and the Prophet ﷺ said: *"That is a man Allāh saved by his loyalty."*[^s5.7][^s5.8]

## Abū Lubābah

As the siege tightened, Qurayẓah asked on the eve of the Sabbath for Abū Lubābah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir to be sent so they could consult him. When he came, the men rose to meet him and the women and children crowded round him weeping, and he was moved. Kaʿb asked whether they should surrender to the Prophet's judgement; Abū Lubābah said yes, and drew his hand across his throat — it would be slaughter.[^s5.8]

*"By Allāh,"* he said later, *"my feet had not moved from where they stood before I knew I had betrayed Allāh and His Messenger."* He went down weeping, left by another way so as not to face the Prophet ﷺ, went to the mosque in Medina and tied himself to the pillar that came to be called the Pillar of Repentance. He swore not to move until he died or Allāh forgave him, and never to set foot in the land of Banū Qurayẓah again. When the Prophet ﷺ heard, he said: *"Had he come to me I would have asked forgiveness for him; since he has gone, leave him until Allāh decides what He will."*[^s5.8] About him was revealed: *"O you who believe, do not betray Allāh and the Messenger, nor knowingly betray your trusts"* (al-Anfāl 8:27).[^s5.8]

He stayed tied, in great heat, without food or drink, until he could barely hear. He remembered a dream he had seen during the siege, of being stuck in stinking mud and then washing clean in a running river, which Abū Bakr had interpreted as a trouble that would be lifted.[^s5.8] How long he stayed is reported differently:[^s5.8][^s5.9]

- **Six nights:** Ibn Hishām, adding that his wife untied him at each prayer;
- **Close to twenty nights:** Ibn ʿUqbah, introduced with "they claim", which *al-Bidāyah* calls the most likely;
- **Twenty-five nights:** Ibn Isḥāq;
- **Ten-odd nights:** Mālik, in a heavy chain, untied by his daughter at prayer times.

His repentance was revealed at dawn while the Prophet ﷺ was in Umm Salamah's house: *"And others have confessed their sins; they mixed a righteous deed with another that was bad. Perhaps Allāh will turn to them"* (al-Tawbah 9:102). Umm Salamah ﵂ heard him laughing, and with his permission called the good news to Abū Lubābah from her door. This was before the verse of the veil.[^s5.9] When people came to untie him, he refused to be released by anyone but the Prophet ﷺ, who untied him on his way out to the dawn prayer. A report that [[Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ|Fāṭimah]] ﵂ came to untie him is judged weak by al-Ṣāliḥī, one narrator being weak and the chain incomplete.[^s5.9] Abū Lubābah offered to leave his people's quarter, where he had sinned, and to give away all his wealth; the Prophet ﷺ told him a third would be enough.[^s5.9]

## The surrender and Saʿd's judgement

When the siege had worn them down, Qurayẓah surrendered to the Prophet's judgement. The men were bound and put to one side under Muḥammad b. Maslamah, and the women and children brought out of the forts and placed under [[ʿAbdullāh b. Salām]] ﵁.[^s5.9] In the forts were found fifteen hundred swords, three hundred coats of mail, two thousand spears and fifteen hundred shields, with much furniture, vessels, camels and livestock. The wine found there was poured away.[^s5.10]

The men of al-Aws hurried to the Prophet ﷺ. Qurayẓah were their allies, they said, and they reminded him how he had given Banū Qaynuqāʿ, the allies of al-Khazraj, to ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy — three hundred men without armour and four hundred in mail. They asked him to give them theirs. He was silent until they had all spoken, then said: *"Will you not be content that the judgement on them be given to a man from among you?"* When they agreed, he said: *"Then it is for Saʿd b. Muʿādh."*[^s5.10] Ibn ʿUqbah's version has him tell them to choose whichever of his Companions they wished, and they chose Saʿd.[^s5.10]

Saʿd was in Medina, in the tent the Prophet ﷺ had pitched for him in the mosque so that he could visit him easily, after he was wounded in the arm at the trench.[^s5.10][^bukh4122] In Ibn ʿUqbah's report it was the tent of Kuʿaybah bint Saʿīd al-Aslamiyyah, who nursed the wounded and cared for those who had no one.[^s5.10] His people set him on a donkey and walked beside him, urging him to treat his clients well, as Ibn Ubayy had treated his. He said nothing until at last he answered: *"The time has come for Saʿd not to fear, for Allāh's sake, the blame of anyone who blames."* One of them, al-Ḍaḥḥāk b. Khalīfah, cried out in grief, went back to al-Aws and mourned the men of Qurayẓah before Saʿd had even arrived.[^s5.10]

When Saʿd came near, the Prophet ﷺ said to the Anṣār: *"Stand up for your leader"* — or *"for the best of you."*[^musl1768] The Muhājirūn understood this as addressed to the Anṣār; the Anṣār said it was meant for all the Muslims.[^s5.10] The men of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal used to say they had stood for him in two rows, each greeting him, until he reached the Prophet ﷺ.[^s5.11]

Saʿd asked those of al-Aws present whether they accepted his judgement. They said they did, hoping he would be generous as others had been to their allies, and he answered only that he would spare no effort for them. He made them swear by Allāh's covenant that the judgement would be what he decided. Then, turning towards the place where the Prophet ﷺ sat but keeping his face averted out of respect, he asked whether the one there accepted it too, and the Prophet ﷺ said yes.[^s5.11]

In the report of ʿĀʾishah in al-Bukhārī, Saʿd said:

> «فإني أحكم فيهم: أن تقتل المقاتلة، وأن تسبى النساء والذرية، وأن تقسم أموالهم»
> *"I judge that their fighting men be killed, their women and children taken captive, and their property divided."*[^bukh4122]

In the report given by al-Ṣāliḥī, he judged that every male old enough to shave should be killed, and added that their houses should go to the Muhājirūn and not the Anṣār. When the Anṣār protested that they had been the Muhājirūn's partners, he said he wanted the Muhājirūn to be independent of them.[^s5.11]

The Prophet ﷺ said: *"You have judged by the judgement of Allāh"* — or, in another wording, *"by the judgement of the King."*[^musl1768][^musl1769] In the report given by al-Ṣāliḥī he said: *"You have judged concerning them by the judgement of Allāh, which He gave from above seven heavens."*[^s5.11] al-Suhaylī explains the phrase as meaning that the judgement came down from above.[^s5.20] Ibn Isḥāq, in a transmission other than al-Bakkāʾī's, adds the Prophet's words: *"With that the angel came to me at dawn."*[^s5.11]

The night before the surrender Saʿd had prayed that if war with Quraysh remained, Allāh should keep him alive for it; that if it was over, his wound should be his martyrdom; and that he should not die until he had seen justice done to Banū Qurayẓah.[^s5.11] In ʿĀʾishah's report the wound later burst open in the tent; the people of a neighbouring tent of Banū Ghifār saw blood flowing towards them, and Saʿd died of it.[^bukh4122]

## The execution

The Prophet ﷺ left on a Thursday, the 9th of Dhū al-Ḥijjah according to al-Wāqidī, Ibn Saʿd and al-Dimyāṭī, or the 5th, and the prisoners were brought into Medina. The men were held in the house of [[Usāmah b. Zayd]] and the women and children in the house of Ramlah bint al-Ḥārith, or all of them there by another account. He ordered loads of dates scattered for them.[^s5.11][^s5.12] The next morning trenches were dug in the market of Medina, between the house of Abū al-Jahm al-ʿAdawī and Aḥjār al-Zayt. The Prophet ﷺ sat with his senior Companions, and the men of Qurayẓah were brought out in groups and beheaded at the trenches; ʿAlī and [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām|al-Zubayr]] carried it out.[^s5.12]

As they were led out, they asked Kaʿb b. Asad what he thought was being done with them. He answered: *"Do you never understand? Don't you see that the summoner does not stop, and whoever of you goes does not come back? By Allāh, it is the sword."* They said this was no time for reproach, and Ḥuyayy told them to stop blaming one another and be patient under the sword.[^s5.12]

Saʿd b. ʿUbādah and [[al-Ḥubāb b. al-Mundhir]] ﵄ told the Prophet ﷺ that al-Aws disliked the killing because of their old alliance. Saʿd b. Muʿādh answered that no man of al-Aws with any good in him disliked it. Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr proposed that the prisoners be divided among the houses of al-Aws, beginning with his own, and so it was done.[^s5.12]

Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab was brought with his hands tied to his neck, in a fine robe that he had torn all over so no one would take it from him. When the Prophet ﷺ asked whether Allāh had not given him power over him, Ḥuyayy said he did not blame himself for his enmity, but whoever Allāh forsakes is forsaken. Then he told the people: *"There is no harm in Allāh's command — a decree, a writ, and a slaughter written for the Children of Israel,"* and sat down and was beheaded.[^s5.12] Nabbāsh b. Qays struggled with the man bringing him, who struck him on the nose. The Prophet ﷺ asked why: *"Was the sword not enough?"* He ordered the prisoners treated well, given shade and water until the heat of the day had passed, *"so as not to join for them the heat of the sun and the heat of the sword."*[^s5.12][^s5.13]

When Kaʿb b. Asad was brought, the Prophet ﷺ asked whether Ibn Jawwās's advice had not served them. Kaʿb said it had, and that he would have followed him but for fear the Jews would say he was afraid of the sword; he would die in the religion of the Jews.[^s5.13] Every male who had reached puberty was put to death. ʿAṭiyyah al-Quraẓī later related that he was a boy, found not to have reached puberty, and let go.[^s5.13] Rifāʿah b. Shamwīl, a grown man, was spared at the request of Salmā bint Qays, a maternal aunt of the Prophet ﷺ, and became Muslim.[^s5.13] It went on until the twilight had gone, and the trenches were filled in, all of it in Saʿd's sight.[^s5.13]

Only one woman was killed: Nubātah, a woman of Banū al-Naḍīr married to a man of Qurayẓah. During the siege, at her husband's urging, she had dropped a millstone from the fort of al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā onto Muslims sitting in its shade, and it crushed the head of [[Khallād b. Suwayd]] ﵁. ʿĀʾishah ﵂, with whom she was sitting and laughing on the day of the executions, said she never forgot how calm and cheerful Nubātah was when her name was called, though she knew she would be killed.[^s5.13][^s5.14]

### Thābit b. Qays and al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā

al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā had spared [[Thābit b. Qays|Thābit b. Qays b. Shammās]] ﵁ at the Battle of Buʿāth, cutting his forelock and letting him go. Thābit asked the Prophet ﷺ for his life as a return for the favour and was granted it, and then his family and property as well.[^s5.14] But al-Zubayr, an old man, asked what had become of Kaʿb b. Asad, *"whose face was like a Chinese mirror in which the girls of the tribe could see themselves,"* and of the leading families of Qurayẓah. Told they had been killed, he said there was no good in life after them. He asked Thābit only to see that his wife and children were freed and their property returned — which the Prophet ﷺ granted, except the weapons — and then to send him to join the others.[^s5.14][^s5.15] According to Ibn Isḥāq, Thābit had him beheaded; al-Wāqidī says Thābit refused to kill him himself, and al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām killed him.[^s5.15]

## Rayḥānah

Rayḥānah bint Zayd b. ʿAmr b. Khunāfah, a woman of Banū al-Naḍīr married among Qurayẓah, was chosen by the Prophet ﷺ for himself. At first she refused Islam and would keep only Judaism, which grieved him. He sent Ibn Saʿyah to her, who urged her not to follow her people after what Ḥuyayy had brought on them. Later, hearing footsteps, the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Those are the sandals of Ibn Saʿyah, bringing me news that Rayḥānah has become Muslim,"* and so it was.[^s5.15] al-Ṣāliḥī defers her further history to her own biography.[^s5.15]

## Spoils and captives

The goods were sold, the captives sold, and the palm groves divided into shares. Each horse received two shares and its rider one, and each foot-soldier one; the Prophet ﷺ led three horses but took a share for only one. The Muslims were three thousand, and the shares came to 3,072.[^s5.15] Shares were also given for Khallād b. Suwayd, killed beneath the fort, and for [[Abū Sinān b. Miḥṣan]] ﵁, who had fought with the Muslims and died during the siege.[^s5.15]

The captives numbered a thousand women and children. The Prophet ﷺ took out the fifth before the sale, by lot. From it he freed, gave away and kept servants as he wished, and he put it in the charge of Maḥmiyah b. Jazʾ al-Zubaydī.[^s5.15][^s5.16] Women who had been present at the fighting were given gifts but no share: [[Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]], [[Nusaybah bint Kaʿb|Umm ʿUmārah]], Umm Salīṭ, Umm al-ʿAlāʾ al-Anṣāriyyah, al-Sumayrāʾ bint Qays, Saʿd b. Muʿādh's mother and Kabshah bint Rāfiʿ.[^s5.16]

Some captives were sent to be sold for weapons and horses: to Syria with Saʿd b. ʿUbādah, according to al-Wāqidī, or to Najd with [[Saʿd b. Zayd al-Ashhalī]], according to Ibn Isḥāq. [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]] and [[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]] ﵄ bought a group between them.[^s5.16] The Prophet ﷺ forbade separating a mother from her child, in division or in sale, until the child reached maturity.[^s5.16]

Those killed on the Muslim side were Khallād b. Suwayd and Mundhir b. Muḥammad.[^s5.16]

## Poetry

The Prophet ﷺ told [[Ḥassān b. Thābit]] ﵁ on the day of Qurayẓah to satirise them, *"and Jibrīl is with you."*[^s5.16][^s5.17] Ḥassān's poems on the campaign, given by al-Ṣāliḥī, include an elegy for Saʿd b. Muʿādh praising his judgement on Qurayẓah as agreeing with the judgement of Allāh.[^s5.17][^s5.18]

## Notes from the *tanbīhāt*

According to al-Samʿānī, Qurayẓah was the name of a man whose descendants settled in a strong fortress near Medina; Qurayẓah and al-Naḍīr were brothers, descendants of Hārūn ﵊.[^s5.18]

The length of the siege is disputed. Ibn ʿUqbah gives ten-odd nights and Ibn Saʿd fifteen. Ibn Saʿd also reports twenty-five nights from ʿAlqamah b. Waqqāṣ; Ibn Isḥāq gives the same through Maʿbad b. Kaʿb, and Aḥmad and al-Ṭabarānī from ʿĀʾishah.[^s5.20]

The number of men killed is also disputed:[^s5.20]

- **600:** Ibn Isḥāq, followed by Abū ʿUmar;
- **700:** a *mursal* report of Qatādah;
- **800–900:** those who give the higher figure, according to al-Suhaylī; Ibn Isḥāq also mentions that nine hundred was said;
- **400 fighting men:** a report of Jābir, with a sound chain in al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī and Ibn Ḥibbān.

To reconcile these, it is suggested that the rest were followers rather than fighters.[^s5.20]
