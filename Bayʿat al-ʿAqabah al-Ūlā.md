# Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā (بيعة العقبة الأولى)
*The First Contact with the Anṣār, and the Pledge of Faith at al-ʿAqabah*

> [!NOTE] On the Book's Own Chapter Numbering
> Al-Ṣāliḥī devotes **two separate chapters** to what this vault treats as a single event. His *Bāb
> al-Khāmis* ("Fifth Chapter"), which he himself labels *"Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā,"* narrates the
> Prophet's ﷺ **initial contact** with a group of Khazraj at al-ʿAqabah. His very next chapter, the
> *Bāb al-Sādis*, which he labels *"Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah,"* narrates the **formal pledge of
> faith** taken the following year by twelve men — the event conventionally known, and known throughout
> this vault, as **the First Pledge of al-ʿAqabah**. Both chapters are presented below as a single
> narrative, since they describe one continuous episode; the book's own third stage — the much larger
> pledge that included a commitment to armed defense — is treated separately, as
> **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]]**.

## 1. The First Encounter
Al-Zuhrī, Ibn ʿUqbah, and Ibn Isḥāq relate that, wishing to make His religion manifest, Allāh brought
the Prophet ﷺ into contact with a group of **Khazraj** at al-ʿAqabah, during one of the pilgrimage
seasons in which he regularly presented Islam to the Arab tribes. Asking who they were, and learning
they were allies (*mawālī*) of the Jews of Yathrib, he sat and spoke with them, calling them to Allāh,
presenting Islam, and reciting the Qurʾān. Allāh had prepared them for this moment through their own
Jewish neighbours, who — being People of the Book — had long told them, whenever discord arose between
them, *"A prophet is soon to be sent; his time has drawn near; we shall follow him and destroy you as
ʿĀd and Iram were destroyed."* Hearing the Prophet ﷺ speak, they recognized at once the very figure the
Jews had long threatened them with, and said among themselves, *"Know, by Allāh, that this is the
prophet the Jews have warned you of — do not let them reach him before you!"* They believed him and
accepted what he offered them of Islam, telling him: *"You know the discord and bloodshed between us
[the Aws and Khazraj]; we are eager for what Allāh has sent you with, and hope to strive for your cause
— we advise you..."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:194).

## 2. The Pledge Itself
The following year, **twelve men** of the Anṣār came at the pilgrimage season and pledged themselves to
the Prophet ﷺ on **the pledge of women** (*bayʿat al-nisāʾ*) — that is, **before fighting had been made
obligatory upon them**. Ibn Isḥāq names them: **Asʿad b. Zurārah**, Dhakwān b. ʿAbd Qays al-Zurqī,
**ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit**, al-ʿAbbās b. ʿUbādah b. Naḍlah, Quṭbah b. ʿĀmir b. Ḥadīdah, ʿUqbah b. ʿĀmir b.
Nābī, ʿAwf b. al-Ḥārith b. Rifāʿah, ʿUwaym b. Sāʿidah, Mālik b. al-Tayyihān [Abū al-Haytham], Muʿawwidh
b. al-Ḥārith (ʿAwf's brother), and Yazīd b. Thaʿlabah Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Balawī, their ally
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:197).

**ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit** ﵁ himself transmits the pledge's exact terms:

> «بَايَعْنَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم بَيْعَةَ النِّسَاءِ وَذَلِكَ قَبْلَ أَنْ تُفْتَرَضَ عَلَيْنَا الْحَرْبُ، عَلَى أَلَّا نُشْرِكَ بِاللَّهِ شَيْئًا، وَلَا نَسْرِقَ، وَلَا نَزْنِيَ، وَلَا نَقْتُلَ أَوْلَادَنَا، وَلَا نَأْتِيَ بِبُهْتَانٍ نَفْتَرِيهِ مِنْ بَيْنِ أَيْدِينَا وَأَرْجُلِنَا، وَلَا نَعْصِيَهُ فِي مَعْرُوفٍ»
> *"We pledged allegiance to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ on the pledge of women — this before fighting had
> been made obligatory upon us — that we would not associate anything with Allāh, would not steal,
> would not commit adultery, would not kill our children, would not fabricate slander we invent between
> our hands and feet, and would not disobey him in what is right."*

He continued: *"He said: 'Whoever among you fulfils this, his reward is with Allāh'"* — in another
wording, *"his is Paradise"* — *"and whoever commits any of it and is punished for it in this world,
that is an expiation and a purification for him; and whoever commits any of it and Allāh conceals it,
his matter rests with Allāh — if He wills, He punishes, and if He wills, He forgives. So we pledged to
him on that"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:197).

## 3. The Dispatch of Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr
When the delegation departed, the Prophet ﷺ sent **[[Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr]]** ﵁ with them (*Subul al-Hudā*,
3:197).

## 4. Sold to Islam by a Poem in the Night
Ibn Abī al-Dunyā, al-Kharāʾiṭī, al-Bayhaqī, and Ibn ʿAsākir preserve a striking omen from these months:
Quraysh heard a voice calling out by night atop **Abū Qubays**, *"If the two Saʿds accept Islam,
Muḥammad will awaken in Makkah fearing no opponent's contrariety!"* — and debated the next morning
which "two Saʿds" were meant. A second night, the voice specified: *"O Saʿd, Saʿd of the Aws, be a
helper! And O Saʿd, Saʿd of the Khazraj, be resolute! Answer the caller to guidance, and hope from
Allāh a lofty station in the Gardens of Eternity — for Allāh's reward for the seeker of guidance is
Gardens of Paradise, luxuriant."* Quraysh concluded this meant **Saʿd b. Muʿādh** and **Saʿd b.
ʿUbādah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:198).

## 5. The Conversion of Saʿd b. Muʿādh and Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr
**Asʿad b. Zurārah** brought **Muṣʿab b. ʿUmayr** into the territory of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal and Banū
Ẓafar, where believers gathered around them. **[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** and **[[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]]** —
Asʿad's own maternal cousin and Usayd, then both chiefs of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal and both still
polytheists — heard of the pair's presence and were troubled that they had come to weaken the resolve
of the clan's own weaker members. Saʿd told Usayd: *"Go to these two men who have come to our
dwellings to make fools of our weak, and rebuke them"* — setting in motion the conversation that would
end, within the same encounter, in both chieftains' acceptance of Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:198). See
**[[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]]** and **[[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]]** for the fuller episode.

## 6. What Follows
Through Muṣʿab's mission and the growing circle of Anṣārī converts, Yathrib was prepared to receive
the Prophet ﷺ. The following year brought the far larger commitment of
**[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah]]**, which included a pledge of armed defense.
