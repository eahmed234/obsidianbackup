# al-Naḍr b. Kinānah (النضر بن كنانة)
*Named for the Radiance of His Face — Some Say the True Father of Quraysh*

## 1. Name
His given name was **Qays**; he was called **al-Naḍr** for the *naḍārah* — radiance and beauty — of
his face and person, the same root as *al-naḍr*, the name for red gold (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:284).

## 2. Kunyah, Mother, and Sons
His kunyah was **Abū Yakhlud**. His mother is given as **Barrah bint Murr b. Add b. Ṭābikhah b. Ilyās
b. Muḍar**. He had three sons: **[[Mālik b. al-Naḍr|Mālik]]**, Yakhlud (his kunyah-namesake), and
al-Ṣalt (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:284).

## 3. A Genealogical Complexity the Book Addresses Directly
Al-Suhaylī is cited for a further report: after al-Naḍr's father died, **[[Kinānah b. Khuzaymah|Kinānah]] himself married Barrah**, al-Naḍr's mother, and she bore him a son also named **al-Naḍr b.
Kinānah**. Al-Ṣāliḥī, transmitting al-Suhaylī, addresses the apparent irregularity directly rather than
passing over it:

> *"This was permissible in the Jāhiliyyah, under an earlier revealed law, and was not among the
> forbidden matters they later violated, nor among the enormities they innovated — for it was a
> matter [that occurred] within the direct line of descent."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:284)

## 4. "Whoever Descends Beyond Fihr Is Not of Quraysh"
Because *Quraysh* is sometimes reckoned from al-Naḍr and sometimes from his own descendant
**[[Fihr b. Mālik]]**, see the discussion at **[[Fihr b. Mālik]]** of al-Zuhrī's dictum that identity
as *Quraysh* is properly bounded at Fihr.
