# Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (أبو بكر الصديق) ﵁
*The Prophet's companion in the cave, and his successor*

**Abū Bakr ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUthmān al-Qurashī al-Taymī**, called **al-Ṣiddīq** and **ʿAtīq**, was a Companion of the Prophet [[Muḥammad b. ʻAbdullah|Muḥammad]] ﷺ and his first successor.[^i4.145][^i4.146] He knew him before the mission, was among the very first to believe in him, stayed beside him through the years in Makkah, and was his only companion in the cave during the [[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah|Hijrah]]. He was with him at every campaign until his death, led the pilgrimage in the ninth year, and was told to lead the prayer during his last illness. After the Prophet ﷺ died the Muslims gave him their allegiance and called him *Khalīfat Rasūl Allāh*, the successor of the Messenger of Allāh.[^i4.145][^s11.252][^s11.258] He died in 13 AH, aged sixty-three.[^i4.150][^s11.260]

## Name and lineage

He was ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUthmān b. ʿĀmir b. ʿAmr b. Kaʿb b. Saʿd b. Taym b. Murrah b. Kaʿb b. Luʾayy, of the clan of [[Banū Taym|Taym]] in [[Quraysh]].[^nasab] His father ʿUthmān was known as [[Abū Quḥāfah]]. His mother, Umm al-Khayr Salmā bint Ṣakhr b. ʿĀmir, was the daughter of his father's paternal uncle.[^i4.145]

[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr|ʿĀʾishah]] ﵂ said that the name his family gave him was ʿAbdullāh, but that the name ʿAtīq came to prevail. The sources explain ʿAtīq in several ways. In one report the Prophet ﷺ, seeing him approach, said that whoever wished to look at a man freed (*ʿatīq*) from the Fire should look at Abū Bakr.[^atiq] Others say he was called ʿAtīq for his handsome face, or because he was early in every good, or because nothing in his lineage could be faulted. Another account has it that none of his mother's children had lived, so when he was born she turned with him towards the House and prayed, "O Allāh, this is the one You have freed from death, so grant him to me." Ibn Sīrīn held that ʿAtīq was his actual name, while Ibn Abī Mulaykah said his name was ʿAbdullāh and ʿAtīq only a byname.[^i4.146][^i4.147]

The title al-Ṣiddīq, "the one who affirms the truth", is tied in the sources to the morning after the [[al-Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj|Night Journey]] (see below). [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib|ʿAlī]] ﵁ used to say from the pulpit, more times than his hearer could count, that Allāh had named Abū Bakr *ṣiddīq* on the tongue of His Prophet ﷺ.[^i4.148]

He is described as fair-skinned and thin, with a sparse beard on his cheeks, a lean face and a prominent forehead, and he dyed his hair with henna and *katam*.[^i4.146]

## Before Islam

Abū Bakr was born two years and six months after the Year of the Elephant.[^i4.145] ʿĀʾishah ﵂ recalled the Prophet ﷺ and her father comparing their births in her presence, and the Prophet ﷺ was the elder.[^i4.145] *Subul al-Hudā* puts his birth "two years and some months" after the Prophet's, and says he was born at [[Minā]].[^s11.251]

He grew up in Makkah and left it only to trade. He was a wealthy merchant, one of the leading men of Quraysh before Islam and among those they consulted, and he was well liked.[^s11.251] Ibn Isḥāq describes him as an easy, friendly man whom his people sought out for his knowledge, his experience and his good company; he knew the genealogies of Quraysh, and the good and bad in their past, better than anyone.[^i4.147][^s2.305] Blood-money cases were entrusted to him: when he took on such a debt and asked Quraysh to help with it, they honoured his pledge, which they would not do for another.[^i4.149] ʿĀʾishah ﵂ said that he never composed poetry, before Islam or after it, and that he and [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān|ʿUthmān]] had given up wine even before Islam.[^s11.251]

## Acceptance of Islam

The sources agree that Abū Bakr was among the very first Muslims and differ over the order. Ibn Isḥāq places him after [[Khadījah bint Khuwaylid|Khadījah]], [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib|ʿAlī]] and [[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]. Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī said that Abū Bakr was the first, and Ibn Kathīr calls that the well-known view of the majority of Ahl al-Sunnah. Al-Ṣāliḥī reports a way of reconciling the reports: the first person to accept Islam was Khadījah; the first male was ʿAlī, then a boy who kept his faith hidden; the first adult Arab man to accept Islam and declare it openly was Abū Bakr; and the first freedman was Zayd.[^s2.302][^s2.303]

Ibn Isḥāq relates that Abū Bakr met the Prophet ﷺ and asked whether what Quraysh were saying of him was true. The Prophet ﷺ told him that he was the Messenger of Allāh, called him to worship Allāh alone and recited the Qurʾān to him, and Abū Bakr accepted on the spot. It reached Ibn Isḥāq that the Prophet ﷺ later said that everyone he had called to Islam had shown some hesitation except Abū Bakr, who did not hold back or waver.[^s2.302]

Having declared his Islam, Abū Bakr began to call to it those of his people whom he trusted. Through him, according to Ibn Isḥāq, came [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]], [[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]], [[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]], [[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]] and [[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]] ﵃. When he and Ṭalḥah accepted Islam, Nawfal b. Khuwaylid seized them and tied them together with one rope, and Banū Taym did not protect them; the two were known afterwards as "the two bound together" (*al-qarīnayn*).[^s2.305][^i4.147]

He had forty thousand dirhams when he accepted Islam, and he spent them in the way of Allāh, freeing slaves and supporting the Muslims, so that he reached Medina with five thousand. He freed seven people who were being tortured for their faith: [[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ|Bilāl]], [[ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah]], Zinnīrah, [[al-Nahdiyyah]] and her daughter, a slave-girl of Banū al-Muʾammal, and Umm ʿUbays.[^i4.147][^i4.148]

## The Night Journey

When the Prophet ﷺ told Quraysh of his journey by night to Jerusalem, they challenged him to describe the mosque there, and as he described it Abū Bakr kept saying, "You have spoken the truth. I bear witness that you are the Messenger of Allāh." They asked Abū Bakr whether he really believed that the Prophet ﷺ had gone to Jerusalem and returned in one night. He answered that he believed him in something greater than that: in news from heaven that came to him in a morning or an evening. For this, the account says, he was named al-Ṣiddīq.[^s3.94]

## The Hijrah

Abū Bakr asked the Prophet ﷺ for leave to emigrate to Medina and was told to wait, since the Prophet ﷺ hoped to be given leave himself. He therefore stayed behind to accompany him, and for four months fed two riding camels in readiness. One day at noon, an hour at which he never visited, the Prophet ﷺ came to Abū Bakr's house and told him that he had been permitted to leave. "Your company, O Messenger of Allāh?" Abū Bakr asked, and the Prophet ﷺ said yes. ʿĀʾishah ﵂ said that she had never known anyone weep for joy until she saw her father weep that day. He offered one of the two camels as a gift; the Prophet ﷺ would take it only for its price.[^s3.238][^s3.239]

They hired a guide and arranged for him to bring the camels to the cave of Thawr after three nights. [[Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr|Asmāʾ]] ﵂ tore her waist-wrap to tie up their provisions, and was called "she of the waist-wrap" for it. Abū Bakr took his money with him, five thousand dirhams, and left his family nothing. His father Abū Quḥāfah, who had lost his sight, feared as much, and Asmāʾ calmed him by guiding his hand to a pile of stones she had covered with a cloth.[^s3.239][^s3.240]

A report transmitted by al-Bayhaqī says that on the way Abū Bakr walked now in front of the Prophet ﷺ and now behind him, thinking by turns of an ambush ahead and of pursuit behind, and that at the mouth of the cave he insisted on going in first, so that anything inside would strike him and not the Prophet ﷺ.[^s3.240] They stayed in the cave three days.[^s3.241] During that time Abū Bakr's son ʿAbdullāh went back and forth with news and ʿĀmir b. Fuhayrah with food, but no one was in the cave with the Prophet ﷺ except Abū Bakr.[^i4.148]

Quraysh tracked them to the mountain and stood above the cave. Abū Bakr related:

> «قلت للنبي ﷺ ونحن في الغار: لو أن أحدهم نظر إلى قدمه لأبصرنا تحت قدميه. فقال: ما ظنك باثنين الله ثالثهما»
> *"I said to the Prophet ﷺ while we were in the cave, 'If one of them looked down at his foot he would see us beneath his feet.' He said, 'What do you think of two whose third is Allāh?'"*[^s3.241][^ghar]

The Qurʾān refers to the moment: "the second of two, when they were in the cave, when he said to his companion: Do not grieve; Allāh is with us" (al-Tawbah 9:40). Ibn Ḥajar counts this verse among the greatest of Abū Bakr's distinctions and says there is no dispute that "his companion" is Abū Bakr.[^i4.148]

## With the Prophet ﷺ in Medina

Abū Bakr was present at every campaign the Prophet ﷺ fought.[^i4.145]

At [[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā|Badr]] a shelter ([[al-ʿArīsh|ʿarīsh]]) was built for the Prophet ﷺ on a rise overlooking the field, and Abū Bakr was the only one in it with him.[^s4.30] When the fighting began the Prophet ﷺ returned to the shelter and pleaded with his Lord for the help He had promised,[^s4.36] and Abū Bakr said to him:

> «يا رسول الله بعض مناشدتك ربّك، فإن الله منجز لك ما وعدك»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh, enough of your pleading with your Lord; Allāh will fulfil what He has promised you."*[^s4.36]

When the trench was dug before [[Ghazwat al-Khandaq|al-Khandaq]], no Muslim held back from the work. Abū Bakr and [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb|ʿUmar]] carried the earth away in their own garments, in their haste, because they found no baskets. They were never apart, the account adds, in work, on the march or in camp.[^s4.365]

At [[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah|al-Ḥudaybiyah]], ʿUmar was distressed by the terms of the truce and put his objections to the Prophet ﷺ. He then went to Abū Bakr with the same questions. Abū Bakr gave him the same answers: that he was the Messenger of Allāh, that he did not disobey his Lord, and that Allāh would help him. He told ʿUmar to hold fast to the Prophet's stirrup until he died.[^s5.52]

On the day of the [[Fatḥ Makkah|conquest of Makkah]], when the Prophet ﷺ had entered the mosque, Abū Bakr came leading his father, whose hair had turned white. The Prophet ﷺ asked why he had not left the old man at home so that he might go to him, and Abū Bakr said that it was more fitting for his father to walk to the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ stroked Abū Quḥāfah's chest and invited him to Islam, and he accepted.[^s5.233]

Abū Bakr carried the banner on the [[Ghazwat Tabūk|Tabūk]] expedition.[^i4.145]

In 9 AH the Prophet ﷺ sent him to [[Ḥajjat Abī Bakr al-Ṣiddīq|lead the pilgrimage]]. He left Medina with three hundred men. The Prophet ﷺ sent twenty sacrificial camels with him, and Abū Bakr drove five of his own. ʿAlī ﵁ was sent after him to recite Sūrat Barāʾah to the people and caught up with him on the road. "Commander, or under command?" Abū Bakr asked. "Under command," ʿAlī replied, and they went on together.[^s12.73]

## The Prophet's last illness and death

When the Prophet ﷺ fell gravely ill, he said:[^bukh678]

> «مروا أبا بكر، فليصلّ بالناس»
> *"Tell Abū Bakr to lead the people in prayer."*[^s11.252]

ʿĀʾishah ﵂ objected that her father was a tender-hearted man who would not be able to lead the people if he stood in the Prophet's place, but the Prophet ﷺ repeated the order, and Abū Bakr led the prayer while the Prophet ﷺ was still alive.[^bukh678][^s11.253]

When the Prophet ﷺ died, ʿUmar would not hear it said. Abū Bakr went in to him and confirmed to the people that he had died.[^s11.257] He said to them:

> «من كان يعبد محمدا فإن محمدا قد مات، ومن كان يعبد الله فإن الله حي لا يموت»
> *"Whoever worshipped Muḥammad, Muḥammad has died. Whoever worships Allāh, Allāh is living and does not die."*[^s12.300]

He then recited, "Muḥammad is only a messenger; messengers have passed away before him" (Āl ʿImrān 3:144). It was, one report says, as though the people had not known the verse had been revealed until he recited it that day.[^s12.300]

## Caliphate

When the Anṣār proposed a leader from each side, ʿUmar reminded them that the Prophet ﷺ had ordered Abū Bakr to lead the people in prayer, and asked which of them would be content to step ahead of him. ʿUmar took his hand and the people gave him their pledge.[^s11.254][^s11.258] Someone once addressed him as "Khalīfat Allāh"; he answered that he was the successor of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and was content with that.[^s11.253]

Among his merits as caliph al-Ṣāliḥī counts sending out the [[Sariyyat Usāmah b. Zayd|army of Usāmah]] against the advice of all, because the Prophet ﷺ had ordered it; fighting the [[Ḥurūb al-Riddah|people of the Riddah]] and going out against them in person; and appointing ʿUmar to succeed him.[^s11.262] His caliphate, in Ibn Isḥāq's reckoning, lasted two years, three months and twenty-two days.[^i4.150] It is treated in [[Khilāfat Abī Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]] and [[Saqīfat Banī Sāʿidah]].

## Family

Before Islam Abū Bakr married Qutaylah bint ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, who bore him ʿAbdullāh and [[Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr|Asmāʾ]], and Umm Rūmān bint ʿĀmir, who bore him ʿAbd al-Raḥmān and [[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr|ʿĀʾishah]]. In Islam he married [[Asmāʾ bint ʿUmays]], who had been married to [[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]] before him and who bore him Muḥammad, and Ḥabībah bint [[Khārijah b. Zayd]], whose daughter Umm Kulthūm was born after his death.[^s11.251] His father accepted Islam and narrated from the Prophet ﷺ.[^i4.145]

## Standing with the Prophet ﷺ

[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]] asked the Prophet ﷺ who was dearest to him. "ʿĀʾishah," he said. "And among men?" "Her father."[^bukh3662][^i4.149] The Prophet ﷺ also said that no one had been more generous to him with his company and his wealth than Abū Bakr, and that if he were to take an intimate friend (*khalīl*) other than his Lord, he would take Abū Bakr.[^s11.255]

He was called *al-Awwāh*, the tender-hearted, for his compassion.[^i4.149][^s11.258] Ibn Ḥajar remarks that when Ibn al-Daghinnah, the chief of al-Qārah, restored his protection to Abū Bakr in Makkah, he described him in the same terms Khadījah had used of the Prophet ﷺ at the start of revelation, without any agreement between the two.[^i4.149]

## Death

According to ʿĀʾishah ﵂, his illness began when he bathed on a cold day, Monday the seventh of Jumādā al-Ākhirah. He had a fever for fifteen days and could not go out to the prayer, which he told ʿUmar to lead. He died on the night of Tuesday, with eight nights of Jumādā al-Ākhirah remaining, in 13 AH.[^s11.260] Another account, from al-Zuhrī, says that he and al-Ḥārith b. Kaladah ate a dish that had been poisoned, and that both sickened and died on the same day a year later.[^i4.149]

As he lay dying he asked ʿĀʾishah ﵂ on what day the Prophet ﷺ had died. She said Monday, and he said that he hoped for the same before night fell. He died on the night of Tuesday and was buried before morning.[^s11.261] ʿUmar prayed over him.[^s11.260]

The best-known and most widely held view is that he was sixty-three when he died, though his age is disputed.[^s11.260] The month is disputed as well. *Al-Iṣābah* dates his death to a Monday in Jumādā al-Ūlā of 13 AH; Ibn Ḥajar then observes that the report of "eight nights remaining of Jumādā al-Ākhirah" is the one that fits the length of his caliphate.[^i4.150]

Before he died he told ʿĀʾishah ﵂ that since taking charge of the Muslims' affairs he had taken none of their gold or silver, and that all he held of their property was a slave, a camel and a piece of cloth, which she was to send to ʿUmar. When they reached him ʿUmar wept and said, "May Allāh have mercy on Abū Bakr. He has worn out those who come after him."[^s11.262]
