# Riḥlat al-Ṭāʾif (رحلة الطائف)
*The Prophetic Mission to al-Ṭāʾif and the Supplication of Helplessness*

## 1. The Journey
When Abū Ṭālib died and Quraysh inflicted upon the Prophet ﷺ persecution they had never dared in his
uncle's lifetime, he set out for **al-Ṭāʾif**, seeking support and refuge among **Thaqīf**. Mūsā b.
ʿUqbah and Ibn Isḥāq say he went alone, on foot; Jubayr b. Muṭʿim's report, per Ibn Saʿd, has
**[[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]]** ﵁ accompanying him, in some nights of **Shawwāl of the tenth year** of the
mission, seeking Thaqīf's backing and protection from his own people, hoping they would accept what he
brought from Allāh (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:438).

## 2. Before the Chiefs of Thaqīf
Arriving, he sought out the three brothers who were then Thaqīf's chiefs and nobles — **ʿAbd Yālīl,
Masʿūd, and Ḥabīb, sons of ʿAmr b. ʿUmayr b. ʿAwf** (one of them married to a Qurashī woman of Banū
Jumaḥ, Ṣafiyyah bint Muʿammar, mother of Ṣafwān b. Umayyah). He sat with them and spoke of his mission
and of standing against his own people's opposition. Each answered him with open contempt: one said,
*"Let him go tear up the coverings of the Kaʿbah, if Allāh really sent him!"*; another, *"Could Allāh
find no one but you to send?"*; the third refused to speak to him at all — *"If you are truly a
messenger from Allāh as you claim, you are too great in station for me to answer; and if you are lying
against Allāh, it is not fitting that I speak with you either."* The Prophet ﷺ left them, despairing of
any good from Thaqīf, but asked them to keep the encounter secret, not wishing word to reach his own
people (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:438).

## 3. Ten Days of Rejection, Then Expulsion
He remained in al-Ṭāʾif — ten days, by one report, a month by another — going to every one of Thaqīf's
notables in turn; none answered him. Fearing his effect on their youths, they told him, *"O Muḥammad,
leave our land,"* and incited their fools and slaves to jeer and cry out after him until a crowd had
gathered.

## 4. Stoned Between Two Ranks
Ibn ʿUqbah (Mūsā b. ʿUqbah) records that they lined up **in two ranks** along his path; as the Prophet
ﷺ passed between them, they pelted his feet with stones every time he set them down, until his feet
bled. Sulaymān al-Taymī adds: whenever the stones' pain forced him to sit on the ground, they would
seize him under the arms, stand him up again, and — laughing — resume stoning him as he walked. Ibn
Saʿd records that **Zayd b. Ḥārithah** shielded him with his own body, suffering head wounds himself in
the process. He finally broke free of them, his feet running with blood, and took shelter in the shade
of a vine-trellis inside an orchard, in pain and distress (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:438).

## 5. The Orchard of ʿUtbah and Shaybah, and the Supplication
The orchard belonged to **ʿUtbah and Shaybah, sons of Rabīʿah**, who were themselves inside it. Al-Ṭabarānī
records, through ʿAbdullāh b. Jaʿfar, that once settled in the shade the Prophet ﷺ prayed two rakʿahs
and then supplicated:

> «اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَشْكُو إِلَيْكَ ضَعْفَ قُوَّتِي، وَقِلَّةَ حِيلَتِي، وَهَوَانِي عَلَى النَّاسِ، يَا أَرْحَمَ الرَّاحِمِينَ، أَنْتَ رَبُّ الْمُسْتَضْعَفِينَ وَأَنْتَ رَبِّي، إِلَى مَنْ تَكِلُنِي؟ إِلَى بَعِيدٍ يَتَجَهَّمُنِي، أَوْ إِلَى عَدُوٍّ مَلَّكْتَهُ أَمْرِي؟ إِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ بِكَ عَلَيَّ غَضَبٌ فَلَا أُبَالِي، وَلَكِنَّ عَافِيَتَكَ هِيَ أَوْسَعُ لِي، أَعُوذُ بِنُورِ وَجْهِكَ الَّذِي أَشْرَقَتْ لَهُ الظُّلُمَاتُ، وَصَلُحَ عَلَيْهِ أَمْرُ الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ، مِنْ أَنْ تُنْزِلَ بِي غَضَبَكَ أَوْ تُحِلَّ عَلَيَّ سَخَطَكَ، لَكَ الْعُتْبَى حَتَّى تَرْضَى، وَلَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِكَ»
> *"O Allāh, unto You I complain of the weakness of my strength, the paucity of my resources, and my
> insignificance before the people. O Most Merciful of the merciful, You are the Lord of the
> oppressed, and You are my Lord — to whom will You entrust me? To a distant stranger who will greet
> me with a scowl, or to an enemy You have given power over my affairs? If You are not angry with me,
> I do not care, though Your pardon is more spacious for me. I seek refuge in the light of Your Face,
> by which the darknesses are illuminated and the affairs of this world and the next are set right,
> from Your anger descending upon me or Your wrath alighting upon me. Yours is the right to be
> displeased until You are pleased, and there is no power nor strength except by You."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:439)

## 6. ʿAddās
Moved with pity at what they had witnessed, ʿUtbah and Shaybah sent their slave **ʿAddās** with a
cluster of grapes. As the Prophet ﷺ took the food he said, *"Bismillāh,"* before eating. ʿAddās,
struck, remarked that this was not how the people of that land spoke. Asked his origin, he said he
was a Christian of **Nineveh**. The Prophet ﷺ said, *"From the town of the righteous man, Yūnus b.
Mattā?"* — and ʿAddās, astonished, asked how an unlettered man among an unlettered people could know
of Yūnus, when scarcely ten people in Nineveh itself knew of him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:439).

## 7. "The Hardest Day I Ever Faced"
ʿĀʾishah ﵂ once asked the Prophet ﷺ whether any day had been harder upon him than Uḥud. He answered:
*"I met from your people what I met, and the worst of it was the day of al-ʿAqabah, when I offered
myself to Ibn ʿAbd Yālīl b. ʿAbd Kulāl and he did not respond to what I asked. I went off, distressed,
and did not come to myself until I was at Qarn al-Thaʿālib."* Lifting his head, he saw a cloud casting
its shade over him, and within it **Jibrīl** ﵊, who told him Allāh had heard his people's rejection and
sent the **Angel of the Mountains** to obey whatever he commanded concerning them. The angel greeted
him and offered: *"If you wish, I will bring the two mountains [of Makkah] together over them."* The
Prophet ﷺ answered:

> «بَلْ أَرْجُو أَنْ يُخْرِجَ اللَّهُ مِنْ أَصْلَابِهِمْ مَنْ يَعْبُدُ اللَّهَ وَحْدَهُ لَا يُشْرِكُ بِهِ شَيْئًا»
> *"Rather, I hope that Allāh will bring forth from their loins those who will worship Allāh alone,
> associating nothing with Him."*
> (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:440)

A further, mursal narration via ʿIkrimah has the Angel of the Mountains instead offer either to crash
the mountains upon them or to swallow them into the earth, with the same reply from the Prophet ﷺ —
who was told in response, *"You are as your Lord named you: Raʾūf, Raḥīm [Gentle, Merciful]"*
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:440).

## 8. Return to Makkah Under Protection
Al-Amawī and Ibn Hishām record that after Thaqīf's refusal the Prophet ﷺ stayed some days at
**Nakhlah**, seeking a man of standing to grant him protection (*jiwār*) to re-enter Makkah, since
Abū Ṭālib was no longer alive to shield him. **al-Muṭʿim b. ʿAdī** agreed. The Prophet ﷺ entered
Makkah and went straight to the Kaʿbah to perform ṭawāf; al-Muṭʿim came out armed with his sons — six
or seven of them — and stood beside him, their sword-belts slung, through the whole of his circuit.
**Abū Sufyān** approached al-Muṭʿim and asked whether he was granting protection or had himself
followed the Prophet's ﷺ religion; al-Muṭʿim answered, *"Rather, protection"* — and Abū Sufyān
conceded the pledge would be honoured. Because of this precedent, when the captives of Badr were
later brought before him, the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Had al-Muṭʿim b. ʿAdī been alive and interceded with
me for these vile men, I would have released them for his sake"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:441).

## 9. The Jinn at Nakhlah
On the same return journey, halting by night at **Nakhlah** to pray, the Prophet ﷺ was overheard by a
company of jinn — seven or nine, of the jinn of Naṣībīn (or, per another wording, of Nineveh) — who
listened to his recitation of the Qurʾān, believed, and returned to warn and summon their own people,
occasioning **Sūrat al-Jinn**'s account of them (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:443–444). This episode belongs to
its own chapter in the book, *al-Bāb al-Thānī wal-Thalāthūn fī Islām al-Jinn* (2:443).

## 10. What Follows
Having lost both the shield of Abū Ṭālib's protection and the hope of a refuge at al-Ṭāʾif, the
Prophet ﷺ was granted the consolation of **[[al-Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj]]**, before eventually finding, at
**[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā]]**, the support of Yathrib that Thaqīf had refused him.
