# Ilyās b. Muḍar (إلياس بن مضر)
*Reformer Who Restored the Sons of Ismāʿīl ﵊ to Their Fathers' Way*

## 1. Name
The book notes his true name was **Ilyās**, though it is reported by some that his given name was
**Ḥabīb**, with the kunyah **Abū ʿAmr** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:289). Several derivations are weighed —
from *alis*, "brave, unyielding in flight," or from *al-yaʾs* denoting fortitude — before the book
settles that Ilyās is simply his known name.

## 2. Restorer of the Fathers' Way
On the authority of **Ibn al-Zubayr**: *"when Ilyās came of age, he took issue with the sons of
Ismāʿīl over what they had altered of their fathers' practice and way, and his excellence over them
became plain, and his judgment gathered them, and they were content with him, so he restored them to
the practice of their fathers"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:289). Ibn Diḥyah adds that he was his father's
executor (*waṣī*) and a man of striking beauty. The book adds that it was related the Prophet ﷺ could
hear the *talbiyah* (the pilgrim's invocation) echoing in his loins.

## 3. Prophetic Praise
Al-Suhaylī transmits:

> «لَا تَسُبُّوا إِلْيَاسَ فَإِنَّهُ كَانَ مُؤْمِنًا»
> *"Do not curse Ilyās, for he was a believer."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:289)

The Arabs, the book adds, revered him as they revered Luqmān and men of comparable wisdom.

## 4. Wife and Sons: the Naming of Khindif
His wife was **Laylā bint Ḥulwān b. al-Ḥāf**. The book gives the celebrated account of how she and
her four sons received their names, on a journey when their camels bolted at the sight of a rabbit
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:288):

- **[[Mudrikah b. Ilyās|Mudrikah]]** — his real name ʿAmr (the view of al-Kalbī, al-Balādhurī, Abū
  ʿUbayd, and Ibn Durayd, with al-Raḍī al-Shāṭibī even claiming consensus for it) — chased the rabbit
  and **caught it** (*adrakahā*), hence *Mudrikah*.
- His brother cooked it (*ṭabakhahā*), and was named **Ṭābikhah**.
- A third brother shrank back from the chase (*inqamaʿa*), and was named **Qumʿah**.
- Their mother Laylā, hurrying after them with quick, close steps (*al-khandafah*), was asked by
  Ilyās, *"where are you rushing to (ayna takhandifīna)?"* — and so she became known as **Khindif**, a
  name that gave its own branch to the wider genealogy of the Arabs.

An alternative version from **Abū Muḥammad al-Baṭalyawsī** has ʿĀmir killing the rabbit while ʿAmr
cooked it and said, *"you caught (adrakta) what we sought, and I have cooked (ṭabakhnā)"* — inverting
which brother received which name (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:288).
