# Umm Ḥabībah (رملة بنت أبي سفيان)
*Umm al-Muʾminīn — Mother of the Believers, Emigrant of Abyssinia, and Daughter of Abū Sufyān*

## 1. Lineage and Early Life

Ramlah bint Abī Sufyān Ṣakhr b. Ḥarb b. Umayyah b. ʿAbd Shams b. ʿAbd Manāf al-Qurashiyyah al-Umawiyyah, universally known by her agnomen **Umm Ḥabībah** (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140). Her mother was Ṣafiyyah bint Abī al-ʿĀṣ b. Umayyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:193). 

She embraced Islam in Makkah in the earliest days of the Daʿwah alongside her first husband, ʿUbaydullāh b. Jaḥsh al-Asadī, despite the fierce opposition of her father [[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]] ﵁, who was then the paramount chieftain of Quraysh and leader of the polytheist opposition (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140).

## 2. Migration to Abyssinia and Severe Trials

Umm Ḥabībah and her husband joined the second migration across the Red Sea to Abyssinia (*[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]*), seeking sanctuary under the righteous Negus, [[al-Najāshī (Aṣḥamah)]] ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:193). In Abyssinia, she gave birth to her daughter Ḥabībah, from whom she took her kunya (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140).

While in Abyssinia, her husband ʿUbaydullāh fell ill and subsequently died, leaving Umm Ḥabībah stranded as a lonely widow in an alien land, cut off from her kin who were waging war against the Prophet ﷺ in Makkah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196).

## 3. Marriage to the Prophet ﷺ (7 AH)

To honor her steadfast devotion and shield her from persecution, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ sent a formal proposal of marriage to Abyssinia in 7 AH via [[ʿAmr b. Umayyah al-Ḍamrī]] ﵁ to the Negus (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196). 

When the Negus's maidservant Abrahah arrived to convey the joyful proposal, Umm Ḥabībah was overwhelmed with gratitude and gifted Abrahah her silver anklets and rings (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140–141). 

The marriage contract was solemnized in the royal court of Axum:
- [[Khālid b. Saʿīd b. al-ʿĀṣ]] ﵁ acted as her guardian (*walī*) on behalf of Banū Umayyah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:141; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196).
- [[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]] ﵁ and the Muslim emigrants attended the wedding assembly (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:195).
- The Negus personally provided her bridal dowry (*ṣadāq*) of four hundred dinars on behalf of the Prophet ﷺ and hosted a feast for all the emigrants (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:141; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:195).
- The Negus then dispatched her honorably to Medina accompanied by Shuraḥbīl b. Ḥasanah ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196).

When news of the marriage reached Makkah, her father Abū Sufyān exclaimed in grudging admiration:
> «هو الفحل لا يقدع أنفه»
> *"He is the noble stallion whose nose cannot be struck back!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:141).

Scholars link this marriage to the revelation of the Qurʾānic verse:
> {عَسَى اللَّهُ أَن يَجْعَلَ بَيْنَكُمْ وَبَيْنَ الَّذِينَ عَادَيْتُم مِّنْهُم مَّوَدَّةً}
> *"Perhaps Allah will put, between you and those to whom you have been enemies among them, affection."* (Qurʾān 60:7) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196).

## 4. Folding the Bed Against Her Polytheist Father

Prior to [[Fatḥ Makkah]] in 8 AH, Abū Sufyān came secretly to [[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah|Medina]] seeking to renew and extend the Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196). He entered the chamber of his daughter Umm Ḥabībah.

When Abū Sufyān moved to sit upon the mattress of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, Umm Ḥabībah swiftly folded it up and removed it from beneath him (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142). 

Astonished, Abū Sufyān asked:
> «يا بنية، أرغبت بهذا الفراش عني أم بي عنه؟»
> *"O my little daughter, did you think this bed was too good for me, or that I was too good for it?"*

Umm Ḥabībah replied fearlessly:
> «بل هو فراش رسول الله ﷺ وأنت امرؤ نجس مشرك»
> *"Rather, it is the bed of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and you are an impure polytheist!"*

Abū Sufyān retreated in defeat, saying: *"By Allah, after leaving me, evil has afflicted you!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196).

## 5. Death and Burial

Umm Ḥabībah narrated numerous traditions recorded in the canonical collections. Before her passing, she sent for [[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]] ﵂ and [[Umm Salamah]] ﵂, seeking mutual pardon for any natural domestic rivalries that occur between co-wives, and both mothers of the believers embraced and prayed for her (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142).

She passed away in Medina in 44 AH during the caliphate of her brother [[Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān]] ﵁ and was laid to rest in [[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]] (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196).
