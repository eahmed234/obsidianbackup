# Nizār b. Maʿadd (نزار بن معد)
*Named, on One Account, for the Light of Prophethood Seen at His Birth*

## 1. The Name
Al-Ṣāliḥī records competing derivations. **Abū al-Faraj al-Umawī** says it is from *al-tanazzur*, "he
was the sole one of his age." **Al-Suhaylī** gives a different account tying the name directly to the
Prophet ﷺ:

> *"His father, when he was born to him and looked upon the light between his eyes — the light of
> prophethood that was transmitted through the loins — rejoiced in him greatly, and slaughtered and
> fed a great deal, and said: **'this is a small (nazr) [gift], little, for the sake of this
> newborn.'** So he was named Nizār for that."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:292)

A third account, from **al-Māwardī**'s *Aʿlām al-Nubuwwah*, says his name was originally **Khaldān**,
and that the king of Persia — seeing that he was thin of body — called him *"nizār"*, which in the
Persian tongue means *"O emaciated one"*, and the name overtook him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:292).

## 2. Kunyah, Mother, and Sons
His kunyah is given as **Abū Iyād** or **Abū Rabīʿah**. His mother was **Muʿānah bint Jawsham**
(*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:292). His four sons — **[[Muḍar b. Nizār|Muḍar]]**, Rabīʿah, Iyād, and Anmār —
are the four brothers of the famous tracking legend and the inheritance judgment of al-Afʿā
al-Jurhumī: see **[[Muḍar b. Nizār]]**.
