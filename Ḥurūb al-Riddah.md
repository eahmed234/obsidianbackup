# Ḥurūb al-Riddah (حروب الردة)
*The Wars of Apostasy — The Steadfastness of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq, the Epic of the Garden of Death, and the Salvation of the Ummah*

> [!NOTE] Scholarly Provenance & Primary Source
> This master entry is derived strictly from the biographical and historical documentations of *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* by al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (Volume 1, p. 414 [biography of al-Barāʾ b. Mālik]; Vol. 2, pp. 18–19 [Ḥabīb b. Zayd]; Vol. 1, p. 184; Vol. 3, p. 364), cross-referenced with *Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād* (6:250, 10:115, 12:72–80).

---

## 1. Context & The Crisis of Succession

Following the passing of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (**[[Wafāt al-Nabī]]**) on the twelfth of Rabīʿ al-Awwal in the year eleven AH, and the pledge of consensus at the Saqīfah (**[[Saqīfat Banī Sāʿidah]]**), an unprecedented existential storm engulfed the young Muslim state (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414; *Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250).

Across the Arabian Peninsula, tribal loyalties resurfaced:
1. **Total Apostates & False Prophets**: Tribes rallied behind impostors claiming revelation—**Musaylimah al-Kadhdhāb** ☠ among Banū Ḥanīfah in al-Yamāmah, **Ṭulayḥah b. Khuwaylid al-Asadī** among Banū Asad and Ghaṭafān, **Sajāḥ bint al-Ḥārith** among Banū Tamīm, and the remnants of **al-Aswad al-ʿAnsī** in Yemen (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414; 2:18).
2. **Withholders of Zakāt (*Māniʿū al-Zakāh*)**: Bedouin tribes that affirmed the Prophetic mission and performed the prayer, but refused to pay the obligatory alms (*Zakāh*) to the central authority in Medina, arguing that their obligation ceased with the Prophet's death (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:115).

In Medina itself, fear mounted. Several senior Companions—including **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁—initially counseled conciliation, suggesting that Abū Bakr refrain from fighting those who pronounced the *Shahādah* until the state's military power solidified (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:446).

---

## 2. The Unshakeable Stance of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq ﵁

**[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ displayed monumental resolve, uttering his immortal declaration that preserved the integrity of Islamic Law forever:

> «وَاللَّهِ لَأُقَاتِلَنَّ مَنْ فَرَّقَ بَيْنَ الصَّلَاةِ وَالزَّكَاةِ! فَإِنَّ الزَّكَاةَ حَقُّ الْمَالِ! وَاللَّهِ لَوْ مَنَعُونِي عَنَاقًا كَانُوا يُؤَدُّونَهَا إِلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَآلِهِ وَسَلَّمَ لَقَاتَلْتُهُمْ عَلَى مَنْعِهَا!»
> *"By Allāh, I shall surely fight whoever separates prayer from zakāt! For verily, zakāt is the divine right upon wealth! By Allāh, if they withhold from me even a female baby goat (ʿanāq) that they used to render unto the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, I will fight them over its withholding!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:446; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).

Seeing his unwavering clarity, ʿUmar ﵁ remarked: "By Allāh, no sooner did I see that Allāh had expanded the chest of Abū Bakr for combat than I recognized that it was the absolute Truth!" (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:446).

---

## 3. The Grand Strategy & The Eleven Corps

Despite the rebellion surrounding Medina, Abū Bakr insisted on executing the Prophet's final order, dispatching **[[Sariyyat Usāmah b. Zayd]]** to the Byzantine frontier (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:250). 

When Usāmah returned victorious after forty or seventy days, having secured the northern borders, Abū Bakr assembled the grand army at **Dhū al-Qaṣṣah** (*Dhū al-Qaṣṣah*) and organized eleven operational army corps, tying eleven banners (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414):
1. **[[Khālid b. al-Walīd]]** ﵁: Dispatched against Ṭulayḥah al-Asadī at Buzākhah, then to Mālik b. Nuwayrah at al-Buṭāḥ, and subsequently to Musaylimah at al-Yamāmah.
2. **[[ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl]]** ﵁: Dispatched toward al-Yamāmah, then onward to Oman and Mahrah.
3. **[[Shuraḥbīl b. Ḥasanah]]** ﵁: Dispatched to reinforce ʿIkrimah in al-Yamāmah.
4. **al-Muhājir b. Abī Umayyah** ﵁: Dispatched to Yemen and Hadramawt against the loyalists of al-Aswad al-ʿAnsī.
5. **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁: Dispatched toward Quḍāʿah and the northern borderlands.
6. **Khālid b. Saʿīd b. al-ʿĀṣ** ﵁: Dispatched to the Syrian desert frontiers.
7. **Ḥudhayfah b. Miḥṣan** ﵁: Dispatched to Dabā and Oman.
8. **ʿArfajah b. Harthamah** ﵁: Dispatched to Mahrah.
9. **al-Ṭulayq b. ʿAmr** ﵁: Dispatched to the central desert tribes.
10. **Suwayd b. Muqarrin** ﵁: Dispatched to Tihāmah.
11. **al-ʿAlāʾ b. al-Ḥaḍramī** ﵁: Dispatched to Bahrain against the rebellion of Rabīʿah.

---

## 4. The Campaigns of Buzākhah & al-Buṭāḥ

Khālid b. al-Walīd ﵁ marched directly upon **Buzākhah** (*Buzākhah*), where Ṭulayḥah b. Khuwaylid had massed the confederates of Asad, Ghaṭafān, and Ṭayyiʾ (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414). Through diplomatic engagement led by ʿAdī b. Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī ﵁, the entirety of Ṭayyiʾ defected back to the Muslim fold. 

Khālid charged the confederate lines at Buzākhah; when Ṭulayḥah's false prophecies collapsed in battle, his ally ʿUyaynah b. Ḥiṣn abandoned the field, and Ṭulayḥah fled toward Syria (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:115). Ṭulayḥah later genuinely embraced Islam, made pilgrimage, and fought heroically as a fierce mujāhid at al-Qādisiyyah and Nihāwand (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:232).

Khālid then subdued Banū Tamīm at al-Buṭāḥ, restoring order throughout central Arabia (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:364).

---

## 5. The Climax at al-Yamāmah: The Garden of Death (*Ḥadīqat al-Mawt*)

The fiercest and most perilous confrontation occurred at **al-Yamāmah** against **Musaylimah al-Kadhdhāb** ☠ and forty thousand fierce fanatical warriors of **[[Banū Ḥanīfah]]** (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414; 2:18).

Musaylimah had previously tortured and butchered the Prophet's envoy, the young Companion **[[Ḥabīb b. Zayd]]** ﵁, severing his limbs piece by piece as Ḥabīb steadfastly refused to recognize Musaylimah's claim (*al-Iṣābah*, 2:18–19).

### The Battle of ʿAqrabāʾ
At the plain of ʿAqrabāʾ, the initial clash was so ferocious that the Muslim lines were pushed back to their encampment (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414). Recognizing the danger, Khālid b. al-Walīd reorganized the army strictly along tribal lines: Muhājirūn under their own standard, Anṣār under their own standard, and desert contingents separately, so that each group's steadfastness could be distinguished (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414).

The venerable standard-bearers rose to heights of sublime martyrdom:
- **[[Sālim Mawlā Abī Ḥudhayfah]]** ﵁ took the standard of the Muhājirūn. When his men asked: "Do you fear we may be attacked from your quarter?", he replied: "What an evil bearer of the Qurʾān I would be if you were attacked from my quarter!" He fought until both his right hand and left hand were severed, hugging the banner to his chest with his stumps until martyred (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:554).
- **[[Zayd b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ (elder brother of ʿUmar) shouted: "O people! Bite down upon your molars, strike your enemy, and march forward! By Allāh, I shall not speak a word until Allāh defeats them or I meet Him!" He fought until he felled Musaylimah's commander and achieved martyrdom (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:579).
- **[[Thābit b. Qays]]** ﵁, commander of the Anṣār, dug a trench for his legs up to his knees, enveloped himself in funeral shroud (*ḥanūṭ*), and held his ground firmly until martyred (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:208).

### The Heroism of al-Barāʾ b. Mālik at the Garden
The routed pagan army retreated inside a vast fortified walled citadel known as the Garden of al-Raḥmān (*al-Ḥadīqah*), barring its iron gates and showering the Muslims with arrows (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414).

Seeing the deadlock, the peerless warrior **[[al-Barāʾ b. Mālik]]** ﵁ (brother of Anas b. Mālik ﵁) cried out:

> «يَا مَعْشَرَ الْمُسْلِمِينَ! أَلْقُونِي عَلَيْهِمْ فِي الْحَدِيقَةِ!»
> *"O assembly of Muslims! Cast me over the wall into the garden upon them!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414).

His companions placed him upon a shield, raised him upon the points of their spears, and hurled him over the high battlements into the midst of thousands of enemy combatants (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414). Single-handedly, al-Barāʾ fought through the enemy ranks, sustaining over eighty wounds from swords and arrows, reached the inner gate, threw it open, and enabled the Muslim army to flood inside (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414).

Within that enclosure—forever commemorated as **Ḥadīqat al-Mawt** (*The Garden of Death*)—the final battle was waged (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414). **[[Waḥshī b. Ḥarb]]** ﵁ hurled his famous javelin at Musaylimah, striking him through his torso, and **Abū Dujānah** ﵁ (or ʿAbdullāh b. Zayd) struck him down with his sword, putting an end to the arch-impostor (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414; 3:364).

---

## 6. Enduring Consequences & The Compilation of the Qurʾān

The Wars of Apostasy concluded with the total victory of Islam and the re-establishment of the Caliphate's sovereignty over every valley and mountain of Arabia (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414).

However, the immense toll of martyrs at al-Yamāmah—over one thousand two hundred Muslims, among them hundreds of memorizers of the Revelation (*al-Qurrāʾ*)—sparked urgent strategic concern (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414). 

**[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ approached Caliph Abū Bakr and urged the systematic compilation of the Holy Qurʾān into a single written codex (**[[Jamʿ al-Qurʾān]]**), warning that the loss of memorizers on battlefields risked the loss of portions of the text (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:446). Abū Bakr commissioned the Prophet's chief scribe, **[[Zayd b. Thābit]]** ﵁, to gather the parchment, palm-stalks, stones, and chests of memorizers, producing the definitive master copy of the Qurʾān that was safeguarded in Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:561).

With Arabia unified, secure, and purified of apostasy, Abū Bakr immediately mobilized the veteran armies of the Riddah northward, initiating the great liberation and conquest of the Sasanian Persian and Byzantine Roman empires (*al-Iṣābah*, 1:414; 4:446).
