# al-Marwah (المروة)
*The White Quartz Crest, Final Terminus of the Sacred Saʿy, and Station of Relief and Release*

## 1. Topography & Geological Character
Al-Marwah is a pale stone knoll and promontory emerging from the foot of Mount Quʿayqiʿān on the northeastern boundary of **[[al-Masjid al-Ḥarām]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:150). Lexicographically, *al-marwah* denotes white, bright, lustrous quartz stone (*al-ḥajar al-abyaḍ al-barrāq*), distinguishing its geologic character from the darker basaltic rock of **[[al-Ṣafā]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:150).

It stands directly opposite al-Ṣafā across the ancient valley course of Baṭn al-Masīl. Together, the two mounts constitute the immutable boundary limits of the ritual course (*al-Masʿā*), consecrated by divine decree: ﴿إِنَّ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةَ مِن شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ﴾ (al-Baqarah 2:158; *Subul al-Hudā*, 8:465).

## 2. Hājar and the Miraculous Deliverance
In sacred history, al-Marwah was the seventh and final station of Hājar's agonized search for water for her dying infant Ismāʿīl ﵊ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:150). After running between the two hills, on her seventh ascent upon al-Marwah, she heard a mysterious voice (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:150). Calling out into the stillness: *«قد أسمعت إن كان عندك غواث!»* (*"You have made me hear! Have you any relief?"*), she beheld the Angel Jibrīl ﵊ descending at the head of Ismāʿīl (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:150).

With his heel (or wing), the angel struck the parched desert floor, causing the subterranean fountain of Zamzam to burst forth in abundance (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:150). Hājar rushed down from al-Marwah, cupped the water with her hands to preserve it, and gave suck to her infant, where the angel comforted her:
> «لَا تَخَافِي الضَّيْعَةَ، فَإِنَّ هَا هُنَا بَيْتَ اللَّهِ يَبْنِيهِ هَذَا الْغُلَامُ وَأَبُوهُ، وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُضِيعُ أَهْلَهُ»
> *"Fear not destruction, for here is the House of Allāh which shall be built by this boy and his father, and indeed Allāh never neglects His folk!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 1:150–151).

## 3. The Rites of Saʿy and the Command of Taḥallul
In Islamic ritual practice, al-Marwah marks the obligatory conclusion of each odd lap (the 1st, 3rd, 5th, and 7th laps) of the Saʿy, with the entire seven-lap traversal culminating permanently at al-Marwah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:465–466).

During **[[Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ]]**, whenever the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ reached al-Marwah, he ascended its crest, faced the Kaʿbah, declared the greatness of Allāh, affirmed His absolute divine unity, and supplicated with the same grand formulas he pronounced upon al-Ṣafā (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:466). 

Upon completing his seventh lap atop al-Marwah, the Prophet ﷺ turned to the vast assembly of Companions and issued the definitive command of deconsecration (*taḥallul*): everyone who had not brought sacrificial livestock (*hady*) with them was commanded to exit the sacred state of ihram completely—cutting their hair (*taqṣīr*), changing into ordinary clothes, and resuming normal marital life until the day of Tarwiyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:466). He proclaimed:
> «لَوِ اسْتَقْبَلْتُ مِنْ أَمْرِي مَا اسْتَدْبَرْتُ لَمَا سُقْتُ الْهَدْيَ، وَلَجَعَلْتُهَا عُمْرَةً»
> *"Had I known before what I know now, I would not have brought the sacrificial animals, and I would have made it an ʿUmrah!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:466).

Hearing this, the Companions fulfilled the rites and trimmed their hair at al-Marwah, establishing the eternal jurisprudence of the pilgrimage rites (*Subul al-Hudā*, 8:466).
