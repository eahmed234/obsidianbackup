# Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Thāniyah (بيعة العقبة الثانية)
*The Pledge of War — Yathrib's Commitment to Defend the Prophet ﷺ*

> [!NOTE] The Book's Own Title for This Chapter
> Al-Ṣāliḥī's own chapter heading for this material is *al-Bāb al-Thāmin fī Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah
> al-Thālithah* — "the Third Pledge of al-ʿAqabah" — since he numbers the initial contact with the
> Khazraj as the first and the twelve-man *bayʿat al-nisāʾ* as the second (see
> **[[Bayʿat al-ʿAqabah al-Ūlā]]**). This vault follows the conventional naming, under which this
> pledge — the one that included a commitment to armed defense — is **the Second Pledge**.

## 1. Ten Years of Seeking a Protector
**Jābir b. ʿAbdullāh** ﵄ narrates: *"The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ spent ten years going to people in their
dwellings — at Mijannah, at ʿUkāẓ, and in the pilgrimage seasons at Minā — saying, 'Who will shelter
me? Who will support me, so that I may deliver my Lord's messages, and his is Paradise?' Yet he never
found anyone to shelter or support him. Even a man coming from Muḍar or Yemen would be met by his own
people and kin, who would say, 'Beware of this young man of Quraysh, lest he tempt you' — pointing him
out with their fingers as he walked among their tents — until Allāh sent us to him from Yathrib. A man
of us would come, believe in him, and have him teach him the Qurʾān, then return to his own family, who
would accept Islam through his conversion — until there was no house left in Yathrib without a company
of Muslims openly professing Islam"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:201).

## 2. Seventy Men Set Out
Jābir continues: *"Then Allāh sent us [again], and we took counsel and gathered, saying: 'How long
shall we leave the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ to wander the mountains of Makkah in fear?' So seventy of us
set out to him, arriving at the pilgrimage season, and arranged to meet him at the ravine of
al-ʿAqabah. We gathered there one and two at a time until we were all present, and said, 'O Messenger
of Allāh, on what do we pledge to you?'"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:201).

## 3. The Terms of the Pledge
The Prophet ﷺ answered:

> «تُبَايِعُونِي عَلَى السَّمْعِ وَالطَّاعَةِ فِي النَّشَاطِ وَالْكَسَلِ، وَعَلَى النَّفَقَةِ فِي الْعُسْرِ وَالْيُسْرِ، وَعَلَى الْأَمْرِ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَالنَّهْيِ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ، وَعَلَى أَنْ تَقُولُوا فِي اللَّهِ لَا تَأْخُذُكُمْ لَوْمَةُ لَائِمٍ، وَعَلَى أَنْ تَنْصُرُونِي إِذَا قَدِمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ يَثْرِبَ، تَمْنَعُونِّي مِمَّا تَمْنَعُونَ مِنْهُ أَنْفُسَكُمْ وَأَزْوَاجَكُمْ وَأَبْنَاءَكُمْ، وَلَكُمُ الْجَنَّةُ»
> *"You pledge to me to hear and obey, in energy and in reluctance; to spend in hardship and in ease;
> to enjoin right and forbid wrong; to speak for Allāh's sake, fearing no reproach of any reproacher;
> and to support me when I come to you at Yathrib, defending me as you would defend your own selves,
> your wives, and your sons — and yours is Paradise."*

Jābir recalls: *"We rose to pledge to him, and **Asʿad b. Zurārah** took his hand — he was the youngest
of the seventy save myself — and said: 'Wait, O people of Yathrib. We have not driven our mounts here
except knowing full well that he is the Messenger of Allāh, and that leading him out today means
severing ties with all the Arabs...'"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:201).

## 4. Al-Barāʾ b. Maʿrūr's Change of Qiblah
The chapter preserves, via Kaʿb b. Mālik's own account, an episode from the journey to Makkah: **al-Barāʾ
b. Maʿrūr**, newly guided to Islam, resolved not to leave the Kaʿbah behind him and prayed facing it
rather than toward al-Shām, against his companions' practice, until doubt entered his heart. Reaching
Makkah, he insisted on consulting the Prophet ﷺ directly — locating him, on a stranger's directions,
seated beside his uncle **al-ʿAbbās** in the mosque. Asked whether he recognized the two visitors,
al-ʿAbbās named **al-Barāʾ b. Maʿrūr, chief of his people**, and **Kaʿb b. Mālik**. Al-Barāʾ explained
his private change of qiblah; the Prophet ﷺ answered, *"You were upon a qiblah, had you but persevered
in it,"* and al-Barāʾ returned to the Prophet's ﷺ own direction of prayer for the remainder of the
journey (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:202).

## 5. Assembled by Night
That night, the delegation slipped from their tents *"as sand-grouse slip away,"* gathering at the
ravine by al-ʿAqabah: **seventy-three men**, with **two women** — **Nusaybah bint Kaʿb (Umm ʿAmmārah)**,
of Banū Māzin b. al-Najjār, and **Asmāʾ bint ʿAmr b. ʿAdī b. Nābī (Umm Manīʿ)**, of Banū Salamah. The
Prophet ﷺ came to them accompanied by **al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib**, who — though still upon his
people's religion — wished to attend and secure his nephew's position. Al-ʿAbbās spoke first, telling
the Khazraj plainly that Muḥammad ﷺ already had the protection of his own people in Makkah, and that if
they were not certain they could fulfil what they were committing to and defend him against all who
opposed him, they should abandon the matter now rather than later (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:203).

## 6. "The Blood Is the Blood, the Ruin Is the Ruin"
When the Anṣār affirmed their readiness — *"By the One who sent you with the truth, we shall defend
you as we defend our own garments"* — **Abū al-Haytham b. al-Tayyihān** interrupted to ask a pointed
question: *"O Messenger of Allāh, there are cords between us and certain men [the Jews], and we are
about to cut them — might it be that, if Allāh grants you victory, you would return to your own people
and leave us?"* The Prophet ﷺ smiled and answered:

> «بَلِ الدَّمُ الدَّمُ، وَالْهَدْمُ الْهَدْمُ، أَنَا مِنْكُمْ وَأَنْتُمْ مِنِّي، أُحَارِبُ مَنْ حَارَبْتُمْ، وَأُسَالِمُ مَنْ سَالَمْتُمْ»
> *"No — my blood is your blood, and my protection is your protection [lit. 'the blood is the blood,
> the ruin is the ruin']. I am of you, and you are of me; I will fight whoever you fight, and make
> peace with whoever you make peace."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:204)

## 7. The Twelve Nuqabāʾ
The Prophet ﷺ then said: *"Bring me twelve leaders (nuqabāʾ) from among you, that they may be
responsible for their people."* Kaʿb b. Mālik names them: from **Khazraj** — **Abū Umāmah Asʿad b.
Zurārah** (Banū al-Najjār), Rāfiʿ b. Mālik b. al-ʿAjlān (Banū Zurayq), **Saʿd b. al-Rabīʿ**, **ʿAbdullāh
b. Rawāḥah** (Banū al-Ḥārith b. al-Khazraj), **Saʿd b. ʿUbādah**, al-Mundhir b. ʿAmr (Banū Sāʿidah),
**al-Barāʾ b. Maʿrūr**, ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. Ḥarām, and **ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit**; from **Aws** — **Usayd
b. Ḥuḍayr** (Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal), Rifāʿah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir, and Saʿd b. Khaythamah (Banū ʿAmr b.
ʿAwf). The Prophet ﷺ told the nuqabāʾ: *"You are guarantors for your people, just as the disciples
were guarantors for ʿĪsā b. Maryam"* — al-Ṣāliḥī notes this appointment of twelve followed the pattern
of Allāh's word concerning the people of Mūsā, *"and We raised up from among them twelve leaders"*
(Qurʾān 5:12) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:204–205).

## 8. Al-ʿAbbās b. ʿUbādah's Warning, and Devout Resolve
**Al-ʿAbbās b. ʿUbādah b. Naḍlah** asked the gathered Khazraj whether they understood what they were
pledging to — that fulfilling it meant war against "the red and the black" of mankind, and that if any
feared losing wealth or lives at its cost, they should withdraw now rather than shame themselves and
the Prophet ﷺ before Allāh later. They affirmed they wished exactly this, asking only what reward it
carried; told *"Paradise,"* they said, *"Extend your hand"* — and pledged. Ibn Isḥāq records a
suggestion that this speech was meant only to delay the pledge that night, in hope that ʿAbdullāh b.
Ubayy b. Salūl might yet be brought to join them, strengthening their position — *"Allāh knows best
which of these was the case"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:205–206).

Kaʿb b. Mālik reports that **al-Barāʾ b. Maʿrūr** was the first to place his hand in the Prophet's ﷺ own
in pledge — a claim disputed by Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal, who held it was **Abū al-Haytham b. al-Tayyihān**.

## 9. The Devil's Cry
As the pledge concluded, a voice — Satan himself, from the top of the pass, in the loudest cry Kaʿb had
ever heard — called out: *"O people of the encampments, do you not know of Mudhammam and the apostates
with him, gathered against you for war?"* The Prophet ﷺ answered, *"This is Azabb al-ʿAqabah [a name
for Iblīs], the son of Azyab — hear me, O enemy of Allāh: I shall attend to you yet."* He then told the
delegation, *"Disperse to your tents."* When **al-ʿAbbās b. ʿUbādah** offered, *"By the One who sent
you with truth, if you wish it we will fall upon the people of Minā with our swords tomorrow,"* the
Prophet ﷺ answered, *"We were not commanded to do that — return to your tents"* (*Subul al-Hudā*,
3:206).

## 10. Saʿd b. ʿUbādah Seized
Quraysh, learning what had passed, went after the departing delegation and caught **Saʿd b. ʿUbādah**
and al-Mundhir b. ʿAmr at Adhākhir — both nuqabāʾ. Al-Mundhir escaped; Saʿd was bound, dragged into
Makkah by his own hair, and beaten, until men who held prior pacts of protection with Qurashī notables
— including **Jubayr b. Muṭʿim** and **al-Ḥārith b. Ḥarb b. Umayyah** — intervened and secured his
release on the strength of those old ties (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:207).

## 11. What Follows
With Yathrib now committed to shelter and defend him, the Prophet ﷺ began permitting his companions to
emigrate, opening the way to **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**.
