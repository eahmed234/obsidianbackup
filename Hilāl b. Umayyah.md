# Hilāl b. Umayyah (هلال بن أمية) ﵁
*Hero of Badr, Participant of Liʿān, and One of the Three Penitent Companions in Sūrat al-Tawbah*

## 1. Lineage & Clan
- **Ancestry**: Hilāl b. Umayyah b. ʿĀmir b. Qays b. ʿAbd al-Aʿlam b. ʿĀmir b. Kaʿb b. Wāqif (whose name was Mālik) b. Imriʾ al-Qays b. Mālik b. al-Aws al-Anṣārī al-Wāqifī (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:428, no. 8998).
- **Clan**: Banū Wāqif of the Aws tribe of Medina.

## 2. Early Milestones: Badr & The Ruling of Liʿān
- Accepted Islam early among the Anṣār, demonstrating martial steadfastness at **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]** (2 AH) and **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]** (3 AH) (*al-Iṣābah*, 6:428; *Subul al-Hudā*, 4:45).
- **Occasion of the Ruling of Mutual Imprecation (*al-Liʿān*)**: Hilāl brought a complaint regarding his wife to the Prophet ﷺ, upon which Allāh revealed the verses of *al-Liʿān* in **[[Sūrat al-Nūr]]** (24:6–9), establishing the permanent legal procedure across Islamic jurisprudence (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*; *al-Iṣābah*, 6:428).

## 3. The Trial of Tabūk & Divine Forgiveness (9 AH)
- In Rajab 9 AH, Hilāl lingered in his orchards during the intense heat of the campaign of **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]** and missed the march (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:473).
- When the Prophet ﷺ returned to Medina, Hilāl came forward alongside **[[Murārah b. al-Rabīʿ]]** ﵁ and **[[Kaʿb b. Mālik]]** ﵁ and confessed with total sincerity that he possessed neither illness nor physical excuse for holding back (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:473).
- The Prophet ﷺ imposed a total social boycott upon them. An elderly man with failing eyesight, Hilāl confined himself to his home, weeping so incessantly that his family feared he would lose his vision entirely (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:475). His wife approached the Prophet ﷺ requesting permission to nurse and serve him, which the Prophet ﷺ granted provided they refrained from marital intimacy (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:476).
- After fifty days of intense spiritual purification, Allāh revealed **[[Sūrat al-Tawbah]]** (9:118) forgiving Hilāl and his two companions, transforming their sorrow into eternal divine rejoicing (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:477).
