# Umm Ḥabībah (أم حبيبة) ﵂
*Ramlah bint Abī Sufyān al-Umawiyyah — Umm al-Muʾminīn (Mother of the Believers), The Emigrant of Two Red Sea Voyages, The Defender of the Prophetic Sanctity*

## 1. Lineage, Ancestry & Early Precedence
### Patrilineal Descent & Clan
She is **Ramlah bint Abī Sufyān (Ṣakhr) b. Ḥarb b. Umayyah b. ʿAbd Shams b. ʿAbd Manāf al-Qurashiyyah al-Umawiyyah** (kunya: **Umm Ḥabībah**) (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:193). 

- **Father**: **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]**, chieftain of Quraysh and leader of the Makkan confederacy prior to his conversion at the Opening of Makkah.
- **Mother**: **Ṣafiyyah bint Abī al-ʿĀṣ b. Umayyah**, paternal aunt of Caliph **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:193).
- **Full Brother**: Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān, scribe of the Prophetic revelation and later founder of the Umayyad caliphate (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:197).

### Early Faith & Migration to Abyssinia
Embracing Islam in the earliest days in Makkah despite her father being the paramount adversary of the new faith, she was wed to **ʿUbaydullāh b. Jaḥsh al-Asadī** (brother of **[[Zaynab bint Jaḥsh]]** and maternal cousin of the Prophet ﷺ). Facing intense persecution, the couple joined the second migration across the Red Sea to **[[Abyssinia]]** (**[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah]]**), where she gave birth to her daughter **Ḥabībah**, from whom she derived her lasting kunya (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:193).

---

## 2. Divine Deliverance & Royal Marriage in Abyssinia
While in Abyssinia, trial struck when her husband ʿUbaydullāh b. Jaḥsh apostatized, while Ramlah remained rock-firm upon Islam (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:140; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:193). ʿUbaydullāh died shortly thereafter in Abyssinia, leaving her completely isolated in a foreign land with her infant daughter (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:193).

### The Prophetic Proposal via the Negus
Learning of her steadfast devotion and solitary plight, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ dispatched ʿAmr b. Umayyah al-Ḍamrī with an official letter to the Christian monarch **al-Najāshī** (the Negus), requesting him to contract the marriage of Umm Ḥabībah to the Prophet ﷺ on his behalf (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:141; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:193).

- The Negus dispatched his royal maidservant **Abrahah** to convey the glad tidings. Overjoyed, Umm Ḥabībah gifted Abrahah her silver bangles and rings (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:195).
- She appointed **Khālid b. Saʿīd b. al-ʿĀṣ** ﵁ (her closest Umawī kinsman in Abyssinia) as her legal guardian (*walī*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:141; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:195).
- In the royal palace in Axum, the Negus convened the Muslim emigrants—presided over by **[[Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁—and delivered an eloquent sermon, personally bestowing a royal dowry of **four hundred gold dinars** on behalf of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:141; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:195).
- The Negus then hosted an elaborate royal wedding feast for all the Muslims present (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:141).
- In 7 AH, the Negus outfitted two sea-vessels to transport Umm Ḥabībah and the remaining Abyssinian emigrants back to Arabia, accompanied by Shuraḥbīl b. Ḥasanah, arriving in Medina while the Prophet ﷺ was concluding the Khaybar campaign (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:197).

Hearing of the marriage while still an enemy in Makkah, her father Abū Sufyān could not withhold admiration, famously declaring:
> «ذَاكَ الْفَحْلُ لَا يُقْرَعُ أَنْفُهُ!»
> *"That stallion's nose cannot be struck [meaning: he is a noble peer who cannot be refused]!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:194).

## 3. Devotion to the Prophetic Sanctity
The marriage of Umm Ḥabībah served as a divine bridge soften the enmity of Quraysh. Exegetes record that Sūrat al-Mumtaḥanah (60:7: *«Perhaps Allāh will put between you and those to whom you have been enemies among them affection»*) was revealed concerning this sacred union (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196).

### Rolling Up the Prophet's Bedding before Her Father
Prior to **[[Fatḥ Makkah]]** (8 AH), when Quraysh breached the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyah, **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** hastened to Medina attempting to renew the armistice. Rebuffed by the Prophet ﷺ, he entered the apartment of his daughter Umm Ḥabībah.

As Abū Sufyān moved to sit upon the mattress of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, Umm Ḥabībah promptly folded the bedding away from him. Perplexed and hurt, Abū Sufyān said: *“O my daughter! Do you think this mattress is too good for me, or that I am too good for it?”*

Umm Ḥabībah replied with fearless monotheistic resolve:
> «بَلْ هُوَ فِرَاشُ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ، وَأَنْتَ امْرُؤٌ نَجِسٌ مُشْرِكٌ، فَلَمْ أُحِبَّ أَنْ تَجْلِسَ عَلَى فِرَاشِهِ!»
> *"Rather, this is the pure bedding of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and you are an unclean polytheist! I would not suffer you to sit upon his bedding!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:196).
Stunned by her uncompromising spiritual loyalty, Abū Sufyān exclaimed: *“By Allāh, evil has afflicted you after me!”* and departed (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142).

## 4. Later Life, Reconciliation & Demise
Umm Ḥabībah was known for profound piety, scrupulous adherence to the Sunnah, and noble efforts to maintain familial reconciliation:
- Following the Prophet's passing, she maintained rigorous devotion in Medina.
- During the tragic siege of her maternal cousin Caliph **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁, she attempted to bring waterskins to the besieged house on her mule, but rebel sentries blocked her and severed the mule's bridle (*Subul al-Hudā*, 10:148).
- Prior to her death, she sent for **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ and **[[Umm Salamah]]** ﵂, saying: *“There often occurred between us what normally transpires between co-wives; may Allāh forgive me and you for anything that occurred.”* ʿĀʾishah replied: *“May Allāh forgive you all of it and excuse you!”* Umm Ḥabībah beamed with joy, saying: *“You have brought me joy, may Allāh bring you eternal joy!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142).

Umm Ḥabībah passed away in Medina in 44 AH during the caliphate of her brother Muʿāwiyah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:142). She was laid to rest in **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]**. She transmitted sixty-five authentic traditions from the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:197).
