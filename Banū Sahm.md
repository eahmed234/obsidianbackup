# Banū Sahm (بنو سهم)
*Sahm b. ʿAmr of Quraysh — Clan of Statesmanship, Juridical Advocacy, and Diplomatic Leadership*

## 1. Lineage and Clan Structure
**Banū Sahm** is an aristocratic and influential clan (*baṭn*) of the tribe of **[[Quraysh]]** in Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:311).

Their lineage ascends: **Sahm b. ʿAmr b. Huṣayṣ b. Kaʿb b. Luʾayy b. Ghālib b. Fihr** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:311). 

Through their common progenitor **Huṣayṣ b. Kaʿb**, Banū Sahm are the closest genealogical brother-clan to **Banū Jumaḥ** (Jumaḥ b. ʿAmr b. Huṣayṣ) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:311).

## 2. Pre-Islamic Status & Political Role
In pre-Islamic Makkah, Banū Sahm held prestigious civic authority among Quraysh:
- **Government of Disputes & Treasury**: They were traditionally entrusted with arbitrating civil debts and overseeing the government of shrines and adjudication (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154).
- **The Alliance of Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl**: When a merchant from Zabīd was defrauded of his goods by al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil al-Sahmī, his public lament from Mount Abū Qubays galvanized the noble clans of Quraysh to convene at the house of ʿAbdullāh b. Judʿān and establish the celebrated justice compact **[[Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl]]**, attended and praised by the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:154).

## 3. The Sahmī Clan in the Makkan Period

### Early Converts & The Migration to Abyssinia
Despite the intense opposition of senior clan elders, several noble members of Banū Sahm embraced Islam early during the secret Daʿwah and joined the migration to Abyssinia (*al-Hijrah ilā al-Ḥabashah*):
- **Khunays b. Ḥudhāfah al-Sahmī** ﵁: Early convert who migrated to Abyssinia and later to Medina, marrying Ḥafṣah bint ʿUmar prior to his martyrdom from wounds sustained at Badr or Uḥud (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:309; 11:185).
- **ʿAbdullāh b. Ḥudhāfah al-Sahmī** ﵁: Celebrated envoy dispatched by the Prophet ﷺ carrying the prophetic missive to Chosroes II (*Kisrā*) of Persia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:406).
- **al-Ḥārith b. al-Ḥārith b. Qays al-Sahmī** and his brothers **Maʿmar b. al-Ḥārith** and **Bishr b. al-Ḥārith** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:398, 406).

### Prominent Antagonists
Before the triumph of Islam, two leading elders of Banū Sahm were among the fiercest adversaries of the Daʿwah:
- **al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil al-Sahmī**: Father of ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ; one of the mocking elders (*al-mustahziʾūn*) and one of the four leaders who attempted to negotiate a syncretic compromise at the Kaʿbah that was rejected in **[[Sūrat al-Kāfirūn]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:425, 462). When ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb publicly embraced Islam and angry mobs surrounded his house to kill him, al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil intervened in his elegant silk-trimmed robe, extended his tribal protection (*jiwār*), and dispersed the mob (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:374).
- **al-Ḥārith b. Qays al-Sahmī** (Ibn al-Ghayṭalah): Another prominent mocker whose hostility was broken through divine retribution (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:461).

## 4. Prominent Figures in the Islamic Era
- **[[ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ]]** ﵁: Chieftain, master diplomat, military commander of the expedition of Dhāt al-Salāsil, conqueror of Egypt, and governor under the Rightly Guided Caliphs (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:167, 198).
- **ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ** ﵄: One of the greatest scholars and prolific hadith compilers among the Companions, author of the authoritative written codex *al-Ṣaḥīfah al-Ṣādiqah*.
