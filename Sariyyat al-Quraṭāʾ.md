# Sariyyat al-Quraṭāʾ (سرية القرطاء)
*The thirty-horseman nocturnal strike against Banū Bakr b. Kilāb and the capture and conversion of Thumāmah b. Uthāl*

## 1. Strategic Context & Mandate

Following the Confederate siege of [[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]] and the subsequent expedition against [[Ghazwat Banī Qurayẓah]], the Prophet ﷺ initiated aggressive outer patrols into Najd to deter hostile nomadic coalitions of Qays ʿAylān (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). 

At the head of 59 months from the Hijrah—specifically on the 10th of al-Muḥarram, 6 AH—the Prophet ﷺ dispatched **[[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]]** ﵁ in command of thirty horsemen (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). Among the distinguished warriors accompanying him were:
- **[[ʿAbbād b. Bishr]]** ﵁
- **Salamah b. Salāmah b. Waqsh** ﵁
- **al-Ḥārith b. Khuzaymah** ﵁

The target of the detachment was **al-Quraṭāʾ**, clans descended from Banū Bakr b. Kilāb of Qays ʿAylān, who encamped at al-Bakarāt in the vicinity of Dariyyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71, 73). The Prophet ﷺ gave Muḥammad b. Maslamah strict tactical orders: to march covertly by night, conceal his force by day (*yakmunu al-nahār*), and execute a swift, surprise dawn attack (*yashunnu al-ghārah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).

## 2. Reconnaissance & Initial Skirmish at al-Sharabbah

Advancing along the northern desert routes, Muḥammad b. Maslamah reached **al-Sharabbah**, where he encountered a traveling party (*ẓuʿun*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71, 73). He dispatched an advance scout who returned reporting that they belonged to the hostile tribe of **Banū Muḥārib**. 

The Muslim company remained concealed while the men of Muḥārib made camp and led their herds to pasture (*rawwaḥū māshiyatahum*). Once they had halted their camels around the watering troughs (*ʿaṭanū*), Muḥammad b. Maslamah launched a sudden assault, slaying several combatants while the rest fled into the wilderness (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). Muḥammad strictly restrained his men from pursuing the fleeing nomads or harming the traveling party of women and families (*wa-lam yataʿarraḍ lil-ẓuʿun*), seizing only their pasturing camels and sheep (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).

## 3. The Strike on Banū Bakr b. Kilāb

Muḥammad b. Maslamah then pressed forward toward the settlements of Banū Bakr at **al-Bakarāt** near Dariyyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). Approaching their encampment, he sent forward **ʿĀʾidh b. Busr** to perform close reconnaissance on the watering place (*al-ḥāḍir*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71, 74). 

Upon confirmation of their disposition, Muḥammad led his thirty horsemen in a decisive dawn charge (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). Ten warriors of Banū Bakr were slain, while the rest scattered into the rugged countryside. The detachment rounded up the vast herds of camels and sheep (*al-naʿam wal-shāʾ*) and commenced an immediate strategic withdrawal toward Medina, anticipating pursuit (*khāfa al-ṭalab*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).

Driving the livestock relentlessly through Dariyyah and al-ʿAddāsah, the sheep slowed their pace upon reaching **al-Rabadhah** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71, 74). Muḥammad b. Maslamah stationed a guard detachment over the sheep to shepherd them carefully, while he hastened forward with the camels to ensure the safety of the main prize, re-entering Medina on the final night of al-Muḥarram after a total absence of nineteen nights (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71). 

Upon their arrival, the Prophet ﷺ set aside the fifth (*khumus*) for the public treasury and divided the remaining four-fifths among the expeditionary force, valuing each slaughtered camel as equivalent to ten sheep (*fa-ʿadalū al-jazūra bi-ʿashrin min al-ghanam*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).

## 4. The Interception of Thumāmah b. Uthāl

During the patrol operations across the Najd roads in that same month of al-Muḥarram 6 AH, the Muslim cavalry apprehended a noble traveler traveling without escort (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:525). Unaware of his identity, they brought him into Medina and bound him securely to one of the pillars of the Prophetic Mosque (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).

When the Prophet ﷺ came into the Mosque, he immediately recognized him and said to the Companions:
> «أتدرون من أخذتم؟ هذا ثمامة بن أثال الحنفي، أحسنوا إسارة»
> *"Do you know whom you have taken? This is Thumāmah b. Uthāl al-Ḥanafī; treat his captivity with kindness!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).

The Prophet ﷺ ordered food collected from his household and instructed that his own milch-camel (*liqḥah*) be brought morning and evening to provide milk for Thumāmah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).

## 5. Magnanimity & Conversion

Over three successive days, the Prophet ﷺ approached the pillar and inquired:
> «ما عندك يا ثمامة؟»
> *"What do you have to say, O Thumāmah?"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

Thumāmah replied with proud tribal resolve:
> «عندي خير يا محمد، إن تقتل تقتل ذا دم، وإن تنعم تنعم على شاكر، وإن ترد الفداء فسل منه ما شئت»
> *"I have good news, O Muḥammad. If you kill, you kill one whose blood will be claimed; if you show favor, you show favor to one who is grateful; and if you desire ransom, ask whatever you wish!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

On the third day, observing his character and dignity, the Prophet ﷺ commanded:
> «أطلقوا ثمامة»
> *"Release Thumāmah!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

Thumāmah walked to a cluster of date palms near the mosque, performed ritual purification (*ghusl*), and returned to enter the presence of the Prophet ﷺ, professing the Shahādah:
> «أشهد ألا إله إلا اللَّه وأشهد أن محمدا عبده ورسوله... واللَّه ما كان على الأرض وجه أبغض إليّ من وجهك فقد أصبح وجهك أحبّ الوجوه كلها إليّ»
> *"I bear witness that there is no god but Allāh, and I bear witness that Muḥammad is His servant and Messenger... By Allāh, there was no face on earth more hated to me than your face, but now your face has become the most beloved of all faces to me!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:525).

## 6. The First Public Talbiyah & The Grain Embargo of al-Yamāmah

After embracing Islam, **[[Thumāmah b. Uthāl]]** ﵁ sought permission to perform ʿUmrah in Makkah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72). Entering the valley of Makkah, he raised his voice with the Talbiyah (*labbayk allāhumma labbayk*), becoming the very first person in history to enter Makkah openly chanting the Islamic Talbiyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

Quraysh seized him in outrage, threatening execution, until a townsman warned them:
> «دعوه فإنكم تحتاجون إلى اليمامة لطعامكم فخلّوه»
> *"Leave him alone! For you are utterly dependent on al-Yamāmah for your food!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

Thumāmah proclaimed to the assembled Quraysh:
> «وواللَّه لا تصل إليكم من اليمامة حبّة حنطة حتى يأذن فيها رسول اللَّه صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم»
> *"By Allāh, not a single grain of wheat from al-Yamāmah shall reach you until the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ gives permission!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:526).

Returning to his homeland of al-Yamāmah—the breadbasket of the Ḥijāz—Thumāmah instituted a complete economic blockade against Quraysh, reducing Makkah to such severe famine that the inhabitants were forced to eat dried blood mixed with camel hair (*al-ʿilhiz*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72). In desperation, Quraysh dispatched Abū Sufyān to appeal to the Prophet ﷺ on grounds of kinship (*ṣilat al-raḥim*), prompting the Prophet ﷺ to write to Thumāmah to permit the grain convoys to resume (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72; *al-Iṣābah*, 1:526), an occasion associated with the revelation of Qurʾān 23:76 (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).

## 7. Strategic Significance

1. **Inner-Desert Power Projection**: The expedition demonstrated that even after the exhausting siege of the Trench, the Muslim state maintained the cavalry reach to strike hostile tribes deep within Najd across nineteen days and hundreds of miles (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).
2. **Exemplary Conduct in Warfare**: Muḥammad b. Maslamah strictly adhered to Prophetic directives by refraining from harming non-combatant women (*al-ẓuʿun*) and restricting engagements to active hostiles (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:71).
3. **The Pacification of al-Yamāmah**: The capture and gracious release of Thumāmah transformed the foremost chieftain of Banū Ḥanīfah into an ardent defender of Islam, securing the agricultural lifelines of central Arabia and exerting decisive economic pressure on Quraysh ahead of [[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 6:72).
