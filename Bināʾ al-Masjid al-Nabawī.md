# Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī (بناء المسجد النبوي الشريف)
*The Construction of the Grand Sanctuary, the Chambers of the Pure Consorts, and the Establishment of the Prophetic Capital*

## 1. Strategic Context & The Halting of al-Qaṣwāʾ
Upon the arrival of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ in [[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah]] following the [[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah|Hijrah]], the clans of the [[al-Anṣār|Anṣār]] vied with one another to host him, each seizing the reins of his camel, *al-Qaṣwāʾ*. The Prophet ﷺ instructed them:
> «خَلُّوا سَبِيلَهَا فَإِنَّهَا مَأْمُورَةٌ»
> *"Let her go her way, for she is under divine command."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335).

The she-camel advanced through the quarters of Medina until she knelt at a date-drying yard (*mirbad*) belonging to two orphan youths, Sahl and Suhayl, sons of Rāfiʿ b. Abī ʿAmr b. ʿĀʾidh of the clan of [[Banū al-Najjār]] (the maternal kinsmen of the Prophet's grandfather [[ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]). The youths were under the guardianship (*ḥijr*) of the chieftain [[Asʿad b. Zurārah]] ﵁ (and under the joint hospitality of [[Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī]] ﵁ and Muʿādh b. ʿAfrāʾ). Even before the Hijrah, Asʿad b. Zurārah had designated this open *mirbad* as a musallā where he led the early believers in the five daily prayers and the Friday assembly (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335).

The she-camel knelt, rose briefly, turned, and then knelt again at the same spot, flattening her neck against the earth. The Prophet ﷺ dismounted and declared:
> «هَذَا الْمَنْزِلُ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ»
> *"This is the dwelling place, if Allāh wills."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335), reciting Qurʾān 23:29: *«رَّبِّ أَنزِلْنِي مُنزَلًا مُّبَارَكًا وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ الْمُنزِلِينَ»* (*"My Lord, land me at a blessed landing place, and You are the best of hosts"*).

## 2. Acquisition & Preparation of the Ground
The Prophet ﷺ summoned the notables of [[Banū al-Najjār]] and said:
> «يَا بَنِي النَّجَّارِ، ثَامِنُونِي بِحَائِطِكُمْ هَذَا»
> *"O Banū al-Najjār! Name me a price for this plot of yours."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335).

The clansmen replied that they sought its reward only from Allāh. The two orphan youths, Sahl and Suhayl, likewise stepped forward and insisted: *«بل نهبه لك يا رسول الله»* (*"Rather, we gift it to you, O Messenger of Allāh!"*). However, the Prophet ﷺ steadfastly refused to take the land without fair compensation (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335). He purchased the *mirbad* for ten golden dinars, which were advanced from the personal wealth of **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:344).

Before construction could begin, the terrain required extensive reclamation:
1. **Graves of Polytheists**: Ancient pagan graves (*qubūr jāhiliyyah*) on the site were excavated, and their skeletal remains were solemnly removed and reinterred elsewhere (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335).
2. **Flora**: Thickets of wild date-palms and thorny boxthorn (*gharqad*) were cut down, and the timber was preserved for structural pillars and firewood (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335).
3. **Depressions & Water**: Stagnant rainwater in the hollows was drained away, and ruins (*kharib*) and uneven ground were completely leveled (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:335).

## 3. Construction, Materials & Dimensions
The Prophet ﷺ personally laid the foundation and participated as an active laborer alongside the [[al-Muhājirūn|Muhājirūn]] and [[al-Anṣār|Anṣār]]:
- **Foundations**: Excavated down to bedrock or the water table, then raised approximately three cubits (*adhruʿ*) using rough-hewn basalt stones (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).
- **Walls**: Erected using unbaked mud-adobe bricks (*al-labin*) fashioned from clay collected at Baqīʿ al-Khabkhabah. Initially, the walls were laid one brick thick (*al-sumayṭ*), and later reinforced with interlocking courses (*al-dhakar wal-unthā*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338). The height was a man's standing stature plus a little (*qāmah wa-shayʾ*), approximately seven cubits.
- **Dimensions**: The initial structure formed a near-square enclosure: 60 to 70 cubits along the sides, or approximately 100 cubits by 100 cubits in later measurement (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).
- **Pillars & Roof**: Palm trunks (*judhūʿ al-nakhl*) served as load-bearing columns. The roof was thatched with palm fronds (*jarīd*). When rains began to seep through (*wakafa*), the roof was sealed with packed mud mortar (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338). The center of the mosque remained an open courtyard (*raḥbah*).
- **Original Qiblah**: Oriented northward facing **Bayt al-Maqdis** (Jerusalem), where it remained for the first sixteen to seventeen months of the Madinan period until the [[Taḥwīl al-Qiblah|command to turn to Makkah]] was revealed (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).

### The Three Gates
1. **Bāb al-Qiblah**: Located in the southern wall (the rear wall before the Qiblah shifted). When the Qiblah was turned south towards the Kaʿbah, this gate was sealed, and an opening was made in the northern wall (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).
2. **Bāb ʿĀtikah (Bāb al-Raḥmah)**: Located on the western wall, named for its proximity to the dwelling of ʿĀtikah bint ʿAbdillāh b. Yazīd b. Muʿāwiyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:342).
3. **Bāb ʿUthmān (Bāb Jibrīl / Bāb al-Nabī)**: Located on the eastern wall opposite the chamber of [[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]] ﵂, through which the Angel Jibrīl ﵊ entered during critical moments, including after the siege of [[Ghazwat al-Khandaq|al-Khandaq]] (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:342).

## 4. Notable Incidents & Prophetic Portents During Construction

### The Laying of Foundation Stones & Sequence of Khilāfah
When the foundation was established, the Prophet ﷺ carried a large stone and set it in place. He then instructed [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]] ﵁ to place his stone adjacent to it, followed by [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]] ﵁, and then [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]] ﵁. When asked regarding this sequence, the Prophet ﷺ remarked:
> «هَؤُلَاءِ وُلَاةُ الْأَمْرِ مِنْ بَعْدِي»
> *"These are the custodians of authority after me."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:337).

### The Martyrdom Prophecy of ʿAmmār b. Yāsir
The Companions carried adobe bricks one by one, but **[[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]]** ﵁ hoisted two bricks at a time—one for himself and one on behalf of the Prophet ﷺ. Observing him laboring to exhaustion with his hair matted with dust, the Prophet ﷺ tenderly wiped the soil from ʿAmmār's face and said:
> «يَا بْنَ سُمَيَّةَ، لِلنَّاسِ أَجْرٌ وَلَكَ أَجْرَانِ! وَآخِرُ زَادِكَ شَرْبَةٌ مِنْ لَبَنٍ، وَتَقْتُلُكَ الْفِئَةُ الْبَاغِيَةُ، تَدْعُوهُمْ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ وَيَدْعُونَكَ إِلَى النَّارِ»
> *"O son of [[Sumayyah bint Khayyāṭ|Sumayyah]]! The people have a single reward, but you have two! Your last worldly sustenance shall be a draught of milk, and the rebellious faction (*al-fiʾah al-bāghiyah*) shall slay you; you will be calling them to Paradise, while they call you to the Fire!"*
> ʿAmmār replied: *«أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الْفِتَنِ»* (*"I seek refuge in Allāh from tribulations!"*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:336–337; *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, no. 447).

During the construction, **[[ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn]]** ﵁ was meticulous about keeping his attire clean, stepping aside to shake dust from his garments. Seeing this, **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ improvised verses:
> «لَا يَسْتَوِي مَنْ يَعْمُرُ الْمَسَاجِدَا ... يَدْأَبُ فِيهَا قَائِمًا وَقَاعِدَا ... وَمَنْ يُرَى عَنِ الْغُبَارِ حَائِدَا»
> *"They are not equal: he who builds the mosques, laboring tirelessly standing and sitting, and he who is seen swerving to shun the dust!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:336).

### Prophetic Chants & The Rajaz of Devotion
The Prophet ﷺ labored until dust coated his chest, joining the Companions in rhythmic chants of perseverance:
> «اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّ الْأَجْرَ أَجْرُ الْآخِرَهْ ... فَارْحَمِ الْأَنْصَارَ وَالْمُهَاجِرَهْ»
> *"O Allāh! True reward is only the reward of the Hereafter; so have mercy upon the Anṣār and the Muhājirah!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:336).

The Companions responded with the famous couplet:
> «لَئِنْ قَعَدْنَا وَالنَّبِيُّ يَعْمَلُ ... لَذَاكَ مِنَّا الْعَمَلُ الْمُضَلَّلُ»
> *"If we were to sit while the Prophet labors, that indeed would be our most misguided deed!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:336).

### The Craftsmanship of Ṭalq b. ʿAlī
Among the builders was **Ṭalq b. ʿAlī al-Ḥanafī** ﵁ of Yamāmah, who demonstrated extraordinary skill in tempering mud mortar with his spade. The Prophet ﷺ observed his deft handling of the clay and instructed:
> «قَرِّبُوا الْيَمَامِيَّ مِنَ الطِّينِ، فَإِنَّهُ أَحْسَنُكُمْ لَهُ مَسًّا، وَأَشَدُّكُمْ لَهُ ضَبْطًا»
> *"Bring the Yamāmī near to the clay, for he possesses the finest touch for it and the firmest grip upon it."* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:339).

## 5. The Pure Chambers (*Ḥujarāt Ummahāt al-Muʾminīn*)
Adjoining the eastern and northern walls of the Mosque, modest residential apartments (*ḥujarāt*) were erected for the Prophet's household (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:348–350):
- Initially, two chambers were built: one for **[[Sawdah bint Zamʿah]]** ﵂ and one for **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:348).
- As subsequent marriages took place across the Madinan era, additional rooms were appended, ultimately totaling nine chambers.
- **Architecture**: Four rooms were constructed of mud-adobe brick with outer courtyards of palm branches, while five were built from rough dry-stone masonry (*ḥijārah marḍūmah*) plastered with clay, roofed with palm branches, and shaded with coarse haircloth curtains (*musūḥ al-shaʿr*) rather than wooden panel doors (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:348–349).
- When the Umayyad governor [[ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz]] was ordered by al-Walīd b. ʿAbd al-Malik to demolish and incorporate these chambers into the Mosque expansion in 88 AH, the elder scholars and sons of the Companions—including [[Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān]] and Khārijah b. Zayd—wept openly, wishing the humble rooms had been preserved so future generations might witness the austere simplicity with which the Master of Creation lived (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:349).

## 6. The Ṣuffah: Academy of the Devout Poor
At the rear northern end of the Mosque, a sheltered portico was constructed known as **al-Ṣuffah** (giving rise to the title **[[Ahl al-Ṣuffah]]**) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).
- It served as an open-air residential college and barracks for unmarried, displaced, and impoverished Muhājirūn who had no clan protection, property, or trade in Medina.
- Companions such as **[[Abū Hurayrah]]**, **[[Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī]]**, and **[[Ṣuhayb b. Sinān]]** lived under the direct tutelage of the Prophet ﷺ, dedicating themselves exclusively to memorizing the Qurʾān, preserving prophetic hadith, and standing ready for immediate military deployment in scouting detachments and expeditions.
- The Prophet ﷺ shared his daily gifts of food with them and forbade accepting zakāt for himself or his household, ensuring that Ahl al-Ṣuffah were sustained by the community.

## 7. Institutional Role in the Early State
With the completion of the Mosque, the early Islamic commonwealth possessed its foundational civic and religious nexus:
1. **Spiritual Axis**: The central congregation where the five daily prayers and Friday khutbah were instituted.
2. **Legislative & Judicial Seat**: The venue where divine revelations were recited, legal disputes arbitrated, and sentences executed.
3. **Diplomatic & Military Headquarters**: The assembly point where banners (*liwāʾ*) were tied for departing expeditions, military detachments reviewed, and tribal emissaries received in state (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:338).
4. **Civic Cohesion**: Directly following the construction, the Prophet ﷺ enacted the grand institutional fraternal alliance between the Muhājirūn and Anṣār (**[[al-Muʾākhāh]]**) and promulgated the constitutional charter (**[[Dustūr al-Madīnah]]**).
