# Salmān al-Fārisī (سلمان الفارسي) ﵁
*Abū ʿAbdillāh al-Fārisī — Salmān al-Khayr, Salmān Ibn al-Islām, Architect of the Trench, Noble Son of the Prophetic Household*

> [!NOTE] Classical Scholarly Provenance
> This biography is anchored directly in ***[[Subul al-Hudā war-Rashād]]*** by **[[al-Ṣāliḥī]]** (vol. 3, pp. 370–375; vol. 4, pp. 364–366) and ***[[al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah]]*** by **[[Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī]]** (vol. 3, pp. 118–120, entry no. 3369).

---

## 1. Lineage, Persian Origins, and The Quest for Divine Truth

### Origins in Persia
- **Name and Origin**: Originally named Mābah b. Būdh (or Bihbūd) in Persia (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:119). Born into an aristocratic Zoroastrian (*Majūsī*) household in Rāmhurmuz (or Jayy in Iṣfahān), where his father was the magistrate and chief of the village (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:119).
- **Kunyas and Titles**: Abū ʿAbdillāh; affectionately celebrated by the Companions as **«سَلْمَانُ الْخَيْرِ»** (*Salmān al-Khayr* — Salmān the Good) and **«سَلْمَانُ ابْنُ الْإِسْلَامِ»** (*Salmān Son of Islam*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:118).
- **Marital Tie**: In Islam, he married a noblewoman from the Arabian tribe of Kindah (*tazawwaja imraʾatan min Kindah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:119).

### The Perilous Journey Across the Levant
Yearning for absolute truth, Salmān abandoned the fire-worship of his ancestors and escaped from his father’s estate. For years he traveled across the Near East, attaching himself to ascetic Christian bishops and hermits in Damascus, Mosul, Nisibis, and ʿAmmūriyyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370–372). 

On his deathbed, his final Christian mentor in ʿAmmūriyyah informed him that no true exponent of pristine monotheism survived on earth, but that the time had arrived for the advent of the final Prophet in the land of Arabia, an exile to a palm-bearing territory between two volcanic tracts (*ḥarratān*). The monk imparted three infallible physical criteria:
1. He accepts and eats of gifts (*hadiyyah*).
2. He does not consume charity (*ṣadaqah*).
3. Between his shoulder blades lies the physical Seal of Prophethood (*Khātam al-Nubuwwah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:372).

### Enslavement in Arabia
Salmān hired a passing caravan of [[Banū Kalb]] to guide him to Arabia. When they reached Wādī al-Qurā, the merchants betrayed him and sold him into slavery to a Jewish man, who later sold him to his cousin of Banū Qurayẓah in Yathrib (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:372; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:119). Salmān passed through more than ten masters (*tadāwalahu biḍʿata ʿashara sayyidan*) during his long years of bondage (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:119).

## 2. Verification of the Prophetic Signs & Emancipation

While tending date palms in Medina, Salmān heard of the arrival of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at Qubāʾ during **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:373).

Salmān tested the three criteria in precise sequence:
1. **Charity**: At Qubāʾ, Salmān offered a dish of fresh dates, saying: *“This is charity (*ṣadaqah*) for you and your companions.”* The Prophet ﷺ instructed the Companions to eat, but kept his own hands back (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:373). Salmān noted: *“This is the first sign.”*
2. **Gift**: When the Prophet ﷺ entered Medina, Salmān brought another offering, saying: *“I observed that you do not consume charity; this is a gift (*hadiyyah*) to honor you.”* The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ placed his hand into the platter and ate alongside his Companions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:373). Salmān noted: *“This is the second sign.”*
3. **The Seal of Prophethood**: Attending a funeral with the Prophet ﷺ at **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]**, Salmān walked behind the Prophet ﷺ attempting to peer beneath his cloak. Divining his intent, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ loosened his mantle from his back. Salmān beheld the sacred physical Seal of Prophethood between his shoulder blades. Overwhelmed with weeping, Salmān cast himself upon the Prophet's feet, kissing the Seal and recounting the entire epic of his search for truth (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:373).

### The Miraculous Emancipation Contract (*al-Kitābah*)
Because of his bondage, Salmān was prevented from witnessing Badr and Uḥud (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:119). The Prophet ﷺ instructed him: *“Contract for your emancipation, O Salmān!”* 

His Jewish master demanded an onerous ransom: planting **three hundred date palm saplings** and paying **forty ounces (*ūqiyyah*) of pure gold** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:374). 

The Companions gathered the palm saplings, and the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ personally excavated the holes and planted every single shoot with his own hands; every palm took root and bore dates without a single sapling withering (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:374). The Prophet ﷺ then provided a small nugget of gold the size of a pigeon’s egg from an expedition spoil, weighed it against the forty ounces, and miraculously settled the entire debt, securing Salmān’s freedom (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:374).

## 3. Fraternization & The Trench (*Ghazwat al-Khandaq*) (5 AH)

Upon achieving freedom, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ paired Salmān in fraternization (*al-muʾākhāh*) with **[[Abū al-Dardāʾ]]** ﵁ (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:119). The Prophet ﷺ later affirmed to Abū al-Dardāʾ:
> «سَلْمَانُ أَفْقَهُ مِنْكَ!»
> *"Salmān is more learned in jurisprudence than you!"* (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:119).

### Architect of the Trench
In Shawwāl 5 AH, when an unprecedented confederate army of ten thousand polytheists marched to annihilate Medina in **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]**, Salmān stepped forward in the council of war and proposed a military strategy unknown to Arabian warfare:
> «يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، إِنَّا كُنَّا بِفَارِسَ إِذَا حُوصِرْنَا خَنْدَقْنَا عَلَيْنَا!»
> *"O Messenger of Allāh! In Persia, whenever we were besieged by cavalry, we dug a trench around ourselves!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:364).

The Prophet ﷺ enthusiastically endorsed his strategy, demarcating forty cubits for every ten men along the northern perimeter of Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:364–366). 

### "Salmān is from us, the People of the Household!"
Salmān worked with tireless physical power, digging deep into the rocky earth. Marveling at his strength and virtue, a loving rivalry arose between the Emigrants and the Helpers (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:365):
- The Muhājirūn shouted: *“Salmān is from us!”*
- The Anṣār shouted: *“Salmān is from us!”*

Hearing their debate, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ stepped between them and delivered the supreme declaration of spiritual ennoblement:
> «سَلْمَانُ مِنَّا أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ!»
> *"Salmān is from us, the People of the Household!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:365; *al-Mustadrak*, no. 6541).

He was also named by the Prophet ﷺ as one of the four whom Allāh specifically commanded him to love: ʿAlī, al-Miqdād, Abū Dharr, and Salmān (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:119).

## 4. Ascetic Governorship of al-Madāʾin & Demise

Following the conquest of Iraq, Caliph **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ appointed Salmān as the governor of al-Madāʾin (Ctesiphon), the ancient imperial capital of Persia (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:375; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:119). 

Despite commanding a governor's stipend of five thousand dirhams, Salmān gave every single dirham away in charity to the poor (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:120). He refused imperial palace quarters, walked among the public wearing a patched cloak, and sustained himself entirely by plaiting date-palm fronds into baskets (*yansiju al-khawṣ*) to sell in the market for his daily sustenance (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:120).

### Passing and Legacy
When death approached him in al-Madāʾin, Salmān wept. When asked why he wept, he replied: *"I do not weep out of love for this world, but because the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ took a pledge from us: 'Let the provision of any one of you from this world be like the provision of a traveler!' and behold all these vessels around me!"* His visitors looked around his chamber and found nothing except an ablution vessel and a prayer mat (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:375).

He instructed his wife to sprinkle a pouch of musk around his deathbed, saying: *"There are beings who visit me now who do not eat food, but love fragrant scent."* 

Salmān passed away in al-Madāʾin in **36 AH** (or 37 AH; and according to others shortly before Ibn Masʿūd) (*al-Iṣābah*, 3:119–120). Classical authorities noted his exceptional longevity, recording that he was among the longest-lived of all the Companions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:375; *al-Iṣābah*, 3:119).
