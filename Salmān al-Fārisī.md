# Salmān al-Fārisī (سلمان الفارسي) ﵁
*Abū ʿAbdillāh al-Fārisī — Salmān al-Khayr, Architect of the Trench, Noble Son of the Household*

## 1. Lineage & The Quest for Divine Truth
He is Salmān al-Fārisī (originally named Mābah b. Būdhakhshān in Persia; kunya: Abū ʿAbdillāh; titled: *Salmān al-Khayr*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370). Born into an aristocratic Zoroastrian priesthood family in Rāmhurmuz (or Iṣfahān), his father was the magistrate of the village.

Longing for absolute divine truth, Salmān abandoned his familial wealth and traveled through the Levant, serving ascetic Christian bishops in Damascus, Mosul, Nisibis, and ʿAmmūriyyah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:370–372). On his deathbed, his final teacher informed him that no true exponent of monotheism remained, but that the time had arrived for the emergence of the final Prophet in the land of the Arabs, giving Salmān three infallible physical criteria:
1. He eats of gifts (*hadiyyah*).
2. He does not consume charity (*ṣadaqah*).
3. Between his shoulder blades is the Seal of Prophethood (*Khātam al-Nubuwwah*) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:372).

Joining an Arab trading caravan from Banū Kalb to reach Arabia, they betrayed him at Wādī al-Qurā, selling him into enslavement to a Jewish master who subsequently sold him to a cousin in Medina (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:372).

## 2. Meeting the Prophet ﷺ & Verification
Upon the Prophet's arrival in Qubāʾ during **[[al-Hijrah ilā al-Madīnah]]**, Salmān brought a dish of dates saying: *“This is charity for you and your companions.”* The Prophet ﷺ told his companions to eat, but refrained from eating himself (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:373). When the Prophet ﷺ moved into Medina, Salmān brought another dish saying: *“I saw that you do not eat charity, so this is a gift.”* The Prophet ﷺ ate with his companions (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:373).

Finally, attending a funeral at al-Baqīʿ, Salmān walked behind the Prophet ﷺ trying to glimpse his back. Understanding his intent, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ cast his cloak from his shoulders, revealing the physical Seal of Prophethood between his shoulder blades. Salmān threw himself upon the Prophet's feet, kissing the Seal and weeping profusely as he recounted his life's journey (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:373).

The Prophet ﷺ arranged his emancipation contract (*kitābah*) from his Jewish owner, which demanded planting three hundred date palms and paying forty ounces (*ūqiyyah*) of gold (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:374). The companions gathered the saplings, and the Prophet ﷺ placed every single shoot into the soil with his own blessed hands; every single palm lived and bore fruit, and the Prophet ﷺ provided a piece of gold from which the debt was liquidated (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:374).

## 3. The Trench (*al-Khandaq*) & Adoption into the Household
Having missed Badr and Uḥud due to enslavement, Salmān participated in every subsequent campaign starting with **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]** (5 AH) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:374).

When news arrived of the 10,000-man polytheist coalition marching to storm Medina, the companions were seized with panic. Salmān proposed an ingenious Persian defensive innovation never before seen in Arabia:
> *"O Messenger of Allāh, in Persia, when we were besieged by cavalry, we dug a trench around us!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:364).

The Prophet ﷺ adopted his counsel, personally taking up a pickaxe alongside Salmān to dig the ditch north of the city (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:364–366). Seeing Salmān’s extraordinary physical strength and dedication, the Muhājirūn cried: *“Salmān is from us!”* while the Anṣār cried: *“Salmān is from us!”* The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ settled the matter with an immortal decree:
> «سَلْمَانُ مِنَّا أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ!»
> *"Salmān is from us, the People of the Household!"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:365; *al-Mustadrak*, no. 6541).

## 4. Demise & Legacy
Under the Caliphate of ʿUmar, Salmān was appointed governor of al-Madāʾin (the former Sasanian capital in Iraq). Despite receiving a governor's stipend of five thousand dirhams, he distributed every single coin to the poor, weaving baskets from palm leaves to earn his daily bread (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:375).

He passed away in al-Madāʾin around 35 AH or 36 AH, living beyond one hundred years of age, leaving behind only an ablution jug, a prayer mat, and a pouch of celestial musk given to him by the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:375).
