# ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb (عمر بن الخطاب) ﵁
*Abū Ḥafṣ al-ʿAdawī al-Qurashī — al-Fārūq (The Criterion), Amīr al-Muʾminīn, Second Caliph*

## 1. Lineage, Ancestry & Physical Description
### Patrilineal Descent & Clan
He is **ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb b. Nufayl b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā b. Riyāḥ b. ʿAbdillāh b. Qurṭ b. Razāḥ b. ʿAdī b. Kaʿb b. Luʾayy b. Ghālib al-Qurashī al-ʿAdawī** (kunya: **Abū Ḥafṣ**; honorifics: *al-Fārūq*, *Amīr al-Muʾminīn*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:237, 263). 

His genealogical line converges with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ at their shared forefather **Kaʿb b. Luʾayy** in the seventh generation (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484). Through **Banū ʿAdī b. Kaʿb**, his clan held the hereditary pre-Islamic office of diplomatic embassy (*al-sifārah*) for **[[Quraysh]]**; whenever tribal disputes or warfare erupted among the Arabs, ʿUmar was dispatched as the paramount arbitrator and envoy (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:263).

### Maternal Heritage & Clan Ties
- **Mother**: **Ḥantamah bint Hāshim b. al-Mughīrah b. ʿAbdillāh b. ʿUmar b. Makhzūm al-Qurashiyyah al-Makhzūmiyyah**, according to the genealogist al-Zubayr b. Bakkār (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:263).
- **Alternate Transmission**: Ibn Mandah, Abū Nuʿaym, and Ibn Isḥāq transmitted that she was Ḥantamah bint Hishām, making her the sister of Abū Jahl ʿAmr b. Hishām, while al-Zubayr b. Bakkār maintained that she was his first cousin (bint Hāshim) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:263).
- Through his mother, ʿUmar was intimately linked to the aristocratic leadership of **[[Banū Makhzūm]]**, one of the most powerful and martial clans of Quraysh.

### Birth & Physical Appearance
ʿUmar was born four years after the Greater Fijār War (*Ḥarb al-Fijār al-Aʿẓam*), thirty years prior to the Prophetic mission (~40 BH / 584 CE), or thirteen years after the Year of the Elephant (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:263). 

He possessed an imposing, towering stature:
- **Build and Height**: Exceptionally tall and broad-shouldered (*ṭawīlan jasīman*); whenever he walked among people, he towered over them as though mounted on a riding beast (*farʿa al-nās ka-annahu ʿalā dābbah*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:263). He was *arwaḥ* (his heels drew near when walking) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:485).
- **Physical Feats**: Blessed with extraordinary agility and strength; he would grip his left ear with his right hand and vault cleanly onto horseback in a single motion, looking as though he were molded to the mount's back (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:485).
- **Complexion**: Originally fair-skinned with reddish undertones (*abyaḍ*); during **ʿĀm al-Ramādah** (the Year of Drought and Famine, 18 AH), he renounced meat, butter, and milk, subsisting strictly on bread and olive oil until his skin darkened into a deep brown (*ādam*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484; *Subul al-Hudā*, 11:263).

## 2. Marriages, Kinship Networks & Progeny
ʿUmar contracted multiple strategic marriages across the foremost clans of Quraysh and the Anṣār, linking his household to the Banū Jumaḥ, Banū Makhzūm, Banū Khuzāʿah, Banū ʿAdī, the Prophet's Ahl al-Bayt, and the Anṣār:

1. **Zaynab bint Maẓʿūn al-Jumaḥiyyah** ﵂:
   - Sister of the foremost Companion **ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn** ﵁ and Qudāmah b. Maẓʿūn ﵁ of **[[Banū Jumaḥ]]**. She embraced Islam early in Makkah and died before the Hijrah or early in Medina (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:85, 161).
   - **Children**: Bore him the celebrated jurist and ascetic **[[ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar]]** ﵄, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān the elder (*al-Akbar*), and the Mother of the Believers **[[Ḥafṣah bint ʿUmar]]** ﵂, wife of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:85, 161).
2. **Mulaykah (Umm Kulthūm) bint Jarwal al-Khuzāʿiyyah**:
   - From Banū Khuzāʿah. She remained a polytheist and was divorced by ʿUmar following the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyah in 6 AH upon the revelation of Sūrat al-Mumtaḥanah (60:10: *«And hold not to the ties of disbelieving women»*) (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:464).
   - **Children**: Bore him ʿUbaydullāh b. ʿUmar (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:464).
3. **Quraybah bint Abī Umayyah al-Makhzūmiyyah**:
   - Sister of the Mother of the Believers **[[Umm Salamah]]** ﵂ and daughter of the Makkan chieftain Abū Umayyah b. al-Mughīrah. She also remained polytheist and was separated from him under the same Qurʾānic injunction in 6 AH (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:464).
4. **Jamīlah bint Thābit b. Abī al-Aqlaḥ al-Anṣāriyyah** ﵂:
   - Noble Anṣārī woman of the Aws tribe; sister of the heroic archer and martyr **[[ʿĀṣim b. Thābit]]** ﵁. Originally named ʿĀṣiyah ("the rebellious"), the Prophet ﷺ renamed her Jamīlah ("the beautiful") (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:67–68).
   - **Children**: Bore him **[[ʿĀṣim b. ʿUmar]]**, who became the father of Umm ʿĀṣim, the mother of the celebrated Umayyad Caliph ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:3; 8:67).
5. **[[Umm Kulthūm bint ʿAlī]]** ﵂:
   - Daughter of **[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]** ﵁ and **[[Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ]]** ﵂, and granddaughter of the Prophet ﷺ. ʿUmar sought her hand out of deep yearning to be connected to the bloodline and kinship of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, who stated: *"Every lineage and bond is severed on the Day of Resurrection except my lineage and my bond."* He gave her a marriage dowry (*mahr*) of forty thousand dirhams (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:464–465).
   - **Children**: Bore him **Zayd b. ʿUmar** and **Ruqayyah bint ʿUmar** (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:465).
6. **ʿĀtikah bint Zayd b. ʿAmr b. Nufayl** ﵂:
   - His paternal first cousin; sister of the Ten Promised Paradise Companion **[[Saʿīd b. Zayd]]** ﵁ and daughter of the pre-Islamic monotheist (*Ḥanīf*) Zayd b. ʿAmr b. Nufayl (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:227–228).
   - **Children**: Bore him **Iyāḍ b. ʿUmar** (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:227).
7. **Luhayyah and Fukayhah**:
   - Luhayyah bore him ʿAbd al-Raḥmān the younger (*al-Awsaṭ/al-Aṣghar*), and Fukayhah bore him Zaynab (*al-Iṣābah*, 5:35, 41).

## 3. Key Sīrah Milestones
### The Dramatic Conversion in Makkah (6 BH)
Before his conversion, ʿUmar was one of the fiercest adversaries of the early Muslim community. Aware of his formidable strength, the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ supplicated to Allāh:
> «اللَّهُمَّ أَعِزَّ الْإِسْلَامَ بِأَحَبِّ هَذَيْنِ الرَّجُلَيْنِ إِلَيْكَ: بِأَبِي جَهْلِ بْنِ هِشَامٍ، أَوْ بِعُمَرَ بْنِ الْخَطَّابِ!»
> *"O Allāh, strengthen Islam with the more beloved of these two men to You: Abū Jahl b. Hishām or ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:485; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:365; 11:263).

Departing with sword drawn intending to assassinate the Prophet ﷺ, he was diverted toward the home of his sister Fāṭimah bint al-Khaṭṭāb and her husband **[[Saʿīd b. Zayd]]** ﵄, who had secretly accepted Islam. Overhearing their recitation of Sūrat Ṭā-Hā, ʿUmar stormed the residence and struck his sister until blood flowed. Stricken by remorse at the sight of her blood, he asked to read the parchment. Reading the opening verses of Ṭā-Hā, his heart was conquered by the divine text. He proceeded immediately to **[[Dār al-Arqam]]**, where he pronounced the testimony of faith before the Prophet ﷺ (*Subul al-Hudā*, 2:365). 

Upon his conversion, the Prophet ﷺ emerged flanked by ʿUmar on one side and **[[Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib]]** ﵁ on the other to pray publicly at the Kaʿbah, prompting the Prophet ﷺ to confer upon him the title **al-Fārūq** (the one through whom Allāh distinguished between truth and falsehood) (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:486; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:365–367). ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd ﵁ recorded:
> «مَا زِلْنَا أَعِزَّةً مُنْذُ أَسْلَمَ عُمَرُ»
> *"We never ceased to possess honor and might from the moment ʿUmar embraced Islam!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 4:484; *Subul al-Hudā*, 2:365; 11:264).

### The Defiant Hijrah
While the believers departed secretly in fear of persecutions, ʿUmar strapped on his sword, slung his bow across his shoulder, carried his arrows, and circumambulated the Kaʿbah openly seven times. Standing before the assemblies of Quraysh, he announced:
> *"Whoever desires that his mother be bereaved of him, his children orphaned, and his wife widowed, let him meet me behind this valley!"* 
Not a single polytheist dared pursue him (*Subul al-Hudā*, 3:233; 11:265).

### Military Service across the Ghazawāt
- **Veteran of Badr (2 AH)**: Stood at the forefront of **[[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā]]**. Demonstrating utter loyalty to the divine bond above all tribal affiliations, he slew his polytheist maternal uncle, al-ʿĀṣ b. Hishām b. al-Mughīrah, in direct combat (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:92; 11:266).
- **Steadfast Defense at Uḥud (3 AH)**: Protected the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ during the disarray of **[[Ghazwat Uḥud]]**, rallying the companions on the mountain slope (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:210). In the aftermath, he conducted the celebrated exchange with **[[Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb]]** across the ravine, shouting: *“Allāh is the Most Exalted and Most Majestic! Our dead are in Paradise, and your dead are in the Fire!”* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:226; 11:266).
- **Khandaq & Ḥudaybiyah**: Maintained frontline guard duty along the trench during **[[Ghazwat al-Khandaq]]** (5 AH) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 4:370). At **[[Ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyah]]** (6 AH), he was an outspoken counselor who took the solemn pledge of **[[Bayʿat al-Riḍwān]]** beneath the tree (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:50).
- **Campaigns of Khaybar & Tabūk**: Took part in **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]** (7 AH) and surrendered half his entire worldly estate for **Jaysh al-ʿUsrah** during **[[Ghazwat Tabūk]]** (9 AH) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 5:124, 445).

### Muwāfaqāt ʿUmar (Qurʾānic Confirmations)
Multiple Qurʾānic verses were revealed in direct confirmation of ʿUmar's judgment and suggestions (*Muwāfaqāt*), including:
1. Adopting the Station of Abraham (*Maqām Ibrāhīm*) as a place of prayer (2:125).
2. The command of the veil (*ḥijāb*) for the Mothers of the Believers (33:53).
3. The prohibition of offering funeral prayers over hypocrites (*al-munāfiqūn*) (9:84).
4. The ruling regarding the treatment of the captives of Badr (8:67) (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:268–270).

## 4. Caliphate, Martyrdom & Legacy
Elected Second Caliph in Jumādā al-Ākhirah 13 AH following the designation of **[[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]]** ﵁, ʿUmar was the first to receive the formal title **Amīr al-Muʾminīn** (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:263, 275). His decade-long administration established the foundational civic institutions of the Islamic state:
- Established the official Islamic Hijrī calendar dating from the migration (*Subul al-Hudā*, 12:36).
- Organized the central treasury (*Bayt al-Māl*), administrative registers (*dawāwīn*), postal relays, and provincial judicial oversight (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
- Directed the liberation of Jerusalem (*Bayt al-Maqdis*), Damascus, the Levant, Egypt, and the collapse of the Sasanian empire at al-Yarmūk, al-Qādisiyyah, Jalūlāʾ, and Nahāwand (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

### Martyrdom and Burial
On Wednesday, four nights remaining in Dhū al-Ḥijjah 23 AH (26 Dhū al-Ḥijjah 23 AH), while initiating the dawn prayer in al-Masjid al-Nabawī, ʿUmar was attacked by Abū Luʾluʾah Fīrūz, a Persian slave of al-Mughīrah b. Shuʿbah (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:274). The assassin struck him with a poisoned two-bladed dagger in the shoulder and flank, and wounded thirteen other worshippers (seven of whom died) before taking his own life when trapped under an Iraqi cloak (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:274–275). 

When offered milk and it seeped from his abdominal wound, ʿUmar realized the injury was fatal. He praised Allāh that his death had not been at the hands of anyone claiming Islam (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:274). He appointed a consultative council (*Shūrā*) composed of six Companions (**[[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]]**, **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]**, **[[Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh]]**, **[[al-Zubayr b. al-ʿAwwām]]**, **[[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]]**, and **[[ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf]]**) to select the next Caliph (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).

He dispatched his son ʿAbdullāh to ask permission of **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ to be buried beside his two companions. She granted him her own reserved place, saying: *"I had wished it for myself, but today I surely give him preference over myself"* (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275). ʿUmar died at the age of sixty-three and was buried on Sunday, the new moon of Muḥarram 24 AH, beside the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq in the sacred chamber (*Subul al-Hudā*, 11:275).
