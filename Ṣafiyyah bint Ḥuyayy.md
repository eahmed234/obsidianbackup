# Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyayy (صفية بنت حيي) ﵂
*Umm al-Muʾminīn — Daughter of Chieftains, Descendant of the Prophet Aaron, and Devoted Matron of the Household*

## 1. Lineage & Ancestral Nobility
She is Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab b. Saʿnah b. Thaʿlabah b. ʿUbayd b. Kaʿb b. Abī Ḥabīb al-Naḍariyyah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:210, no. 11407; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:132; 11:210). She traced her direct patrilineal descent through the Israelite priestly tribe of Levi b. Jacob to the Prophet Hārūn (Aaron) b. ʿImrān ﵊, brother of Mūsā (Moses) ﵊ (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:210). Her father, Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab, was the supreme leader and chieftain of the Banū al-Naḍīr. See **[[Banū al-Naḍīr]]**.

Her mother was **Barrah bint Samawʾal**, sister of Rifāʿah b. Samawʾal of Banū Qurayẓah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:210).

Prior to Islam, she was married first to Sallām b. Mishkam, a prominent poet and leader of Banū al-Naḍīr, and subsequently to Kinānah b. al-Rabīʿ b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq, the treasurer of Khaybar who was slain during its siege (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:210; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:132).

## 2. The Vision of the Moon & Marriage at Khaybar (7 AH)
While residing at Khaybar as a young bride, Ṣafiyyah saw in a dream as though the sun or moon had detached itself from the heavens and fallen into her lap (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:211; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:132). When she recounted the vision to her husband Kinānah, he struck her face in fury, crying: *“You desire none other than this King of Yathrib who has descended upon us!”*, leaving a persistent green mark on her cheek (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:211; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:132).

Following the fall of the fortresses of **[[Ghazwat Khaybar]]** (7 AH), Ṣafiyyah was taken amongst the captives. Recognizing her dignity as the daughter of chieftains and mistress of her people (*sayyidat qawmihā*), the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ offered her Islam, which she embraced with sincere conviction (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:210–211). The Prophet ﷺ emancipated her and made her freedom her bridal gift (*ṣadāquhā ʿitquhā*), wedding her at Sadd al-Ṣahbāʾ outside Khaybar (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:210; *Subul al-Hudā*, 5:132). She was not yet seventeen years of age at the time of her marriage (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:212). The wedding feast (*walīmah*) was celebrated with dates, ghee, and dried cheese (*ays*) poured onto leather spreads for the entire army (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:211).

## 3. Arrival in Medina & Prophetic Defense
Upon her arrival in Medina, she was accommodated in the residence of Ḥārithah b. al-Nuʿmān. The women of the Anṣār and **[[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]]** ﵂ came veiled to behold her famed elegance and beauty (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:211). Generous by nature, Ṣafiyyah arrived wearing golden earrings (*khūṣah min dhahab*), which she immediately gifted to **[[Fāṭimah al-Zahrāʾ]]** ﵂ and other women of the household (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:211).

When ʿĀʾishah and Ḥafṣah ﵄ once expressed pride in their Qurayshite descent and closeness to the Prophet ﷺ, Ṣafiyyah wept. The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ entered, and hearing of it, comforted her with the sublime words:
> «أَلَا قُلْتِ: وَكَيْفَ تَكُونَانِ خَيْرًا مِنِّي وَزَوْجِي مُحَمَّدٌ، وَأَبِي هَارُونُ، وَعَمِّي مُوسَى؟!»
> *"Why did you not say: 'How can you two be better than me, when my husband is Muḥammad, my father is Hārūn, and my uncle is Mūsā?!'"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:211; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī).

## 4. Forbearance, Sincerity & Defense of ʿUthmān
Ṣafiyyah was celebrated for her gentle forbearance (*ḥilm*), intelligence, and scrupulous piety:
- **Pardon of Her Maidservant**: A maidservant falsely reported to Caliph **[[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]]** ﵁ that Ṣafiyyah still observed the Jewish Sabbath and maintained contact with Jewish clans. When ʿUmar inquired, Ṣafiyyah answered: *“As for the Sabbath, I have not kept it since Allāh replaced it for me with Friday; and as for the Jews, they are my kin, and I uphold the ties of kinship (*ṣilat al-raḥim*).”* Turning to her maid, she asked: *“What impelled you to do this?”* The girl replied: *“Satan.”* Ṣafiyyah responded: *“Go, you are free for the sake of Allāh!”* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:211–212).
- **Sincerity at the Prophet's Terminal Illness**: During the final illness of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, Ṣafiyyah stood beside his bed and wept, saying: *“By Allāh, O Prophet of Allāh, I wish that what afflicts you were upon me!”* When certain wives exchanged skeptical glances, the Prophet ﷺ rebuked them sharply:
  > «مَضْمِضْنَ! مِنْ أَيِّ شَيْءٍ؟ مِنْ تَغَامُزِكُنَّ بِهَا، وَاللَّهِ إِنَّهَا لَصَادِقَةٌ!»
  > *"Rinse your mouths! From what? From your skeptical glances at her! By Allāh, she is speaking the absolute truth!"* (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:212).
- **Heroic Aid during the Siege of ʿUthmān (35 AH)**: When rebels blockaded the home of Caliph **[[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]]** ﵁, withholding water and provisions, Ṣafiyyah mounted a mule to break through the cordon and deliver relief. When the rebel commander al-Ashtar al-Nakhaʿī struck the face of her mule, she withdrew and ordered a wooden plank bridge built between the roof of her dwelling and ʿUthmān's house, across which she personally ferried food and water to the beleaguered Caliph (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:212).

## 5. Demise & Burial
Ṣafiyyah passed away in Medina in 50 AH (or 52 AH according to al-Wāqidī) during the caliphate of Muʿāwiyah (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:212). She was laid to rest in **[[Jannat al-Baqīʿ]]** alongside the other Mothers of the Believers (*al-Iṣābah*, 8:212).
